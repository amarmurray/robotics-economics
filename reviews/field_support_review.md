## Independent source checks

**Guro medication delivery.** Retrieved the [EuropePMC primary JATS](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13039597/fullTextXML) directly and inspected §§2.1–2.4, 2.6–2.6.1, 3.1–3.5 and 4.5. Its electronic publication date is 31 March 2026. The observation period is 18–29 June 2025. The platform is one DOGU IROI with a dedicated module for the TK50M staff-only elevator; the one-hertz logging interval is supported.

Section 2.4 supports 135 requests, 13 urgent exclusions and 122 eligible missions, split 80 weekday/42 weekend. A trained operator accompanied the robot for medication-handling safety and recorded co-occupants/cargo. The same section defines failure as more than one minute stationary **or** manual assistance due to obstruction. Consequently, 14 failed missions need not equal 14 human interventions. Section 2.3 separately supports pharmacist loading/start, staff dispatch and retrieval, and occasional nurse retrieval. The source does not quantify accompaniment, routine handling or recovery minutes. The dossier correctly preserves those costs as unknown without asserting that a research escort must remain forever.

Section 3.2 reports four corridor failures, two elevator-call communication failures and eight elevator blockages, totaling 14. Section 3.4 analyzes 108 completed trials. These fit 122 minus 14, but §3.1 independently reports 87.03% overall success. The disagreement is real and remains unresolved. No different denominator was recovered. The package leaves its calibration probability null and labels 108/122 an audit contrast rather than a corrected truth.

The 59.01% elevator-operating-rate cutoff, 95.52% lower-congestion subgroup result and filtering of non-elevator failures to approach 100% all appear in §3.1. They do not constitute a prospective randomized dispatch-policy effect. Section 4.5 makes the threshold site-specific and says multi-robot operations were outside scope. The package correctly avoids transferring it to other hospitals or treating deferred/urgent work as free. Sections 2.6–2.6.1 say the survey follows four weeks, unlike the reported mission dates; that second source ambiguity is retained. The 22 respondents and absence of significant overall NASA-TLX group differences are supported, with no conversion to labor minutes.

**Qilu endoscope delivery.** Independently fetched the [publisher page](https://www.giejournal.org/article/S0016-5107(25)02075-9/fulltext) and [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41076017/) through Exa. Despite `/fulltext` in the URL, the publisher extraction supplies abstract and references, not the detailed FTE or financial methods. A separate web-reader attempt returned HTTP 403. The abstract confirms one manual day randomly selected within each five-day week, four robot days, 21 May–21 November 2024, 5,393 manual and 21,048 AMR-assisted deliveries, and eight robots. Day-level assignment must not be described as independent randomization of each delivery.

The four phase means, their reported dispersion values, `P < .001`, 0.15% AMR delivery-failure rate, no unsuccessful manual delivery, 2.18 FTE workload reduction and approximately 12.8% ROI all match the abstract. The author preserves the distinction between the verified headline and unverified calculation methods. The absence of an exact failure numerator, phase sample counts, failed-case timing rules and full support boundary remains visible. Transport does not establish autonomous reprocessing or clinical-equivalence outcomes.

**ChristianaCare.** Independently retrieved the [original full study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11892717/) and confirmed 23 observation sessions, 33 observation-hours, 89 end-to-end deliveries, and 107 stated interventions. The listed categories sum to `72 + 20 + 4 + 7 + 7 + 1 = 111`. They are not reconciled by later cumulative deliveries.
