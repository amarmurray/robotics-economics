## Limits that remain material

Effective experience is assumed to produce a known, validated local linear cost reduction. Raw data, benchmark improvement and promised future qualification savings do not establish that transition. Rights, quality and cost of collecting the experience must enter eligibility or the initial net-cash vector. Negative transfer, stochastic candidate failure, nonlinear learning, new-service eligibility and delayed commissioning are not solved by this corollary.

Future provider/operator capture remains a stipulated implementable contract, with the customer's output demand inherited from the earlier model. Individual payoffs are attractive only against the stated alternatives. The model does not solve bargaining or demonstrate a welfare improvement, and a separate-firm contract is not a counterexample to Stein's different authority/incentive assumptions.
