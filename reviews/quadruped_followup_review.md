## Consequential checks that pass

**385 instruments and incomplete service coverage.** Fresh primary chapter text retrieved through Exa confirms §6 evaluates 385 instruments across the pump, water-treatment and HVA/C rooms. It reports 92% and 85% of points can be successfully inspected in the first two rooms and 54% scanned in HVA/C. Hidden, wall-facing valves are given as a reason for the last result. The paper passage does not supply each room's denominator. The follow-up correctly refuses a pooled percentage, a 385-completed-inspections claim, a repeatability/defect-detection rate, or an economy-wide labor coefficient. Its declared outcome is an analytical service target, not an observed full acceptance pass.

**Configuration, participant interests and operating restrictions.** The primary chapter identifies TenneT coauthors Marcel Potz and Lars Gerstenberg alongside supplier authors; the ETH wrapper gives publication January 2021 and the introduction dates deployment to 2018. Figure 7 identifies B300 with a prototype inspection payload. Section 3.1 supports the roughly half-site energized-access restriction and 50 interesting rooms out of approximately 200; neither is a labor share. Section 7 leaves doors, platform-control integration and long-distance communication unresolved and frames daily inspection as a future possibility. The dossier correctly does not inherit current ANYmal X capability or infer a fully integrated unattended service from local wireless operation. I retrieved these substantive passages and §4.3's control-interface text through the search index, not a direct chapter PDF; the review is not an exhaustive audit of every commissioning sentence or figure.

**Different route and calendar descriptions remain different.** Chapter §6 says more than 30 autonomous missions during two weeks and illustrates a 19-point route. The [manufacturer's 25 October 2018 post](https://www.anybotics.com/news/worlds-first-autonomous-offshore-robot/), introduction and “Successful Pilot Installation,” says a one-week September pilot and a video with 16 points. No inspected text establishes whether these are differing subsets/phases or inconsistent descriptions. The follow-up preserves both, supplies no success denominator, and neither sums nor averages them. More than 30 missions is not proof of 30 successful attempts or two weeks of autonomous uptime.

**The 36-point card is old planning, not measured 2024 output.** I independently downloaded the [TenneT presentation hosted by NEN](https://www.nen.nl/media/wysiwyg/Presentatie_TenneT_Digitale_transformatie_onder_hoogspanning.pdf), extracted the PDF text and visually inspected physical pages/slides 10 and 13. Slide 1 is dated 18 September 2024. Slide 10 visibly retains the 24 October 2022 footer. Action 2 calls for defining 36 frequently inspected points that the robot can inspect well; actions 7–8 call for testing output quality/cost savings and reviewing results/scale-up. Cost, benefit and result fields are blank. Its generic provider illustrations and “In use” graphic do not establish this cohort's retained operating status. Slide 11 states nine months, January–September 2024; slide 13 depicts an ANYbotics-branded robot with sensing options, without a sufficient software/SKU pin. The follow-up handles these boundaries correctly.

**Network savings and partial price are claims with different accounting boundaries.** The June operator interview conditions 55,000 person-hours per year on deploying across 350 locations, then still under consideration. It does not report realized net savings or deductions for review, exceptions, commissioning and service. Energeia reports €90,000 excluding sensors and a two-person human access rule, without a complete procurement/configuration/service boundary. The article's extrapolative staffing arithmetic is not a controlled time study. The follow-up correctly leaves full cost per accepted inspection null, does not allocate drone/fixed-sensor benefits to Sparky, and does not infer payroll actually removed.

**Human interpretation and retention.** Fresh [NRC reporting, 13 June 2024](https://www.nrc.nl/nieuws/2024/06/13/de-onderhoudsinspecteur-bij-de-hoogspanningscentrale-van-tennet-iseen-robothond-a4856397), “Geen plas vloeistof,” describes inspectors viewing a robot photo on an iPad and judging that no leak is visible. It supports a human review step, not its labor duration or an autonomous maintenance decision. “Docking station” says the test runs to end-2024, unlike the later operator slide's September window; both remain recorded. The [operator's 18 September post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/thijspruijssers_innovation-digitalization-ai-activity-7242222109484023808-_RHU) describes a visit and cross-TSO image-database discussion; it does not grant an open-data license or demonstrate gains from the proposed ecosystem. No fetched source proves post-pilot retention or abandonment. The 2024 onshore project is not proof of continuity of the 2018 offshore cohort.

## Review limits and next decision

This review checked the consequential TenneT chapter passages, operator slides/post, two economic/mission reports and NRC's human-review scene. It did not re-audit the rejected Grace case, reproduce the author's whole search log, contact an operator, inspect telemetry, execute a benchmark or obtain a complete cost schedule. Direct PDF text/visual inspection applies to the NEN deck; chapter and article checks used independently retrieved primary/indexed source text. Neither publication access nor interested-party corroboration is independent field auditing.

The useful next evidence remains a closeout tied to an actual cohort: due/selected/excluded points, attempted and accepted measurements, total human review/support time, maintenance dispositions, full configured costs and the dated scale/repeat/redesign/stop decision. Until then, both episodes remain foreign comparators joined to the US utility cell without US labor totals or numerical calibration. Missing continuation evidence is not evidence that a deployment failed.
