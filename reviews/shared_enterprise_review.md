## Review boundaries

The deterministic mandatory-payment order is due time, issue time and stable invoice ID. It is an explicit simulation policy, not a legal priority finding. Opening operator obligations outside phase-3 due dates are rejected. Default suspends delivery/new commitments while retaining assets, customer assignments and noncancelable obligations; no termination, recovery, debt discharge or cross-firm rescue is assumed. Fixed overhead continues through active failed periods by contract assumption. A customer-return attempt before customer capture can fail; this does not model preemptive competitor strategy.

Materials are supplied on explicit trade credit, while human shifts require paid staffing commitments. Their providers' complete capital, payroll and capacity constraints are not reconstructed. The consolidation distinguishes physical and enterprise payments, outside sales and equity; end cash is not profit. Original asset costs, uncollected receivables, future material dues and terminal values prevent a lifetime return claim.

The experiment has one flat recurring market, one work profile, two operators, explicit customer moves and a fixed allocation rule. Human/robot work joins exactly, but no observed hours-per-bundle conversion or payroll removal is supplied. The annual model and K lifetime architecture remain separate; no full portfolio, autonomous reinvestment, loan maturity, optimal competition or empirical demand model is established. W1-CASH remains partial. Further work should prioritize consequential evidence and the remaining finance mechanisms, not infer completeness from these passing software tests.
