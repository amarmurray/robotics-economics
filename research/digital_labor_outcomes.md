# Digital time savings, work reallocation and paid labor

Edition 0.40 · 8 November 2026 · Substantial H, with customer and provider implications. The same [decision campaign](../DEPLOYMENT_ENTRY_CAMPAIGN.md) remains in progress through 14 November. This is a public-source audit and measurement design, not a new simulation or a target staffing estimate.

## Decision: preserve the possible benefit and identify its form

A digitally improved human organization is a serious alternative to installing robots. Its benefit can appear as more accepted work, better quality, shorter queues, greater resilience or different tasks before a wage bill changes. An operating newco might sell that capacity; an incumbent might use it to clear a backlog. Neither can finance payroll savings that have not been connected to a feasible staffing decision. The relevant investment claim must specify which outcome pays, when, and to whom.

The [existing digital evidence package](../data/digital_evidence.json) now connects a linked survey/register study to the historical customer-support study, the developer selection problem, conventional hospital dispatch and documented AGV staffing changes. These sources measure different outcomes. Together they sharpen the required observations without supplying a common percentage to insert into the model.

## What the new source actually measures

Humlum and Vestergaard's [public author paper](https://www.andershumlum.com/s/chatbots_260313.pdf), revised **13 March 2026**, studies eleven exposed Danish occupations. Surveys were conducted in late 2023/2024; administrative outcomes end in **December 2024**. Table B.1 records 115,000 invitations, 25,241 complete responses and 24,796 linked responses. Table 1 separately reports 14,790 ever-users and 10,006 never-users. These counts do not supply Table E.3's unprinted conditional sample size. [H-DK-2026-DESIGN]

Table E.3 reports savings for 75.6% of ever-users. Among respondents reporting savings, 84.7% select other-task allocation and 29.4% select more of the same tasks. The questionnaire asks what respondents **expect** to do. Choices overlap. Its 3% work-time estimate also depends on category/frequency coding and an assumed eight-hour day. These are neither observed redeployment nor accepted-service gains. [H-DK-2026-TASKS]

Labor analyses compare adopter/non-adopter changes under stated trend assumptions; pooled estimates add adopter-specific trends. Authors describe a 2% pooled earnings bound and a distinct 3% dynamic bound. Occupational mobility changes despite near-zero average labor contrasts. Selection, timing, response and common-effect/spillover limits remain. No physical payroll coefficient follows. [H-DK-2026-LABOR]

### Audit qualifications that change the decision

The record preserves each estimand, population, date, locator and missing value. Figures 3, 5 and 8 and Tables 1, B.1 and E.3 were visually inspected alongside methods and questionnaire text. The author PDF's SHA256 is `dc6f69de66b49d875a41bfa4bcc35621b9c022310c3184cefeceb790c884c892`. Three claim records describe this one paper; they are not independent corroboration. [The author index](https://www.andershumlum.com/research) identifies the revision and previous title. Published aggregates were inspected; confidential microdata were neither requested nor re-estimated.

The practical consequence of the questionnaire qualification is to keep an intention outside the realized-work ledger. A respondent can expect to perform other tasks while the organization lacks demand, authority, equipment or a suitable time window. Conversely, valuable quality improvement may occur with unchanged recorded hours. A null labor contrast therefore cannot be substituted for zero productivity, and a reported saving cannot be substituted for a paid staffing reduction. The positive occupational-mobility result also prevents a blanket conclusion that work organization did not change.

A study's identification controls belong in its evidence card. They cannot be carried into a new investment case as a guarantee of causality. A target rollout still needs its own comparison: which demand, personnel, qualifications and process changes coincided with it, and how would the same service have been delivered otherwise? Common effects on competitors or non-users matter to a market-entry thesis even when a relative comparison differences them out. The latest paper date does not extend the observed outcome window.

| Evidence already accepted | Outcome it helps investigate | Additional observation needed for the investment claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Historical customer support](digital_ecosystem.md), H-QJE-2025 | Accepted resolutions per agent-hour and worker heterogeneity | Matched quality/demand, a feasible staffing or additional-sales decision, and all implementation costs |
| Developer study, H-METR-2026 | Submitted-task time under changing tool use and selection | Concurrent work, task selection, validated engineering output and critical-path time |
| [Conventional patient dispatch](conventional_coordination.md), H-PROXIMITY-2025 | A competent digital-human coordination alternative | Complete work including cancellations/outside-window demand, active labor and paid staffing |
| [Historical AGV workforce audit](field_support_denominators.md#employment-operating-windows-and-fallback-edition-036), FSD-E010/E011 | Redeployment, retained duties, operating windows and manual contingency | Current target rosters, actual payroll/contractor invoices and complete service through failures |
| New linked digital labor audit | Separate reports/expectations from administrative labor outcomes | Actual time disposition, quality-matched whole service and the target organizational transition |

These comparisons are complementary. A task study can identify a plausible capacity improvement, while an administrative study tests whether a different margin changed. Neither makes the other redundant. Avoid averaging incompatible measures into an assumed automation effect. Preserve strong human/conventional operation in each physical case and measure new review, integration and exception work, including work imposed on people who do not directly use the technology.

## Six physical-service measurement contracts

The following are proposed observations for existing cells, not completed field studies or requests to contact operators.

| Cell | Join the released task to retained service and actual labor |
| --- | --- |
| Hospital logistics | Follow dispatch, pickup, receipt, nursing/porter duties, supervision and urgent manual contingency. Retain canceled and outside-window requests. A faster routine delivery may leave the same staff necessary for the rest of the service. |
| Utility inspection | Link remote review, residual visits and revisit decisions to a qualified field roster. Shorter observation time need not remove travel or a required site visit. |
| Automotive presentation | Observe variant completeness, buffers, line deadlines, replenishment, quality and changeovers alongside workcell staffing. Local pick-time gains can leave the line constraint unchanged. |
| Floor service | Reconcile floor work, nonfloor duties and response obligations in the shared crew calendar. Notice, overtime, agency labor and cash collection dates determine which staffing action can execute. |
| Tote logistics | Link moves to downstream handoff acceptance and fulfilled orders, including receiving labor and exception queues. Additional movement alone is not the customer service. |
| Construction installation | Retain trade qualifications, setup, sequencing, inspections and travel. Released minutes must fit a feasible crew and site schedule before becoming saleable capacity or avoided cost. |

Each contract separates active task time, elapsed service time, paid time, accepted output and cash. Preserve failures and repeated attempts, then reconcile unique accepted work to demand. Record the worker or contractor who performs new duties and the legal payer. A lower customer wage bill accompanied by greater provider support is a change in cost allocation until consolidated resources are measured.

## Consequences for the three packs and company formation

The hospital, utility and automotive [decision packs](deployment_entry_decisions.md) now include an explicit labor-outcome gate. They retain their service-specific alternatives and unresolved economic metrics. The source does not supply a robot price, staffing ratio, site qualification or deployment deadline.

For an **incumbent**, a useful first claim can be capacity or service resilience while the paid schedule remains fixed. Count overtime or a hire as avoided only against documented workload and a credible staffing alternative. Do not retroactively describe unchanged staffing as a failed investment if the approved benefit was quality or backlog clearance; evaluate the benefit actually claimed.

For an **acquisition or hybrid platform**, inherited payroll, support obligations and working capital remain liabilities of identified entities. Acquiring operating authority may enable a different roster, but authority does not establish that the roster can meet all duties. Any transition reserve must cover retained service and new work through notice, qualification and collection delays. The [joint entry/recovery results](entry_transitions.md#funded-recovery-and-actual-retention-edition-039) already show why creditor cash, human-service restoration and productive robot reuse require separate tests.

For a **standalone operating newco**, there is no historical payroll to remove. The comparison is the cost and reliability of the actual feasible crew and technology mix for contracts it can win. Digital assistance may help output or expertise acquisition; demand, qualification, funding and customer retention still have to support the capacity. Treating an incumbent survey's expected savings as a newco margin would skip those steps.

For a **provider, integrator or financier**, record who supplies training, review, integration, exception response and fallback. A service fee and the provider resources it finances belong to different accounting boundaries. Underwriting against a customer's expected time saving leaves open both the customer's cash conversion and the provider's support burden.

## Ranking reversals and next evidence

Three plausible observations would change a recommendation:

1. The same paid crew meets more accepted demand and preserves all prior obligations. That can support a capacity case even with no payroll reduction, provided realized contribution covers the additional inputs.
2. A proposed smaller crew misses retained work or requires contractor/overtime replacement. The apparent payroll saving shrinks or the lower-cost claim fails the service condition.
3. New oversight and integration work moves across entities or occupations. A customer cash benefit can coexist with weak provider economics or higher consolidated resource use.

These are hypotheses and measurement gates, not findings attributed to the Danish sample. The [shared-workforce](shared_workforce.md) and conventional-coordination fixtures make some constraints executable; they do not estimate how frequently these outcomes occur in the field. All existing numerical assumptions and scenario results remain unchanged in this source tranche.

The next empirical requirement is a matched target record joining complete service, actual allocation of work and dated paid obligations across the transition. Existing unanswered questions on whole-service cost, rights, financing and broader resource integration remain partial.
