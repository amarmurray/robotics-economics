# Teach-back 05 — worked solutions

1. Specialist entry bill: 9−4κ+4=13−4κ. Available cash is 54/5. Thus 13−4κ≤54/5 exactly when κ≥11/20.
2. Source-first provider value 2(24/5)−12+124/5−(13−4κ)=47/5+4κ. Direct B value 124/5−13=59/5. Source-first strictly wins when 4κ>12/5, i.e. κ>3/5. At equality the cheaper/less-committed route may be selected by tie-breaking.
3. Supplier may keep the learning benefit as margin. The purchaser still owes invoice 9 and fixed B charges 4, so no extra immediately available buyer cash arises under this contract. A consolidated value calculation must eliminate transfer payments between these actors and count the manufacturer resource saving once. Real gain may be allocated without solving the deployer's liquidity constraint.
4. If capacities do not bind, extra capacity is not useful output; the true q* is determined by demand and may be identical across the options. The linear capacity-binding surplus difference and claimed threshold then fail.

Observe repeated actual manufacturing cohorts within stable component/design, factory resources/yield and input prices, current production rate, engineering/BOM revision, supplier invoices and contracts, and customer-side supported-service acceptance.
