# Teach-back: demand, receipt maturity and qualification

Work before consulting the separate solutions.

1. A customer accepts complete A service quantity q in {1,2}. The provider earns invoice R_A(q;m)=m q-q(q-1), pays q now and collects after L periods. For m>=4 find the change in earned **net** A contribution when shared training reduces q from 2 to 1. Why is that not automatically the cash change on the training date?
2. Initial cash is 4, A q=2 in period 0, and one-period collection. At t=1 derive funding thresholds when shared training costs an additional 13 and delivers q=1, whereas independent specialist training costs 20 and preserves q=2. Which direction does a marginal increase in m push the shared route's operating value, and which direction does it push liquidity?
3. There are two postqualification B windows, accepted incremental net contribution 14 per window, and success probabilities 3/4 (shared) and 9/10 (specialist). Derive one-shot expected value premiums and compare at m=4,7,11, respecting affordability. What universal monotonicity claim fails?
4. Repeat with L=2 and opening cash 4. Show why financing A itself is just feasible through t=1 but neither training route can start t=1. Why can waiting to t=2 be unprofitable even if more money has arrived?
5. Suppose a lender advances 12 at t=1 in the m=7, L=2 case, with principal plus fee f due at t=4. What is the correct terminal wealth increment? Explain why adding the entire advance to project value is double counting.
6. You observe across robotics customers that higher operating receipts predict more task expansion. Is that enough to infer a binding finance constraint? Design a matched negative control and identify two critical missing observables in the GXO/Agility public record.
