# Solutions: qualification events and retained productivity

1. Let \(H_S(b)\) be the best expected optimized accepted-service operating contribution at stopping **minus actual acquisition expenditure**, starting with qualified set S and capacity to spend no more than b. The firm can always stop and keep \(f(S)\); thus \(H_S(0)=f(S)\) and \(H'_S(b)=[\max_j\lambda_j(H_{S\cup A_j}(b)-H_S(b))-1]_+\). A lender or corporate treasury may require enough unrestricted cash to cover **every possible realized expenditure** up to b; a low expected cost does not itself make the path financeable. If intermediate production generates receipts, cash and customer state change while the program runs: the simple effort-clock model must be replaced by a dated liquidity/production-control process, not by silently adding a data premium.

2. With independent single-success events, the minimum number of events is two, so the gross increment is \(\Theta(b^2)\); with a genuine one-event joint program it is \(\Theta(b)\). For a single joint event with rate μ and reward W, the gross value is \(W(1-e^{-\mu b})\sim W\mu b\). Merely assigning two independent Bernoulli labels to the same success changes the joint law and artificially changes the economic order. The real event/outcome unit must be observed and typed.

3. The initial net marginal gain rates are \(av-1\) for targeted A, \(-1\) for targeted B, and \(\mu W-1\) for joint. The inequalities \(av>\mu W>1\) ensure targeted A wins at tiny b. At A, finish B if \(W-v>1/a\). Under this condition, an unlimited-budget targeted two-event route yields \(W-2/a\), whereas joint yields \(W-1/\mu\). If \(\mu>a/2\), joint is better. To keep the targeted successor program more efficient use \(\mu<a\). With \(a=2,\mu=1.2,v=7,W=10\), the marginal slopes are 13 versus 11, but unlimited values are 9 versus \(55/6\approx9.16667\). The finite-budget numerical Bellman solution selects A at b=.05 and joint at b=4. The two cases compare **different available budgets**, not returns from a single strategy using equal actual expenditure.

4. No. Marketing documents a possible recipe and product functionality, not acceptance, paid order completion, a site's A/B qualification partition, or a development success hazard. The minimum panel links the same site/order mix and acceptance standard to actual successful and failed qualification events, dated outlays, complete-order receipts, repair/rework and the outside labor/automation option. A competent alternative is continued human picking coordinated with existing unloading automation, or a digitally coordinated specialist fleet. These may eliminate the modeled complementarity once all customer costs and service quality are matched.
