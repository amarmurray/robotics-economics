# Instructor solutions — safe pool factorial identification

Companion to [SAFE_POOL_FACTORIAL_EXERCISES.md](SAFE_POOL_FACTORIAL_EXERCISES.md). These are exact conditional exercises, not robot-field solutions.

1. Conditional on the full potential outcomes, E[1(A_i=a)Y_i(a)/p_i(a)]=Y_i(a). Average over N_d pools. A shared controller/update/customer market introduces within-pool interference: assigning individual robots independently does not give the hypothesized complete-vector exposure. Dropping future zero-order customers selects on a posttreatment outcome.
2. Pairwise extrapolation f3=3(7.4)-3(7.5)+7=6.7. Third-order beta=f3-6.7 in [-0.7,2.3]. The known best k=1 has 7.5, so beta must exceed 7.5-6.7=0.8 to make k=3 best, assuming both actions approved.
3. F(x,z)=7+1.5x-1.8x²+(z-6.7)x³. Worst z=6. F_low' =1.5-3.6x-2.1x²; root x=(sqrt(71)-6)/7≈0.34659 and F_low≈7.27452. A fixed one-R saturation yields 7.5. Every independent Bernoulli x>0 creates P(RRR)=x³>0; if RRR is unapproved, the entire rule is infeasible even if mean operator time is low. A fixed one-R rule still needs its own complete-vector certification.
4. Global mix 0.7(7.5)+0.3b spans [6.75,7.95], relative to global G 7 is [-0.25,0.95]. A mixed/B-G is 0.7(7.5)+0.3(7)=7.35, advantage .35 before segment administration/rights costs. Cost <.35 gives strict advantage; equal cost gives tie. If B mixing is unapproved rather than only unobserved, global mixing is not a legal candidate.
5. A route might induce new customer orders or pricing (demand) or share scarce operators more efficiently (coordination) without changed controller qualification. Fix customer tests and economics; compare frozen-controller vs updated-controller end-to-end held-out jointly accepted recipes under identical initial target states, and record full customer economic effect separately. Mean resource use hides a positive probability of an above-cap recovery; each supported path requires approved fallback/abort, or the route is excluded.
