# Exercises — two-date data acquisition and qualified service (student version)

All payoffs are hypothetical captured complete-customer-service contributions, not robot benchmark scores. Read [TWO_DATE_DATA_ACQUISITION.md](TWO_DATE_DATA_ACQUISITION.md). Solutions appear in the separate instructor file.

1. Starting with D1, identify the exact point where future data enters captured economic value. Explain why a second explicit future-data premium would double-count output.

2. Prove the conditional productive-routine Blackwell dominance result. Give two examples in which its physical-state matching condition fails.

3. With equal H/L priors, q_H=3/5, q_L=2/5 and report-specific inclusion ρ_H=2/3, ρ_L=1 (only high recorded), derive the selected-record likelihood ratio for any n observations. What does recording 100 selected candidate trajectories tell you about the latent demand regime?

4. Using the terminal net table I=(2,2), Z=(6,−4), X=(13/2,−7/2), G=(7,−6), E=(4,3), calculate the best uninformative terminal policy, competent productive routine value, and perfectly informed audit value after audit cost 1/5.

5. For a symmetric noisy audit of accuracy α, derive high- and low-report posteriors. Show rigorously where the preferred high-report acquisition switches E→X→G, and solve the accuracy needed for the paid audit to beat productive routine. Verify continuity of value at boundaries.

6. Holding the qualified menu fixed, show why optional free additional observations cannot reduce optimized gross contribution. Then demonstrate with Q={I,Z} that adding robust E or target-specific G can drive the *incremental* information premium in opposite directions.

7. Design a physically authorized, policy-closed empirical comparison that could distinguish (a) data quantity, (b) independent target report support, and (c) improved joint physical qualification. State what cannot be inferred if only accepted customer trajectories and test-bench success percentages are observed.
