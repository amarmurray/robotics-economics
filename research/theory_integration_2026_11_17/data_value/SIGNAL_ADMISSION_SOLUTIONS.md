# Instructor solutions — information versus admitted capability

1. Without a report G yields (3-1)/2=1, above S=3/4; with a perfect report use G for H and S for L, yielding (3+3/4)/2=15/8. The negative G-L entry is an economic opportunity cost, not unsafe operation.
2. Learned G=(3,1) dominates the other actions in both states, giving W(1,0)=W(1,1)=2. Information fell from 7/8 to zero. Delta=-7/8.
3. At posterior p, G=4p-1 and T=10p-6. T>G iff p>5/6. The negative report selects S once q>9/16; before that both reports select G. For 1/2≤q≤9/16, W=1; for 9/16<q≤5/6, W=2q-1/8; for 5/6<q≤1, W=5q-21/8. At q=1, W=19/8 and Delta=1/2.
4. Retain 1, report 15/8-9/10=39/40, train 1-3/10=7/10, both 19/8-3/10-9/10=47/40. In the high-q branch require 5q-21/8-6/5>1, i.e. q>193/200. Other q branches cannot make joint profitable under these costs.
5. Four independently randomized, approved, complete-arm policy means determine their own four-term difference. They do not reveal the same network's unobserved cross-controller Q coupling. Mechanism attribution fails if the controller changes signal-generation K or visited states, report disclosure independently changes customer demand/operator safety, action menus lack safety approval/positivity, or networks share learning and dispatch.
