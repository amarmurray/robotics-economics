## The constructive question

An old controller R can remain executable while an admitted source-trained controller X irreversibly changes a workpiece, consumes the customer window, blocks the operator or breaks the route needed for R. Rather than treat “fallback” as a binary software property, specify a **sequential physical policy** and carry the resulting qualifications, buyer acceptance, resource use and future source-data creation through the same optimized production value V and intertemporal J. What can original-policy outcomes identify without a hypothetical counterfactual oracle?

Use an original eligible, pre-policy **whole customer–learner–operator–embodiment network history** h, with all customer refusals, zero orders, service failures and displaced options in its denominator. R0(h) indicates that the incumbent *complete physical recipe* qualifies safely under the original state and deadlines. X(h) indicates that the newly trained controller safely completes the same service on its first permitted attempt. H(h) means that an **actually permitted, fully resourced and state-valid** old-controller recovery completes after X fails; it is defined on X=0 (irrelevant elsewhere). Unlike “weights can be rolled back”, H incorporates residual time, workpiece state, body, collision risk, shared human recovery and actual contractual dispatch rights.

The sequential fallback's complete-service qualification is

```text
Q_F(h) = X(h) + [1-X(h)] H(h).
F(h) = 0  iff  X(h)=0 and H(h)=0.
```

The formula applies only when the sequential fallback itself is physically permitted. Where X can create a disqualifying unsafe failure, a mere attempted X is not assumed admissible and H cannot be imputed as success. The safety/quality question is **not** solved by a probability model.

## Physical retention proposition and a sharp identification envelope

**Pathwise result.** F preserves all originally qualified cases if and only if no original eligible h has R0=1, X=0 and H=0 (up to null histories for an almost-sure claim). This condition must hold for the *entire original network*, not just the robot's isolated manipulator test. A certified gate that rules out these histories, a verified non-destructive retry that can finish within all residual resource/deadline bounds, or genuinely independent certified redundant physical resources could make it true. Mere policy-set inclusion cannot.

Set r=Pr(R0=1), x=Pr(X=1), b=Pr(X=0,H=0) on a common original network law. Thus q_F=1-b, and the mass of previously qualified cases lost by F is `ell=Pr(R0=1,X=0,H=0)`. If only r, x and b are measured, with 0≤b≤1−x, the sharp unrestricted-response interval is

```text
max(0,r+b-1) <= ell <= min(r,b).                 (1)
```

It is sharper than the X-alone worst-regression bound `min(r,1-x)` when a measured physical retry reduces b. Still, `b>0` and `r>0` generally prevent a zero-loss certificate. If an independently defensible paired baseline/X qualification design additionally identifies `a=Pr(R0=1,X=0)`, and `k=Pr(X=0)=1−x`, then a second sharp envelope is

```text
max(0,a+b-k) <= ell <= min(a,b).                 (2)
```

These are Fréchet event-intersection results, not new probability theorems. “Paired” refers to a validated common-hypothetical initial state or appropriate non-destructive approved test; one must not pretend two conflicting physical attempts can be simultaneously observed on an actual customer order. An independently measured fallback's own mean qualifies `q_F` but cannot reveal whether it destroyed old R-success histories.

Both worlds have r=1/2, x=3/4, b=1/8 and q_F=7/8, with indistinguishable **separate** whole-policy arm qualification means. The first world has zero original-case regression; the second loses 1/8. Formula (1) gives exactly the compatible range [0,1/8]. This is a structural change in overlap, not a parameter sweep. The improvement from 3/4 to 7/8 in F does not prove safe retention.

## Physical success does not imply *paid* new production

Let `B_R(h)` and `B_F(h)` record a legally accepted, timely, quality-qualified buyer commitment under each *specified menu and service policy*; let `v` be a fixed captured contribution **only for this illustrative one-order case**. Let `C_R,C_F` include scarce operator/robot-time opportunity costs, source/curation rights and recovery resources not already accounted inside contribution. Then

```text
J_F - J_R = v E[B_F Q_F - B_R R0] - (C_F-C_R).    (3)
```

For stable B and the same value/rights, the expectation is new paid complete cases minus lost old paid complete cases. Without stable B, physical nested Q does not justify substituting a technical qualification percentage into sales; with an operator pool, (3) is only a one-order reduced case of optimized V under all displaced work.

In the **nested** world, suppose every original opportunity is a buyer at v=4 normalized units, and there is no extra cost. Old R contributes 2 per original case and F gross contributes 7/2: F is productive. But with an additional *genuine* resource/curation/opportunity expense of 2, F net is 3/2 and R remains preferable. In another admissible customer world with the **same physical** Q arrays and no extra cost, F's new menu/timing retains paid commitments only on histories 1–3. F realizes 3/2 against R's 2, even though `Q_F>=R0` casewise. This is not a measured demand shift, simply a counterexample to inferring paid production from physical nesting. A globally retained full R policy still offers gross optimized value at least 2 if unchanged and genuinely selectable; these are comparisons to **forcing F**, not contradictions of set inclusion.

## Implementable architecture and identification choice

| Claimed fallback | Time and physical witness | Legitimate conclusion without more evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Store old controller/choose R before an entire shift | Old full-network resources, buyer rights and environment unchanged | Old strategy remains an **option**; no per-history F guarantee |
| Preaction gate between R and X | Gate observes original preaction state and physically excludes all R-success/X-failure cases it admits | Conditional nested Q if a signed, complete supported safety case establishes it; marginal means do not |
| X then retry R | Proven reachable nondestructive state, residual SLA time, operator/embodiment access and safe dispatch | Nested Q only if `R0=1,X=0 => H=1` on every original supported history |
| Parallel body / operator redundancy | Independent qualified workcell, full task rights and actual noninterfering capacity | A potential qualified OR recipe **after** joint physical and customer certification, with real extra resources |
| Randomized preaction whole-network R/X/G/E | Physically authorized arm-wise policies and closed spillover boundaries | Identifies **expected** whole-policy production under assignment, not counterfactual individual safe retention |

The old R action set, source-data inputs X (field trajectories, failures/recoveries, teleoperation, simulation, synthetic/public/pretrained), target-specific G and embodiment E remain distinct. The correct investment is the most valuable **physically approved** whole-network policy under full original demand and resource closure. Optional free source availability may preserve the old optimum; forced training may not. A feedback-generating retry or accepted production episode contributes to **future continuation inside J**, never a second added data premium. In model B the retry changes later visit/selection and hence the distribution of future data; an isolated first-period success comparison cannot value that effect.

## Closest prior mechanisms and decisive empirical discriminants

Reactive safety shielding explicitly concerns action monitoring and temporal-logic requirements (Alshiekh et al., *AAAI* 2018, https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v32i1.11797); safe policy improvement with baseline bootstrapping establishes **expected-return** performance protection under specified offline-RL conditions (Laroche, Trichelair and Tachet des Combes, *ICML/PMLR* 2019, https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/laroche19a.html). Neither abstract alone establishes the full customer SLA, body, operator and recovery-tail premise here. This note's event-overlap bounds are established Fréchet mathematics; its production-resource interpretation is a **candidate synthesis**, not verified independent novelty. Farboodi–Koh–Xia's *Data-Driven Automation* already values future data in §5 and data aggregation complementarities in Appendix B; no novelty is assigned to those ideas.
