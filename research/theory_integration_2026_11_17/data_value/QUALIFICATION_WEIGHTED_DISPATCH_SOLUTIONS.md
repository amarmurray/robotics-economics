**1.** For a vector with k ones, P(θ=0,S=s)=g_k/2 and P(θ=1,S=s)=g_(3−k)/2. Compare (g_k/4)/2 to (3g_(3−k)/4)/2; choose A iff g_k≥3g_(3−k). For k=1, choose minority B iff 3(1/16−t)>1/8+t, equivalent to t<1/64.

**2.** Enumerating k=0,1,2,3 and using multiplicities 1,3,3,1, q_X=(1/2)Σ C(3,k)max(g_k/4,3g_(3−k)/4). Its first branch is 55/128−t/2 for 0≤t≤1/64; second 13/32+t for 1/64≤t≤1/16. Sharp range [27/64,15/32] since the minimum occurs at 1/64 and maximum at 1/16, both feasible. Majority technical accuracy 13/16+2t strictly rises; majority qualified yield 13/32+t. The two t worlds are not asserted to be nested additional-information experiments.

**4.** A perfect audit allows average qualified yield (55/128+15/32)/2=115/256. Without audit the mixture of error laws has t=1/32 and optimized qualified yield q_X(1/32)=7/16. Incremental accepted-paid contribution is 10(115/256−112/256)=15/128, conditional on already choosing X. E=10 dominates, so this cannot be added as a global option premium. Varying third-order error dependence at fixed marginals is not Blackwell nesting; a free optional signal can be ignored and cannot decrease the optimum with the policy menu held fixed.
