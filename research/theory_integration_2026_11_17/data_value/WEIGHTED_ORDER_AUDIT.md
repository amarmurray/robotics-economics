## Conditional setup
Within a fixed, approved arm a, let O be its measured discounted net contribution from the original and induced order stream, including paid costs already booked once. There remain n original eligible order IDs whose terminal on-time acceptance is not linked to their settlement. Their incremental *net* conditional contributions w_i are independently known or contracted, not inferred from benchmark scores. Assume each unresolved record has two possible accounting outcomes, 0 or w_i, after subtracting any known baseline; signed w is allowed. Other residual costs/induced demand/continuation are separately bounded in C, with no duplicate accounting. Let k be the independently reconciled count of accepted timely outcomes among these n, e.g. from a complete arm-specific recipient register before linkage. This is not a count of robot successes. If no such reconciliation exists, use the unrestricted count range; if prices, returns or conditional expenses are unknown, the weights are not identified. Do not assign clinical time a dollar value without an economic basis.

Given only k and w, the residual S=sum_i w_i y_i, y_i binary, sum y_i=k, lies in [sum of k smallest w, sum of k largest w]. Both endpoints attained by assigning acceptance to those indices; an arbitrary feasible assignment can be transformed by exchanging high accepted and low unaccepted weights. This is standard finite rearrangement and bounded-outcome partial identification, not a novel general theorem. For a count range [l,u], minimize and maximize those endpoint sums over all integers in the range, not merely the high count if weights can be negative. This yields the sharp hull under only these constraints; shared service/worker/capacity restrictions may narrow it. Do not assert every interior point is feasible for integer records.

For separately bounded arm residuals [L_a,U_a] inclusive of their remaining C, the fixed-arm G-P contrast is bounded by O_G-O_P + [L_G-U_P,U_G-L_P]. Sharpness of that contrast requires independent attainability across arms, not a common-law restriction that couples their residuals. On a random cluster experiment these are within-cluster accounting bounds subsequently averaged with the predeclared cluster/order weights; sampling uncertainty is additional. A fixed-program lower bound is not the optimized program value. For a robust investment comparison to optimized E, retain the earlier lawful policy witness and upper certificate; this residual correction cannot itself supply the latter.

## Measurement design: which unresolved order IDs should be audited?
Suppose an audit reveals one exact y_i at equal cost, selected before outcomes are revealed; k remains known and all assignments consistent with k are possible. Pick m IDs to audit. The unaudited set R of r=n-m weights has j unresolved successes, feasible j from max(0,k-m) to min(k,r). After any audit, the known audited contribution translates the interval but does not affect its width. Define D_R(j)=sum of j largest weights in R minus sum of j smallest. D_R(j)=D_R(r-j), and D_R(j+1)-D_R(j)=w_(r-j)-w_(j+1)>=0 before the midpoint (ascending ordering). Hence the worst residual width for this fixed audit set is D_R(j*) with j* the integer clamp of floor(r/2) to the feasible count interval; this is attainable by a possible audited outcome.

**Proposition (uniform-cost nonadaptive audit).** There exists a minimax-width audit leaving r consecutive weights in the globally sorted list. Proof: for any remaining index set i_1<...<i_r, put h=ceil(r/2). Choose a contiguous block with its h-th element at index i_h (valid since i_h>=h and i_h+r-h<=n). For ranks l<=h the block's index i_h-h+l is >=i_l, and for ranks l>=h it is <=i_l. For j<=floor(r/2), the block's j smallest weights therefore weakly increase and its j largest weakly decrease. Thus D_block(j)<=D_R(j), and symmetry handles larger j. The feasible j range is identical for both sets. Minimize the midpoint-clamped width over n-r+1 contiguous blocks. Cases r=0 or j*=0 have zero width; ties permit many optima. The proof holds with signed weights. This optimizes worst-case interval width, not expected value of information, audit cost, adaptive design or chance of crossing a particular investment threshold.

Synthetic example: w=[1,2,3,10,20,21], k=3. With counts alone the residual value can be 6 or 51 (same accepted count, width 45). Auditing the two highest weights leaves [1,2,3,10], worst residual width 10. Auditing two middle weights (3,10) leaves [1,2,20,21], worst width 38. This differs from maximizing the number of confirmed successes or sampling uniformly. If all w are equal the count already identifies the residual; auditing further has zero width-reduction benefit. If audit of a high weight is infeasible or unusually expensive, the contiguous-block result need not apply. If the exact count is missing, worst-case width is sum of absolute remaining weights, so a different design follows: audit largest absolute weights under uniform costs.
