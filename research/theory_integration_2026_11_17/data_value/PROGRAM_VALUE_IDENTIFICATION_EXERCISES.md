# Exercises — program identification and productive data coupling

**Student version.** Complete all questions from the definitions in [the chapter](PROGRAM_VALUE_IDENTIFICATION_HIERARCHY.md); the instructor's solutions are maintained separately.

1. **Program means vs cross-world response.** Four whole-network programs R,S,G,SG are assigned with positive known conditional probabilities e_z(W). Prove the conditional mean identity for each z and the average S×G economic factorial difference. Explain the role of cluster interference and why observing no outcomes on refused customer orders breaks the intended original-buyer estimand.

2. **Two observationally equivalent response worlds.** Let U be uniform on {0,1}. In world C, all four potential success outcomes equal U. In world P, (Y_R,Y_S,Y_G,Y_SG)=(U,1-U,1-U,U). Compute the four arm averages, average factorial interaction, distribution of *individual* factorial interaction, and discuss precisely what arm-randomization identifies.

3. **The relevant qualification copula.** Two services each qualify with probability 1/2 under SG. An authorized end-to-end original-buyer order contributes 12 if and only if both qualify. SG costs 1; E yields net 4. Give sharp bounds on SG net without within-arm paired observations, construct endpoint joint laws, and compute which intervention wins in each. Does knowing the *cross-world* correlation between Y(S) and Y(G) resolve this particular missing datum?

4. **Production-generated data.** Useful licensed data D and a future qualified accepted sale Q each have marginal probability 1/2. The data program contributes 8DQ−1, versus E net 1. Compare perfect concordance with perfect negative dependence. Explain why the observed correlation is not necessarily a causal benefit of using D in training, and name a legitimate intervention capable of identifying such an effect.

5. **Sequential identification.** State the additional support conditions needed to estimate a policy that chooses a follow-on G training program after observing a predecision report generated by first-period production. Explain why a predecision report under G-observe cannot be treated as an identical observed covariate in R without a report-generation/transport assumption. Why must future data value be inside the continuation contribution rather than added once more?
