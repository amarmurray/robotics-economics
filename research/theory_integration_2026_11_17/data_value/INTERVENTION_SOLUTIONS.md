# Intervention-selection solutions — keep separate from exercises
Private teacher key, conditional on the toy model. None of the numbers are estimated market amounts.

**1.** Pr(C=1|H)=3/16 and Pr(C=1|L)=1/16. Therefore Pr(C=1)=1/8; Pr(H|C=1)=(3/32)/(1/8)=3/4. The noncorrection posterior is (1/2)(13/16)/(7/8)=13/28. Under independent exposure, X=0 has equal likelihood 3/4 under both regimes and leaves the prior 1/2 unchanged. Thus C=0 mixes an unvisited risky state and an encountered but uncorrected or error-free state.

**2.** After correction, H posterior=3/4 so source reuse yields 7+1=8, beating T=5. Otherwise source reuse is at most 5, so choose target. Terminal V(e)=5+3e/2. Routine total 6+5+3/8=91/8; probe 5+5+3/2=23/2; specialist 63/10+5=113/10. Probe wins. With no persistent update (d=0), V(e)=5+e, totals 45/4 for routine and 11 for probe: routine wins. With data physical outcomes unchanged but the report arriving only after route commitment, choose source ex ante, terminal=5+e/2, totals 89/8 and 21/2: routine wins. The +1 is a hypothetical qualified terminal operating payoff conditional on a learned update, not an intrinsic label price.

**3.** In both worlds e=1, and the gated conditional probabilities are Pr(C=1|H)=3/16 and Pr(C=1|L)=1/16, so all observed X,C event probabilities match. Under equal prior the would-be error rate is 1/2 in A and 1/4 in B, while missed-error probabilities (1-g)*mean p are 3/8 and 1/8 respectively. An independently supervised, safety-approved audit measuring gate recall relative to an external would-be-error evaluator separates g from p. Correction counts alone do not identify p or the uncorrected-error hazard.

**4.** With T=25/4, after C=1 source reuse still pays 8, after C=0 target wins. Terminal V(e)=25/4+(e/2)(8-25/4). Routine total=399/32=12.46875; probe total=97/8=12.125: routine wins. With routine providing the same relevant exposure e=1 and same training effect for no extra cost, routine total=6+13/2=25/2, dominating probe=23/2. If matching data require extra paid human monitoring, that cost must be restored in the comparison.

**5.** Generate a q=1/4 report from a q=1 report by retaining each positive with probability 1/4 and converting the remainder to zero; this matches each regime's Bernoulli(1/4*p_h) distribution. It proves information dominance for otherwise fixed primitives, not safe operating dominance: the higher-coverage activity may have lower accepted-service contribution and higher costs. Free optional retention of old feasible policies and acquisition rights weakly expands the optimized feasible set and cannot lower its maximum; compulsory new training or deprecating the old controller may destroy this monotonicity.
