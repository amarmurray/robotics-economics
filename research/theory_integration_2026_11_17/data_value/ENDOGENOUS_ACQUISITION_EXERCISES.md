# Student exercises: producing versus learning deliberately

1. Two equiprobable buyers require A or B. R earns 1/2; the two lawfully qualified specialists earn 1 when matched and 0 otherwise. For an authorized symmetric binary report of accuracy alpha≥1/2, derive the best future accepted-paid value. P pays current r and report alpha=3/4. D earns no current service and costs 1/8 with a perfect report. Derive the P–D switch. Why is subtracting r twice incorrect?
2. Recompute D when only A specialist and fallback R are qualified. Recompute the two-specialist program when a perfect report is legally unusable before dispatch. Discuss why a better sensor cannot imply a better compound data action.
3. Construct the 2×2 optimized paid-production table when source input improves report accuracy from 1/2 to 3/4 and target grounding qualifies A and B; without grounding only R exists. Compute the economic cross-difference.
4. Starting from a finite-history Bellman recursion, prove the fixed-policy occupancy derivative with variable data intensity x and state the fixed-policy-set assumption behind taking the maximum over optimal policies.
5. Describe a legal evidence clock for original offered customers including no-orders, attempted P/D, shared robot/operator slots, data rights, checkpoint qualification, dispatch, refunds and final payment. Identify success-only selection bias.
