# Student problems — original buyer menus, safe data acquisition, and qualified production

All numbers are synthetic normalized contribution units. Assume independently certified joint G and E **only within the mathematical questions**; no safety approval is implied for real robots. Work from [theory](MENU_TRANSPORT_IDENTIFICATION.md). Do not read the [instructor solutions](MENU_TRANSPORT_SOLUTIONS.md) before attempting.

**Problem 1 — contract transport.** Half of original buyer parents have budget 6 and half budget 8. On the natural joint G menu, a high-budget parent buys second service B with probability theta_N. Net G is 7/2+(3/2)theta_N, E is 9/2. A binding target offer C has take-up theta_C=1, but it may alter warranty and payment obligations. Prove the sharp G identified interval if |theta_N-theta_C|<=1/4. Identify which action wins robustly and explain which assumption would fail with a changed menu.

**Problem 2 — reversed worlds.** Construct two economic worlds with identical observed C take-up theta_C=1, identical R/mixed-G/E records and unchanged physical qualification, but opposite G-versus-E rankings on the unoffered natural menu. Explain why infinitely many C-only commitments or R trajectories need not resolve their natural-menu difference.

**Problem 3 — technical versus commercial transport.** A source-trained X increases robot task success on isolated A and B but has no signed joint recovery policy. A legally binding C contract says the buyer will purchase B if jointly fulfilled. Can one infer (i) safe joint X service, (ii) economic value of natural GG, (iii) value of the new contract-specific service? State the minimum separate evidence for each.

**Problem 4 — imperfect passive report.** Prior H/L is equal. G produces 5 in H and 7/2 in L, versus E=9/2. A passive binary report has symmetric accuracy alpha∈[1/2,1]. Derive the posterior-decision threshold and EVSI(alpha). Why is an informative accuracy of 3/5 not necessarily economically valuable?

**Problem 5 — robust prior.** With p=P(H)∈[p_low,p_high], a perfect passive signal reveals H/L and is followed by optimal feasible action. Derive the improvement in *maximin expected value* compared with acting without the signal. Contrast the cases p_low=0 and p_low=1/2 and explain why this is not the same as maximin regret.
