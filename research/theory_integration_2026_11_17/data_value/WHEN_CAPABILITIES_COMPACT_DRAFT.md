# When capabilities become production
## Economic data value with evolving production alternatives
Compact first-paper draft, 24 November 2026 Eastern. Conditional theory and empirical design; no field estimate or independent novelty claim.

### Abstract
A robotic producer chooses not simply how much data to acquire but which feasible production and learning program to operate. We distinguish technical transfer from its contribution to customer-accepted service, incorporating scarce dated embodied and organizational resources. A cheaper routine-learning program may emulate targeted grounding; coordination may either generate redundant learning or unlock capacity for newly qualified work. We derive sharp missing-combination investment bounds, relax their cross-program structural assumptions, and show how pre-treatment customer strata tighten the decision. Existing technical and field evidence supports individual mechanisms but not complete causal economic returns.

### 1. The economic object
Let H_t contain controller and embodiment lineage, source-specific usable histories and rights, dated resources, demand and information observable before action. Model A maximizes accepted contribution over the jointly qualified recipe menu: max v(H)'x subject to A(H)x<=K(H), B(H)x<=d(H), x>=0; A accounts for bodies, workers, coordination and compute, B for original customer demand. The divisible LP dual bounds the value of resources; queues, indivisibility and deadlines may instead require time-expanded feasible sets. A technical success score is neither a qualification nor a settlement.

Model B chooses complete learning/production programs g in {R retained, P routine production-generated reuse, X additional source, G deliberate grounding, C coordination with evolving routine learning, E new embodiment/access, lawful combinations}. Define
J_g(pi,m)=E_m^{g,pi}[sum_t beta^t r_t+beta^T phi_T]-c_g,
V_g(m)=sup_{pi feasible and nonanticipating} J_g(pi,m).
r is accepted net paid contribution; c committed incremental cost; phi distinct lawful successor production. Do not add a separate data premium. Source fields, failures/recoveries, teleoperation, simulation, synthetic and pretrained inputs are distinct; their effects on joint qualification and later data generation depend on the policy, target, body and rights. Demand and competition belong in m. New embodied access changes both recipes and future experience. Retaining old lawful feasible policies makes free optional data weakly valuable in gross optimum, not necessarily costly training. If every G policy has a feasible P emulator whose gross loss is at most epsilon, taking suprema yields V_G-V_P<=epsilon-(c_G-c_P). A uniform emulator is needed for a law-independent robust policy. See [full formulation](POLICY_CLOSED_DATA_VALUE.md).

### 2. Why coordination changes data value
In the one-target, two-date restriction, H is authorization, W0<=W1 usable capacity, L baseline readiness, S C-induced routine readiness and T G-induced readiness. Define R=H(1-L)T. With common contribution v>=0, OR-readiness, timely observation, stable demand and additive costs,
CG-C-G+P = v E[R(W1-W0)(1-S)] - v E[R W0 S].
This follows by substituting the four Boolean service indicators. The first term is new capacity that still requires grounding; the second is grounding rendered redundant by routine learning on already usable capacity. Neither sign is automatic. A frozen-controller coordination test is diagnostic, not the evolving C rival. [Model and counterworlds](COORDINATION_ROUTINE_MODEL_AB.md).

### 3. Main decision certificate
For a common original-opportunity law let mu(A)=E[v 1_A], with v nonnegative, fixed across programs. Let F0 subset F1 be baseline and expanded lawful usable capacity events; P,G subset F0 and C,Z (combined) subset F1 be accepted-service events. One service per window, no other competing order, policy-invariant contribution. Write masses p,g,c,z,f0,f1 and d=f1-f0. When P is contained in G and C, combined service retains both and no joint-only qualification occurs inside F0, the sharp missing-arm bound is
max(g,c) <= z <= min(f1,c+g-p+d).
The corresponding interaction interval with additive costs is [p-min(g,c), min(d,f1-c-g+p)]. Thus three arm marginals cannot establish strictly positive complementarity; they can establish negative complementarity if c+g-p>f1. The incremental grounding return after C is z-c-k for additional cost k with no other changed J terms. Compare upper/lower endpoints with zero for rejection/adoption; compare all alternatives for full-menu choice.

Sensitivity is part of the result, not a separate paper. Let upper budgets a>=mu(P minus G), b>=mu(P minus C), r>=mu(G minus Z), s>=mu(C minus Z), h>=mu((Z intersect F0) minus (G union C)). These are economically weighted exception masses, not technical error rates. Feasibility: 0<=p,g<=f0<=f1; 0<=c<=f1; g>=p-a,c>=p-b and all budgets nonnegative. Set m=max(0,p-a-b,p+g-f0-b). Then
L=max(0,g-r,c-s), U=d+min(f0,g+c-m+h).
Proof: Z retains at least g-r,c-s; nested P,G,C with Z an initial segment of length L attains the lower endpoint. Inside F0 the P/G overlap must be at least t=max(0,p-a,p+g-f0). At most b is lost from C, so G/C overlap is at least m=max(0,t-b). Add h to their union bound and d outside. For attainment choose exactly t P/G overlap; put u=min(c,f0) of C inside F0, selecting the necessary max(0,p-b) P mass outside G first, then remaining C outside G when possible. Its overlap with G is max(0,g+u-f0,t-b). Fill Z with G union C, up to h extra inside F0, and all outside F0. The resulting mass equals U; both regression budgets hold. Mixtures attain intermediate values in the divisible law. Indivisible heterogeneous weights may narrow the interval. Zero budgets recover the exact structural result. The lower endpoint is independent of a,b,h; upper independent of r,s, conditional on feasibility. Rejection and adoption therefore require different audits. [Detailed proof](FOCUSED_STRUCTURAL_SENSITIVITY.md). Retained executable controllers alone imply none of these event restrictions.

### 4. Demand-conditioned identification, not pooled technical averages
Partition original opportunities by a pre-treatment characteristic j (customer, service recipe class, deadline or fixed catchment). Express every mass in common unconditional economic units and compute L_j,U_j from Section 3. Then sum L_j <= z <= sum U_j. Sharpness holds if stratum laws can be combined independently, with no cross-stratum resource, budget or history restrictions beyond the stated moments. Otherwise safe but not necessarily sharp. Proof: add inequalities; assemble extremal stratum laws on disjoint supports. Pooled bounds are weakly wider: the lower is a maximum of affine functions (subadditive under summing arguments); the upper is a minimum of affine functions after expanding m, plus linear d (superadditive). Thus the sum of lower bounds is at least the pooled lower; the sum of upper bounds is at most the pooled upper.

Example: two five-unit strata, f0=f1=5,p=0 in each, zero budgets. World A has (g,c)=(5,0),(0,5); world B has (5,5),(0,0). Both give pooled (p,g,c,f0,f1)=(0,5,5,10,10), z in [5,10]. Stratified arm measurements identify z=10 in A and z=5 in B. With incremental grounding cost 2, C-to-CG net return is +3 versus -2. These are synthetic economic units. Conditioning on a post-intervention successful task instead would change the original opportunity law and invalidate the calculation. Use predeclared strata and joint sampling intervals, not selected strata chosen because their results are favorable.

If grounding can be licensed selectively by stratum without interfering with the shared learner, resources or demand, and incurs separate incremental k_j plus fixed setup K>=0, then a targeted menu has conservative guaranteed net improvement sum_j max(0,L_j-c_j-k_j)-K when that expression is positive; omit the investment otherwise. Optimistic upper improvement is max(0,sum_j max(0,U_j-c_j-k_j)-K). Proof: each chosen stratum has bounded additive return; choose all positive lower-return strata for the guarantee and maximize the upper bound over subsets including the empty subset. Under independently attainable endpoints these are the extrema of the optimized selective value; with shared transfer effects, nonseparable costs or common resource constraints, use Model B instead. This clarifies precisely when customer segmentation changes the *decision*, not merely the estimate. No such separability is claimed for real fleets.

### 5. What existing evidence identifies
[Evidence section](FOCUSED_FIRST_PAPER_EMPIRICAL_SECTION.md) and [protocol](COMPACT_WHOLE_PROGRAM_VALIDATION.md) separate technical transfer (Robin, CEL, Learning while Deploying), original service and clocks (Vulcan, hospital, motor cell), demand selection (Cainiao), and a production coordination alternative (Amazon scheduler). These are different studies and denominators; never multiply them into a causal commercial estimate.

A bounded new search for independently evolving commercial learning arms found technical deployment-learning work but no complete matched P/G/C/CG records. A new primary-source disclosure, Agility Robotics S-1, SEC accession 0001213900-26-118037, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2074973/000121390026118037/ea0311221-s1_agility.htm, selected risk factors and accounting passages (lines 705–724, 758–763, 2009–2011, 6713–6715, 6834; inspected 24 November Eastern), describes a paid qualification program, with approximately $500,000 participation fee, four signed CAP customers as of May 2026 and uncertain conversion to commercial deployment. It distinguishes integration burden, multi-obligation revenue and prepayment from later earned revenue. This is company disclosure, not observed learning return, realized fee receipts, or an observed conversion cohort. Do not impute four times the fee as sales, or equate a qualification fee to data value. The disclosure reinforces separate original evaluation opportunities, qualification payments, actual accepted production and customer continuation in J. No private records were accessed. A BEA search returned historical benchmark and general national accounts methodology pages, not contemporary numerical use/supply edges; no new official crosswalk or topology claimed.

### 6. Prospective empirical decision
Assign comparable pre-treatment catchments to separately evolving lawful P/G/C/CG (and X/E where feasible). Start at common checkpoint; avoid shared cross-arm updates or model assignment at the interference-cluster level. Track every original opportunity and refusal, deadline, fallback and accepted settlement once, dated body/worker/compute occupation, rights, training lineage, successor demand and cost. For fixed-arm ITT use randomized means with valid assignment and follow-up; this does not identify arbitrary optimized V or cross-world exception masses. For missing CG, validate capacity and stable contribution, independently bound weighted exception sets by predeclared class, and use Section 3–4; otherwise report nonidentification. Audit priority: rejection requires upper bounds on incumbent losses and joint-only gains; adoption requires upper bounds on combined regressions. If cheaper P/C matches accepted value and successor opportunities at lower cost, the grounding premium fails in that menu. If demand or resource costs change, use the complete J contrast. Separately quantify sampling, identification and accounting uncertainty.
