# Proof ledger: sequential fallback reachability, sharp regression overlap and paid output

**20 November 2026 UTC. Standard event algebra and sharp two-event Fréchet bounds applied to original qualified robotic service.** No actual safety proof, field evidence or independent theorem novelty.

**P1 — qualification transducer.** Under a physically admissible X-first, R-on-failure recipe, success is `Q_F=X+(1-X)H`. For binary variables `R0,Q_F`, the relation `Q_F>=R0` on every eligible complete history holds precisely when the event `{R0=1,X=0,H=0}` is empty. The implication is **not** obtained by retaining R software: H is an entire reachable physical/market/operator continuation after attempting X, and may be zero even on histories originally qualified by R0. The old and F safety cases must be defined on the same fixed pre-policy whole-network population and horizon.

**P2 — observed retry failure sharpens, but does not establish, nesting.** Define event A=`{R0=1}` of measure r, D=`{X=0,H=0}` of measure b. Then `ell=P(A∩D)`; since `P(A∪D)<=1`, `ell>=r+b-1`; by event inclusion, `ell<=min(r,b)`, and `ell>=0`. For every r,b in [0,1] (with a legitimate X/retry partition `0<=b<=1-x`) attain both endpoints by placing A inside/outside D as far as measure permits; the rest of A can be placed anywhere in the complement of D, including the X-success and retry-success categories. There is no constraint from x alone on the A intersection beyond `b<=1-x`. Thus (1) is sharp among unrestricted compatible original response laws. In particular, measuring q_F=1-b directly gives the same two-policy Fréchet interval; it does not reveal the crossworld overlap.

**P3 — improved envelope from true paired X-failure support.** Conditional on `K={X=0}` of measure k, let `A_K={R0=1,X=0}` of mass a and `D={X=0,H=0}` of mass b, both contained in K. Replacing population capacity 1 with k in the same overlap proof gives `max(0,a+b-k)<=ell<=min(a,b)`. All endpoints attainable by distributing A_K over the D and K\D cells, given 0<=a,b<=k, then assigning old R successes outside K subject to the original r. This bound is meaningful only if a has a valid common-initial-state definition, not inferred from separately selected post-failure logs.

**P4 — payment/resource identity and rejection of a false monotonicity claim.** For one service with fixed captured margin v, define the original buyer purchase/contract indicators `B_R,B_F` and net resource costs `C_R,C_F`; linearity gives (3). If `B_R=B_F=B`, value change is `v[P(B=1,R0=0,Q_F=1)-P(B=1,R0=1,Q_F=0)]-(C_F-C_R)`. Physical nesting eliminates the second event only. It cannot force additional accepted buyer orders, preserve payment under menu/quality drift, or pay for scarce fallback/operator resources. With competing customer work the full `V(Q;omega)` reoptimizes recipes and subtracts displaced qualified service before evaluating J. Conversely if the entire old R policy is still free and feasible, maximizing over policies (including R) weakly improves optimized **gross** J under an unchanged common environment by elementary set inclusion. Net intervention cost and changing demand/operator laws invalidate careless extension.

**P5 — synthetic counterworld verification.** In eight equal histories set `X=(1,1,1,0,1,1,1,0)`, `H=(0,0,0,1,0,0,0,0)` (irrelevant where X=1), `R_N=(1,1,1,1,0,0,0,0)`, `R_C=(1,1,1,0,0,0,0,1)`. Then `F=(1,1,1,1,1,1,1,0)`, so r=4/8,x=6/8,b=1/8,q_F=7/8 in both worlds. `ell_N=0` and `ell_C=1/8`, attaining (1)'s endpoints. Under R_N all buyers B_R=1, v=4: R=2, F=7/2 if B_F=1. Additional F-only resource cost 2 changes F to 3/2. Alternatively B_F=(1,1,1,0,0,0,0,0) and cost zero also gives 3/2. The economic counterexample uses a *different buyer law* from the fixed-B theorem; it does not disprove that theorem.

**P6 — finite audit scope.** The exact self-contained JavaScript source enumerates integer original-network populations of size D=8. Split the X-failure mass `k=8-x` into retry-success mass `k-b` and retry-failure mass b. Let `r1,r2,r3` be counts of R0 successes in the X-success, retry-success, retry-failure cells respectively. For fixed (r,x,b), their feasible `r3` extrema equal `max(0,r+b-8)` and `min(r,b)`; for fixed (r,x,b,a=r2+r3) they equal `max(0,a+b-k)` and `min(a,b)`. Exhaustive small-grid agreement tests formula implementation but **cannot prove** a general theorem. The analytical event construction above does. A separate Python Fraction calculation independently verified the two worlds and the economic arithmetic; full historical repository CI and outside replication were not run.
