## New empirical demand-side anchor

Li, Ma, Han, Tang and Fu, *Human or Robot? Evidence from Last-Mile Delivery Service*, arXiv:2509.11562v1, September 15 2025, https://arxiv.org/html/2509.11562v1 (sections 4, 5.2, 6.3–6.4, 7.2 and Tables 3, 4, 6 inspected November 24 2026; selected HTML only). Proprietary observations cover 32 Chinese Cainiao campus stations during Jan–Nov 2024. Of 7,453,438 inbound package records, 241,517 used delivery rather than collection in person. Robot share within that delivery subset was reported at 57%. Both modes were offered as delivery choices. The full-population delivery fraction is 3.2403% (computed from counts); implied robot penetration is about 1.85%, not 57%. Under nearest-integer percentage rounding, robot count lies in [136458, 138872], corresponding to [1.831, 1.863]% of inbound packages. This rounding interval is conditional, not a reported exact count or uncertainty interval.

Table 6 compares station-specific fee changes in selected delivered records: robot fees 2.2→2.8 RMB and 1.5→2.5 RMB correspond to estimated robot-share changes of -0.0133 and -0.1469; human fees 2→1 and 1→0 RMB correspond to -0.2721 and -0.1974. Treat these as estimates from the authors' specification, not whole-population demand elasticities or independently verified causal effects. Table 6 marks the -0.0133 estimate ** while prose assigns p<0.001; retain discrepancy. The design has four price-changing stations, selected delivery records and potential endogenous platform fee changes; exogeneity to individual users is not sufficient to validate parallel trends or eliminate post-treatment selection. Section 7.2 notes that the weather association loses significance with user fixed effects. Past delivery choices predict later choices but state dependence versus persistent heterogeneity remains unresolved. No individual records accessed, no actual costs/settlement/rights/controller lineage identified. The published aggregate population count is useful demand-side evidence, not matched P/G/E results.

## Identification bridge

Let N be a common inbound population in a fixed cohort, D the number using delivery (including both modes), R robotic deliveries. Let d=D/N and q=R/D when D>0. Then R/N=dq. With d=0 define robot penetration as zero without requiring q. For a known conditional share increase q_G>q_P>0, robot penetration improves iff d_G/d_P>q_P/q_G (d_P>0). This is an identity and direct division by positive terms, not a structural demand law. If d_G unknown in [0,1], a known q_G gives only [0,q_G] penetration, sharp over feasible continuous population fractions; finite count grids restrict attainable points. Robot gross receipts at known uniform fee p are pNdq, not profits; actual refund and capture ledgers are still needed. If the learning intervention changes inbound N, compare absolute cohort contributions over a fixed catchment-time window, not penetration alone.

Synthetic example: P has N=100000, D=3000, R=1710. Both G worlds have conditional robot share 60% (P 57%); G-low D=1000,R=600 and G-high D=4000,R=2400. The robot production contrast reverses with identical selected shares. These are artificial counts, not Cainiao policy outcomes. Costs and successor value can independently reverse the investment ranking. The new public evidence motivates recording the self-pick/no-purchase alternative and original eligible population even when a complete delivery dataset exists.

## Protocol for the first paper (prospective, not performed)

Primary estimand: expected discounted incremental captured contribution of an independently evolving, lawfully retained learning program over a fixed catchment-horizon, including original orders, induced orders and actual approved fallback. Define arms R retained policy, P optional production-generated data reuse, X additional source acquisition, G target grounding, E additional independently qualified embodied access. Start from a documented common checkpoint and comparable resource budgets; preserve lawful old actions where feasible. No uncontrolled shared model updates, operators, customers or hidden spillovers between arms; if interference cannot be isolated, assign the joint cluster/network and estimate that program's effect rather than pretending order-level independence. Fix preassignment population and calendar; follow entrants and outside-option choices separately. Randomize only if approved and feasible; otherwise describe selection and defend a transport/identification assumption or report bounds.

Minimum linked tables (stable pseudonymous keys, no personal payload):
1. Catchment-window and original opportunity: assigned arm, inbound ID, eligibility/offer menu, posted terms and timestamp, refusal/self-service/human/robot choice, arrival, deadline, actual recipient acceptance and payment/refund. Include every original opportunity regardless of dispatch, failure or censoring; tag induced orders and cancellations distinctly.
2. Service and resource: attempt lineage, safe admission, fallback and rework, downstream quality, accepted service time, concurrent operator/body/QC/compute clocks, paid staffing and actual wage avoidance versus productive redeployment. Record capacity, reservations and occupancy separately.
3. Learning: source modality and count, lawful rights and expiry, curation/training costs, grounding changes, controller/body version, training lineage, held-out joint qualification, successor menus and measured continuation service. Record deliberate and routine acquisition separately; failure/recovery logs are not interchangeable by definition.
4. Accounting: dated captured net settlements, resource and acquisition cash/allocated costs once, no duplicated labor savings, horizon terminal rights and continuation scenario separately; fix discount convention and arm-level shared cost rule.
5. Audit: reconcile original count and status totals by arm/window; log missing joins and independent recipient counts. For unresolved binary settlements with independently known signed conditional contributions, use weighted-order fixed-count residual bounds; without an independently known count use sum of min(0,w_i) and max(0,w_i) or supported joint restrictions. Never infer w from technical score or merchandise value. Audit may prioritize economically influential orders subject to lawful access; it is not a change to operational dispatch.

Report nested denominators N→offer→choice→eligible attempt→technical success→downstream accepted on time→settled; fallback is credited once to original ID, not twice to robotic throughput. Record conditional measures as diagnostic only. Report whole-arm contribution as observed reconciled value plus bounded unresolved contribution and disjoint continuation/cost residual. For two arms with intervals [L_a,U_a], contrast [L_G-U_P,U_G-L_P] is safe, sharp only when the endpoints are jointly feasible. G beats all competitors robustly only if its lower bound strictly exceeds every rival upper bound (sufficient, not necessary under coupled restrictions). Report sampling uncertainty separately from identification; a cluster-randomized design targets a mean across assigned clusters, not the exact missing finite accounting identities.

Falsifier: if approved inexpensive P matches G's dated accepted contribution, resource occupation and successor lawful opportunities at lower acquisition cost, the asserted grounding premium fails in that menu. If G improves technical success but loses outside-option demand or requires excess paid support, the contribution comparison can reverse. E must be assessed under the same horizon/resource opportunity costs. No premium added on top of continuation already in J. DDA future data and aggregation remain prior art; this is a conditional accounting/selection synthesis, not independently established novelty.

## Shared-capacity censoring amendment (25 November Eastern)
[Accepted-log envelope](CENSORED_CAPACITY_ENVELOPE.md) distinguishes current observed terminal paid output, total weighted overflow (requires independent same-population marginal readiness), and the marginal capacity counterfactual (requires clipped joint residual counts or sharp bounds). Record original concurrent opportunity sets and rank/dispatch rule before assignment; distinguish fully observed dispatcher readiness from analyst-only missingness and from truly latent qualification at decision time. In a known deterministic rank-max unit-slot restriction, unselected higher-ranked orders and unserved orders in idle windows must be unready; lower-ranked unserved opportunities in saturated windows are censored. Test the implied restrictions against independent qualification evidence, and never assume a technical pass or dispatch implies paid acceptance.
