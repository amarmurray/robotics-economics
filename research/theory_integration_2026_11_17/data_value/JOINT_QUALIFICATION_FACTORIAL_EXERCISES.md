# Student exercises: learning, joint qualification and whole-production factorial effects

These are mathematical exercises, not field instructions. Do not consult the separate instructor solutions until attempting the questions.

1. **Joint marginals.** Two target qualifications each pass with probability 1/2. In 00,10,01 they are independent. In 11 they may be perfectly positively or negatively associated. A jointly qualified customer recipe contributes 4. Find the two possible source-grounding factorial interactions. Explain why observing each marginal pass rate with arbitrary precision fails to answer the decision question.

2. **Whole-service versus benchmark complementarity.** Use the 00/10/01 independent qualification distributions and perfectly positive 11 distribution. Let baseline f(q)=4 if both qualify, otherwise zero. In arm 11 the optimized customer contribution for both is only 2 because of a shared B-order/operator response. Compute the qualification interaction, service-response term and net interaction. Say what additional evidence would be required to call the response a measured displacement effect.

3. **Physical resource substitution.** A service enabled by x alone and a service enabled by g alone each earns 1, but both require one indivisible operator unit so at most one can run. Show the four optimal values and calculate the interaction. Is the negative interaction evidence of harmful technical transfer?

4. **Randomization.** Four independently eligible complete networks have potential outcome rows (00,10,01,11): (0,1,2,4), (1,2,2,3), (3,1,4,5), (2,0,1,3). One network is randomly assigned to each arm uniformly over the 24 balanced allocations. Compute the population factorial average, and explain why the observed single-allocation contrast is unbiased yet not necessarily exactly equal to it.

5. **Missing approval.** Suppose arm 11 cannot be deployed safely, and the hypothetical whole-service contribution under arm 11 is known only to fall within [0,5]. The other three arm means are those in Exercise 4. Derive the sharp algebraic interval for I. Can that result be used to authorize arm 11?

6. **Changed embodiment and optionality.** Explain why moving to a new h may alter both the probability distribution of jointly qualified recipes and their economic value table. Prove that giving an optimizer free permission to ignore additional source data cannot lower optimal value, and construct a verbal example where *forcing* that source into an approved training recipe lowers value.
