# Cross-cluster occupancy and feasible schedule contrasts

23 November 2026 — extension of [shared-lineage masked closure](SHARED_LINEAGE_MASKED_ECONOMIC_CLOSURE.md) within *When Capabilities Become Production*. Conditional mathematical synthesis, not measured returns.

## 1. Economic state under distinct early data policies

A firm choosing production-generated routine P, deliberate target grounding G, or additional embodiment E can change both the set of feasible joint customer-production schedules and the law of reports at subsequent decisions. More source records need not expose the same report or release the same worker. Include in the economic state source/checkpoint and validation provenance, buyer authorizations, clocks, remaining work, fleet capacity, physical admissibility, and lawful report use. A history-dependent action must be measurable with respect to the actually available report. For shared resources the unit is the joint interference cluster, not each customer separately. A feasible dispatch is a schedule across all connected original orders, and includes any retained old controller when feasible.

Fix two implementable policies pi and sigma, one common structural law theta, and a finite horizon T with discount beta in (0,1]. Augment histories with program/rights identity if necessary to make transition and paid reward conditional expectations well defined under each action. Let mu_t and nu_t be their respective joint history/action occupation probability measures on a common disjoint-union domain Z_t (including a terminal absorbing null state); probability is on the original assignment denominator, not the survivors. For any common approximate potential vhat_t with vhat_(T+1)=0 define d_theta,t(h,a)=E_theta[r_t+beta vhat_(t+1)(H')|h,a]-vhat_t(h), the actual Bellman residual. Prefix investments, opportunity costs and terminal value if any are in rewards; no separate data premium. Let dhat_t be a computable approximation to d_theta,t and e_t=d_theta,t-dhat_t. Conditional expectations can be taken on a sufficient observable history with policy identity; if histories are not sufficient, use the full latent-state law for analysis while keeping the decision rule observable. No latent-type dispatch.

Telescoping under each policy gives the exact difference

    J_theta(pi)-J_theta(sigma)
       = E_pi vhat_1 - E_sigma vhat_1
         + sum_t beta^(t-1) [ <mu_t-nu_t,dhat_t> + <mu_t-nu_t,e_t> ].

Here angle brackets denote finite sums. The initial difference cancels under a common initial law and common vhat_1. Proof: conditional expectation, then telescope vhat_t. No approximation of qualification transitions is required to establish the identity. It remains true when the candidate creates entirely new report cells. Such cells require explicit residual/support bounds; incumbent residual samples are not sufficient.

### A cross-policy, resource-aware error certificate

Suppose a nonnegative edge cost C_t(z,z') bounds |e_t(z)-e_t(z')| on every pair allowed in a coupling of mu_t and nu_t. For any coupling gamma_t of these occupation measures,

    |<mu_t-nu_t,e_t>| <= sum_(z,z') gamma_t(z,z') C_t(z,z').

Proof: use the two marginals to write the integral of the difference and apply the triangle inequality. Hence the minimum finite transport cost over any nonempty admissible coupling family is an upper bound. This is a mathematical coupling of distributions, not a claim to observe two physical counterfactuals on the same order; a restricted coupling (e.g. preserve a prepolicy covariate) requires a valid witness or compatible marginal constraints. Unmatched states get absolute bounds or remain unsupported, never zero-filled. This cost may be asymmetric if only a one-sided bound is needed. A valid upper bound needs no optimum solver: any certified coupling and its cost suffice. With only |e(z)|<=b(z), exact worst-case error over the independent rectangular class is sum_z b(z)|mu(z)-nu(z)|, achieved by e(z)=b(z) sign(mu(z)-nu(z)); it is also the transport cost of moving excess mass with off-diagonal cost b(z)+b(z') and zero diagonal. Match common mass first, then move excess to deficits. This is sharp for that residual class, not for arbitrary structural laws with further restrictions.

Useful stronger structure: suppose the residual admits e(h,a)=alpha(u(h))+b(h,a), where u is a common economic anchor feature (not necessarily the full report). If the two occupation measures have the same pushforward onto u, the alpha contribution cancels exactly for every alpha. More generally with |alpha(u)|<=A(u), its worst-case contribution is bounded by the weighted L1 difference of those pushforwards. With |b(h,a)|<=B(h,a), bound the remaining error by the corresponding weighted occupation difference. If comparing schedules at the same sufficient report with shared component coefficients, use the predecessor's stronger weighted symmetric-difference bound plus the interaction-difference bound for C. For different reports, such cancellation requires an actual common residual coefficient/anchor across programs; otherwise use a separately justified cross-report bound. Rights, embodied state and deadline differences may break this assumption.

For a candidate approximation to occupancy, muhat, nuhat, add the signed occupancy error term <(mu-nu)-(muhat-nuhat),dhat>. It can be bounded by transport costs for dhat differences or directly by measured original-denominator contrast moments. Do not optimize each error, occupancy and qualification law separately and call the result sharp. A robust lower ranking certificate takes an infimum of the approximate signed value minus the applicable error bounds over the *same* admissible theta. Exact identified moments under each policy reduce the set of required inputs. The certificate is stronger than adding two absolute policy errors when there are provably shared anchors and common schedule activities, but not if the structural law is allowed to alter those components arbitrarily. Unknown law cannot select the policy; every candidate rule is fixed ex ante or responds only to its lawful realized report.

## 2. A physical two-customer-cluster witness

One shared specialist worker is available for one customer schedule covering dates 2 and 3, never two overlapping schedules. Original cluster A has a safe qualified retained recipe and an observed binary order/renewal report X. Its net contribution is 2+6X (low: 2 current; high: 4 current plus 4 separately authorized follow-up). Cluster B has an old recipe worth 2 over the same slots. Its improved, validated recipe becomes available after G only if the newly licensed target report is available (H=1) and qualification plus authorized upgraded offer Y=1. In that case it pays 6 (3 current plus 3 distinct authorized follow-up). Otherwise B's old recipe remains available. The binary Y is a latent common potential paid-opportunity type until a lawful report reveals it under H=1; do not treat it as an actual purchase or usable signal on H=0. All payoffs already net of the stated normal resource costs; deadlines prevent sequentially serving A and B on the same worker. The early grounding consumes contribution 1/2 in a distinct earlier slot (including any displaced routine opportunity and curation/validation cost). P has no incremental cost. G retains the A recipe and can ignore its report at dispatch, but its sunk early cost is not refundable.

P observes X and retains A (ties B when low). G additionally observes H and Y only on H=1. A common lawful dispatch chooses B exactly on X=0,H=1,Y=1 and A otherwise. Compare E with an independently staffed additional validated embodiment that preserves A and serves old B=2 at investment cost 7/4; E has separate staff, so no displacement of A. These are stipulated feasible schedules, not physical approval. In an alternative E shares the one worker, cannot add B and incurs the same committed cost.

On the original cluster-pair denominator let (X,H,Y) be uniform over even parity triples with probability p and over odd triples with probability 1-p. Every marginal and complete pair table is invariant. In particular separate A report/paid outcomes and B report availability and qualified upgraded offer have identical marginals; the B-only feasible schedule payoff law is invariant. The latent paired proxy tests do not imply right to use H,Y under P. The missing joint opportunity event X=0,H=1,Y=1 has mass p/4. Under the specified common law,

    J_P=5; J_G=5-1/2+4 p/4=9/2+p; J_E=21/4.

G outranks E exactly when p>3/4. At p=0, G=4.5; at p=1, G=5.5. Independently valuing both customers would assign 5+3-1/2=7.5 to G for every p; the second schedule is physically infeasible. The error is not a data valuation premium, but illegal addition of two uses of the same scarce resource. Even knowing both complete separate marginal paid outcomes and complete pairwise technical/context tables cannot identify the ranking. Direct joint G report/paid histories *would* differ; do not claim observational equivalence of those histories. Without G's report, even knowledge of the hidden type law does not license its use for dispatch. A free optional routine source that reproduces the entire G feasible menu, timing, report rights and transitions achieves 5+p without G's cost; free unqualified bytes alone do not imply this dominance. Committed shared-staff E is 13/4; the optional old P remains feasible, so buying that embodiment is not necessary.

### A strict robust comparison from a sparse lawful audit

Now allow the retained A payoff to differ from its approximation by arbitrary alpha(X) in [-1,1], identical across early programs, and let the incremental B-over-A contribution on the joint hit differ from 4 by delta in [-1/4,1/4]. On other report cells A remains the specified dispatch, with no new incremental gain. E adds a verified incremental 1/4 relative to the same A anchor. These are separate structural restrictions, not implied by source performance or common X marginals alone. Both G and P have the same X marginal so alpha cancels, although full report occupation differs and P has no G report rights. Candidate G-minus-P is exactly

    -1/2 + (4+delta) p/4.

The original-denominator joint hit audit p>=7/8 certifies G-P >= -1/2+(15/4)(7/32)=41/128. Hence G-E >=9/128>0. This is a support-restricted certificate valid under shared anchor transport and bounded incremental economic contrast, even when each absolute policy value has an error of order one. A uniform absolute-value error bound would miss this strict ranking. If alpha differs by early program, or G changes the distribution of X, then its contribution need not cancel: e.g. alpha_G=-1, alpha_P=+1 reduces the comparison by 2, reversing the claim. A lawful audit of p alone cannot establish anchor transport. A matched audit of original-denominator incremental joint schedule gains, with E measured against that same anchor, can replace these restrictions. No dollars inferred from a qualification percentage.
