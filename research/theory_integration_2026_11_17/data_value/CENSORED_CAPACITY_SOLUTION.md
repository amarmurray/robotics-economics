# Solution
A is always ready and both B,C are hidden. The marginal total weighted overflow is 8(.5)+6(.5)=7. For one slot, the gross gain is 2*Pr(B)+6*Pr(B or C)=1+6*Pr(B or C). The union is between .5 and 1; therefore gross return is [4,7] and net [-1,2]. Perfect coincidence attains the lower endpoint and mutual exclusion the upper. Two slots cover both, so gross return is 7 regardless of dependence (subtract the appropriate total two-slot cost, not twice an assumed marginal benefit). Audit joint residual readiness of B,C in original windows, not only completed A; the union is sufficient for one slot in this example. A shared learner or altered customer demand makes the fixed-readiness counterfactual inappropriate for the whole-program contrast, which must include the new joint law, costs and distinct continuation. No benchmark-to-dollar inference.
