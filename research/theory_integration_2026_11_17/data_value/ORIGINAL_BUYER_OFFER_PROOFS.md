# Proof ledger — original buyer offer support and value
**19 Nov 2026; conditional classical results.** [Chapter](ORIGINAL_BUYER_OFFER_SUPPORT.md).

**P1 (threshold interval).** Given a fixed original buyer, one-dimensional accepted service specification and a stable threshold a_ip=1{w_ip≥t_ip}, observation L≤w_iR<U, and |w_ip−w_iR|≤δ, we have w_ip≥L−δ and w_ip<U+δ. Thus t_ip≤L−δ implies a_ip=1, while t_ip≥U+δ implies a_ip=0. If t_ip lies between these cutoffs, then suitable admissible values on either side exist **provided** the interval is populated and both endpoints can be approached; degenerate intervals require care. A price/quality drift error voids the result. A refusal at exactly t means w<t under the maintained tie-acceptance convention.

**P2 (bounds through complete-service optimization).** For any actual acceptance set S satisfying C⊆S⊆P and fixed qualified feasible complete-recipe family F, any nonnegative-contribution feasible solution using C is available under S, and any feasible accepted solution under S is feasible under P. Therefore V(C)≤V(S)≤V(P). For rectangular independent baseline valuations and policy shifts that attain all ambiguous acceptance combinations, both envelope endpoints are attainable, so the bounds are sharp. For joint substitutes, shared buyer pools or policy-dependent F, the inequality may survive as an outer bound only after validating the set/feasibility inclusions; sharpness cannot be asserted. Nonnegative contributions can be imposed by permitting nonproduction of loss-making orders.

**P3 (dual).** For 0≤x≤1 and Ax≤b, add λ≥0 to b−Ax. Maximizing the Lagrangian over 0≤x_i≤1 gives λ·b+Σ max(0,c_i−λ·a_i). Taking its infimum gives the linear relaxation dual optimum under feasible bounded LP conditions. Every integral accepted-order plan is feasible for the relaxation; thus this provides an upper bound. With bundle configurations, model a column for every full jointly accepted recipe and avoid crediting its component motions separately.

**P4 (baseline information nonidentification).** Each counterworld (w_A,w_B)∈{(8,5),(5,8)} implies accepted-at-5 and refused-at-9 standard buyer tests. Both maintain zero policy-induced quality drift within δ=1, same physical/cost menu and same observed R production6. World 1 delivers X=10,G=5; world2 X=5,G=10. Therefore the same original coarse quote register does not rank X vs G. In fact with rectangular w∈[5,9) and |shift|≤1, both new offers at7 are potentially accepted or refused; pure lower net values X=5,G=5,E=−1,R=6. This proves pure maximin R **for that restricted menu**, not an empirical result or all-optimality statement for randomization.

**P5 (new offer support).** Additional A accepted at8 gives w_AR≥8, additional B refused at6 gives w_BR<6. Together with initial upper A<9 and lower B≥5, δ≤1 implies w_AX≥7 and w_BG<7. Hence a_A^X=1 and a_B^G=a_B^E=0, while A is also accepted under E. Substitute into the fixed physical menu to obtain X10>R6>G5>E4. The audit itself must not alter the price, opportunity or controller, otherwise recompute continuation and charge its costs.

**P6 (unbounded off-support receipts).** Suppose a policy adds one qualified slot and a previously unregistered eligible customer can pay M without independently certified upper bound. Construct markets agreeing with every R quote, refusal, cost and qualification record, but varying M arbitrarily. A feasible full order with gross captured contribution M violates any finite uniform policy revenue ceiling. Finite robot/operator slots bound counts, not price. This does not say real payments are unbounded; it is an identification counterexample when limits are unobserved.

**P7 (conditional Bayesian audit arithmetic).** With equal prior on only counterworlds 1 and 2, and zero source/target action drift, pre-audit E[J_X]=E[J_G]=15/2, R=6 and E=4. A perfect audit reveals which incremental service will be accepted; contingent X/G yields10. EVPI=10−15/2=5/2 before audit costs. These prior probabilities and audit feasibility are not inferred from original buyer tests.

Rejected assumptions: (a) a free old policy can always be retained after shared model update; (b) quoted prices equal captured payment; (c) accepted-only sales identify the original market; (d) continuous demand is necessary for a certificate; (e) independent order acceptance follows from separately observed margins; (f) a new physical slot automatically creates a uniform price ceiling.
