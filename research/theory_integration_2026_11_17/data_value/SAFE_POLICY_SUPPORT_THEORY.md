# When Capabilities Become Production — safe support, identified whole-policy contrasts and economic limits

**18 November 2026 | private continuation of the same data-to-qualified-production argument.** This addresses the precise gap left by [the network auditability chapter](AUDITABLE_NETWORK_CERTIFICATE_THEORY.md): what can be concluded from an approved matched policy experiment when an architectural cross-world coupling trigger is unavailable? Neither a new data price nor a general scientific novelty theorem is asserted. All examples are synthetic normalized *captured accepted-service contribution*.

## The target estimand, before choosing an estimator

For pre-assignment independent interference-network unit i, let Y_i(p) be the **complete-horizon** outcome under a wholly specified policy p: starting controller/embodiment, rights, data source mix (routine field, failures/recoveries, teleoperation, simulation, synthetic and public/pretrained distinct), target grounding, subsequent learned qualifications, actual accepted complete customer services, resource use, post-assignment orders including zeros, captured prices and costs. A predeclared terminal production value enters Y **only if its horizon has not already been counted**. Retained old controller F and competent specialist S are available comparators; new embodiment E is eligible only where independently physically admissible. Ex ante site/pool strata D, not post-assignment customers or visited states, define target weights w_d.

The direct economic target is J(p)-J(s)=E[Y(p)-Y(s)], not an unknown unit-level pairing probability. Randomizing entire genuinely independent *controller/dispatch/customer interference clusters* to each **physically approved complete policy** with known positive propensities, holding the eligibility denominator and measurement calendar fixed, design-identifies population means E[Y(p)|D=d] and E[Y(s)|D=d]. It does not require observing the latent persistent transfer regime or a paired counterfactual on any individual pool. The [safe-pool factorial result](SAFE_POOL_FACTORIAL_THEORY.md) already gave the Horvitz–Thompson argument; here we work out its consequences for **nonrepresentative safe support, whole-network economics and decision certification**.

## Proposition — sharp absent-support outer interval and the selective-policy escape

Let E_d be an eligibility/enrolment indicator fixed **before** assignment, possibly selected on latent transfer and thus nonrepresentative. Its target share is e_d=P(E_d=1|D=d), measured on the common pre-action registry. Within E_d=1, approved full-pool randomized assignments identify delta_d^E=E[Y(p)-Y(s)|D=d,E_d=1]. Let Y(p),Y(s) both lie in the *same justified complete-service bounds* [L_d,U_d] on unsupported E_d=0, with R_d=U_d-L_d. Assume the future target population is described by the original pre-action pools (including subsequent zero orders and bounded entrants). Then

  A = sum_d w_d e_d delta_d^E,
  B = sum_d w_d(1-e_d) R_d,
  J(p)-J(s) belongs to [A-B,A+B].                                        (SS1)

The interval is **sharp within this unrestricted bounded-potential-outcomes class**: two worlds can agree on all observed eligible assignments and on E_d and w_d while setting every unobserved Y(p),Y(s) at opposite endpoint combinations. The unknown potential joint dependence does not affect the mean-difference identification on E. Extra restrictions, such as a known specialist mean on E=0, can make (SS1) conservative: if E[Y(s)|E=0,d]=m_d is identified and L_d<=Y(p)<=U_d, replace that cell's symmetric interval by [L_d-m_d,U_d-m_d]. A hypothetical globally deployed p on unapproved cells is **not a policy one may implement**, irrespective of the numeric bound.

Instead compare two actually approved *selective* policies H_p and H_s. Both take the identical approved fallback F in E=0; in E=1 they take p versus s, with identical predeclared calendar and input budgets. If complete interference units are independent across eligible/noneligible pool allocations and there is no customer-demand or operator spillover between them, then

  J(H_p)-J(H_s) = A.                                                       (SS2)

This **does not compare H_p to S deployed everywhere**; doing that additionally requires the measured F-versus-S contribution on E=0. If a separately justified *complete-policy* residual spillover bound Gamma limits the change from the isolated mixture, and additional uncounted isolation/coordination cost is at most c, a sufficient bound is

  J(H_p)-J(H_s) >= A - Gamma - c.                                         (SS3)

Gamma may come from the earlier policy-uniform conditional influence recursion with verified complete controller, dispatch and demand edges; it cannot be inferred from the randomized eligible mean alone. If isolation modifies learning or orders, evaluate that isolated policy directly. The prior 7.09675 versus 7.08 **synthetic** comparison is not updated by an arbitrary new Gamma: double-counting an existing allowance is forbidden.

**Interpretation.** Missing unsafe or never visited sites may prevent a global grounding-versus-embodiment ranking while allowing a smaller, beneficial safe covered policy. This is standard partial identification plus policy restriction, not an exemption from network or physical certification. Where new learning checkpoints are shared globally, E=0 pools may not actually receive unchanged F even if no direct intervention occurs, so SS2 fails.

## Exact constructive illustration

Two pre-action target strata have weights w_A=3/5 and w_B=2/5. All of A is safely covered; only one-quarter of B is covered. In the covered randomized evaluation the full-policy accepted-service differences p minus s are 3/10 in A and 1/10 in B. Thus A=19/100 and uncovered target mass is 3/10. Let [0,2] be a genuinely justified per-cluster *complete-horizon captured contribution* range, including post-policy order response, not a robot benchmark score.

With no unsupported-policy measurement, (SS1) gives **[-41/100,79/100]**. Observing the unsupported specialist mean equal to 1 narrows this to **[-11/100,49/100]**. Both endpoints are constructed by setting unsupported policy means to 0 or 2; increasing the eligible trial indefinitely does not identify the missing side. But the approved selective-versus-selective comparison has gain **19/100** without spillovers; an independently justified Gamma=2/25 would leave **11/100**; a further incremental c=3/20 would make that lower bound **-1/25**. Gamma and c are illustrative assumed limits, not measured properties. The distinction is **which policy** is being ranked, not a magical increase in dataset value.

The accompanying [exact-fraction executable](safe_policy_support.js) and [results](safe_policy_support_results.json) enumerate the unsupported endpoint worlds and all 16 assignments of a four-pool Horvitz–Thompson example. In that example E[HT_p]=5/4, E[HT_s]=1, hence an identified mean difference 1/4 without ever observing both potential outcomes for one pool.

## Three failure modes that matter for production

**Persistent learning makes switchback periods unequal.** In a two-period illustration, R produces 10 now and does not improve the next controller; G produces 9 now but enables next-period R to produce 13. Sequence G→R returns 22 versus R→R at 20; R→G returns only 19 in the same horizon. Naively comparing G's observed period (9) with the following R period (13) ranks the actions backward for the *initial full-policy* investment decision. This is a carryover/sequence effect, not evidence that R itself learned or that a single-period task metric equals a dollar return. Entire starting-controller history and retained/frozen branches belong to the treatment definition.

**Customer and dispatch interference can make a cluster design invalid.** If changing site A's policy reallocates one valuable order away from B through a common dispatcher or sales network, A's +1 can coincide with B's -1 and zero total accepted contribution. Measuring A alone mistakes a customer reallocation for new economic production. The true randomized unit must include all connected demand, operator and controller edges, or an explicit exposure mapping/verified bound must handle cross-unit interference. A single globally connected network does not produce many independent experimental units simply because it contains many robots.

**A favorable population mean cannot certify every persistent regime or hard operator feasibility.** Two hidden regimes with policy-minus-specialist effects +1.4 and -1, each at 50%, have population mean +0.2 but worst-regime difference -1. Without stratified regime information, ordinary full-policy randomization identifies the former, not a robust same-theta guarantee. Even a strongly positive mean cannot authorize a recovery path with positive support above a hard simultaneous-operator capacity. Require independently certified pre-danger admission/safe stop.

## What the public architecture actually establishes (not robot fleet evidence)

A pinned **Open-RMF public example**, [rmf_demos README at 05f5e3f](https://github.com/open-rmf/rmf_demos/blob/05f5e3faad507c0d67d9343efc81093ee2e30610/README.md) (repository commit November 17, 2026), describes a Dispatcher sending bids to fleet adapters and choosing among competing proposed assignments. [rmf_task README at 92832a6](https://github.com/open-rmf/rmf_task/blob/92832a6dbd204f7fce5b3e6ea000339e0dc4ac96/README.md) (repository commit November 18, 2026) describes multi-fleet allocation and task-phase interruption/cancellation behavior. These are concrete examples of a **possible cross-fleet dispatch pathway**: a priori per-robot independence is not warranted merely because the learners differ. Neither repository is evidence that any intended commercial robot deployment uses this stack, that model weights are broadcast, that human operator reservations satisfy a hard safety invariant, or that customer orders change. No such fleet-specific architecture was available.

**Prior art / limitation.** Missing outcome sharp bounds and safety of estimands are classical, cf. Horowitz–Manski (2000, JASA 95:77–84, doi:10.1080/01621459.2000.10473902). Rosenbaum (1984, JRSS A 147:656–666, doi:10.2307/2981697) demonstrates the danger of conditioning on treatment-affected variables. Only abstracts/selected source descriptions were checked on November 18; no complete external proofs have been reproduced. The contribution candidate remains an application to qualified robotics production, not a claim to have invented missing-data bounds.
