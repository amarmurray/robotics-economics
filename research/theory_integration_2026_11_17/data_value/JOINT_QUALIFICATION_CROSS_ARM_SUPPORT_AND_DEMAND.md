## The target-level potential-qualification lattice

For a fixed target context, seed/exposure mapping and operator/rights envelope \(\omega\), denote the *jointly assessable* potential qualification of complete service \(j\in\{A,B\}\) under \(z\in\{R=00,S=10,G=01,SG=11\}\) by \(Q_j^z(\omega)\in\{0,1\}\). These are **whole learner/operator/network potential outcomes**, not independent per-episode training labels. Put \(H_z=Q_A^z Q_B^z\), \(p_j^z=\mathbb E Q_j^z\), \(h_z=\mathbb E H_z\), over one common predeclared target distribution. Cross-world coupling is not identified simply by randomizing separate clusters: stable matched units or defensible structural restrictions are additional assumptions.

**Proposition 1 (monotone cross-arm bounds).** If each \(Q_j^z\) is pathwise nondecreasing in both S and G along the Boolean intervention lattice, then
\[
\boxed{\max(h_S,h_G)\le h_{SG}\le
\min\!\left\{p_A^{SG},p_B^{SG},
h_S+\sum_j(p_j^{SG}-p_j^S),
h_G+\sum_j(p_j^{SG}-p_j^G)\right\}.}\tag{A}
\]
Proof: the event \(H_{SG}=1\) contains \(H_S=1\) and \(H_G=1\). Moreover, for binary monotone variables,
\(H_{SG}-H_S\le(Q_A^{SG}-Q_A^S)+(Q_B^{SG}-Q_B^S)\) pointwise. Take expectations and repeat with G. The upper marginal limits are immediate. The inequalities are necessary under the stated pathwise restriction, but are not asserted jointly sufficient at arbitrary unobserved margins.

**Proposition 2 (stronger pathwise modularity).** Suppose additionally, for **each service and target unit**, \(Q_j^{SG}=Q_j^S\vee Q_j^G\) and \(Q_j^R=Q_j^S\wedge Q_j^G\). This says S and G have no task-level AND synergy and no task-level OR redundancy on a unit; it is much stronger than zero *average* S×G interaction in marginal qualification. Then
\[
\boxed{h_{SG}\ge h_S+h_G-h_R,\qquad
\Delta_{S,G}h=h_{SG}-h_S-h_G+h_R\ge0.}\tag{B}
\]
Proof: the intersection of joint-success events \(H_S\cap H_G\) is exactly \(H_R\); their union lies within \(H_{SG}\). Inclusion–exclusion yields (B). Combined with the fixed-buyer production identity, if both marginal qualification interactions vanish, the source×grounding *economic* interaction has the sign of \(\kappa\); this sign is conditional on the buyer's complementary bundle or substitutive scarce-slot production table, not a fixed intrinsic property of source data.

**Why the assumptions matter.** Task-level monotonicity cannot be inferred from an overall nested *option set*: a forced neural update can degrade an individual task, even if the firm can still choose an older checkpoint. Pathwise modularity is not implied by \(\Delta p_j=0\); positive AND and negative OR changes can cancel in expectations. Shared weights, site access, joint safety certification and interference may make even a matched \(\omega\) across four whole-learner programs infeasible. If these assumptions fail, revert to the original conditional Fréchet region; do not impose them to get a preferred investment.

## Sharp conditional counterworlds, not merely a parameter rerun

Reuse **exactly** the preceding hypothetical paid bundle \(F(00)=0,F(10)=F(01)=2,F(11)=12\) and investment costs \(K_S=K_G=1/2\). Retain E-only net 4 from a separately qualified and signed hypothetical service. Marginal complete-task qualifications remain \(p_A=p_B=(2/5,1/2,1/2,3/5)\) under (R,S,G,SG), with observed joint \((h_R,h_S,h_G)=(1/5,1/4,1/4)\). A marginal-only Fréchet analysis admits \(h_{SG}\in[1/5,3/5]\).

Each task has exactly **six** coordinatewise monotone Boolean response types over (R,S,G,SG): never (0000), AND (0001), S-only (0101), G-only (0011), OR (0111), always (1111). A joint learner-target response law is a nonnegative 6×6 table. Exact rational sparse *witnesses* and (A) establish that the sharp feasible interval under task-level monotonicity is **[1/4,9/20]** for these same measured margins and R/S/G joint outcomes. At its endpoints, SG net \(=7/5+8h_{SG}\) ranges from \(17/5\) to \(5\), straddling E net 4. Both worlds agree on all those measured moments and satisfy taskwise monotonicity, yet the optimal capital program is E in the first and SG in the second.

With the *stronger*, testable-in-principle pathwise modularity restriction only four task response types remain (never, S-only, G-only, always). The 4×4 exact integer witnesses and (A)–(B) make the sharp interval **[3/10,9/20]**; SG net ranges from \(19/5\) to \(5\), **still** straddling E. The earlier example's \(h_{SG}=1/5\) is admissible **without** this stronger cross-arm restriction, but cannot be carried into a model that asserts it. The old numerical counterexample remains valid in its original unrestricted environment.

## Original customer demand is an endogenous state, not merely a coefficient

Let \(\pi_z(d)\) be the **actual distribution of original eligible offered orders** by customer segment/task-bundle \(d\) under complete program \(z\), and \(m_z(d)=\mathbb E[F_{e,d,N}(Q^z)\mid d,do(z)]\). Include actual settled acceptance, refunds, resource/time displacement and qualified fallback in signed \(F\); E can change that table and manufacturing constraints. For a fixed number of eligible offers,
\[
W_z=\sum_d\pi_z(d)m_z(d)-K_z.
\]
With a common predeclared reference market \(\bar\pi\), an exact decomposition of a choice \(z\) against \(k\) is
\[
W_z-W_k=
\underbrace{\sum_d\bar\pi(d)[m_z(d)-m_k(d)]-K_z+K_k}_{\text{matched-market production effect}}
+\underbrace{\sum_d[\pi_z(d)-\bar\pi(d)]m_z(d)-\sum_d[\pi_k(d)-\bar\pi(d)]m_k(d)}_{\text{intervention-induced demand composition}}.\tag{C}
\]
The per-segment Möbius identity remains valid with \(p_j^{z\mid d},h_z^{d}\). **Pooled task qualification frequencies can change because the buyer mix changes even if no learner changes.** Under bounded \(m_z\in[L_z,U_z]\), the absolute composition term for program z is at most \((U_z-L_z)\operatorname{TV}(\pi_z,\bar\pi)\) for probability distributions. Thus a fixed-market net margin is robust to unmeasured but bounded shifts if it exceeds the sum of those conservative bounds. If *arrival counts* respond as well, track the offer intensity \(\Lambda_z\); a probability-mixture TV bound alone does not cover expansion or contraction of total orders.

**Same physical learner, reverse investment decision.** In the modular upper-witness world \(p_A=p_B=3/5,h_{SG}=9/20\), the signed original-buyer bundled market yields SG gross 6, net 5; E-only net 4. If 1/4 of eligible orders instead become one-slot substitute work, for which the **same** task qualifications yield \(F=(0,2,2,2)\) and gross \(3/2\), expected SG gross is \((3/4)6+(1/4)(3/2)=39/8\), net \(31/8<4\). For this *constructed* comparison the E embodiment serves a distinct, separately contracted workstream whose net 4 is invariant to this A/B order-mix shift. The ranking reverses without any change to qualification outcomes or data-transfer accuracy. This is a counterexample to taking one fixed-demand Fréchet decision threshold into a new customer market—not a claim that deploying SG actually reduces demand.
