# Exercises: data, demand information and qualified production

Read [the chapter](MENU_INFORMATION_INTERACTION_AND_DECISION_REFINEMENT.md) before attempting these; no empirical robotics outcome is supplied.

1. Let buyer orders be A or B, equally likely. An old robot can reserve only the A recipe, yielding (1,0), or idle. Source training makes B=(0,1) available without replacing A. Calculate four optimized *gross* accepted-paid values (old/new, with/without perfectly revealing buyer order). Derive the economic interaction and explain whether positive interaction implies positive intrinsic technical transfer.
2. Start instead from an old menu with A=(1,0) and B=(0,1). Training unlocks generalist U=(3/4,3/4). Recalculate the four values, demonstrate source-data optionality in both information regimes and determine the sign of interaction.
3. For finite Z,A and strictly positive conditional probabilities, prove that the one-step information loss E[max Q]-max E[Q] is zero exactly when at least one action is optimal at every hidden state. Explain why this statement does not establish a valid full-horizon Markov quotient.
4. Source costs 0.10, buyer-order grounding/report 0.05, alternative embodiment E costs 0.10 and delivers constant 0.75 without a report. In exercise 1's world, compare R, S, G, SG and E under costs paid once. Name two real interventions that would invalidate the fixed-demand/fixed-action-payoff identity.
5. Suppose one history licenses a newly trained B recipe and another does not, though both have the same aggregate paid-service statistic C. Can a single C-only policy commit B in both histories? State the legal-menu condition before applying a Jensen-gap formula. What changes if training data generated by today's paid task also enables a new buyer tomorrow?

Keep answers distinct from instructions for any real physical experiment.
