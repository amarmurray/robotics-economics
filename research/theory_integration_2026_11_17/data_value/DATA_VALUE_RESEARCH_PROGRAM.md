# Data value within the integrated production theory

Amar's November 17 instruction prioritizes theoretical work on data transfer and data value, building on but not overfitting to _Data-Driven Automation_. This is the next sustained part of _When Capabilities Become Production_, not a restart, a new Q-numbered paper, or permission to drop the broader roadmap.

## Starting point now established

Read DATA_VALUE_THEORY.md. Its conditional source-value and interaction identities connect a learning technology to the existing optimized production function. Its exact optimal-mixture problems show that customer economics can change the best acquisition portfolio, that greater transfer can reduce optimal acquisition diversity, and that technical-score and economic objectives can disagree. Exponential and rational saturation give separate solvable examples. Standard multilinear/optimization mathematics is acknowledged; scientific novelty is not established.

The source audit must remain honest: DDA already contains directed spillovers, production-generated data, a forward-looking planner, and an appendix discussion of complementary data aggregation. Do not spend another run claiming those as missing ideas. ReMix and cross-embodiment evidence supply competing technical objectives and data-structure mechanisms, not economic parameters.

## Next sustained question: the transfer operator is itself an investment outcome

An enterprise may possess abundant data that a new embodiment cannot yet use. Additional volume, targeted grounding, representation/alignment work, a changed tool/body, or a deliberate experiment can be alternative uses of the same resources. The next model should explain the choice rather than impose a fixed improvement coefficient on each action.

Build the smallest formulation in which source stock x, target grounding g and an embodiment/alignment choice h jointly determine the admissible learning map. Start with a physically interpretable condition: visual coverage can transfer while action realization still requires target grounding. Avoid making one data modality the master ontology; allow field, teleoperation, simulation and synthetic inputs where they solve different information/control needs.

Keep customer contribution, accepted-service requirements and competent alternatives inside the operating value function. Separate technical complementarities in the learning map from economic complementarities in production. Derive whether and when an alignment investment increases the marginal value of previously collected source data. Test it under a second learning function, a capped physical capability and retention of old policies. Do not assume all model or hardware changes are automatically compatible with all stored data.

The strongest objection is that an arbitrary product x*g can manufacture a complement. Address it with an explicit data-generation/identification or representation mechanism, or label the product a reduced-form scenario and bound what it can support. The preferred deliverable is a proof or counterexample about the investment choice plus a matched technical experiment that could distinguish the mechanisms.

## Then endogenize which data production supplies

The one common dynamic state includes retained data/metadata, qualified configurations, physical resources and customer opportunity states. A productive deployment earns current contribution and generates selected observations. A deliberate experiment may generate different observations at a cost. A simulated trajectory is not automatically a real-world control label. The data's economic gain enters the continuation value, never a second strategic bonus.

Study whether current commercial production oversupplies familiar examples while leaving rare recovery states or target-body grounding scarce. Compare a competent profit-only policy, a fixed general-purpose technical-data objective, and a customer-opportunity-aware policy under the same resources/information. Do not deliberately handicap the comparators. Start with an exact finite or analytically tractable model; no GPU training or new orchestration platform.

An important test is whether the advantage survives demand change and a serious alternative data source. A low-margin deployment should not win merely because its learning payoff was assigned a favorable constant. Source-to-target evidence must define or bound the transition.

## Acquisition is not attribution or price

Maintain a separate distinction between the marginal value of an additional dataset, an accounting allocation of total value among existing sources, and an implementable price to obtain usable rights. Interaction means these can differ. Do not impose additive per-trajectory prices that sum to more than total incremental value. Retain nonrival reuse, access rights and different beneficiaries as eventual extensions; do not let the next runs revert to contract-law detail before the data-production argument is developed.

## Empirical bridge and learning materials

Use a two-stage design: a controlled source/grounding/mixing intervention measuring persistent target qualification, followed by an operating comparison across attained qualification sets under matched demand/resources. Keep fixed-budget reallocations separate from adding data at additional cost. Record joint outcomes and held-out sites/tasks/embodiments. Technical score, accepted customer service, provider margin, and willingness to pay remain distinct.

Technical empirical seed set: DDA sections 2, 5 and Appendix B; ReMix; Data Analogies; CrossFormer; the prior controlled transfer and qualification dossiers. Introduce additional papers only to solve a specific mechanism or prior-art objection. No new public dataset is claimed to provide a complete customer-data-value panel.

Keep the sector/value-chain atlas active. The official numeric BEA route remains unresolved; do not replace it with corporate announcements or retry denied paths without a genuinely different route. This data subtheory supplies the capability/learning layer for that broader atlas.
