# Economic influence closure: when a blocked recovery state matters one date later

23 November 2026 · Integrated continuation of *When Capabilities Become Production*, especially SERVICE_RESPONSE_FOURWAY_CAUSAL_AUDIT.md. **The November 22 Models A–B scientific gate remains OPEN.** All numerical values and distributions here are stipulated synthetic normalized contributions, not commercial robotics estimates or a claim of novel stochastic-decision mathematics.

## The connected question

The prior causal audit identified the t3 contribution of a licensed grounding program G from G-assigned accepted early service H, original late buyers A/C and an available shared recovery worker W. With its t3 deadline, a blocked worker (W=0) could contribute no t3 service; a joint A/C original-offer audit restricted to H=W=1 was sufficient for the *one-date fixed policy* once G-specific lower-order moments were known. But an unfinished repair can clear in time for t4 and a buyer can place a new original order. Does the earlier audit still identify the *whole-program* investment choice between source learning P, target grounding G, independently staffed embodiment E and the retained safe policy R?

**Answer:** No in general. Economic relevance propagates backward through continuation states, even if an earlier state generated zero contemporaneous output. A finite-policy value admits an exact representation as a linear functional of the one common, physically coherent causal scenario law. Decision-sufficient evidence must identify that functional (or robustly certify its ranking), not merely the last-period event. This chapter proves the statement, supplies an explicit four-date counterworld with genuine paid work, and specifies a lawful conditional audit that closes the missing joint moment in this *restricted* service menu.

## 1. General theorem: value, measurement and an economic influence closure

Consider a finite, physically closed buyer/robot/support network. A causal world \(\omega\in\Omega\) fixes exogenous buyer propensities, physical shocks and potential responses to all permitted R/P/G/E programs. The same world is used to evaluate all programs: we do **not** give each investment its own independently selected adversarial scenario. A policy \(\pi\) maps available histories into qualified, rights-permitted actions before their deadlines. Under that policy define \(g_\pi(\omega)\) as the *total* discounted captured contribution from actually accepted units, less incremental labor, compute, validation, hardware and data-rights costs, counting each once. The baseline safe recipes remain optional.

Let \(p=(p_\omega)_{\omega\in\Omega}\) be a probability distribution satisfying the common-law causal restrictions and physical zeroes. Then

\[
 V_\pi(p)=\sum_\omega p_\omega g_\pi(\omega)=g_\pi^\top p.
\]

Suppose prospective records identify a finite set of original-offer, paid-service and worker-history moments \(Ap=b\), including normalization, and let \(\mathcal L(b)=\{p\ge0:Ap=b,\ p\text{ respects the causal/physical constraints}\}\).

**Finite-menu identification proposition.** If the admissible set has a relative-interior distribution positive on all permitted atoms and the structural constraints are linear equalities or structural-zero restrictions, \(V_\pi\) is point identified on \(\mathcal L(b)\) if and only if \(g_\pi\) lies in the row space of the combined observation/structural-equality matrix on its permitted support. For a *value difference*, replace \(g_\pi\) with \(g_\pi-g_{\pi'}\). Otherwise exact lower/upper values are the linear-program extrema \(\min_{p\in\mathcal L(b)}g_\pi^\top p\), \(\max_{p\in\mathcal L(b)}g_\pi^\top p\); a ranking can be robust without point identification whenever the lower bound of \(V_\pi-V_{\pi'}\) is positive (or its upper bound negative). If the feasible set lacks an interior in the stated affine hull, the criterion must use its *actual* affine-hull direction space rather than an unrestricted kernel.

*Proof.* Any identified row-space functional is a linear combination of observed moments and structural equalities. Conversely, when \(g_\pi\) is outside that row space there is a permitted null direction \(v\) with \(Av=0\), \(g_\pi^\top v\ne0\). Positive interior permits small perturbations \(p\pm\epsilon v\), which preserve observations and normalization but change value. The extrema and robust-sign certificate follow immediately from linearity and compactness. This is standard finite linear identification and partial-identification logic, not a claimed new theorem.

For multistage policies the quantity to measure is not decided by whether a variable predicts *today's* output. A history \(h_t\) belongs to the economic influence closure of a fixed program/policy if varying a supported, unrecorded successor mechanism at \(h_t\), while holding the recorded mechanisms fixed, can change its full continuation value:

\[
 Q_t(h_t)=\mathbb E\left[r_t+\beta J_{t+1}(H_{t+1})\mid h_t,\text{permitted action}\right].
\]

If the reachable continuation payoff is constant across a missing event's realizations (under all relevant feasible responses), that event is payoff-irrelevant for the specified menu and no *additional* audit is needed for its value. If not, its relevant *joint* outcome must be identified or bounded. This is a necessary influence screen, not a universal minimal-covariate algorithm; algebraic cancellation and substitutable policies can eliminate otherwise apparent ancestors.

The causal comparison additionally requires consistency, appropriate action support, credible sequential exchangeability or an approved randomized design, and no omitted interference outside the registered network. Observed *selected invoices* do not automatically identify original buyer offers. Without G assignment, no purely historical R audit manufactures the G response kernel.

## 2. Four dates, two embodiments and a worker who can clear later

Preserve the preceding physically ordered t0–t3 G experiment. The installed arm A and mobile C share one late qualified production/repair slot. Customer A has one payable unit; customer C has two, with at most one accepted at t2 and one later. t0 selects R, P, G or E. G pays \(k_G=1/5\); at t2 a target-C accepted paid success \(H\in\{0,1\}\) earns \(c=2H\) and qualifies C at the old fleet by t3. A new G-created repair job is assigned **after** the t2 operating window. At t3, \(W=1\) denotes that this common worker is free; when W=0, t3 service cannot occur. Original t3 A/C buyer offers \(A,C\) precede dispatch. One qualified t3 accepted unit earns \(v_3=4\). The retained policy R and productive source P retain their old qualified recipes and independently staffed E has a genuinely separate robot/worker slot, as in the preceding common-law witness.

G's original t3 gross contribution is

\[
 2H+4W\bigl[A\lor(HC)\bigr].
\]

The G-assigned structural histories use independent fair pre-existing bits \(H,U,V\):

\[
 A=U\oplus H,\qquad C=V\oplus H,\qquad W=H\oplus U\oplus V.
\]

This is the even-parity example from the preceding proof, held FIXED across the two new worlds. It gives the same *entire* G distribution of (H,A,C,W), including its four-way event, in both worlds; all the old t3 original-offer audits are therefore identical. R/P/E potential outcomes remain the same old-policy histories and prices, with \(V_R=2,\ V_P=21/10,\ V_E=43/20\).

**New at t4:** A newly generated original C order D can be paid at t4 at contribution \(v_4=6\) only if (i) H=1 had qualified C and used just C's first lifetime payable unit, (ii) W=0 blocked t3 and therefore no second C unit was used, (iii) the previously owed repair clears before t4 (\(L=1\)), and (iv) the buyer actually places that original t4 order (\(D=1\)). The previously blocked t3 order expires rather than being invoiced twice. The shared worker can then perform *one* t4 qualified order; otherwise the late slot is unavailable or used elsewhere. A no-G program does not induce this G-specific repeat-order opportunity in the witness. There is no retroactive clearance and no duplicated customer purchasing budget.

The complete fixed G program has

\[
 \boxed{g_G=2H+4W[A\lor HC]+6H(1-W)DL-\tfrac15}.
\]

Define the missing economic moment

\[
 m_G=\mathbb E_G[H(1-W)DL].
\]

Here the first three terms are accepted paid service at their actual dates, and the fourth is the one-time trial cost. The earlier G t3 expectation equals \(9/5\) in this fixed even-parity law. Thus

\[
 \boxed{V_G=\tfrac95+6m_G.}
\]

The moment is *not* a technical pass rate, the price of the data itself, or a second continuation premium. It is the probability that original customer demand and post-repair productive capacity coincide when the licensed target model is qualified.

## 3. Counterworld: identical terminal audit, opposite investment decision

At t0 each cluster also has an independent fair latent bit Z, jointly affecting the later customer propensity and the recovery-clearing environment. Set \(D=Z\). In the only productive t4 risk set \(B=\{H=1,W=0\}\), let

\[
 L=\begin{cases} Z & \text{world }+\\ 1-Z & \text{world }-.\end{cases}
\]

Outside B, set L=Z in BOTH worlds. All these variables are functions of shocks fixed before the relevant events; t4 demand does not cause earlier t2 repair. The two worlds differ in a common customer–repair correlation, not in an intervention after observing a future order. Both have \(\Pr(B)=1/4\). In B, each marginal \(\Pr(D=1\mid B)=\Pr(L=1\mid B)=1/2\) is identical, as are their marginals conditional on *every* preceding (H,A,C,W) history. The entire t3 G history, the earlier H=W=1 joint original-offer audit (even if D and L are later recorded in that *other* risk set), the separate D/L marginals, and all complete R/P/E histories are identical across worlds.

But their joint conditional probability differs:

| G-assigned world | \(\Pr(D=L=1\mid B)\) | \(m_G\) | \(V_G\) | Preferred program |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| + : customer order aligns with completed repair | \(1/2\) | \(1/8\) | \(51/20=2.55\) | G |
| − : customer order arrives while repair remains | \(0\) | \(0\) | \(9/5=1.80\) | E |

With known conditional marginals \(d=\ell=1/2\), the Fréchet inequalities give

\[
 0=\max(0,d+\ell-1)\le \Pr(D=L=1\mid B)\le\min(d,\ell)=\tfrac12.
\]

Multiply by \(\Pr(B)=1/4\): \(0\le m_G\le1/8\). Both extremes are realized by a *single, common structural law* over the whole network, and convex mixtures are physically attainable across independent clusters, so

\[
 \boxed{V_G\in[9/5,\,51/20]}
\]

is a sharp conditional interval in this specified information class. E=43/20 lies in its interior. G ranks ahead of E exactly when \(m_G>7/120\), not when its benchmark pass rate alone crosses some threshold.

The key negative result is stronger than "we need more data": **adding perfect t3 H=W=1 original-offer coverage still cannot recover a t4 contribution concentrated in H=1,W=0.** Terminal and continuation economic influence sets differ.

## 4. Robust, executable choice; not an oracle

At t0 the hidden regime cannot be used to select a different program for the same cluster. A feasible precommitment randomizes fraction \(x\) of independent clusters to G and \(1-x\) to E. R=2 and P=2.1 are retained feasible options but E dominates both in THIS illustrative menu. The world-conditional regrets relative to the best admissible single program are

\[
 \mathcal R_+(x)=\tfrac25(1-x),\qquad
 \mathcal R_-(x)=\tfrac7{20}x.
\]

Equalizing gives the exact minimax-regret commitment

\[
 \boxed{x_G=\tfrac8{15},\quad x_E=\tfrac7{15},\quad
 \sup_\theta\mathcal R_\theta=\tfrac{14}{75}.}
\]

Maximizing guaranteed production instead selects E entirely (guarantee 43/20). Neither certificate assumes the firm can see Z, a future t4 order, or the true correlation at t0. A prospective audit can improve *later cohorts'* decisions, not retroactively reroute the current cohort. Giving an oracle the true world before commitment would describe a different feasible policy and overstate the economic return to evidence.

## 5. The lawful history-specific audit and its limits

For the **fixed R/P/G/E and t4 service menu only**, suppose lawful, prospective G assignments and verified lower-order G t2/t3 paid histories already identify the earlier 9/5 term. A complete worker-clearance log identifies L; a randomized original t4 C offer/no-offer sample, inclusive of unserved orders, identifies D. The missing *additional* statistic is \(m_G\), which is zero outside B. Its audit can therefore be concentrated on the prior *paid-early, t3-worker-blocked* risk set B, in addition to whatever prospective t3 observations were needed for the earlier term. This is **opposite** to the previous one-date H=W=1-only extra audit.

Let Z_G indicate assignment of the entire interference-closed deployment cluster to G with known probability \(p_G>0\). Inside B, independently sample original-offer documentation M with known inclusion probability \(\eta(X,H,W)>0\), assigned before the unreported D is known, conditional on eligible pre-audit X. Assume D and L are actually verified; worker clearance and the offer process must be observed through t4 and actual paid service settled. Then the following standard inverse-observation identity estimates the G-specific event from appropriately randomized clusters:

\[
 \boxed{
 m_G=\mathbb E\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{Z_G=1\}}{p_G}
 \frac{M\,H(1-W)DL}{\eta(X,H,W)}\right].
 }
\]

The expectation is over the prospective experiment's clusters. This is not permission to condition on the post-treatment B event when estimating an R-to-G treatment effect: **B is a post-G risk set**, and its probability must be retained in the program-level weighting. Sequential outcome regression or a properly specified longitudinal g-formula can instead integrate measured, supported histories. If audit inclusion depends on the unseen D/L coincidence, this identity fails. If the worker and buyers share interference beyond the registered cluster, randomize or model the whole sharing network. If G was never assigned, \(p_G=0\), the contrast is not identified. A consented t4 offer audit cannot provide past G-qualified outcomes from unsupported R histories.

For *new* target-grounding policies, t4 A service, alternative embodiment repair routes, or t4 demand that changes after t3 accepted service, the fixed risk set and one scalar cease to be sufficient. Recompute the backward economic influence closure and prospective measurement requirements for the enlarged action menu. Field work must link original buyer opportunities, accepted payments, qualified technical recipes, worker occupation and distinct failure/recovery/teleoperation/simulation/synthetic/public-pretrained data sources under their actual retention and training rights. A free, optional extra corpus cannot reduce optimized value when the old recipe remains feasible; the low-G world is a costly, service-changing G program, **not proof of harmful free data**.
