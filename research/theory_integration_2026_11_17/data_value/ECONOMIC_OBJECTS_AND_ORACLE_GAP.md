# From the economics of learned production to a decision on robotic capital
25 November 2026 Eastern. Integrated theoretical scaffold and evidence synthesis for *When Capabilities Become Production*, not an additional paper.

## Priors: which productive objects can change?
The task-based production tradition distinguishes tasks from the factors assigned to perform them. Acemoglu and Restrepo, *Automation and New Tasks*, NBER WP 25684 (2019), https://www.nber.org/papers/w25684, abstract inspected 25 November 2026, studies the allocation of tasks between capital and labor and the displacement and reinstatement effects. Data-Driven Automation (Farboodi, Koh and Xia, arXiv:2606.10127v1, sections 5 and Appendix B; earlier version audit) already models forward-looking data acquisition and complementary aggregation. Our premise is not that those ideas are missing. The robotics-specific problem is that a change in the stock of know-how may change which physical actions can be performed, which production recipes are qualified, and which complementary inputs are required on a particular date. The task allocation itself can therefore be endogenous to a joint learning, embodiment and coordination program.

Distinguish (1) embodied capital, a rival durable stock of mechanically feasible service opportunities; (2) controller capital, reusable but costly-to-serve instructions or policies, not necessarily transferable across tasks or bodies; (3) source-specific data capital, lawful reusable evidence with conditional value and heterogeneous transfer, not a scalar count of demonstrations; (4) qualification, a relation among a controller, body, customer, workflow, time and rights; (5) organization, the coordination of scarce bodies, recovery labor, compute, and release slots; (6) original and induced customer demand, which determines where feasible work actually has value. Mechanical degrees of freedom do not imply capability, capability does not imply qualification, and qualification does not imply output. A shared controller may economize on adaptation or simultaneously generate correlated regressions. Coordination may free capacity without any new learning, or change the future evidence distribution. Different evidence sources remain distinguishable: field trajectories, exceptions/recoveries, teleoperation, simulations, synthetic trajectories, and pretrained/public input.

The economic object of interest is the value of the feasible *program*, with its own evolving information, demand, qualification, resource incidence and lawful data retention. Use Model A's common production objective for each dated feasible state and Model B's full discounted program J for learning and investment. A useful notation is an embodied-capital return vector: MPK_r,i^g is the change in optimized qualified net production when embodied type i is incremented, holding the stated learning program g fixed (finite differences for indivisible bodies; physical unit return is not a rate on dollars). Dynamic marginal value also reflects induced experience, qualification and future demand in J, with all costs and continuation counted once. Data capital and embodied capital are economic complements if the incremental net program value of extra embodied capacity is greater with the data intervention than without it, for matched feasible policies and common accounting. They can substitute when learned resource efficiency replaces capacity. There is no necessary sign. This is a comparative statement about changes in feasible production, not a benchmark elasticity.

In a production network, a recipe may consume intermediates and produce outputs which become inputs to a later recipe. Let y be activity levels, M the net-output incidence matrix, A dated primary-resource occupation and d final demand. A planner's final-output value can be represented by max v'f subject to My>=f, Ay<=K, 0<=f<=d, y>=0 with resource costs included once. With fixed coefficients and a locally stable dual optimum, the derivative in K_r,i is the corresponding resource shadow price, not the sum of all downstream gross sales. The recipe's net-output dual values transmit final demand upstream; binding physical and organizational constraints determine where capital adds value. A provider's captured contribution uses its contract settlements and opportunity costs instead of the planner's final value. Network changes alter both the incidence matrix and price system; no invariant multiplier is claimed. The preceding top-K result is a special case of this construction. Upward aggregation to a vector MPK_r requires identified exposure/capacity and demand edges, plus ownership and price assumptions; summing the marginal products of isolated tasks is invalid if they compete for the same robot or recovery worker. Dynamic aggregation must use the actual program-dependent transition law, not add a separate learning premium to J.

## What evidence can support the program comparison?
A field trial of fixed executable policies estimates J_G(pi_G)-J_P(pi_P) at the specified horizon under valid assignment and follow-up; it does not estimate V_G-V_P. A common checkpoint or a frozen controller is not an independently evolving competitor. There is an additional issue even at a terminal window: accepted-order censoring with *nonoptimal* dispatch. Earlier [censored capacity bounds](CENSORED_CAPACITY_ENVELOPE.md) assumed an optimal top-K dispatch. Real paid records do not establish that premise. Relax it below; this is an economically relevant measurement bridge, not an assumption that the firm actually sees latent readiness.

## Terminal oracle gap without assuming optimal dispatch
Same unit-slot model, fixed sorted nonnegative w_i, K integer slots, common original opportunities. A is the potential predispatch ready/authorized set; observed settled S is a subset of A with |S|<=K. Every order in A would yield w_i if selected, with no other constraints or interference within the window. No optimality of S assumed. A may be hidden to the real dispatcher. Set q_s=Pr(S=s), r_i=p_i-Pr(i in S), x_si=Pr(S=s,i in A minus S). Then feasible X has 0<=x_si<=q_s for i not in s, zero otherwise, and sum_s x_si=r_i. Any joint law satisfying these constraints and S subset A is compatible with the log (not necessarily with a particular known dispatch policy; incorporate further policy restrictions if known). Let a_sj=|s intersect {1,...,j}|, h_sj=K-a_sj>=0, d_j=w_j-w_(j+1), w_(n+1)=0.

The realized oracle gain over the recorded feasible settlement is
f_K(A)-sum_(i in S) w_i = sum_j d_j min(h_Sj, M_Sj),
where M_sj counts hidden ready orders in the prefix. This follows by telescoping f_K(A)=sum_j d_j min(K,a_Sj+M_Sj) and w(S)=sum_j d_j a_Sj. Unlike the previous optimal-dispatch restriction, the hidden ready order may outrank a served order, and even an idle slot may have missed work.

For T_h(z) the sum of the h largest entries, the sharp population envelope for expected oracle gap is
L=min_(x in X) sum_s sum_j d_j T_(h_sj)(x_s,1:j),
U=max_(x in X) sum_s sum_j d_j min(h_sj q_s,sum_(i<=j) x_si).
Proof: conditional on s, lower bound each truncated prefix by the largest h_sj marginal sum, upper by Jensen. The comonotone coupling of the unselected indicators attains all lower bounds at once. The cumulative-interval cyclic coupling attains every upper bound, because each prefix count lies in adjacent integers and the truncation level h_sj is integer, even though it varies across prefixes. For q_s=0 the row vanishes. Construct rows independently and mix by q_s; compact X attains extrema, mixtures fill the interval. LP representations use top-h epigraph and min hypograph respectively. The finite-panel checker also verifies endpoint attainment for its three-window integer lattice, separately from the proof. Without p, allow each x_si freely in its bounds. The additional restrictions in the earlier optimal-dispatch theorem are not imposed here.

## Consequence: adoption and rejection require different rival certificates
For terminal programs with comparable fixed costs and unchanged within-window opportunities, let j_g be observed fixed-policy mean net contribution and U_g the upper oracle gap (costs are identical across dispatch policies within the program). The observed feasible policy gives j_g<=V_g<=j_g+U_g. The oracle lower gap L_g is NOT a lower bound on feasible optimization gain when information is latent. Thus observed delta=j_G-j_P implies
delta-U_P <= V_G-V_P <= delta+U_G.
This is a valid outer interval; not generally sharp for optimized policies because the oracle may use unavailable information or cross-arm restrictions. A positive observed contrast supports adoption relative to the observed rival, but rules out a stronger feasible rival only when delta>U_P (or another valid tighter upper certificate). Rejection of G against a feasible P requires delta+U_G<0. With full histories and dynamics, replace the terminal oracle by an actual valid program-wide upper relaxation: improving dispatch changes successor data, demand and costs. Summing terminal gaps computed only on the logged policy's endogenous state distribution is not generally a valid bound on V. No additional data premium.

Illustration reuses the previous weights 12,8,6, K=1. P always accepts the eight-unit order; independent lawful audits show both that and the twelve-unit order always qualified. Its terminal oracle gap is 4. G serves the twelve-unit order at incremental cost 1; its observed net is 11, versus P=8, delta=3. If both orders are known qualified before dispatch, the feasible P policy can earn 12, so the optimized ranking is -1 despite the observed +3. If the rival can only obtain a noisy signal or faces incompatible information or deadlines, the feasible gap may differ. This is a diagnostic of the prior's dispatch assumption, not a separate new contribution or measured return.
