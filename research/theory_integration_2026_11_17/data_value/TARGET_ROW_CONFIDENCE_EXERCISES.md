# Exercises — finite report evidence and whole-service data economics

Use fixed *already qualified* complete-service actions; all numeric payoffs are synthetic. Keep inferential certification distinct from physical admission and expected label value.

1. Derive the exact worst rectangular target report-row value of a possibly randomized binary-report production rule. Why need not a general maximin choose a deterministic rule?
2. Derive a simultaneous 1−δ Hoeffding confidence region for m independent, precommitted target-row samples, including a row with zero observations. What does selected acceptance do to the assumptions?
3. Prove W(K*)−G(C)≤Σp_zR_z width(C_z), on the coverage event. Interpret its units and the role of retained qualified specialist production.
4. For p=(1/10,9/10) and whole-service spans R=(10,1), allocate fixed equal-cost target labels to minimize the conservative confidence width. Compare to prevalence-proportional sampling.
5. Given F=(0,0), S=(3/4,3/4), G=(3,−1), T=(4,−6), derive V(G/S)=3/4+9q_H/8−7q_L/8 and its robust improvement condition. State a valid saddle-certificate method for proving a full randomized maximin.
6. Construct two target low-opportunity report kernels with identical selected-only high-report data but different optimized original-order production. Name the missing identifying measurement.
7. Explain why reporting a better target confusion matrix cannot directly certify either safe physical target grounding or the net value of a changed embodiment.
