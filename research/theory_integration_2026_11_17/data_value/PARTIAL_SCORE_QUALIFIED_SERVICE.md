## Conditional sharp rubric-to-complete-service bound
Let n fixed, stage scores s_i in {0,1/2,1}, S=sum s_i/n, observed mean m=E S. Q=1 if every required stage meets a specified rubric. Two illustrative predicates (neither is a certified commercial definition): strict all s_i=1; permissive all s_i>=1/2.

General elementary moment lemma: given accept-score min/max a,b and reject-score min/max c,d, the feasible (m,p=Pr Q) satisfy p a+(1-p)c <= m <= p b+(1-p)d. These are sufficient as well when extrema are attainable and arbitrary mixtures over stage vectors allowed: for fixed p mix the two extremal vectors within each Q class, interpolate to any mean in the interval. Hence for strict, a=b=1,c=0,d=1-1/(2n), and
    max(0,1-2n(1-m)) <= p <= m.
For permissive, a=.5,b=1,c=0,d=1-1/n, and
    max(0,1-n(1-m)) <= p <= min(1,2m).
For n ranging 5–8 with unknown distribution, n=8 bounds apply and are sharp over that unrestricted family (all episodes may have n=8). If actual n is known, use its conditional means and combine using original episode weights. If some stages optional or substitutes, compute true extrema from the qualification recipe; these formulas no longer automatically apply. Rounding of published m widens bounds (use endpoint means); no confidence bounds from unreported sample sizes. No assumption of stage independence. Prior mathematical foundation: elementary bounded moment problem/union bound; not novelty.

For .93 cocktail score, illustrative strict n=8 probability can be anywhere from 0 to .93; even with n=5 it can be .30–.93. Permissive n=8 bound .44–1. For .89 tea, strict n=8 0–.89, permissive .12–1. These are feasibility bounds on a hypothetical predicate conditional on exact reported means and assumed rubric structure, not field acceptance estimates. A 0.91 mean can fail to establish any positive lower bound on strict full completion with n=8. If a minor retry consumes paid reserve, acceptance itself is insufficient; preserve stage timing and QC/recovery occupation.

## Economic implication within Models A/B
Under a deliberately restrictive common original-order population, one attempt per order, strict Q identical to accepted service, no other costs except incremental program acquisition c, one net margin v>=0 per complete service, and no distinct continuation, delta J=v(p_G-p_P)-c. With independent freely varying arm laws the interval [v(L_G-U_P)-c, v(U_G-L_P)-c] is sharp; with common-law joint constraints it is a conservative outer interval, not necessarily sharp. For heterogeneous v, condition on observable original demand class or use joint weighted bounds; neither score average nor product of marginal means identifies the result. Outside the restricted model use the actual resource and terminal accounting of policy-closed J.

Synthetic n=5: G mean .90 and P .80. World A: G always one half-credit stage and four perfect (strict Q=0), P 80% all-perfect and 20% all-zero (Q=.8). World B: G 90% all-perfect and 10% all-zero (Q=.9), P always one zero and four perfect (Q=0). Scores unchanged in both worlds; at v=10 and extra cost 1, forced G minus P is -9 versus +8. Optional P makes the optimized incremental option max(0,delta J) only if the firm knows the world and can choose before paying; no automatic positive premium under uncertainty. Routines can be economically sufficient even when technical average lower. Additional grounding that converts a binding half-credit state into timely accepted service can be valuable, but a new embodiment or upstream fixture that removes the same constraint may dominate. Compare complete feasible policies, not summed stage rewards. No second data premium; distinct licensed continuation belongs to J once.

Empirical discriminator: collect original order ID, full joint vector and rubric, stage-specific retry and elapsed/worker/QC intervals, actual acceptance and payment/rework, source modality and checkpoint lineage, paid training/compute/curation and rights, plus independent evolving P/X/G/E programs at shared learner/operator catchment. Distinguish whether a half-credit stage is actually payable, uses scarce recovery, or creates later useful data. If routine P yields the same dated accepted service and continuation at no higher cost, emulation still defeats costly G. LWD improves the technical program comparison but does not identify these economic variables. No new official contemporary numeric IO edge in this tranche; historical edge retained.
