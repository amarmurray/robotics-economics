# Joint original-buyer choice, endogenous price menus, and qualified robotic production

**19 November 2026.** A continuation of *When Capabilities Become Production*, and explicitly a conditional application of standard discrete-choice/revenue-management and optimization mathematics, not observed robotic returns or independently established novelty.

## Research question

The preceding [original-buyer support chapter](ORIGINAL_BUYER_OFFER_SUPPORT.md) gave threshold-based acceptance margins and a one-sided full-production certificate under fixed posted offers. Its separable order indicators cannot justify adding the receipts of two service classes when the same customer buys **one substitute**, nor can a quoted price be assumed unchanged after source training X, target grounding G or embodiment investment E. The common object remains optimized qualified production **V** and paid investment **J=V−K**, not benchmark performance priced in dollars.

An original buying unit *i* is one eligible procurement decision, with immutable order/procurement-group ID. Its eligible alternatives *j* are **complete, qualified** service recipes, including robot, human operator, recovery, integration, rights and safety capacity. A buyer may choose at most one substitute in its procurement group (sequential distinct purchases require a dynamic extension). A policy *p* in R/X/G/E determines available recipes, known delivery costs and admissible offer menus *m*. The provider must choose a legally credible, physically supportable menu, subject to any upfront controller qualification and capital cost *K_p*. Across the state set Θ are unknown customer opportunity/quality/outside-option and competing-service responses, possible joint learner/operator histories and supported price/receipt restrictions.

For a fixed menu with service price t_ij(m), an economically meaningful but strong benchmark is

    u_ij(p,m,theta) = w_ij(p,theta) − t_ij(m),
    y_ij(p,m,theta) = 1{j uniquely maximizes (0,u_i1,...,u_ik)}.

The buyer's net willingness-to-pay w_ij is relative to its best outside alternative, and is **not provider revenue**. Use a documented tie rule; the strict conditions below avoid any dependence on ties. Provider contribution for a selected, physically delivered complete service is c_ij=t_ij−k_ij, with actual collection/penalty rights specified. No buyer can yield multiple y_ij in this one-purchase model. A fixed price vector is only a special menu: the provider may omit service classes or select prices **ex ante** from a documented feasible set M_p. Adapting prices to information learned later is a different policy with explicit observation timing.

For each feasible production state, complete-service assignment variables x_ij satisfy 0≤x_ij≤y_ij, Σ_j x_ij≤1 and A_px≤b_p; true integer, timing, recovery and contingent commitments may tighten this relaxation. With credible **selective reservation/confirmation rights** the provider may reject unconfirmed demand and allocate capacity; if the offer contract obligates all acceptances, every offered menu must itself satisfy the worst-case admission/penalty conditions. Do not treat a failed/forbidden robot dispatch as a valid option. Denote the physically authorized full-market value V_p(m,theta), where any lost existing business and spillovers are inside theta and V, never added as a free data premium.

The research investment comparison is

    J_p^rob = sup_(m in M_p,safely admissible) inf_(theta in Θ) [V_p(m,theta)−K_p].

The **sup–inf order matters**: choosing the price after seeing the true buyer state instead gives the generally larger, usually infeasible inf–sup quantity. The retained policy R is a comparator only if its controller, operator, customer and data rights can actually remain intact.

## Conditional demand certificate under endogenous pricing

Suppose for every original buyer/recipe **and for every menu** in a specified policy, independently supported bounds l_ij≤w_ij(p,theta)≤h_ij hold simultaneously across Θ. These may come from matched, enforceable original-buyer accepted/refused offers, service-quality drift and alternative-supplier audits; incumbent receipts alone do not establish them. This is a stronger condition than having credible bounds under just one posted menu.

* **Guaranteed unique choice:** if l_ij−t_ij>max(0, max_(k≠j)(h_ik−t_ik)), buyer i selects j regardless of correlation across alternatives or customers.
* **Possible choice:** for any potentially selected j, necessarily h_ij−t_ij≥0 and h_ij−t_ij≥max_(k≠j)(l_ik−t_ik). A tie-dependent equality is only a *necessary* condition; correlated valuations can rule j out even when these pairwise inequalities pass. Define P_i(m) as this conservative candidate set.
* These margins do **not** imply simultaneous realizability of all possible choices. A complete procurement group contributes **at most one** sale even when each alternative would separately be accepted. For cross-buyer budget/shopping overlap, merge the original buying unit or introduce further shared constraints.

A **constructive lower certificate** is available from any safe admitted menu whose selected, strictly dominating buyer-recipe pairs have nonnegative captured margins, independently reserved complete qualified resources, and defensible payment/fulfillment rights. Summing those **simultaneously feasible guaranteed** pair contributions is a valid floor, after subtracting K once. It is not a valid floor if an unmatched buyer may displace a reserved confirmed buyer, cause compulsory unprofitable fulfillment or alter a shared operator/learner/customer regime beyond the supported envelope.

For a **fixed menu**, known nonnegative resource minima a_ij, valid resource maxima b and nonnegative upper captured contribution caps cbar_ij for each candidate P_i, every feasible complete-order plan is contained in the choice-aware LP relaxation. Weak duality gives the one-sided certificate

    V_p(m,theta) ≤ inf_(lambda≥0) [
       lambda · b + Σ_i max(0, max_(j in P_i(m))(cbar_ij − lambda · a_ij))
    ]  =: U_p(m).

Empty candidate sets contribute zero. This bound is uniform in theta; the max over j *inside* each original buyer replaces the preceding chapter's additive per-order maxima. It is not generally tight for discrete assignment, correlated choice, time sequencing, product bundles or learner/operator cross-effects. For an entire feasible precommitted price-and-menu policy, U_p^* = sup_(m in M_p) U_p(m) is an upper bound; a supported guaranteed safe-menu floor L_p^* is a lower bound to J_p^rob after costs. If L_p^*−K_p>U_q^*−K_q for every q≠p, p robustly dominates, subject to retention, market closure and feasibility. With unbounded off-register eligible buyers or payments, no finite uniform revenue upper bound follows from finite physical robot hours alone.

The lower/upper derivations need **no independence** of qualifications, buyer valuations or original-customer market shocks. But all population, policy quality, captured payment, hardware and operator assumptions must cover the same full policy; a pooled incumbent quote panel is not a certificate of a policy-specific response.

## Three exact countermodels / constructive comparison

**Price reoptimization changes the investment.** One original buyer has A willingness to pay 8 and B willingness to pay 6; it purchases at most one. Qualified X offers A (delivery cost 3, investment cost 2); qualified operator-saving G offers B (delivery cost 0, investment cost 1); E offers both (investment cost 3), and a legitimately retainable R contributes 2. Price choices are 5,6,7,8; zero surplus accepts with a documented deterministic tie rule. At the arbitrary common price 7, (R,X,G,E) net contributions are (2,2,−1,1). After **ex-ante price optimization with the true fixed market state**, they are (2,3,5,3). G now wins, without changing any motion/qualification probability. This is a synthetic demonstration that using one fixed historical offer to rank a source, grounding and embodiment is unsafe.

**Correlated substitution defeats double counting.** A *single* original procurement group values A and B each at 8. At price 7 it would accept A if offered alone and B if offered alone; adding the two separate receipts predicts 14, yet a joint one-purchase menu collects at most 7 (with no costs). The buyer exclusivity row produces a dual upper bound of 7 even if two physical slots are available. With two distinct independently eligible buying units competing for **one** qualified slot, the capacity-price dual similarly caps the aggregate at 7 rather than 14. Separate robot success and accepted-only counts cannot resolve which regime is relevant.

**Conditional robust choice survives joint unknown valuations.** For one original buyer with policy-invariant bounds w_A∈[8,9), w_B∈[5,6), ex-ante prices 5–8, A delivery cost 1, B delivery cost 0, and investment costs K_X=2, K_G=1, K_E=5, a guaranteed safe single-A menu at price 8 yields X=5; a guaranteed single-B menu at 5 yields G=4; optimized E≤2 and an independently retained R=3. Thus X robustly wins across **all correlated** joint buyer types in the interval rectangle (and every subset thereof), given actual price-quality transport and qualified capacity. In contrast, the two worlds (w_A,w_B)=(8,5) and (5,8) generate identical baseline accepted-at-5/refused-at-9 offer data but pointwise optimized values (X,G)=(5,4) and (2,7) under A cost1/K_X2 and B cost0/K_G1. Their X/G ranking reverses, showing why the extra interval evidence changes the decision.

**Alternative choice functional form (negative stress test).** With a standard multinomial-logit outside option and two substitutable offerings with systematic utilities zero, each service offered *alone* receives 1/2 of buyers; when offered **together**, each obtains 1/3 and no purchase obtains 1/3. At price 7, summing separate-market expected receipts yields 7 per eligible buyer whereas the actual joint menu yields 14/3. This is a model identity under iid extreme-value shocks, not calibrated customer evidence. A deterministic tie-resolving threshold model is not essential to the double-counting objection. The strict interval-dominance lower certificate, however, does **not** survive logit noise with unbounded support: a guaranteed order becomes a probabilistic quantity and must be rebuilt from measured choice probabilities and confidence/structural bounds.

## Integration, identification and next proof gap

Technical transfer changes which complete recipes pass target qualification; source field/failure/recovery, teleoperation, simulation, synthetic and pretrained inputs may differently change X, but cannot be assigned a cash premium without changed qualified service. G can improve target alignment and save scarce recovery resources; E changes possible capacity and demand quality. This chapter does not replace joint qualification, data acquisition/production feedback or the network interference results. An optional free data source cannot reduce the optimized policy value when all old menu/controller choices remain feasible; a forced model update, privacy restriction, coupled operator pool or unpriced retraining can.

Prospective matched interventions should record the **original procurement choice set**, offers including refusals/no-purchases, all alternatives and quality, precommitted prices and allowed repricing, actual captured receipts, full qualified service delivered or declined, operator/recovery resource reservations, policy-specific rights and network exposure. Without original buyer identity and unselected offers, standard sales data confound preferences with offered choices and capacity rationing. For experimental design, compare approved whole-market policy randomization against the interval/dual bounds; the former directly estimates complete-policy contribution under valid interference closure and enough support, whereas the latter may reject investment with weaker mechanism identification but stronger envelope assumptions.
