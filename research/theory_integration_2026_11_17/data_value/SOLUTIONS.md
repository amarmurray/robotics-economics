# Solutions: economic data value

1. The other qualifications may enable or crowd out i's contribution. Let m_i=E[f(A_-i union {i})-f(A_-i)]. Differentiating the multilinear expected production value yields dF/dn_s=sum_i T_is(1-p_i)m_i. The saturation factor and production interaction are both missing from an isolated-profit weighting.
2. With subsets in bit-mask order (none,1,2,both), the complementary table is (0,0,0,1); the substitute table is (0,1,1,1). Their interactions are +1 and -1. Under independent qualifications with probabilities p1,p2, values are p1*p2 and p1+p2-p1*p2. The economic interactions, not technical marginals, cause the difference.
3. The optimum is x*=min(R,R/2+log(1+u/v)/(2(1-epsilon))). With u=0 it is 1. With u/v=1/2 it is 1+log(1.5)/1.5, approximately 1.27031, leaving approximately 0.72969 to source 2. Larger u/v eventually puts all acquisition into source 1. The average technical qualification score is instead maximized at x=R/2 under the symmetric technology.
4. For an interior solution with u>0, dx*/d epsilon=log(1+u/v)/(2(1-epsilon)^2)>0. The more commercially valuable source now supplies more of qualification 2 as well, reducing the need for a separate source. Optimal gross value weakly rises by pointwise monotonicity. At epsilon=1 every mixture supplies the same target exposures, so the unique-mixture formula is invalid and concentration cannot be predicted.
5. Put H=2+(1+epsilon)R and B=v(1-1/H), A=u+B. The objective is u+v-A/L1-B/L2, with L1+L2=H. Strict concavity gives L1/L2=sqrt(A/B), or L1=H sqrt(A)/(sqrt(A)+sqrt(B)). Subtract 1 to obtain z1, invert z1=epsilon R+(1-epsilon)x, and clip. Customer value still shifts mixtures; the exponential budget threshold is not universal.
6. Vary source exposure and target grounding independently while holding policy architecture, evaluation sites, compute rule, other data, safety/acceptance criteria and rights fixed. A cost-expanding factorial differs from a fixed-budget reallocation. Measure joint qualified sets and actual training/adaptation costs. Holding technical outcomes fixed while changing customer demand/resource alternatives isolates their economic use. Marginal probabilities can have different dependence structures, and task-level correctness may not equal complete customer service.
