# Student exercises — original-buyer SG/E exposure under shared learning

These synthetic exercises are separate from the instructor solutions and do not represent an approved deployment.

1. **Full-market exposure proof.** With fixed original-buyer accounting units, derive the design expectation of an inverse-exposure weighted accepted-and-paid contribution for the all-SG program under independently assigned SG/E site programs. State why a mixed-site direct effect is a different estimand, and which physical or market causal edges belong in each site's full-horizon exposure set.
2. **Joint assignment covariance.** Show the exact covariance between the all-SG exposure of buyer i and all-E exposure of buyer j when their exposure neighborhoods overlap, and when they are disjoint. Derive the variance formula for their estimated program difference, without incorrectly treating the arm estimators as independent.
3. **Four-buyer exact case.** With `H=(12,123,234,34)`, Bernoulli p=1/2, SG paid numerator values (8,12,18,16)/20 and E values (10,6,15,13)/20, compute the target gap and enumerate assignment-based variance. Compare with the neighborhood d/M upper bound and say whether the result certifies SG superiority.
4. **Endogenous learning horizon.** A routine field-data update from every installed SG site is admitted into the single global checkpoint and immediately changes all deployed controllers. Determine the revised full-horizon neighborhoods. Give the probability of seeing at least one all-SG or all-E exposure when N=20 and p=1/2. Explain why more independent robot trajectory logs do not supply new randomized all-program support.
5. **Policy-option vs causal-certainty counterexample.** Explain why zero-cost legally optional source availability, preserving all old policies, cannot reduce optimized gross production value, while compulsory shared retraining can increase causal exposure degree and lower informativeness of a naive mixed-site rollout. Distinguish compute/data-rights costs, physical qualification and accepted paid production.
