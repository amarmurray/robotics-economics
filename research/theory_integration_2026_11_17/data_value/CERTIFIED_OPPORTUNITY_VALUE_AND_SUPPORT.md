## 1. Economic object: permission versus observation

For each preenumerated original customer/order–shift–robot–operator–procurement state \(z\), let \(\mathcal F_C(z)\) denote the **independently signed and legally permitted complete policies** after certification state \(C\). A complete policy specifies controller, source version, grounding sensor, embodiment, reported observations, allowed physical recovery, original-customer menu/price, deadline, shared operator resources and training/data rights. Merely retaining old code is not evidence that the old physical controller can safely rescue an attempted source-trained action. A missing signature removes the relevant policy from the *admitted* set, although it can still be studied counterfactually.

Let \(Y_z(a)\) be the **capturable original-order contribution** of complete policy \(a\): refusals, unserved and rejected orders, purchased qualified service, penalties, collected remittances, operator displacement and costs counted once. Its mean \(m_a(z)\) is not a task success percentage. A dynamic \(J\) already includes future acquisition of permitted experience and conditional continuation production; do not add a second data premium. Let \(S_a\) be the original-market strata in which an admitted whole policy has positive lawful assignment probability and complete recorded outcomes. **Physical feasibility \(\mathcal F_C\) and statistical market support \(S_a\) are distinct.**

A safe shadow observation may reveal technical sensor/report performance without identifying customer purchase. An approved original-order randomized complete-policy pilot can add statistical support, but only **after** the whole physical/rights/market policy is admitted and approved. Field successes, failures/recoveries, teleoperation, simulation, synthetic and public/pretrained inputs have different recording, cost, qualification and legal reuse structures. Source-trained \(X\) with unsigned post-failure support is excluded from the customer policy set regardless of benchmark accuracy.

## 2. Exact gross option value of a new certificate, under separability

Suppose original customer strata \(z\) have preassignment weights \(p_z\), no resource sharing across strata, independent price/quality menus and no buyer interference. Previously admitted best whole-policy expected contribution in stratum \(z\) is \(r_z\). On newly certifiable subset \(D\), a grounded complete policy \(G\) would have \(g_z\). If certification expands the feasible menu **without removing old policies**, then

\[
V(C\cup D)-V(C)=\sum_{z\in D}p_z(g_z-r_z)_+,\quad (x)_+=\max(x,0).\tag{1}
\]

**Proof.** Before certification each independent stratum contributes \(r_z\). After certification it contributes \(\max(r_z,g_z)\); subtract, then sum. This is ordinary optional investment/process-flexibility mathematics, not a new theorem. A certification/curation expenditure \(K_D\) must be subtracted **once** when deciding whether to certify, not from \(g_z\) and again from the option value. Free availability preserves gross value, while forced retraining can destroy old production.

If G has never been lawfully assigned on \(D\), but conditional limits \(L_z\leq g_z\leq U_z\) are independently justified, then

\[
\sum_{z\in D}p_z(L_z-r_z)_+
\ \leq\ V(C\cup D)-V(C)\
\leq\ \sum_{z\in D}p_z(U_z-r_z)_+ .\tag{2}
\]

**Sharpness and negative result.** With no restrictions linking missing G outcomes, construct two worlds with identical incumbent orders, technical qualifications and observations yet set every unobserved G contribution to \(L_z\) or to \(U_z\). Both fit available data and attain the endpoints. Even perfect technical qualification cannot tell whether the same original buyer will accept a priced, quality-constrained G service. An unsigned G cannot simply be assigned to manufacture treatment overlap. A prospective signed conditional purchase offer can be informative only under explicit, legally valid response/fulfillment assumptions; it is not itself a collected receipt. The expression fails under shared dispatch, customer budget coupling, menu-response shifts or noncomposable physical certification.

## 3. Indivisible joint certificates can have zero standalone and positive combined production value

Two original buyers A and B place simultaneous orders, each service indivisible. One common operator network supplies 2 supervision units. Incumbent R-A earns 4 and consumes 2; incumbent R-B earns 3 and consumes 2. Independently and **jointly** admitted target-grounded G-A earns 4 using 1 unit and G-B earns 3 using 1 unit. A buyer's original order can be fulfilled at most once, and all menus are stipulated accepted. No policy upgrade is forced. In exact normalized contribution units:

| Physically admitted whole-service menus | Best feasible indivisible original-order production | \(V\) |
|---|---|---:|
| R only | R-A | 4 |
| R plus G-A | G-A or R-A | 4 |
| R plus G-B | R-A | 4 |
| R plus both G-A and G-B, with a **joint** signature | G-A and G-B | 7 |

Hence \(V(AB)-V(A)-V(B)+V(\varnothing)=3\). No single certification improves realized output; both together free enough operator capacity to sell two qualified original services. **This is established resource complementarity, not a universal supermodularity result.** Two separately qualified controllers do **not** imply a simultaneously safe/contractually lawful operator bundle. A new embodiment E that raises operator capacity from 2 to 4 could also permit two incumbent orders for gross 7; E's separate hardware/access/operating cost decides whether it wins. Source X may modify future qualification probability but, while physically unapproved, adds no current feasible customer recipe.

**Original-demand dependence, not benchmark-to-dollar conversion.** Keep individual customer request probabilities equal to 1/2 in two different demand laws. Under joint arrival \(\Pr(A=B=1)=1/2\), R expected contribution is 2 while jointly admitted G expected contribution is \(7/2\). Under alternating requests \(\Pr(A=1,B=0)=\Pr(A=0,B=1)=1/2\), each earns \(7/2\). Thus marginal demand and technical qualifications are held fixed while the actual gross grounding-bundle benefit is either \(3/2\) or 0. This reuses the previous joint-demand/coupling results but connects them to **certification of an entire customer–operator network**, not independent benchmark improvements.

## 4. Resource-price certificate: a ceiling on new economic columns, not evidence of their existence

Use the manuscript's existing production-price certificate. The incumbent divisible relaxation is \(\max c^\top x\) subject to \(Ax\leq b, x\geq0\). A nonnegative incumbent dual \(\lambda\) satisfies \(A^\top\lambda\geq c\). A potential new physical/market-qualified column \(j\) has capturable original-order contribution \(d_j\), resource demands \(B_j\), and bounded deployment \(0\leq u_j\leq M_j\). Preserve the incumbent recipes and resources. Every feasible augmented allocation satisfies

\[
0\leq V_{\rm new}-V_{\rm old}\leq
(\lambda^\top b-V_{\rm old})+
\sum_j M_j[d_j-\lambda^\top B_j]_+ .\tag{3}
\]

**Proof.** Old \(c^\top x\leq\lambda^\top Ax\). Hence \(c^\top x+d^\top u\leq\lambda^\top b+(d-B^\top\lambda)^\top u\), and each bounded nonnegative \(u_j\) contributes at most \(M_j[d_j-\lambda^\top B_j]_+\). Retained incumbent feasibility gives the lower zero. If the old problem is divisible and the dual optimal, the first term vanishes; with indivisible original services it is an **explicit old LP integrality gap**, not silently zero. Customer budget, contractual purchase and interference constraints must be in the resource system or stated as missing.

The incumbent 2-slot example has a tight relaxation, dual operator price \(\lambda=2\), and \(V_{\rm old}=4\). G-A reduced contribution is 2 and G-B is 1, each available for at most one customer. The resulting upper certificates are 6 for G-A only, 5 for G-B only and 7 for both; the *actual indivisible optima* are 4, 4, 7. A **positive** reduced contribution is only an upper-bound possibility and does not prove an individually monetizable qualification. A nonpositive reduced contribution for every new recipe establishes zero incremental value only under a tight incumbent LP dual and the entire unchanged-resource/market premise. Missing customer \(d_j\) cannot be filled with a success-rate percentage. Column-generation and LP duality are longstanding prior art.

## 5. Safe information acquisition, competitor E, and a conditional value of observation

Consider a **separate**, noninterfering two-stratum population, each mass 1/2. Stipulate already signed R and G-A policies with A values R=2, G-A=4, and B incumbent value 3. Current gross opportunity is \(7/2\). Prospective G-B is *not physically certified* and has unknown original-order contribution \(g_B\in[0,6]\). If it could be lawfully admitted while retaining R, its gross option lies between \(7/2\) and 5, a sharp gain between 0 and \(3/2\) by (2). An incumbent R log and a G technical test are compatible with both demand worlds.

For a **hypothetical** competing, independently certified E embodiment, B contributes 4 and A remains on G-A; a one-time cost \(1/5\) yields E net \(19/5\). Prospective G-B physical certification costs 1 once; in the low market world \(g_B=0\), its net is \(5/2\); in the high market world \(g_B=6\), it nets 4. Give the two worlds an **invented** equal prior and allow a hypothetically perfectly binding, free, legally permissible *precertification customer signal* that reveals B's purchase response without physical G deployment. Without information, E at \(19/5\) beats a fixed certification choice at \(13/4\). With the signal, choose E in the low world and certified G-B in the high, yielding \((19/5+4)/2=39/10\). **Gross signal decision value is \(1/10\)** normalized units, not a measured price for customer data. An unbinding survey or changed procurement rules may remove that value entirely. It must never be added to \(J\) again if \(J\) already includes signal acquisition and contingent action.

This separates **more source data** (which might change learning/qualification only if permitted and transferable), **target grounding and its physically observable/recovery certificate**, a **new embodiment E**, and a **safe customer demand report** (which may change the information set but not robotic competence). Production-generated routine data may be free and useful yet selection-biased away from missed target orders. Each candidate's acquisition cost, timing, rights, prospective demand and shared operator constraints belongs in the same optimized production/continuation model. None receives an exogenous dataset-dollar multiple.
