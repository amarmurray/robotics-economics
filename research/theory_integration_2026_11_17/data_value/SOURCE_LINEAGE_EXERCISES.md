# Student exercises — joint lineage, information and qualified production

Based on [theory chapter](SOURCE_LINEAGE_HIGHER_ORDER_TRANSFER_AND_ECONOMIC_DECISION.md). These are synthetic, not robot observations. Instructor answers are in [separate solutions](SOURCE_LINEAGE_SOLUTIONS.md).

1. Let \(E_i\in\{0,1\}\), each \(\Pr(E_i=1)=1/4\), and each pair \(\Pr(E_i=E_j=1)=1/16\), with an exchangeable law. Write the eight atom probabilities using \(t=\Pr(E_1=E_2=E_3=1)\). Prove the sharp feasible t interval. What is the unique t corresponding to mutual conditional independence?

2. Let \(S_i=\theta\oplus E_i\), \(\theta\) have a uniform binary prior, and let the error law be regime-invariant. Prove that majority vote is Bayes-optimal for every feasible t, and derive the sharp probability of selecting the correct, qualified service.

3. Original-customer contribution equals 12 after a correct qualified policy and 2 with a separately admissible R otherwise. X costs 1/4 for two additional source signals; G costs 1/4 for a 7/8-accurate target signal; E is net 10. G is conditionally independent of the original 3/4 signal. Calculate J_X, J_G and the X/G decision threshold. Explain why the result is not an episode-price or benchmark monetization.

4. An independent matched target cohort provides n three-signal error tuples and reliable customer-regime labels. Write a valid concentration interval for t. Identify at least three violations that prevent using this interval to justify a robotics deployment or revenue result.

5. Prove that optional free signal availability cannot reduce an optimally selected fixed signed-policy information value, while forced costly training may. Explain why positive triple mutual dependence does not by itself imply positive *economic* marginal value.
