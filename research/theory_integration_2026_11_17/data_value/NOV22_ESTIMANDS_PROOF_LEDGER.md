# Proof ledger — source-policy estimands and coverage

**Status:** conditional standard constructions within the integrated manuscript; no independently established novelty, empirical robotics estimates or physical approval.

1. **Sequential policy law:** complete pre-choice H, consistency, conditional randomized/exchangeable stage-two assignment and positivity identify m_x(h,a). Iterated expectation gives J_x(pi). Finite maximizing across actually approved actions gives the population optimal J_x^*. Cost and original customer output enter B_x and Y_x once. Policy selection/evaluation on the same finite sample biases naive maximization; require preregistration, honest split or independent testing. The source assignment must be randomized/exchangeable to estimate J_x for the intended target population.
2. **Source contrast without t:** J_1(pi_1)-J_0(pi_0) involves only separately identified marginals and histories; a joint (Q0,Q1) counterfactual coupling is unnecessary. If stage-two histories lack positivity, the claimed value is only partially identified. An unapproved action is not part of a feasible economic menu.
3. **Two cross-world couplings:** nested (1,0,2,1)/4 and nonnested (0,1,3,0)/4 in (both,only0,only1,neither) order yield p0=1/4,p1=3/4, t=1/4 vs 0, source gainers=1/2 vs 3/4 and regressors=0 vs 1/4. If complete accepted-service reward conditional on stage-one success is 2 and approved post-failure continuation reward is 1, J0=5/4 and J1=7/4 in both worlds. No observed marginal distribution distinguishes nested from nonnested. This is the Fréchet/principal-strata non-identification theorem, not original causal statistics.
4. **Partial-support bound:** for each permitted action with known conditional lower/upper mean, maximizing lower and upper vectors yields bounds on the optimum. If conditional joint laws can vary independently across H and limits are each attainable, endpoints are achievable and the interval is sharp; with a common persistent regime or coupled market constraints it is only valid outer identification. Cross-source subtraction gives a valid average contrast interval. The feasible retained F action has known value and must not be dropped.
5. **Exact coverage example:** x0 baseline 2(1/4)+1(1/2)=1, unstudied approved eligible quarter adds [0,2]/4=[0,1/2], hence [1,3/2]. x1 baseline 2(3/4)+1(1/4)=7/4; difference [1/4,3/4]. Known source cost 2/5 reduces that difference to [-3/20,7/20]. All values are normalized complete service, not wages, revenue forecasts or field observation.
6. **Negative results:** improving p1-p0 does not identify monotone unit-level qualification improvement; a positive J contrast does not identify technical transfer; static arm means and paired t do not identify source→grounding r; r does not identify original-customer order displacement or cash; free optional data cannot lower the maximized feasible old-policy menu, while forced training and real costs can.
7. **Closest prior:** Murphy 2003 dynamic rules and g-formula; Frangakis/Rubin 2002 principal stratification; Fréchet coupling, ordinary partial identification, DDA §5/App B economic data feedback, LP/process flexibility. The only candidate synthesis is a network-closed, qualified-original-order implementation and empirical falsification program; novelty open.
