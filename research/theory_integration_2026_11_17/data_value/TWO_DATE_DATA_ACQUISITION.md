# The two-date value of production-generated data versus deliberate target support

**19 November 2026 — continuation of _When Capabilities Become Production_.** This chapter follows the target-report selection and finite-support results. It treats data collection and physical investment as mutually competing **policies in one customer-production objective**, not as distinct data premia. The mathematical examples are synthetic; no robot/customer panel or independent safety authorization is claimed.

## 1. The question

When should a robotics enterprise (i) keep a qualified system producing and retain its natural logs, (ii) give up a service opportunity to audit a target situation, (iii) pay for another heterogeneous source/training intervention, (iv) physically ground the controller at the target, or (v) qualify a changed embodiment? A large dataset and an accurate diagnostic are not necessarily substitutes. Production changes which situations appear in the record; the learned or physically modified controller may change the next qualified menu, operator load and actual customer orders.

Use a *policy-closed* customer network, not an isolated movement or benchmark trial, as the economic unit. Let h_t contain the pre-action original-order registry, approved controller/embodiment/dispatcher/operator and recovery versions, qualified recipes Q_t, retained source stocks with rights, complete past service and reporting events, and cash/resource capacities. Let θ denote payoff-relevant latent customer, production and learning states, with belief b_t. A history-dependent policy chooses only actions in a physically approved safe menu K(h_t). Rejected, displaced, timed-out and never-admitted original orders are included when evaluating captured complete-service contribution c_t. Rights, compute, operator and physical bottlenecks enter feasibility or cost, not a made-up dollar-per-trajectory conversion.

At date 0 choose k from competent routine R, nonactuating deliberate audit A, source training X, target physical grounding G or new embodiment E, or an approved combination within budget. The choice may change current service c_0, physical qualifications Q_1, report observation O_1, retained raw data D_1, future original-customer opportunities M_1, and future resource availability x_1. At date 1 select the best physically approved, validated whole-service recipe and feasible remaining investment after observing the authorized history.

Write the *entire-history* transition law T_k(dh_1,dθ_1|h_0,θ_0); it includes missing/selected reports. For a finite horizon and a single declared capturable contribution objective,

\[
J_0(h_0,b_0)=\max_{k\in K(h_0),\,c_k\le B_0}
\mathbb E_{b_0,T_k}\left[c_0(k,\theta_0)-c_k+
\delta\max_{\pi_1\in\Pi_{\rm approved}(h_1,B_0-c_k)}
\mathbb E\{C_1(\pi_1,\theta_1;M_1,Q_1,x_1)\mid h_1\}\right].
\tag{D1}
\]

All quantities are on the same valuation date; replace c_k by its dated cost or financing constraints if different. C_1 is *optimized whole original-order production*, with qualified fallback recipes, not revenue from a benchmark score. Current c_0 and the continuation term exhaust the accounting; an extra “value of data generated” term would double-count it. If an acquisition modifies customer demand, resource prices or operator recovery, those are coordinates of T_k and C_1 rather than an unjustified post hoc premium.

An optional, free, noninterfering source cannot lower the optimized value, because the old history-contingent policy remains available. This is a policy-set inclusion statement; it fails for mandated training, rights costs, incompatible compute, or a source that changes the feasible original-order process merely by being admitted. Under a fixed qualification/production environment, the preceding target-selection bounds apply inside the date-1 expectation. They cannot simply be transported to k=G or E if the reporter, Q, demand, y or safe action set changes.

## 2. A restricted information-sufficiency theorem

**Proposition (productive routine replaces a deliberate audit only under a coupling).** Suppose two available date-0 choices R and A have the *same* conditional law of all date-1 payoff-relevant physical states, Q_1, original orders, resource capacities and rights, and the same subsequent feasible decisions. Suppose an experiment generated by R Blackwell-dominates the report generated by A conditional on those states; i.e., A's report can be simulated by a stochastic garbling of R's observation, with no extra action-relevant information. Suppose for every coupled original history the date-0 net current contribution from R is at least A's (including incremental audit costs) and the same budget is available afterward. Then J(R)≥J(A).

**Proof.** Take any A-contingent continuation rule. The R planner uses the declared garbling to simulate the A report and then implements exactly that continuation rule. Conditional date-1 states and feasible service are equal, so its continuation expectation is unchanged. Its date-0 net contribution is no lower. Optimizing over R rules preserves the inequality. This is the familiar Blackwell simulation argument, applied to qualified customer production; it is not a new theorem of experimental design.

**Crucial failure modes.** (a) The recorded routine sample is accepted/dispatch-selected and not the audit's target population; (b) the source controller changes target visitation or physical recovery; (c) audit consumes operator/fleet time or creates a labeled, otherwise inaccessible failure population; (d) R produces valuable current service but no report about the target; (e) sampling or training changes the stage-1 approved menu. The frozen-row target-selection interval from the preceding chapter survives only after fixing the entire sampling/physical policy and defining a pre-action potential report for every original opportunity. In particular, a known original-order denominator alone does not establish a Blackwell ordering.

## 3. An exact customer-conditioned intervention example

The following *hypothetical* policy-closed customer network has two equally likely original-order demand regimes H and L, one productive opportunity at date 0 and a date-1 investment slot. The qualified incumbent I contributes 2 in either regime; an already-approved state-sensitive specialist Z contributes (6,−4). These are whole-service **net contributions** relative to the specified counterfactual, not motion scores. All options in this mathematical example are assumed validated and physically approved; a negative contribution is an economically poor but safe service, not permission to conduct an unsafe act. Unsuccessful or unattractive service can be declined and I retained.

At date 1 the enterprise can retain I or Z, train from heterogeneous source X at total incremental cost 1/2 to enable a qualified (7,−3) target service, perform physical target grounding G at cost 1 to enable (8,−5), or change embodiment E at cost 1 to enable a robust (5,4) service. Costs are paid once; net date-1 contributions are therefore:

| Date-1 approved choice | H | L |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Retain I | 2 | 2 |
| Retain qualified Z | 6 | −4 |
| More source training X, **after cost** | 13/2 | −7/2 |
| Physical grounding G, **after cost** | 7 | −6 |
| New embodiment E, **after cost** | 4 | 3 |

These investment choices are mutually exclusive at date 1 (a single acquisition/validation slot). A common spending cap of 6/5 covers either date-0 audit costing 1/5 plus the largest date-1 investment costing 1; date-0 routine has zero incremental acquisition cost. The training, grounding and hardware payoff vectors are assumed *post-qualification* complete-service contributions on the original-order population, not evidence they exist in reality.

Routine R supplies date-0 service worth 1 and generates up to 100 candidate traces, but its admission/logging rule records only high pre-action reports, with P(recorded high|H)=P(recorded high|L)=2/5 and P(no record|H)=P(no record|L)=3/5. A concrete selected-report mechanism has latent pre-action high probabilities q_H=3/5 and q_L=2/5, high-report inclusion propensities ρ_H=2/3 and ρ_L=1, and zero low-report recording. Thus every observed routine-log sequence, however long, has the same law in H and L; the record yields **zero regime information**, despite a technically informative latent report. This is a comparison of *diagnostic information*, not a claim that the trajectories cannot improve a controller under a different training experiment.

An independent, nonactuating target audit A displaces the date-0 production opportunity, costs 1/5, and delivers a symmetric binary report that correctly identifies the regime with probability α∈[1/2,1]. A is not assumed free or economically harmless; its opportunity loss enters by comparing R's +1 with A's −1/5. Date-1 action selection is after the report; the investment itself still must pass qualification before original customer orders are served.

With no informative report, E is the optimal date-1 choice: its expected net contribution is 7/2; routine value is therefore **J_R=1+7/2=9/2**. With a perfect target audit, choose G when H and E when L; audit value is **J_A(1)=−1/5+(7+3)/2=24/5**, exceeding R by 3/10. If R supplies an equally accurate, rights-cleared noninterfering report as a free production byproduct instead, its value becomes **1+(7+3)/2=6**, strictly dominating A. These are exhaustive comparisons under the one-slot qualified menu, not a “100 trajectories versus 1 label” price conversion.

**A richer consequence: information quality changes *which physical investment is optimal*.** Under the symmetric audit, the high-report posterior is α and the low-report posterior is 1−α. In the entire range α∈[1/2,1], low-report optimal investment is E. High-report expected contributions as functions of α are:

\[
V_E=3+\alpha,\qquad V_X=10\alpha-7/2,\qquad V_G=13\alpha-6.
\]

I and Z never beat these candidates on this branch. Hence the high-report choice is E for α≤13/18, X for 13/18≤α≤5/6, and G for α≥5/6 (ties at boundaries). The **exact optimized audit policy value**, including its date-0 cost but not adding a data premium, is

\[
J_A(\alpha)=
\begin{cases}
33/10,&1/2\le\alpha\le13/18,\\
(9/2)\alpha+1/20,&13/18\le\alpha\le5/6,\\
6\alpha-6/5,&5/6\le\alpha\le1.
\end{cases}\tag{D2}
\]

The audit beats qualified productive routine **if and only if α>19/20**, tying at 19/20. A signal can alter the optimal choice between source training and physical grounding *long before* it is worth sacrificing a productive opportunity to purchase that signal. This separates value of information from opportunity cost, qualification spending, and raw data volume. The numerical thresholds depend entirely on the synthetic complete-service table and independent balanced demand regimes.

## 4. Information-value interactions with new embodiments

Let I(Q) be perfect-report optimized terminal production less the best terminal production without the report under a fixed known joint original-order distribution and qualified menu Q. Adding an optional approved choice never lowers gross optimized production *with or without* information, but **the incremental value of information need not increase**.

Using gross service contributions before intervention expense, Q={I,Z} gives no-report 2, perfect-report 4, and I(Q)=2. Adding robust E=(5,4) gives no-report 9/2, perfect-report 5, and I(Q∪{E})=1/2: new physical capability **substitutes for diagnostic data**. Instead adding G=(8,−5) gives no-report 2, perfect-report 5, and I(Q∪{G})=3: target-specific physical capability **complements diagnostic data**. Both directions arise from the same qualified production envelope, not an invented positive transfer coefficient.

This is a simple Blackwell/option-set interaction, related to prior information-versus-admission results in the manuscript. Its use here is to expose which *learning architecture, target audit and hardware qualification* intervention ought to be tested against retained service. No inference from benchmark pick rate to money is valid.

## 5. Empirically discriminating implications

A meaningful comparison needs (i) signed original orders before robot admission/dispatch, with independently verified sampling and nonrecord events; (ii) version-pinned target reporters, inclusion propensities, rights and nonactuation proofs; (iii) held-out joint qualified service outcomes for I/Z, source-X, grounded-G and embodiment-E, including recovery and original-order displacement; (iv) accounting for current productive opportunity, staged investment cost/time, operator/compute/capacity use, demand shifts and receipts; and (v) safety-approved whole-policy intervention assignment at the policy-closed network level if shared learning, customer demand or operators interfere.

An independent audit may be indispensable for target support if routine recording hides the relevant strata. But selected-log nonidentification is not a recommendation to perform an unapproved audit. Neither the synthetic selected-report law, the source-to-qualification payoff vectors, α, demand shares nor the G/E intervention menus are observed. If no viable real-world design exists, keep the partial-identification sets and evaluate only approved retained alternatives. The BEA–BLS numeric crosswalk and measured customer production/opportunity atlas are separate outstanding research dependencies.
