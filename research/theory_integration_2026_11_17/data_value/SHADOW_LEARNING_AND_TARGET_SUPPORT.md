# When Capabilities Become Production — read-only transfer, qualified admission and target support

**18 November 2026. Integrated theoretical continuation; no field causal claim or safety approval.**

## The next actual question

The preceding [protection–learning frontier](PROTECTION_LEARNING_FRONTIER.md) proves that preserving existing customer orders can consume the controller, compute, operator and qualification resources needed to realize new target production. It leaves an unnecessarily stark choice between a shared trained controller (valuable learning, possible displaced orders) and a pinned controller (protected orders, possibly lost learning). **Could the firm preserve current *actuation* yet reuse data from the protected operation to learn a separately qualified policy?** Conversely, when does apparently abundant routine data fail to identify the target action or qualify a complete customer service?

This is not a claim that every robotic system supports an information/control split, that a read-only replay is behaviorally equivalent to a real target experiment, or that an offline model is safely deployable. It is a deliberate separation of (i) observational reuse, (ii) learning and qualification, and (iii) new production admission in the existing production-value model. DDA already assigns future economic value to production-generated data and models complementary aggregation; that is not this project's novelty claim.

## Common environment and three distinct transitions

Fix a finite horizon T, the pre-action customer opportunity census, persistent latent transfer regime θ, original qualified operating policy F, retained configurations Q, and the optimized **captured** production objective V(Q;ω). F includes competent specialists, physical capacity, original customer alternatives, acceptance rules, and safe human recovery. All new candidate policies must also be independently physically approved. Source records are not qualified recipes.

For a policy with active service B, distinguish three arrows:

1. **Data plane:** accepted B service under F emits a source-specific, versioned history D^B_t of observations, actions, outcomes, failures, recoveries, human interventions, contexts and rights. Read/copy/replay can consume compute, bandwidth, attention or commercial rights. It need not change B's deployed weights, commands or dispatcher.
2. **Learning and verification plane:** a separate A-side training rule K maps selected admissible records (and possibly deliberate probes, teleoperation, simulation, synthetic or public inputs), grounding g, embodiment h, training resources and θ into a **joint** qualification outcome Q^A. No conditional independence of qualifications is assumed. Offline technical progress or benchmark scores do not establish the kernel K for accepted whole customer services.
3. **Production plane:** only after A's *specific* controller/body/recipe and recovery resources are approved can a new service be admitted. The full-network policy then changes customer demand, prices, possible B substitution, dispatcher load, and production V; preservation of B before admission does not certify preservation afterward.

A shadow path is: keep F operating and generating D^B; copy permitted D^B to an offline learner; qualify A using safe, appropriately representative validation; choose among the retained F, competent S, and safely approved A admission. Data-copying, model overwriting and robot actuation are separate actions. A may have a distinct body. We must neither count B's already-paid cash twice nor append a data bonus after the future qualified production in V.

## Proposition 1 — conditional pre-admission retention and option-set expansion

Let S^B_t contain *all* customer-B-determining state: original orders, willingness to buy, prices/contract acceptance, B controller, dispatch, operator queue, physical resources, rights, downstream substitutions and feedback. Couple F and shadow policy H by identical initial S^B_0 and identical exogenous disturbances. Suppose for every safely reachable history **until a stopping time τ of A admission**:

- H executes exactly the F B-side action/transition/output rules;
- all copied records are already generated by F, their capture/replay has permitted rights, and the read-only data pipeline cannot change B state through actuation, bandwidth, human time, compute throttling, payment, market response or any other directed path;
- offline training and validation do not reserve a B-critical resource and do not create new B customer offers.

Then B's full accepted-service path and captured contribution under H equal F's **pathwise for t<τ**. This proof does *not* depend on whether source data technically transfers to A.

**Proof.** Under coupled disturbances, equality of B state holds at t=0. The same closed B transition functions and actions imply equality at t+1, and hence the same complete-service acceptance and margins. Induct to τ. The stopping time cannot itself have a B effect before admission under these assumptions.

If an otherwise identical offline learner can use an extra free dataset D with no change in resources, rights or timing and the old policies/training choices remain available, then the *ex-ante optimized* value of the expanded decision set weakly increases: max over Π(D∪{d}) is at least max over Π(D). This is **feasible-set inclusion**, not an assertion of strictly positive technical transfer or of a safe newly trained controller. Forced retraining, expensive curation, nonoptional checkpoint swaps and time-consuming validation fall outside the premise.

**Boundary.** At t=τ, offering A service or routing an order to A may affect B demand/dispatch/recovery even if A's controller is separate. The preceding policy-closed-network or protected-order conditions must be re-established for the *post-admission whole policy*. The lemma proves no production isolation merely from read-only ROS messages.

## Proposition 2 — what F's data cannot establish about A

Absent validated structural transfer or safely observed target actions, a complete record of F's accepted services is generally insufficient to identify the target A joint-qualification kernel or accepted-customer contribution. Construct two worlds with identical F state/action/observation/recovery records and exactly the same A offline score on that record. The untried target action required by an A customer service succeeds in world 1 and fails in world 2. Since F never executes the action, these laws are observationally equivalent under F but imply different A contribution and possibly different physically allowable policies. This is the standard missing-support obstruction in off-policy evaluation, now applied to **whole accepted-service and recovery** outcomes, not an original identification theorem.

A positive target *support* claim must specify a validated equivalence class of pre-action customers, full service recipes, physical embodiments, action/recovery states and training checkpoint versions in which the A outcome is identified under an approved comparison. Common visual state frequencies, overlapping simulator states or one-task benchmark accuracy are not enough.

### Sharp bounded contribution conditional on real target support

Fix a physically approved candidate A admission policy and an ex-ante, pre-treatment customer distribution μ. Partition demand strata into C where that candidate's incremental **whole-order captured contribution conditional on admission** m(x) is identified through a valid matched intervention/transport argument, and U where it is not. Assume each additional order's A contribution (after separately accounted B disruption) lies in [0,M(x)] and there are no unmodeled shared resource or cross-stratum restrictions. Then its expected A increment obeys

    G_A ∈ [ ∫_C m(x) μ(dx),  ∫_C m(x) μ(dx) + ∫_U M(x) μ(dx) ].

The endpoints are sharp **within this unconstrained identified set**: assign respectively 0 or M on every unsupported cell. Joint qualification, global operator capacity, bundle interactions or unknown admission feasibility can invalidate sharpness or the aggregation itself; then use the full joint feasible-policy identified set, not an unjustified point estimate.

If copying, learning, approval and post-admission residual losses cost at most C and a proven B-retention/loss certificate applies, subtract these **once** from the lower contribution and compare against the competent specialist and F. A positive interval upper bound is not an affirmative deployment choice. A certificate on mean contribution does not supersede any hard, joint recovery-capacity requirement. When A has no safely approved policy, the bound is only an exploratory counterfactual and cannot authorize admission.

**Example.** Conditional on approved A service, suppose an ex-ante customer population consists of supported L (3/5) with measured *whole-service* increment 1/2, and unsupported H (2/5) with increment anywhere in [0,2]. An illustrative cost 1/4 gives shadow A net contribution in [1/20,17/20]. An independently assumed competent specialist yields net 2/5. Shadow A beats S iff its otherwise unknown H mean exceeds 7/8. The observed L rate alone neither decides the ranking nor tells us which data to buy. These are invented normalized contributions, not dollar estimates, robot performance or calibration. Demand-induced changes in strata weights require a new ex-ante target distribution.

A target grounding intervention might **change m_H** as well as reveal it. A purely diagnostic matched test changes only information; mixing treatment effects and information value would duplicate or misattribute economic returns. Without a prior over H outcomes (or an explicit minimax objective), interval width is not an expected value of information.

## Proposition 3 — dynamic acquisition with a qualification window

Suppose F continues unchanged until a separate policy passes joint qualification at date ℓ. Let J(ℓ,ω) be the set of dates remaining when real customers will accept the new complete service. For each θ and pre-action stratum d, let q_A(D,g,h,θ,d) be the probability of *joint* qualification under the actual read-only plus grounding regime. If only after joint qualification each date t provides incremental safe A captured contribution w_t(θ,d) and the full post-admission B interactions are separately bounded, the gross incremental shadow opportunity before costs is

    E_{θ,d}[ q_A(D,g,h,θ,d) · ∑_{t ∈ J(ℓ,ω)} β^t w_t(θ,d) ].

The expectation is under one persistent regime and genuine demand process; q and w need not be independent. This is the *continuation part* of the same optimized production value, not an additive data premium. If qualifying and admitting A changes future data, demand or costs, replace the one-step expression with the integrated policy-dependent dynamic V. Under a zero-customer window no data accumulation alone gives positive accepted-production value.

**Joint-qualification caution.** If two essential A qualifications have marginal probabilities p1=1/2, p2=3/4, their joint success probability may lie anywhere in [1/4,1/2] by Fréchet bounds. Assuming independence pins it at 3/8 without evidence. At two remaining service dates, unit safe incremental contribution per jointly qualified date, and cost 1/4, the illustrative *forced* project net lies in [1/4,3/4], with 1/2 under independence. If the opportunity expires before qualification the same forced project nets −1/4; keeping F and declining to invest yields zero incremental contribution. Marginals or low-level skill scores alone cannot remove the joint uncertainty.

These claims are elementary causal modularity, missing-support partial identification, set inclusion and finite-horizon expectation logic. The contribution, if any, is the resulting **coherent data-read/qualification/admission economic contrast** and its empirical falsifiers, not the mathematics in isolation.

## A real alternative to both controller sharing and strict data quarantine

The separate data plane means **protecting B need not delete transferable source information**. A feasible A-side training system may consume B's licensed historical trajectories, including failures/recoveries, while B continues on a pinned checkpoint. This is a counterexample to *unconditionally* treating lost shared-controller adaptation as the cost of protecting service. The stronger counterargument is also true: B's routine logs may miss exactly the target embodiment's action consequences, recovery tail or rare states needed for A qualification. More historical volume cannot identify unobserved actions without defensible invariance or target grounding.

Thus the economic decision is jointly: (a) retain free production byproducts and rights; (b) spend on curation/replay and target grounding only if they improve a *qualified* target customer opportunity; (c) acquire deliberate safe support if admissible; (d) choose embodiment, source schedule and approval latency; and (e) compare whole-network A+B production with F and S. The model does not privilege teleoperation, simulation or field data. The relative value is conditional on their target support, rights, latency and qualification effect.

## Closest prior foundations and architecture illustration

- Farboodi, Koh and Xia (2026), *Data-Driven Automation*, arXiv:2606.10127, Sections 5 and Appendix B: directed heterogeneous learning, endogenous production and future data value, complementary aggregation already covered; prior work: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10127.
- Laroche and Trichelair (2017), safe policy improvement with baseline bootstrapping, https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/safe-policy-improvement-baseline-bootstrapping/; and Simão and Spaan (2019), factored baseline bootstrapping, https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/4427. Their safe-policy methods are algorithmic prior art; they do **not** certify a robot deployment's actual recovery or customer-order network.
- Sachdeva, Su and Joachims (2020), deficient support in off-policy bandits, https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09438. Khan, Saveski and Ugander (2024 version), partial identification beyond overlap, https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11812. Lack of overlap and bounds are well-established, not a new discovery here.
- Public ROS 2 rosbag2 documentation describes recording and replaying time-stamped ROS messages (https://index.ros.org/r/rosbag2/). ROS 2 DDS-Security and security-enclave design discuss access-control artifacts and permissions (https://design.ros2.org/articles/ros2_dds_security.html; https://design.ros2.org/articles/ros2_security_enclaves.html). These show *possible technical building blocks*, not an audited signed target architecture, a proof of noninterference, legal data rights or safe operation. Replay into a live command topic could violate the read-only premise.

**Empirical discrimination:** independent version-pinned logging/read channel; no B-state, demand, contract, queue or resource effects during copy; match A target qualification across no-copy, copy-only, shared-training, pinned+copy+grounding and target-only data with equal rights/resources; record B original orders and full-network demand; verify A's held-out joint service, recovery and admission; compare net contributions to F and competent S. Never replace hard safety with expected service. No such physical/custodial observation is in this repository. See [protocol](SHADOW_LEARNING_EMPIRICAL.md), [proof ledger](SHADOW_LEARNING_PROOF_LEDGER.md), [exact synthetic source](shadow_learning_support.js) and separate learning exercises.

**Exact next question:** can an admissible, versioned read-only source channel be both genuinely *B-inert* and target-useful, and can safe matched A whole-service trials quantify conditional qualification and demand effects at the correct customer strata? If neither can be demonstrated, distinguish the technical architectural possibility from identified value and move the theory toward experimentally testable source-support sets—not an invented safety threshold.
