## Exact question

The preceding two-embodiment audit showed that, under *program-invariant* late customer/worker distributions, one original buyer–buyer–worker triple moment can reverse the P/G/E investment ranking. That invariance is precisely the vulnerable assumption: licensed target grounding G can produce accepted **early** service, alter two buyers' future original order offers, and create a new shared repair job. Can even G-specific tables of all one-, two- and three-way outcomes be economically insufficient? Which *lawfully supported* audit can close the gap without imputing unsupported G counterfactuals?

**Answer.** Yes. When a t2 accepted-quality event gates t3 mobile qualification, the incremental G value includes the *four-way* coincidence of successful early service, both t3 original buyer offers, and worker availability. Two nonanticipative structural causal worlds below agree on the entire R history and on every G-specific marginal of up to three of those variables, but reverse the optimal G/E investment. For a fixed policy menu, the additional scalar \(\zeta_G=\Pr(H_G=A_G=C_G=W_G=1)\) is sufficient given the verified lower-order moments. It is not identified by R histories, and cannot be estimated from G without lawful G assignment and appropriately observed original orders. This is a finite decision-sufficiency result, not a universal minimal data ontology.

## 1. One three-date economic law: two embodiments and delayed qualification

There is an installed arm A and an installed mobile robot C. Customer A has one remaining payable unit; customer C has two, of which at most one may be accepted at t2 and at most one at t3. At t1 each buyer has a registered opportunity and any inherited repair obligations are cleared; the witness starts t2 with no backlog, only to isolate *new* G-created repair demand. A single worker/robot-time slot handles **one** qualified late A or C order at t3. A new repair obligation \(J_2\) created **after** the t2 clearance window occupies that entire t3 shared slot. It cannot be cleared retroactively. E is a separately qualified mobile embodiment with its own worker and slot; it can complete C while the old arm completes A. Original t3 buyer offers are recorded before dispatch, including unserved and refused units. Any quoted value is net captured service contribution after real current-service costs; customer willingness to pay is a separate measured quantity in a field study.

At t0 the firm chooses one of the rights/qualification-feasible programs R, P, G or E before seeing its hidden regime. R retains already-qualified A and its fallback. P admits productive-source data and independently qualifies shared mobile C by t3 with probability \(q_P=1/10\), while retaining A. Its work is already scheduled in this *example* and has no incremental P cost. G pays \(k_G=1/5\) for a licensed target-grounding attempt. At t2, the target C buyer receives **accepted and paid service** \(H\in\{0,1\}\), contributing \(c=2\) if accepted; successful H also permits independently validated mobile C qualification by t3. If unsuccessful, the old A recipe remains available. The attempt, successful or not, can generate a new repair job. E pays \(k_E=37/20\) for independent mobile C hardware, qualification and coverage. All programs have the same offered t3 prices; each accepted t3 unit contributes \(v=4\). Source field/recovery, teleoperation, simulation, synthetic and public/pretrained evidence remain separate candidate acquisition actions outside this witness. No benchmark score is converted to cash.

Let \(A,C\in\{0,1\}\) denote **original** late A/C buyer offers under the selected program, and \(W=1-J_2\) denote whether the shared worker is free at t3. In the G arm a feasible late dispatch chooses A if A offers, or C if C offers and H has qualified mobile C; it cannot sell twice in the shared slot:

\[
g_G=2H+4W\,[A\lor(HC)]-\tfrac15.
\]

The early H contributes once and uses only the first of C's two possible paid units. The term \(A\lor HC\) is a *union of qualified customer service opportunities*, not a product benchmark. R/P/E use the separate no-G late-order law below; E can serve A and C simultaneously on independent slots.

## 2. Structural response worlds, with no backward causation

For each connected buyer–worker cluster let \(H,U,V\) be three independent fair bits fixed by physical and buyer primitives. At t2 the assigned G trial realizes H; then the new repair obligation is created by the trial and shared conditions. Original t3 orders follow subsequently. A regime \(\theta\in\{0,1\}\), fixed for that cluster before t0 and **not reported to the allocator**, specifies a possible response mechanism:

\[
\begin{aligned}
A^G&=U\oplus H,\qquad C^G=V\oplus H,\\
W^G&=H\oplus U\oplus V\oplus\theta,\qquad
J_2^G=1-W^G .
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(\oplus\) is exclusive OR. These are deliberately stark potential-outcome equations, not realistic demand or incident forecasts. They provide a lawful *timing order*: the worker job uses pre-existing U,V and realized H at the end of t2; the later offers use the same pre-existing buyer propensities and H. The worker job is **not** caused by a future t3 order. Changing H changes the customers' potential offers and incident outcome even though unconditional marginal treatment effects cancel in this symmetric construction.

Under no G attempt, \(H^R=0\), \(A^R=U\), \(C^R=V\), \(W^R=1\): no trial-created job. P preserves this R order/worker law and uses an independent Bernoulli Q_P; E preserves R orders but has an independently staffed mobile slot. Thus the **entire R original-order, worker, invoice and program history** is identical in \(\theta=0\) and \(\theta=1\), not merely its accepted bills. There is one common structural set of potential outcomes per regime, not independent per-date or per-policy adversaries.

Under G,
\[
H\oplus A^G\oplus C^G\oplus W^G=\theta.
\]
The eight equally likely realizations are therefore all even-parity four-bit states when \(\theta=0\), and all odd-parity states when \(\theta=1\). Projecting either parity distribution on **any proper subset of at most three** of (H,A,C,W) yields independent fair bits. Hence every G marginal, pairwise table, and three-way table agrees across regimes. This is strictly stronger observational equivalence than the earlier *three*-variable A/C/W parity example. The fourth-order event differs:
\[
\zeta_\theta=\Pr(H=A=C=W=1)=
\begin{cases}
1/8,&\theta=0,\\
0,&\theta=1.
\end{cases}
\]

Both worlds conserve C's lifetime two-unit customer budget, the single shared late slot, t2/t3 job timing and E's separate operator capacity. They differ in a latent *joint causal response* to G, not in a fabricated independent technical score for each arm.

## 3. Exact economic contrast and robust decision

For any admissible G law and this fixed late policy,
\[
\begin{aligned}
V_G
&=c\,\mathbb E[H]
+v\bigl(\mathbb E[WA]+\mathbb E[WHC]-\mathbb E[WAHC]\bigr)-k_G\\
&=1+4\left(\tfrac14+\tfrac18-\zeta_G\right)-\tfrac15
=\boxed{\tfrac{23}{10}-4\zeta_G}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The inclusion-exclusion subtraction is essential: when a qualified A order and qualified C order arrive together with one free worker, G can earn only one service margin. Merely adding expected stage completion would overstate economic data value. The lower-order moments are \(E[H]=1/2\), \(E[WA]=1/4\), \(E[WHC]=1/8\), identical in both worlds.

For the R response population, \(E[U]=E[V]=1/2\) and \(E[(1-U)V]=1/4\). The competing *whole-program* values are
\[
\begin{aligned}
V_R&=4E[U]=2,\\
V_P&=4E[U]+4q_PE[(1-U)V]=\tfrac{21}{10},\\
V_E&=4E[U+V]-\tfrac{37}{20}=\tfrac{43}{20}.
\end{aligned}
\]

There is no additional "data premium": future qualification is monetized only through the accepted t3 orders in these payoffs. All values are after the stated costs; no shared worker is counted twice.

Since all lower-order G tables are fixed fair marginals, \(0\le\zeta_G\le1/8\). Both endpoints are attained by the coherent structural regimes, as are every convex mixture of their whole-cluster laws. This is a **sharp** bound under the specified lower-order evidence and causal world class:
\[
\boxed{\tfrac95\le V_G\le\tfrac{23}{10}}.
\]

When \(\theta=1\), \(\zeta=0\): G earns \(23/10=2.30\), beating E at \(43/20=2.15\). When \(\theta=0\), \(\zeta=1/8\): G earns \(9/5=1.80\), while E wins. P=2.10 and R=2.00 are preserved feasible alternatives, although E dominates them in this **particular constructed** menu. In a different customer or cost law that dominance need not hold. G's lower payoff is due to a *costly trial and service-created worker congestion*, not because acquiring free optional data forces destructive training: the firm may choose R or decline G.

For a t0 randomized commitment assigning share \(x\) of independent deployment clusters to G and \(1-x\) to E, with \(\theta\) hidden,
\[
\mathcal R(x)=\max\left\{\tfrac3{20}(1-x),\tfrac7{20}x\right\}.
\]
The first term is regret for not choosing G in the good regime; the second is regret for choosing it in the bad regime. Equalizing gives
\[
\boxed{x_G^*=\tfrac3{10},\quad x_E^*=\tfrac7{10},\quad
\mathcal R^*=\tfrac{21}{200}}.
\]
The **maximin** policy instead chooses E entirely since its guaranteed value is \(43/20\). These are precommitment policies, not an infeasible clairvoyant assignment to unobserved regimes. Mixtures are over whole deployments, not within-cluster physical lottery tickets.

The precise policy switch threshold is
\[
V_G>V_E\iff \zeta_G<\tfrac3{80}.
\]
Thus an independent, legally permitted G-specific estimate of the single fourth-order event could identify the *investment ranking*, despite not identifying every latent cause of H, offers and repair.

## 4. The minimal extra lawful audit, and where it fails

Suppose complete G-assigned and R/P/E-assigned cohorts are prospectively supported: independent clusters are assigned before t2 quality and response; signed source/target retention and use rights, qualifications, original buyer offers/no-offers, actual t2 accepted C service, job creation/clearance and remaining customer budget are logged. To apply the **fixed-menu** formula, G-specific lower-order moments \(E[H]\), \(E[WA]\), \(E[WHC]\) and baseline R/P/E values must already be identified. The only *additional* G moment then required is \(\zeta_G=E[HACW]\). Because this event is zero outside the **paid-early, worker-free risk set** \(H=W=1\), the extra audit of *both original late buyer offers* need be randomized with positive probability only in that risk set, provided H and W are themselves always ascertained and the assumed lower-order information is genuinely supported. Specifically,
\[
\zeta_G=\Pr(H=W=1)\Pr(A=C=1\mid H=W=1).
\]
Here \(\Pr(H=W=1)=1/4\), while the second factor equals \(1/2\) in the even-parity regime and \(0\) in the odd-parity regime.

Let M indicate inclusion in that optional original-offer audit and let \(\eta(H,W,X)>0\) be its known inclusion probability *on H=W=1*, fixed before seeing unreported original A/C offers conditional on recorded pre-audit X. Conditional independent missingness yields the identity
\[
E\!\left[\frac{M\,H W A C}{\eta(H,W,X)}\right]=E[H W A C].
\]
This is standard inverse-observation weighting. If reports are triggered by a completed invoice or an operator's choice after seeing the unreported A/C joint event, the needed independence fails; an invoice-only panel does not estimate \(\zeta\). A full G original-offer registry is preferable where lawful and feasible. Selective audits in blocked H=0 or W=0 states are not necessary **for this terminal fixed-policy \(\zeta\)**; they can be essential for later dates, new qualification policies or estimating other contrasts.

If G was never assigned, or if a G trial is unauthorized, \(\Pr(Z=G)=0\) and neither the weighted moment nor a transported R triple identifies the G response. The two structural regimes prove this directly: both produce identical **complete R records** and identical G lower-order hypotheticals but opposite correct investment decisions. A pre-t0 exogenous report about \(\theta\) could change the decision only if it is actually observed before commitment; a t3 audit cannot retroactively reassign the same cohort. An audit today can train the *next* investment rule, charging its acquisition and reporting cost once.

In a fully recorded randomized G cohort of n independent closed clusters, \(\hat\zeta=n^{-1}\sum_i H_iW_iA_iC_i\) is unbiased and obeys the conservative Hoeffding interval \(\hat\zeta\pm\sqrt{\log(2/\delta)/(2n)}\), before clipping to [0,1/8]. With coverage \(1-\delta\), a sign certificate chooses G only if its upper \(\zeta\) bound is below \(3/80\), E only if its lower bound exceeds \(3/80\), and otherwise leaves the ranking unresolved. This assumes truly independent cluster assignments and exact original-order/worker measurements; correlated shared models and staff require clustering/whole-network randomization. Such intervals reflect sampling uncertainty conditional on this economic law, **not** uncertainty about whether it is correct.
