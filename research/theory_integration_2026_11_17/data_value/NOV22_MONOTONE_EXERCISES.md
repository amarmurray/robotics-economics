# Exercises — November 22 demand-matched qualification and market support

**All numerics synthetic and normalized. No field customer economics.**

1. Let B denote an original eligible parent's legally committed second-service purchase; Q0 and Q1 are complete safe qualification with Q1>=Q0. The original joint distribution implies M=1/4, N=1/4; an authorized experiment identifies d=3/8. Derive the **sharp** interval for E[B(Q1-Q0)]. If a paid complete second service has net contribution 4 and training costs 1/4, bound incremental net value. Explain what requires field verification.

2. Two pre-treatment original strata of four cases each have respectively (M_A,N_A,d_A)=(2,0,1)/4 and (M_B,N_B,d_B)=(0,2,1)/4. First ignore the stratum labels, then use them. Explain which additional observation made the interval narrower without creating more technical transfer.

3. Derive the identity B1Q1-B0Q0=B0(Q1-Q0)+(B1-B0)Q1. Does qualification nesting alone guarantee nondecreasing paid output if buyer demand changes? Why does identical mean purchase across randomized arms not determine P(B1!=B0)?

4. For lawful precommitted menu 6 (one service at price6) and menu 4 (up to two services at price4), let first purchase probabilities be q6=4/8 and q4=6/8; under menu4 the joint second paid/qualified event is s4=1/8. Calculate the revenue-optimal precommitted menu. Explain why the earlier one-q formula fails.

5. Define a physically credible scenario where retaining baseline policy Q0 as an available software file does **not** ensure Q1>=Q0 on the original qualified customer window. State the correct empirical route when no safe fallback exists.

6. Distinguish the effect of a free optional raw data source from mandatory retraining that removes access to the old dispatch recipe. Identify where current and future service value should be counted in the same dynamic policy objective.
