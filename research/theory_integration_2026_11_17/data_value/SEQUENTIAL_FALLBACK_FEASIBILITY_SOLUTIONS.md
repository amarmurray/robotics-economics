# Instructor solutions — sequential fallback

**Do not distribute as an exercise handout. All examples are synthetic.**

1. `q_F=1-b=7/8`. Regression `ell=Pr(R0=1,X=0,H=0)` is between `max(0,1/2+1/8-1)=0` and `min(1/2,1/8)=1/8`. R's old ability may be destroyed by the new action through workpiece state, operator contention, rights or elapsed time; software rollback does not restore those.

2. Restrict to X-failure mass k=1/4. With a=b=1/8, `ell in [max(0,1/8+1/8-1/4),min(1/8,1/8)]=[0,1/8]`. With a=1/4 and b=1/8, `ell in [1/8,1/8]`; regression is unavoidable in any joint table matching those marginals. An actual no-regression safety case requires a certified structural implication `R0=1,X=0 => H=1` for all original physically supported histories, not a small sample estimate.

3. R gross and net is `4·(4/8)=2`. F gross is `4·(7/8)=7/2`, net after incremental cost 2 is `3/2`, so R wins. With F paid only on histories 1–3, its gross/net is `4·(3/8)=3/2` despite nested physical qualification. Customer interest, quality, timing and resource scarcity affect paid complete service. Retry-generated observations can improve later policy in the two-date model, but the resulting future qualified production and associated opportunity costs are included once in J rather than assigned a separate premium atop the same terminal value.
