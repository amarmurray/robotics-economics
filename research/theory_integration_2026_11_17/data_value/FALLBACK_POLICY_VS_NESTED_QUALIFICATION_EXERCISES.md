# Student exercises — optional policies versus genuinely safe fallback

**Separate from instructor solutions. Synthetic units; do not infer real robotic economics.**

1. In a common fixed world, prove that enlarging the admissible *complete* policy set cannot reduce optimized gross value, then explain why a nonrefundable training fee or changed customer law invalidates the same statement for net value.
2. Four original histories have R=(1,1,0,0); two X policies have X_N=(1,1,1,0) and X_C=(0,1,1,1). Calculate average safe qualification, old-success/new-failure mass, and the hypothetically executable OR success probability in both worlds. Why does choosing R globally not constitute an OR fallback?
3. Fix original eligible buyer fraction b=3/4, paid-and-qualified rates h_R=1/2 and h_X=5/8. Find the sharp interval for formerly paid-and-qualified cases lost under X. Is the zero-loss statement empirically identified?
4. Let a preaction original-stratum selector choose X with probability t_z, independently of complete-policy potential outcomes conditional on Z. Derive the expected paid-qualified difference and characterize exact casewise safety. Does a positive averaged qualification difference suffice?
5. In two equally weighted strata with B=1, A has (q_R,q_X,v,c)=(1/2,3/4,8,1/2), B has (1/4,1/2,4,0). Optimize randomized stratum selection under an exact worst-case expected physical regression budget alpha=1/16. Explain why this is not a deployed hard-safety approval.
