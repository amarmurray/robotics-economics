# Student exercises — source-selected failures and data-acquisition decisions

Use the two-type example from SELECTION_AND_SEQUENTIAL_DATA_VALUE.md. Contributions are normalized *complete-service* captured increments, not robot motion scores. The original incumbent is qualified and retained.

**1. Eligibility selection.** With pi_0=1, pi_1=1/2, m=1/2, g_G(easy)=8/5, g_G(hard)=-2/5, derive conditional delta_0(G), delta_1(G) and the exact decomposition. Explain why a lower post-source observed success rate of G need not mean G deteriorated technically.

**2. Embodiment substitution.** With g_B(easy)=-7/10, g_B(hard)=13/10, calculate the preference for G versus B before and after additional source training. State which technical transfer parameters actually changed.

**3. Diagnostic countermodel.** Compute the x0 option value if an independently permitted, error-free pre-choice diagnostic reveals easy or hard and the acquisition action can be conditioned on that report. How costly may that diagnostic be before paid x1+body is preferred? Distinguish this information value from actual learned competence.

**4. Identification design.** A firm randomizes x0 and x1 across robots whose tasks, operators, learner and customers all overlap, then compares G results only among failed robots. Name two failures of the intended causal identification and propose the minimum measurement/assignment correction, subject to independent safety approval.

**5. General proof.** Let nested source qualification failure sets be F_1 subset F_0. Derive pi_0 delta_0(a)=pi_1 delta_1(a)+m delta_R(a) and state precisely why it fails if source x1 changes the target embodiment's post-failure recovery policy.

Do not use the instructor solutions while first attempting the exercises.
