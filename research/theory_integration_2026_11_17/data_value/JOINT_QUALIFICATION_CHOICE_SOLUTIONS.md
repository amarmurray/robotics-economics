# Instructor solutions — joint qualification and investment choice

**Keep separate from JOINT_QUALIFICATION_CHOICE_EXERCISES.md.**

1. Nonnegative states \(\Pr(11)=h,\Pr(10)=0.6-h,\Pr(01)=0.6-h,\Pr(00)=h-0.2\) imply \(0.2\le h\le0.6\). At 0.2 the masses in order 00/10/01/11 are 0/0.4/0.4/0.2; at 0.6 they are 0.4/0/0/0.6. Marginal rates are exactly 0.6 in both.

2. \(a=b=2,\kappa=12-2-2=8\). \(\Delta V=2\Delta p_A+2\Delta p_B+8\Delta h\). For the four p vectors \(2/5,1/2,1/2,3/5\), both marginal contrasts are zero. For h=(1/5,1/4,1/4,1/2), \(\Delta h=1/5\), giving +8/5; with h11=1/5, \(\Delta h=-1/10\), giving -4/5. Gross R/S/G/SG: 16/5,4,4,32/5 or 16/5,4,4,4. S and G cost 1/2 each; E costs 1 and adds a signed gross alternative of 5. Eight net programs listed in the chapter; high SG=27/5 wins, low E=4 wins. SG net=7/5+8h11; E=4, hence h11>13/40.

3. With \(F(11)=2\), \(\kappa=2-2-2=-2\); high \(-2(1/5)=-2/5\), low \(-2(-1/10)=+1/5\). Only original-customer demand/slot opportunity changed. Source-trained joint qualifications did not.

4. Fréchet feasibility is \(L=\max(0,p_A+p_B-1)\le h\le\min(p_A,p_B)=U\). Since interaction is linear in four h variables with signs (+,-,-,+), assign h11,h00 to L and h10,h01 to U for the lower bound (reverse for upper); \(\kappa<0\) reverses orientation. Product-coupling arms is admissible only absent structural cross-arm restrictions. If outcomes must share learner weights or the old program must remain an available checkpoint, the true feasible set can be smaller, requiring a coupled LP or randomized design.

5. Under nesting of algorithms/dispatch and unchanged costs/demand, old choices remain feasible and a supremum over a larger feasible set cannot fall. Mandatory training, costly acquisition/curation, restricted rights or displaced slots can reduce net value even if free optional access never does.
