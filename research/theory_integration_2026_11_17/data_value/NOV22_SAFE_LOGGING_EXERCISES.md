# Student exercises: two independent closures for robotics-economic data value

**Private teaching companion to [Nov22 safe-logging identification](NOV22_SAFE_LOGGING_DATA_VALUE_IDENTIFICATION.md).** Mathematical synthetic examples; no robot/customer evidence.

1. **Whole-policy propensity.** In a two-stratum original customer population with P(L)=P(H)=1/2, suppose approved full-policy G is assigned with probabilities e_G(L)=1/2 and e_G(H)=1/4, independently of potential economic outcomes conditional on Z. Write the inverse-probability estimand of E[Y(G)] and explain why recording only fulfilled robot picks fails the original population estimand.
2. **Sharp unsupported-stratum bound.** The G-on-L contribution mean is 3; G was never assigned on H, and a justified whole-cluster net contribution bound for G-on-H is [0,5]. Calculate the sharp population interval for G versus retained R=2 and embodied E=9/4. Which option is robustly ranked?
3. **Value of a target-only audit.** Place equal *illustrative prior probabilities* on G-on-H contribution 0 and 5. An independent safe audit perfectly reveals this state without affecting rights, costs, production or buyer behavior. Derive the expected improvement from conditioning the future R/G/E choice on the report. How would a price greater than the gain change the selection?
4. **Two certificates.** A source-trained X proposal has possible first-step states alpha and beta; an old recovery has distinct actions for each and deployed sensors label both retry. A perfectly observed bench test says each can be recovered individually. Is X physically admitted? Does a randomized approved G-versus-R field study prove X can be safely deployed?
5. **Selective production data and rights.** Explain why collecting one million new incumbent-R successful field trajectories cannot identify the original-order contribution of unlogged G-on-H without a transport assumption. State how teleoperated target observations, failure/recovery trajectories and simulation could help—and what each cannot establish by itself.
