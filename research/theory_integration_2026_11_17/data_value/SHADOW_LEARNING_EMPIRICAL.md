# Matched data-plane / target-service identification protocol

**18 November 2026; prospective design only. No customer records, training or field trials acquired.**

## The hypothesis to discriminate

A version-pinned read-only copy of production F logs may preserve all B original accepted orders **and** improve qualification of A under a separate controller/body. Alternatively, shared operator/demand/compute resources make it noninert, or F source visitation lacks target action/recovery support. Both are plausible. A policy's skill benchmark and a full-service contribution are different endpoints.

## Evidence objects and separately falsifiable conditions

| Object | Required observable / governance artifact | Failure interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B closure under copy | Signed before/after candidate graph of controller-actuation permissions, ROS communications, copy/record CPU and bandwidth, data rights, original B orders, prices, routing, operator recovery, supply constraints, demand feedback, versions and exceptions | A controller-pinned policy can still alter B; reject pre-admission pathwise protection |
| Data provenance | Hashes of raw timestamps/actions/contexts, training inclusion/exclusion, failures, recovery, teleoperation, simulation/synthetic labels, public/pretrained licenses and reuse rights | Unusable data or different training stock invalidates matched source comparison |
| Target policy | Checkpoint/body/recipe version, intended A customer/population, complete service acceptance, episode-level failures/recovery, human support, original order margin/capture and specialist comparator | Offline metric cannot identify qualified, economically accepted A |
| Support and transport | Baseline pre-action customer stratum, target state/action/recovery support, propensity under *approved* intervention where applicable, valid conditional invariances, joint qualification measurements | Unobserved target actions produce partial identification/abstention, not point valuation |
| Joint approval | Explicit physical safe-stop, hard simultaneous operator-capacity admission certificate, signed conditions and policy-closed dispatch/demand network | Even positive mean A gain cannot authorize a controller rollout |
| Time | Training, validation, safe admission date, real customer service window, price/cost movements and retained F use | Late qualification may have no realizable customer benefit |

## Competent matched comparisons

Use customer-stratified, ex-ante planned treatment cells and the *same* net-production accounting: F continued; R routine source byproduct with no extra intervention; C pinned-B read-only copy and offline A training; G shared controller update; Gpin pinned-B training with copy plus target grounding; S competent current specialist. E new embodiment only if it has its **own** approved safe recipe and rights. Capture source trajectories and metadata separately from deliberate target teleoperation, simulation and intervention probes. Do not infer intervention exposure from nominal data count; record the selection policy and state reached.

Before A admission, measure any copy-induced B impact on signed original order IDs, cancelled/redirected orders, accepted invoice contribution, operator queues, latency and customer offers. After A admission, evaluate a **whole policy** including B demand/recovery, not A-only task success. Randomizing individual robots inside a common controller/market network cannot identify whole-network effects; any actual network-policy randomized design requires safe approved units and positive assignment support. User authorization does not extend to executing the physical experiment.

For source-to-qualification, record the full joint outcome of complete A recipes, not independent marginal skill rates. Record the actual customer revenue/cost/capture net of matched feasible specialist and original F baselines. Keep retention, curation, compute, personnel, recovery and production expenses in one cost ledger. If target unsupported mass remains, present an identified interval tied to the observed pre-treatment customer distribution and a separately described hard-safety eligibility result.

## Verified public illustration, not field evidence

Public rosbag2 documents say ROS 2 records/replays timestamped messages and discuss selective topics (https://index.ros.org/r/rosbag2/, checked 18 November 2026). ROS 2 DDS Security design documents describe signed governance/permission artifacts and the enclave model (https://design.ros2.org/articles/ros2_dds_security.html, checked 18 November 2026). Their existence establishes neither a live fleet's information-flow separation nor that replay is read-only, resource-free, contractually licensed or safe. The first targeted verification requires *actual* graph/policy/version evidence from an authorized deployment—not an inference from ROS documentation.

## Atlas/backbone connection

Map each observed service to specific accepted customer units and end markets before using official BLS 2025–35 occupation by industry and a consistent-vintage BEA input-output system to approximate exposure. The numerical BLS–BEA crosswalk and robot-qualified share are still missing; neither wages nor an industry sales figure is automatically a robotics addressable market. There is no available authorized private accepted-order panel. The next non-field improvement is a reproducible support/identification schema plus a real official quantitative backbone when accessible, without a fabricated crosswalk.
