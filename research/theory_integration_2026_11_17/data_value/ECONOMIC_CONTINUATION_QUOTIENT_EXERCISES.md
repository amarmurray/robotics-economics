# Student exercises — economic continuation and buyer-demand state

Use the linked [theory](ECONOMIC_CONTINUATION_QUOTIENT_AND_DEMAND_SIGNAL.md). Synthetic normalized contributions only; do not assume these are measurements.

1. **Exact closure.** For a two-stage licensed fleet, write a backward-induction proof that a partition preserving every lawful action, expected net buyer-paid stage return and conditional next abstract-state law preserves the optimal full-history program value. Give one way retaining only C=UVL can fail, even when C is perfectly observed.
2. **Quantitative error.** Assuming all net one-stage rewards are in [-B,B], projected conditional mean errors ≤ε_t and total-variation projected transition errors ≤δ_t uniformly for every reachable full history and legal action with identical feasible menus, derive a finite-horizon bound for |J−\bar J| and a sufficient SG-versus-E sign test. Explain why a logged mean regression error of zero is not enough.
3. **Demand-conditioned source transfer.** A current lawful source controller can serve only customer type A. New licensed source-plus-grounding learning qualifies B too; one shared slot serves exactly one offered type. The next order Z is A with probability p and B otherwise. Compare old R, SG with Z hidden, SG with Z known before dispatch, and an E program paying 3/4 in expectation and retaining R. Derive the economic cross-difference of adding B and revealing Z; calculate both SG−E gaps for p=1/2.
4. **Program-dependent continuation.** Give a counterexample where equal date-0 C and equal near-term payment rates fail to transport an economic quotient from R to SG because SG changes the legal action menu or creates a later qualified service. Specify the smallest extra history fact needed in your example.
