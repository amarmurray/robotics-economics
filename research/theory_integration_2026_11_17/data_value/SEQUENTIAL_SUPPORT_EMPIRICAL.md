# Sequential support to accepted customer production: discriminating empirical design

**Design only — no robot experiment, accessed customer panel, model training or economic calibration. 18 November 2026 UTC.** This extends INTERVENTION_EMPIRICAL_PROTOCOL.md; obtain permissions before any real-world work.

**Experimental unit.** A matched accepted service attempt and its sequential controller lineage, not an intervention count. Fixed site/customer workflow, safety protocol, acceptable joint recipe set, hardware/tooling, human availability, capacity, demand window, operator costs, rights and calibration budget. Record q0, the first safe observation X1/gate/C1, retraining inclusion and q1 version, the second action chosen after the actual logged history, X2 by state class, independent gate auditing, all assists and recovery, and held-out B qualification including whether customer service would actually be accepted and priced. Distinguish eligible opportunities from realized visits.

**Four relevant policies under matched resource budgets:** retained old controller and ordinary production; deliberately supervised first target grounding (permitted states only); upgraded controller plus controlled safe B exposure; different body/tool or competent target-only specialist. Optional information may be used or ignored. A customer-facing comparison must subtract foregone accepted production, monitoring/operator hours, compute, curation and changing hardware, and use the same qualified f(Q;omega). Preserve a robot-human/specialist baseline rather than choosing a straw man.

**Factorial identification intervention.** Where approved, randomize training inclusion of already-collected licensed corrections across matched q0 seeds, then separately randomize the safe approved second *coverage policy* in the retained-q0 and updated-q1 arms. This distinguishes direct technical effect from the effect of changes in visited-state distribution. Treat inaccessible B states as missing support, not zero errors. With safety-independent auditors, estimate P(X2=B|q,policy,H-proxy), gate sensitivity given exposure and held-out joint B qualification. Estimate branch U_B with customer demand and actual complete-service cost, not a per-correction price. Do not purposely delay safety signals, route into unsafe states, or withhold urgent intervention.

**Critical disconfirmation tests.**
1. If B support is unchanged between q0 and q1 under comparable safe policies, the endogenous reachability mechanism is not operative here even if overall accuracy improves.
2. If q1 reaches B more often but no retained held-out B recipe qualifies, e rises while s remains zero: no productive B value from this pathway.
3. If B qualifies but customer production remains saturated or a specialist dominates, technical transfer has zero incremental optimized customer-production value.
4. If the selected correction group has lower B qualification at the same marginal B success and same customer window, the independence product is biased upward; report conditional heterogeneity/covariance rather than a stable additive transfer coefficient.
5. If ordinary production produces the same relevant data at equal safety and smaller cost, special acquisition need not be selected.
6. If no approved arm reaches B, only conditional bounds or explicit transport assumptions are legitimate; do not report an identified expected marginal data value.

**Outcome bundle and evidence type.** Report joint accepted service eligibility, output, rework, qualified downtime, operator/robot/coordination resource use, demand-by-task/site/value chain, contractual access and provider captured margin. Field, recovery, teleoperation, simulation/synthetic and public pretrained inputs get separate provenance, rights and validation protocols; only matched re-training interventions connect them to qualification. Technical success metrics and reported demonstration hours have no mechanical exchange rate to dollars.
