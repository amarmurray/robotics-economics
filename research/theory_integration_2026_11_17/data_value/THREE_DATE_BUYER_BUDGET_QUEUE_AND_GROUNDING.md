## Question and economic environment

When a productive source deployment generates transferable competence but consumes a buyer's finite purchasing opportunity and recovery capacity, how should a full-stack robotics firm compare **P** (a current accepted paid source service with field learning), **G** (licensed grounding of already-held data before the target service), **E** (a genuinely additional embodiment and worker), and retained safe **R**? If G has no historical action support, what can a lawful original-buyer audit actually identify, and how much could an early, legally permissible G-specific signal be worth?

Dates are t=0 commitment, t=1 early original-buyer service, and t=2 later original-buyer services. Customer A has **one-unit lifetime budget** across t=1,2, while distinct customer C has its own budget at t=2. The t=1 robot is safe to operate under the admitted recipe; an incident after completed t=1 service can create two units of repair workload. A single real worker processes one unit in (t=1,t=2), leaving one unit at t=2 when an incident occurs. At most one standard robot job is performed at t=2; E purchases a separate robot-time and recovery resource for C and does not magically double the original A purchasing budget. All costs and customer cash contributions are net of acceptance, contractual collection and genuinely displaced service, and each acquisition cost is subtracted **once**.

Let u in {0,1} be the nonanticipative t=1 decision to serve A's genuine offer (u=1) or preserve the A budget and worker availability (u=0), possible only if t=1 qualified, legal, safe and actually demanded. The observed t=1 information must precede J, later D and later C; no t=2 oracle is allowed to select u. If u=1, A's t=2 budget is B_A,2=1-u=0. With incident J^a in {0,1}, repair workload is 2uJ^a units, one worker unit clears before t=2, and the remaining shared queue is L_2^a=max(0,2uJ^a-uJ^a)=uJ^a. Thus the same worker cannot be called simultaneously free and engaged. An eligible late A original offer D_2^a is still well-defined as *latent demand if budget remained*, but the actual accepted A purchase is gated by B_A,2. Target C qualification Q_C^a and its own original offer C_2^a are separate: a benchmark pass is not either an order or collected revenue.

For P, a source success yields a new learner checkpoint L^P only on t=1 production. With initial C qualification Q_C,0 and a rights-permitted training pipeline, Q_C,2^P(u)=Q_C,0 OR (u AND L^P). For G, Q_C,2^G is obtained from licensed grounding, with its own action-dependent original demand C_2^G and repair J^G; no historical G sample is presumed. For E, independently staffed/embodied C can operate concurrently with standard A at t=2, subject to its own qualification, demand, entry delay, and cost. Field trajectories, failures/recoveries, teleoperation, simulated, synthetic and public/pretrained data have separate learner transition and cost/right parameters outside the special witness below; none is an interchangeable unit of "data."

## Production theorem: the finite customer budget creates an overlap discount

For any lawful single-standard-robot t=2 policy that sees original orders before the t=2 dispatch, and service values b,c>=0, set T=Q_C,2 C_2. Conditional on preserving A's budget at t=1 and having a free worker at t=2, exact optimized late value is

    E[max(b D_2, c T)] = b Pr(D_2) + c Pr(T)
                          - min(b,c) Pr(D_2=1,T=1).             (1)

For a productive early service that consumes A's only purchase, with terminal C qualification depending on that operation, its expected complete paid contribution under a known conditional law is

    V_a(serve | I_1)=a + c Pr(T^a=1,J^a=0 | I_1,serve),           (2)

assuming no other t=2 customer and no legacy backlog. The comparable preserve-budget action is

    V_a(skip | I_1)=E[max(b D_2, c T^a(skip)) | I_1,skip].        (3)

Choose the larger **conditional expectation before** t=2 demand, learning and recovery outcomes are seen. More generally, write the finite dynamic Bellman value over actual customer budget B_t, learner Q_t, worker workload L_t, legally observed information I_t and independent embodiment capacity E_t, with B_{t+1}=B_t-accepted purchases and L_{t+1}=[L_t+new repair workload-real service]_+. The stage reward is actually accepted, qualified, paid and capacity-feasible customer production. There is **no second continuation-data premium**; continuation services already appear in the Bellman recursion.

**Proof of (1).** For any D,T in {0,1}, max(bD,cT)=bD+cT-min(b,c)DT by checking the four outcomes. Taking expectations proves (1), without an independence premise. Equations (2)-(3) follow from B_A,2=0 after the early A purchase, from L_2=J after one-worker repair service, and from the t=2 feasible dispatch menu. In particular, a marginal improvement in target qualification cannot be priced by c times a benchmark pass rate: its incremental paid value is conditional on C's original demand, the competitor A's offer, and worker capacity. Under known admissible laws, a new option that **preserves old policies with no compulsory cost** weakly raises optimized value; a required license/training fee or forced overwriting can lower net contribution without contradicting that option result.

**A negative inference.** Customer demand and training outcomes measured in P, even perfectly, are not a lower bound on Pr(T^G=1,J^G=0). If G has zero historical action support, all G causal demand/qualification/recovery response may differ while observed P histories are held fixed. Formulas (1)-(3) are an objective and physical feasibility restriction, not an identification shortcut.

## Coherent synthetic witness: late original buyer substitution changes the investment

All numbers are **normalized, hypothetical captured contribution**. For any arm, A's date-1 offer and qualification are certain, early accepted payment is a=4/5; A's latent date-2 order D has Pr(D=1)=1/2. A late sale contributes b=2, qualified C contributes c=3. A can purchase only once. E has an actually separate date-2 robot-time slot and worker (by synthetic assumption), C qualification and original offer probability 1/2, and a total new-embodiment cost 2/5.

P: if u=1, the paid source operation qualifies C (L^P=1), while Pr(C_2^P=1)=1/2 and Pr(J^P=1)=1/5 independently. The t=2 worker is clear with probability 4/5, so the productive source program pays P_serve=4/5+3(1/2)(4/5)=2. If P skips early service, old R remains lawful, C is not qualified, and P_skip=2(1/2)=1. Thus V_P=2, **including** present paid source production and its future qualified C contribution.

G: the target qualification Q_C^G=1 is obtained from appropriately licensed already-held data, at zero *incremental* G cost in this deliberately isolating example. The C original order C_2^G has Pr(C_2^G=1)=1/2; J^G from early A operation has probability 1/2 and is independent of demand. G_serve=4/5+3(1/2)(1/2)=31/20. But G_skip=5/2-2x, where x=Pr(D=1,C_2^G=1) lies in [0,1/2]. This x is customer-demand **overlap** competing for the same t=2 robot-time and one A purchase budget; it is not a technical transfer coefficient.

E: skip t=1 and retain A's purchase opportunity; independent new C robot-time and worker permit both late orders to be paid. The outcome is E_skip=2(1/2)+3(1/2)-2/5=21/10. Its early action earns only 4/5 rather than the expected later A sale of 1, so E_serve=19/10 and E optimally skips. E's additional robot, staff, capital, power and commissioning ability are **stipulated for this toy** and not observed or free.

Take two single, fully coherent potential-history laws indexed by a common market regime W. Within each law draw one shared D~Bernoulli(1/2), an independent J^G~Bernoulli(1/2), and independent regime-invariant P and E learner/incident/demand histories as specified above. In W=F (*separate late segments*), define C_2^G=1-D, so x=0; in W=H (*simultaneous late demand*), define C_2^G=D, so x=1/2. All P logs, every stipulated G marginal Q_C, original C order and J probability, all observed A demand and E histories are identical across these worlds. Only the **unassigned G action's customer overlap response** changes. This is one jointly defined potential-outcome law per world, not separately selected arm oracles; the differing G cross-buyer correlation is not asserted identified from P records.

| Fixed, advance-chosen program/policy | W=F | W=H |
|---|---:|---:|
| P serve and learn | 2 | 2 |
| G preserve A budget, C qualified from licensed data | 5/2 | 3/2 |
| G serve A early, C qualified, risk shared recovery | 31/20 | 31/20 |
| E preserve budget, operate independent C capacity; cost included | 21/10 | 21/10 |

In F, G_skip beats E; in H, E beats P and every G policy. The **program-specific known-law** G optimum is 5/2 in F but 31/20 in H: using this hindsight switch in an uninformed policy is illegal. With arbitrary overlap x, G_skip exceeds E precisely when x<1/5; G switches from skip to serve only when x>19/40. These distinct thresholds show why dispatch and capital commitments cannot be collapsed into a technical transfer score.

## Implementable regret versus the unreachable oracle

Assume *only for this witness* W∈{F,H}, both physically coherent and otherwise consistent with every observed P and E record, and no informative precommitment report. Against the best world-informed legal program (G_skip in F; E_skip in H), both P and G_serve are weakly dominated by E. If q is the **actual advance probability of G_skip**, with 1-q on E, regret is max{(1-q)(5/2-21/10), q(21/10-3/2)}. The unique minimax solution is q=2/5 and worst regret=6/25; maximin *paid contribution* instead chooses E with 21/10. Regret and guaranteed production are different decision objectives. A plausible-looking "G optimal within each world" surrogate would allow a free post-hoc serve/skip switch in H and yields a different, nonimplementable certificate.

Under a separately stipulated 50/50 prior, ex-ante no-signal Bayesian choice is E: E=21/10 while G_skip averages 2. An authentic legally available **precommitment** binary report S with symmetric accuracy h>1/2 for W, received in time, has favorable-signal expected G_skip value 3/2+h, versus E 21/10. For h in [1/2,1], the report's gross selection value is

    VOI(h) = (1/2) max{0,h-3/5}.                                (4)

Thus at h=3/4, gross VOI=3/40, before any real probe/report cost. h<=3/5 does not change the decision. A report based solely on P telemetry is independent of W by construction and has **zero** identification or switching value. A complete G-specific paired-original-order report could distinguish these extreme worlds, but it requires legal access, genuine target exposure, sufficiently transportable G response and time before commitment. Report accuracy 3/4 is *stipulated*, not derived from technical benchmarks or an available dataset. Even after G is irrevocably chosen, a perfect early report could in this witness change G's serve/skip action (gross 1/40 under the equal prior); the noisy 3/4 report does **not** change that postcommitment action. Signal timing and policy menus matter.

**Proof of regret and VOI.** In F the oracle earns 5/2; in H the oracle earns 21/10. Conditional expected regret of a fixed lottery is affine in its G_skip share q; equality of the two slopes yields q=(2/5), regret 6/25. The other fixed policies are pointwise dominated by E in both worlds. For the signal, Bayes' rule under a balanced prior gives posterior Pr(F|S=favorable)=h. Expected G_skip is h(5/2)+(1-h)(3/2)=3/2+h. Under the unfavorable signal posterior 1-h, expected G_skip is 5/2-h, never exceeding E for h>=1/2. Both signals have probability 1/2. The favorable signal changes the choice only for h>3/5; averaging the chosen conditional values proves (4). These are finite elementary decision calculations, not newly discovered information theory.

## Identification, alternative assumptions, and empirical implications

(1) A full original-buyer/no-order ledger and engineering-certified queue duration can test the customer-budget and recovery constraints for **observed P**. They cannot reveal x_G or G-specific target uptake in an untried action. Without even the hypothetical G marginals stipulated here, the economic interval is wider; in particular, physical reachability alone does not create a positive G return floor. Recording only successful C invoices selects on *both* accepted service and no worker backlog and therefore cannot reconstruct original A/C overlap.

(2) A rights-feasible, safety-approved, exposure- and interference-aware **G-specific** intervention, plus *joint* original A/C offers (including refused/unserved offers), verified target qualification, true worker recovery and actual paid acceptance, can identify x and the relevant policy's customer contribution when assignment/reporting have justified positive support. A random G assignment probability e and known positive report probability rho give the conditional expectation identity E[1{G assigned}R D C/(e rho)]=E[D^G C^G] under exchangeability, consistent observed histories, and correctly modeled missingness. If rho depends on latent D,C and is not known or has structural zeros, this equality fails; bounds or additional restrictions are required. A lawful proxy report with only engineering Q accuracy need not predict original buyer overlap at all.

(3) If G changes A's latent date-2 willingness to order as a *function of observed prior t=1 outcomes*, or both A and C have multiunit budgets, replace (1) by the full causal demand and budget transition. If repair workload has more than two units, the assumed L2=J identity fails. If the t=2 independent E robot lacks separate staffing, E is not additive. If the buyer can observe a service delay and revise bids, the original-order kernel is endogenous. These are concrete falsifiers, not reasons to revert to unconstrained sums.

(4) The expected market and learning value of a source trajectory depends on its **conditional** target-production effects. Source production may create the only relevant data to qualify C but consume a unique A sale and block the worker. Grounding may unlock preexisting, licensed data but shift target C demand into already crowded buyer windows. Embodiment investment may relax scarce robot-time while preserving customer demand constraints. This defines distinct economic reasons to choose P/G/E at equal qualification percentages. External transfer benchmarks cannot be multiplied by customer labor replacement wages to obtain contribution.

**Closest methodological foundations:** the two-event overlap identity, Fréchet bounds, potential outcomes with missing action support, inventory-constrained dynamic control, nonrectangular robust decision and Bayesian value of information are established mathematics. DDA §5 already incorporates valuable future data and its Appendix B discusses complementary data. The present work is an integrated *application and counterexample*, with novelty relative to specific robotics production/learning evidence still unestablished.
