# Learning packet — from target technical transfer to defensible network production

**Student edition | 18 November 2026.** All amounts are synthetic normalized captured contribution. Do not equate any number with wages, benchmark accuracy or observed commercial economics. Use [the service-loss chapter](AUDITABLE_ORDER_LOSS_BOUNDS.md) after trying the problems. Separate [instructor solutions](AUDITABLE_ORDER_LOSS_SOLUTIONS.md) must not be included in a student-only handout.

1. **The right unit.** A hotel delivery requires reliable grasping *and* navigating to an occupied room before a customer accepts the service. A held-out benchmark improves grasp success 20%. Explain why neither an extra 20% in revenue nor a positive additional service receipt follows. State the qualification and demand variables you would measure.
2. **Top-k bound.** Baseline B orders have contribution margins \((1.2,0.6,0.2)\). A grounding policy adds 2.4 accepted A contribution and costs 0.4, but may make at most one B order disappear. Derive the sharp worst-case complete-policy gain. What changes if the highest-margin B order is independently guaranteed preserved?
3. **Loss-price dual.** For B margins \(b=(1.2,0.6,0.2)\), suppose two independently certified disruption constraints are \(\ell_1+\ell_2\leq1\), \(\ell_2+\ell_3\leq1\), with \(0\leq\ell_j\leq1\). Calculate the largest plausible loss and exhibit a dual \((\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\mu_1,\mu_2,\mu_3)\) certifying it.
4. **A real alternative.** A robustly feasible specialist adds A contribution 1, costs 0.1 and leaves B intact. Compare its guaranteed whole-policy gain against routine/source data (A +1.1, cost 0.3, no B losses), shared grounding (problem 2) and a pinned grounding variant (A +2.4, cost 0.6, no B losses). Explain why preserving A +2.4 under isolation is an additional technical hypothesis.
5. **Joint law or marginals?** Two B orders have margins 1.2 and 0.6. Each is lost with probability one half. Compare (i) exactly one is always lost and (ii) both or neither are lost with equal probability. Show that expected *additive* lost contribution is identical. Now suppose both recoveries simultaneously require two operators but capacity is one; explain the different safety conclusions.
6. **Reject an unjustified certificate.** One dispatcher configuration allows at most one task preemption at a time, and a researcher asserts that at most one B order can be lost over a year. Give a causal counterexample with no more than one simultaneous preemption and formulate a minimal audit or residual bound needed before using the claim in a policy comparison.
