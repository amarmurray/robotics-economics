# Instructor solutions: coupled learning and production

**1.** Define z=Pr(VZ). Two jobs prevent any A service only when V=Z=1, giving \(Pr(A\text{ paid})=1-z\) with Fréchet \(\max(0,a+b-1)\le z\le\min(a,b)\). Extreme common laws align repair flags maximally or anticorrelate them within their actual marginal constraints. The separate source arm laws do not identify z.

**2.** \(J_{GK}=(m+\beta\gamma n)(1-z)\), derivative \(-m-\beta\gamma n\). The buyer continuation is already a component of the optimized production-value recursion; pricing identical buyer renewal a second time as a 'data premium' duplicates value.

**3.** Fixed rule: process K iff V=0, retain G when V=1. Expected value \(5(1-1/2)+3/2=4\), any coupling. With z known, conditional on V=1, K earns \(5(1-2z)\) and G only earns 3, so choose K iff z<1/5; value \(\max(4,5-5z)\). It is invalid to implement this threshold using only separate arm histories if z is unknown.

**4.** \(J_{GK}=5(1-z)\); GK beats E=17/4 when \(z<3/20\). Endpoints z=0,1/2 give GK 5 and 5/2. If fraction x receives GK, regrets are \(3(1-x)/4\) and \(7x/4\); x=3/10 balances them at 21/40. Assumed E qualification, buyer agreement, worker independence, rights, cost and coupling class have no field validation.

**5.** With three slots, up to two validation jobs leave an A-service slot; the contemporaneous queue-overlap loss vanishes. If B demand is independent of A, its contribution does not automatically multiply A's acceptance probability and the stated derivative overstates it. When GK changes either job flag, the two isolated arms cannot transport V,Z to GK; rights-permitted whole-regime joint job/customer observations and suitable sequential support are needed.
