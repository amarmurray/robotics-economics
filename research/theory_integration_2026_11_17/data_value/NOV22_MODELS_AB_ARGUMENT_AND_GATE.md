## One mechanism, two analytical layers

**Model A: signed qualified production.** Fix learner checkpoint, embodiment(s), original offered-order cohort, customer/competitor willingness to pay, rights and operator/robot capacity. Each feasible complete-service recipe is a column that consumes robot/operator time, access and verification. Production/dispatch optimization uses realized demand, available qualified recipes, fallback, payment terms and resource constraints. Formally, if \(q\) is a vector of jointly qualified recipe states and \(d\) original buyer requests, \(F_{e,d,N}(q)=\max_{\pi\in\Pi_{\rm signed}(q,e,d,N)} C(\pi)\), where \(C\) includes accepted/settled contributions and rejected/refunded orders and counts outside-slot displacement once. Existing LP resource-price certificates, O-ring service bundles, process flexibility and joint qualification partial ID are preserved. No dual shadow price is treated as an actual competing paid receipt. The preceding report-valuation identification results apply to a fixed learner and fixed physically supported action menu only.

**Model B: endogenously changing capability and feasible production.** Let \(S\) acquire/allow new licensed and lineage-identifiable source episodes, \(G_t\) train on or align an existing archive with target evidence, \(G_o\) provide a new *predecision report* for an unchanged trained learner, and \(E\) alter embodiment/manufacturing and feasible services. The common program optimizes
\[
J(z)=\max_{i\in\mathcal I(z)}[-K_i+\mathbb E\{C_i(z,\xi)+\beta J(z'_i)\}],
\]
where \(z\) includes qualified policies, rights and data mixtures, operator access, buyer demand, upstream manufacturing, and next-round production-generated data. The source-to-qualification map generates a *joint* distribution, not only per-task accuracy. Current and future paid production are valued exactly once. Past R checkpoints and legal controllers are retained where feasible; free optional data access weakly expands the choice set. G-observe's Blackwell value cannot be bolted on as a second investment premium if the complete B policy already chooses/observes that information.

## A compact rigorous bridge

With two complete-service qualifications \(Q_A,Q_B\), four signed optimized after-certification production payoffs \(f_{00},f_{10},f_{01},f_{11}\) admit an exact expansion
\[
\mathbb EF=r+a p_A+b p_B+\kappa h,\quad h=\Pr(Q_A=Q_B=1).
\]
The source–grounding interaction at a fixed demand/resource/embodiment table is
\[
\Delta_{S,G}\mathbb EF=a\Delta p_A+b\Delta p_B+\kappa\Delta h.
\]
Here \(\kappa=f_{11}-f_{10}-f_{01}+f_{00}\) can be positive for an end-to-end order or negative when one robot/operator slot makes qualified recipes substitutes. Fréchet lower/upper limits on \(h\) produce **sharp conditional bounds** if no stronger cross-arm constraints exist. Whole-fleet weight sharing, intended old-checkpoint option preservation, correlated interventions and rights can create stronger *cross-arm* restrictions; they must be justified, not assumed. This is standard expectation algebra and partial identification applied to customer production, not a proof of scientific novelty.

The paired illustrative world in the source chapter has the *same* marginal qualifications under R/S/G/SG but two admissible SG joint qualification rates; at a fixed hypothetical bundled customer contribution, **SG** is best with \(h_{SG}=1/2\) and **E** best with \(h_{SG}=1/5\). The precise crossing at \(13/40\) is *endogenous to the stipulated customer production/capital table*, not a universal learning scaling law. In another valid customer market with one scarce slot, the same joint learning response changes sign economically. This gives one decisive way to design empirical validation without mapping benchmark-percent gains to original-customer currency.
