# Student problems — economic data value with network interference

18 November 2026. Work solely from conditional assumptions, not field estimates. Companion to [NETWORK_INTERFERENCE_THEORY.md](NETWORK_INTERFERENCE_THEORY.md). Teacher answers are **separate** in [NETWORK_INTERFERENCE_SOLUTIONS.md](NETWORK_INTERFERENCE_SOLUTIONS.md).

**Problem 1 (safe selective deployment and falsification).** In two pre-treatment customer strata A and B, old qualified service yields 7 normalized captured units per customer. Shares are 0.7 and 0.3. Matched approved source-learning at A adds 0.5 to A's contribution. When this A policy is deployed in the *connected* market/controller network, B's net contribution is 7-s, and network coordination costs c=0.08. Derive total value and the strict worst-case dominance condition if s in [-0.2,1.1]. Compute the lower and upper differences versus 7. Explain why ignoring B orders cannot validate the conclusion.

**Problem 2 (identification with interference).** Two *independent connected network units* are randomly assigned complete approved policy vector a, with probabilities p_1=.2 and p_2=.8. Their fixed complete potential net customer contributions under a are 10 and 12. Construct the Horvitz–Thompson estimator for the finite population mean. Enumerate the two possible assignments for each network unit and prove its expectation is 11. Explain why independent robot assignments inside a common controller or operator network are not the same design. Can this experiment identify each network's joint individual payoff difference between a and b?

**Problem 3 (operator tail and coupling).** Two robot sites each need one operator with probability 0.1 under a target policy. Capacity is one operator at an instant. Construct two joint distributions with these exact marginals, one with positive probability of two simultaneous recoveries and one where simultaneity is impossible. Are expected operator hours sufficient to certify the two-site policy? Under what additional physical timing or abort assumption might a conservative sum-of-site maxima falsely rule out an otherwise feasible route?

**Problem 4 (attribution and source experiment).** A source-data deployment changes downstream B-site economic contribution by -0.6. One hypothesis is pure controller negative transfer -0.6 with no order shift. Another is better held-out qualification +0.2 but lost downstream opportunity -0.8. Show why observed total contribution does not distinguish them. Design the minimum matched source/rights/compute, checkpoint isolation and demand-measurement intervention to distinguish the mechanisms, without assuming benchmark percentage translates to financial margin.

**Problem 5 (sequential support).** Let two binary approved whole-network actions a0, a1 occur over two periods. Behavior assigns a0 with P=1/2 and then a1=1 with probability 1/4 if a0=0 and 3/4 if a0=1. The target assigns a0 uniformly then chooses a1=1-a0. Terminal *nonoverlapping* accepted-service contribution is Y=7+0.5a0-0.6a1+0.9a0a1. Derive the behavior history probabilities, likelihood ratios for histories used by target, and target expected contribution both directly and by importance weighting. Explain what changes if behavior assigns zero probability to a1=0 after a0=1. Identify the assumption needed for the target and behavior policy to share a conditional outcome law.
