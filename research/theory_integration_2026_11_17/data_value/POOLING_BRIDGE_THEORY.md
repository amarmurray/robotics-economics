# Isolated experiments versus pooled fleet value: a source-transfer bridge

**21 November 2026 — same *When Capabilities Become Production* Nov22 Models A/B (scientific gate OPEN).** Extends [full original-buyer program exposure](WHOLE_PROGRAM_EXPOSURE_PAID_VALUE.md), not a new research question. The technical question is whether severing source-model, operator and customer links to create statistically independent SG/E experimental units identifies the **fully pooled** deployed economic value. It does not without a scale- and network-specific bridge. These are applications of established interference and transport principles, not independent scientific novelty.

## 1. Common production environment

Let a∈{SG,E} be a complete physically authorized investment program: SG combines additional licensed heterogeneous source data and target-specific grounding, E changes robotic embodiment and feasible production recipes. Old R/specialist fallback remains selectable when approved. Let b∈{cut,pool} specify predeclared full-horizon data/checkpoint/replay, fleet/operator, manufacturing, buyer procurement and settlement links. For original eligible buyers i in the same size-N target market (including orders never accepted), define J_a^b(N) as expected **optimized, qualified, accepted and finally paid** network contribution net of all applicable capital, rights, compute, curation, access and operator costs **once**. Production-created licensed data affects future capabilities and future customer payments within J, not an extra dataset premium. Buyer demand, prices, displaced outside orders and negative settlements may all be endogenous to a and b. Field successes, failures/recoveries, teleoperation, simulation, synthetic and public/pretrained episodes have distinct provenance and rights.

Put Δ_pool(N)=J_SG^pool(N)−J_E^pool(N), Δ_cut(N)=J_SG^cut(N)−J_E^cut(N) and g_a(N)=J_a^pool(N)−J_a^cut(N). The **exact sharing bridge** is

    Δ_pool(N)=Δ_cut(N)+g_SG(N)−g_E(N).                        (1)

This is an accounting identity, not a learning scaling law. Estimating the cut contrast cannot identify the pooled investment ranking. With a simultaneous valid confidence interval Δ_cut∈[d−r,d+r] and defended program gain restrictions g_SG∈[l_S,u_S], g_E∈[l_E,u_E], one has

    Δ_pool∈[d−r+l_S−u_E, d+r+u_S−l_E].                    (2)

Proof: substitute the interval extrema in (1). With a **rectangular joint feasible set**, endpoints are attainable; common resource/buyer/state constraints can shrink the set, so without rectangularity this is only an outer bound. If r is a deterministic identification width instead of statistical uncertainty, do not assign a coverage interpretation. If gain restrictions or the cut interval have nontrivial sampling uncertainty, construct a valid joint coverage event first. SG is economically certified only if the lower end is strictly positive, and E only if the upper end is negative.

**Synthetic examples.** Cut contributions 4 and 3 imply Δ_cut=1. Free optional own-program gains g_SG,g_E∈[0,3] permit Δ_pool anywhere in [−2,4], achieved at (0,3) and (3,0): both investments individually benefit (weakly) from information, yet their ranking reverses. Conversely a stipulated cut contrast d=4,r=1/4 and gain ranges g_SG∈[1/2,2], g_E∈[0,1] give [13/4,25/4]. These are hypothetical contribution units and unverified bound assumptions, never benchmark-to-dollar estimates.

**Optionality versus forced pooling.** If pool means costless *optional licensed* information, the preexisting safe controller/production menu remains feasible, all other customer/physical environments and rights are fixed, and the optimizer can decline to use the data, then optimized gross g_a≥0 by feasible-set inclusion. This does **not** imply g_SG≥g_E or Δ_pool≥Δ_cut. Forced checkpoint retraining, costly pooling, operator reallocation, licensing restrictions and externality-induced buyer changes can break nesting and produce negative g_a. An observed negative transfer under one training recipe is not proof that an optimally optional free dataset has negative value.

## 2. Scale is an additional transport unknown

Complete independent islands of size m reveal J_a^cut(m), possibly J_a^pool(m) if within-island pooling is legal. They do not reveal the N-market cut and pooled outcomes when network effects or buyer compositions differ. The exact scale decomposition is

    Δ_pool(N)=Δ_cut(m)+[Δ_cut(N)−Δ_cut(m)]
                          +g_SG(N)−g_E(N).              (3)

Nothing makes either bracket zero. Source complementarities across islands, learned new embodiments, target-grounding reuse, manufacturer scale and customer substitution may all emerge only at N.

**Constructive non-identification counterworld.** Each single-site island contains one licensed source/task type, has a valid old controller and yields normalized accepted-paid original-buyer contribution 2 under both programs with or without *within-island* sharing. The full two-site N-market instead has complementary source types A and B, whose joint training availability yields one additional qualified, accepted and paid contribution **per buyer** for a compatible program. World W_S makes only SG compatible, yielding pooled (SG,E)=(3,2) per buyer and Δ_pool=+1. World W_E makes only E compatible, yielding (2,3) and Δ_pool=−1. Every one-island SG/E×cut/pool observation, customer price, single-source technical marginal and available old policy is identical across these worlds. Both programs' own pooling gains are nonnegative; neither counterworld relies on harmful data. Arbitrarily many independent size-one islands cannot identify the untested **cross-source training mechanism** or the target whole-market ranking. A bridge must observe or validly restrict joint A+B learning at scale, plus qualified dispatch/acceptance/payment.

Likewise marginal task qualification probabilities are inadequate for bundled paid work. For each of two tasks with marginal 1/2, perfectly positive versus perfectly negative within-program dependence gives 4 versus 0 expected contribution for an 8-unit service paid only when both qualify. No task benchmark percentage maps mechanically to buyer dollars. Interactions with scarce operator slots can reverse the economic complement without changing technical marginals.

## 3. Auditable, strong edge-cut transport bound

Let G be the **pre-treatment, full-horizon directed causal influence graph** of authorized source episodes, retained replay, model/checkpoint updates, robotic embodiments, operator/dispatch pools, original buyer orders/substitutions, settlement and future productive data. Cut a set C of edges to separate experimental islands. For every physically and legally feasible intermediate graph T and each e∈C, assume a uniform **whole-network** sensitivity restriction

    |J_a(T∪{e})−J_a(T)|≤L_(a,e).

Then telescoping edge additions gives |g_a|≤B_a:=Σ_(e∈C)L_(a,e), and |Δ_pool−Δ_cut|≤B_SG+B_E. Neither statistical independence of robot attempts nor additive positive transfer is required; **the total reoptimized downstream buyer contribution** is bounded at each step. Proof: enumerate edges e1,...,ek, insert into T sequentially, use the triangle inequality; apply (1). This general bound need not be sharp with common buyer/resource constraints or licensed update dependencies; intersect with genuine joint feasibility restrictions rather than claiming sharpness.

**Critical counterexample to local edge accounting.** With synthetic F(x,y)=2+3x+5y+7xy, the maximum incremental effect of edge x over other-edge states is 10, and of y is 12; F(1,1)−F(0,0)=15≤22. Pricing both at their empty-graph increments 3 and 5 wrongly claims 8 as an upper bound, losing the 7-unit learning/qualification complement. More seriously, a *single* permitted source-to-shared-checkpoint edge can change one downstream paid unit for **each** of N original customers; at N=20, the global effect is 20, not the one local source effect. The L bound must include all recursive downstream updates, demand and physical resource reallocation. When no defensible finite bound on cascading payment consequences exists, (2) is not decision-informative. Mere graph logging does not measure L. A change of market exogenous distribution/rights across graph modes requires a separate transport assumption; the edge algebra alone cannot supply it.

## 4. How legitimate evidence could resolve the comparison

If multiple physically and legally independent **whole-market** replicas at the target scale exist, randomization of four complete regimes SG/E × cut/pool with complete original-buyer paid-order ledgers can identify the four mean J values (given valid assignment and outcome support), the source-program interaction and Δ_pool directly. Within-site random assignments under a globally reused learner are **not** independent replicas. If only small islands are available, prospectively compare multiple supported source-type compositions and island sizes; a discontinuity when mixed A+B data become available falsifies a constant size-transport assumption. A lawful narrow intervention enabling one cross-source learner edge may inform its effect for represented contexts but does not justify the missing global sensitivity bound without tested upper controls. A physically unavailable E or unsafe shared-controller regime remains unidentified; do not turn missing intervention support into an estimated zero.

**One economic implication:** experimental isolation is itself a production architecture change. It can reduce spillover learning and dynamic diffusion, while improving causal support. The correct research object jointly optimizes *the information gained by comparison* and the *foregone qualified paid-service options* during isolation; the latter must be measured once within market production, not added as an artificial 'data value'. This fits the broader robot-fleet, multi-market, manufacturing, demand and data-rights roadmap.
