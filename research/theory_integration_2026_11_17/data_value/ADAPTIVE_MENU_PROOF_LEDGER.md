# Proof / falsification ledger: adaptive qualification menu value

**Prior:** [static joint-qualification factorial ledger](JOINT_QUALIFICATION_FACTORIAL_PROOFS.md). All proofs here are conditional standard finite probability/decision constructions.

1. **Arm mean versus coupling.** On independent approved policy-closed networks with consistent potential outcomes and randomized positive assignment, E[Y(z)] is identified. No individual network reveals two counterfactual potential outcomes just through arm randomization, so the cross-arm joint Q_x,Q_g and any after-source adaptive branch remain unidentified. Four fixed arm means (and I) can be identical across distinct cross-arm couplings.

2. **Fréchet endpoints and sharpness.** Let P11=t, P10=p-t, P01=q-t, P00=1-p-q+t. Nonnegative entries yield t∈[max(0,p+q-1),min(p,q)]. Every feasible t defines a coupling. Enumerating payoffs (s,a,b,max(a,b)) gives V_both=s+(a-s)p+(b-s)q-(min(a,b)-s)t; the endpoints are attainable and sharp absent further joint constraints. If extra resource/history constraints are present, intersect feasible couplings before claiming sharpness.

3. **Staging.** With a≥b and no source→g carryover, x testing costs c_x. After x fails, g's expected incremental contribution is (b-s)(q-t)/(1-p)-c_g; choose if positive, times Pr(x fails)=1-p. The result is s+(a-s)p-c_x+max[0,(b-s)(q-t)-c_g(1-p)]. If p=1, the branch vanishes. If b>a, a second test following x success can be profitable, hence the one-branch expression is not general. With endogenous transfer replace static conditional overlap by independently specified r=P(Q_{g|x}=1|Q_x=0); static trials do not identify r.

4. **Identical static-arm counterworld.** p=q=1/2, t=0 versus 1/2 gives the same Q marginals (and can be embedded in fixed independent 00/11 outcome distributions), yet staged values 37/10 vs 14/5. Source-alone value is 14/5 in both. The optimal acquisition decision is therefore not determined by a static factorial causal effect.

5. **Triple parity.** For even/odd parity supports 000/011/101/110 and 001/010/100/111, all singleton probabilities are 1/2 and pairwise co-pass 1/4. Probability of any success is 3/4 vs 1. With s=2 and a=b=h=4 and costs sum to 3/5, net menus are 29/10 and 17/5. Pairwise technical measurements alone cannot identify this optional three-policy portfolio.

6. **Accounting and physical boundaries.** Menu expressions require ability to test each alternative without destroying retained controller availability or perturbing qualification, original B orders, resources or demand. A nonapproved policy is not in the menu; paid tests, customer-clock delay and embodied change must be charged once. Whole-service value includes continuation learning where already modeled. Free *optional availability* enlarges a feasible policy set but does not ensure a profitable *paid training rollout*.

7. **New discriminating gap.** Can versioned, physically approved paired qualification and sequential after-failure testing establish cross-arm t or genuine transition r, plus captured full-network contributions under the same eligible orders? Until then the conditional formulas are mathematical outer bounds, not robot deployment estimates. The result does not establish a first-paper scientific novelty claim.
