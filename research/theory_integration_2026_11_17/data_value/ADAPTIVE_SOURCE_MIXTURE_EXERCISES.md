# Exercises — adaptive source mixtures and qualification topology

23 November 2026 · Student version. Work within the same accepted-customer-production model, not abstract accuracy percentage conversion. Define all resources and information times.

1. **Interaction proof.** With F,T binary and fixed marginals p,q, derive the feasible range of x=Pr(F=T=1). Write E[w(F,T)] for an arbitrary table w00,w01,w10,w11. Give the sign of the derivative with respect to x for an OR qualified service and an AND qualified service. Explain why neither sign implies free optional data have negative optimized value.

2. **Backward induction with costs.** F is observed before T can be acquired. T passes a matched target test with conditional probability Pr(T=1|F=i). Derive the optional acquisition rule with gain Li=w_i1-w_i0 and cost ci. State which terms change if failed acquisition causes a recovery job or misses the customer deadline.

3. **Exact synthetic laws.** For 20 equally weighted atoms construct all laws with F and T marginal successes 10/20 and joint successes k/20, k=0..10. Prove that the committed O-audit-if-F0 and A-audit-if-F1 payoffs in the chapter are (19-k)/20 and (3k-1)/20. Verify the k=0,5,10 known-law choices against independently staffed E=3/4.

4. **Information feasible versus oracle.** Show that a three-quarter O / one-quarter A randomized allocation across separately eligible clusters, with the corresponding audits precommitted, earns 7/10 in expectation for every common k. Explain why a controller cannot simply choose max{VO*(k),VA*(k)} without a lawful advance signal, and why the E program wins a maximin comparison in the stipulated economy.

5. **Identification design.** Suppose historical O source policy acquired T only when F=0. Does this observation identify the causal T qualification rate for F=1? What additional assumptions or randomized interventions make the AND investment evaluable? Give an example where an audit of an original buyer's no-order record changes an economic estimate without changing a single technical model score.
