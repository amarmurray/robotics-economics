# Prospective lawful observation selection: SG paired paid production versus E arm

**21 November 2026. Design only—no research participant, customer, physical trial or approval obtained.** Companion to [decision-sufficient evidence theory](DECISION_SUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE_AND_PROGRAM_CHOICE.md) and [whole-program identification hierarchy](PROGRAM_VALUE_IDENTIFICATION_HIERARCHY.md).

**Predecision risk set:** predeclare original eligible buyer offers including rejected, diverted and canceled orders; site, product, price/terms, buyer parent, market/time, signed complete recipe, operator capacity, outside-order opportunities and horizon. The randomization unit must close over robot/fleet, shared learner weights/data updates, supervisor queues, procurement exposure and displaced customer orders, rather than task episodes. Record preassignment W and do not condition on postassignment buyer uptake.

**Distinct evidence tiers:**

1. Source provenance and qualification only: separate newly admitted source S (public, synthetic, simulation, field success, failure/recovery, teleoperation; family lineage and reuse rights), target G-train and target G-observe, SG accepted updates and retained R fallback. Pair matched qualified complete-service A/B at the same target; capture joint Q, not merely marginals. *This alone cannot be treated as paid bundle completion.*
2. The decisive SG within-arm original-buyer joint row: for **every eligible offer**, join trained checkpoint, fully signed recipe, task A/B qualification, actual buyer demand/acceptance, service attempt, operator availability and displaced outside work, interruption/refund and settled net contribution. Follow to the horizon; record nonbuyers and impossible services as outcomes. This identifies k or, when more complicated than 12k, directly estimates the full program net Y_SG−K_SG.
3. Whole-E arm: only if embodiment access, manufacturing lead-time, ownership/rights, controller and operator signoff permit; measure the same complete original-market risk set, cost amortization, service quality and paid settlement, not vendor claims. R/S/G arms remain explicit alternatives. If E absent, keep it an unsupported conditional comparator and do not declare a supported winner between SG and E.
4. Dynamic production-created trajectories: record originating customer order and episode, teleop/failure/recovery modality, selection, legal reuse, training admission/randomized downstream learner choice, changed qualification and subsequent paid original buyer service. Evaluate total continuation once, no added “data dividend”; impose sequential support and interference closure.

**Decision discrimination:** estimate the joint success-and-paid fraction (or the appropriate accepted-customer net value) and its uncertainty interval, and compare with actual supported E net value. In the toy fixed-customer case a=12k−1, E=b: SG is uniformly preferred when the lower justified k bound exceeds (b_U+1)/12; E uniformly preferred when upper justified k bound is below (b_L+1)/12. Otherwise retain overlap. The identifying row requires k, not qualification h alone. For nonrectangular joint demand/resource restrictions, use a joint identified set; for nonparametric market shifts record preassignment W and reweight only on actual support. A single observational before/after comparison without exchangeability is not causal evidence. No finite sample is an exact oracle; report uncertainty and power before operational decisions.

**Mechanism versus program:** whole-arm randomization identifies S/G/SG/E *program* effects on net service, including endogenous procurement and competition. It does not identify the direct source-data mechanism, which requires separately justified training/admission interventions with comparable update compute, data-lineage rights and unchanged physical/safety envelope. Source availability is an option, forced training may cause negative transfer. Never map task benchmark improvement directly to gross merchandise, margin or paid contribution.

**Official production-network route verified as documentation only (21 November 2026):** [BEA Input-Output application](https://www.bea.gov/itable/input-output), [BEA table guide](https://www.bea.gov/resources/guide-interactive-industry-input-output-accounts-tables), and [BEA InputOutput API](https://apps.bea.gov/api/) expose use/make/requirements schemas. Use *industry purchases* from actual retrieved and vintage-matched use tables before building any quantitative buyer–supplier edge; requirements coefficients measure direct/indirect supply-chain dependence and are not robotics deployment rates. No official numerical transaction coefficient was retrieved, verified, or fabricated in this tranche. Preserve customer–operator/integrator–robot manufacturer–data/compute supplier as distinct typed atlas edges, with cost, ownership and rights on their proper nodes.
