# Instructor solutions: causal program-dependent data value

1. On X=1 source T pays r today, then late qualified P service is possible only if Jp=0. G on X=1 has no paid immediate service and may consume recovery capacity; if free, it serves the higher-valued target when QG DG=1 and otherwise the old buyer Z **in the same slot**. X=0 retains R. Net costs appear once and continuation is already paid service.
2. Fix q,d. Summing masses over f yields (1/4-t)+t=1/4, hence each pair is fair independent. Single marginals are 1/2. All G parity-even states get 1/4 at t=0, odd states zero; the support reverses at t=1/4. The full 111 event is odd and has mass t. Pairwise reports cannot recover it.
3. Old \(E[Z]=1/2\). Source \(E[(1-X)Z]=1/4\), immediate \(rE[X]=1/4\), late source \(E[X(1-Jp)]=1/4\): P=3/4. G \(1/4+(1/2)[(9/2)t+(1/2)(1/2-t)]-1/16=5/16+2t\). Optional G is max of this and 1/2. Since P dominates E=23/32 and R, G wins only for t>7/32, tying at equality. G action-specific target-demand bit is independent of old Z under the synthetic construction but is not equal to it; observed Z cannot replace the potential outcome.
4. P's 3/4 payoff is law-invariant, while G calibrate's worst payoff is 5/16 and E=23/32, R=1/2. P is maximin. Any E or R weight is dominated by P in every t; for G probability λ, maximum oracle regret is max{7λ/16,(1−λ)/16}. Equate to obtain λ=1/8, regret 7/128. Pure P worst regret 1/16. This is ex-ante randomization; no action is conditioned on the hidden t.
5. Both common laws have the same product distribution for X,Z,Jp, so all old policy selected/full reports (measurable functions of those variables) have the same law. All stipulated G pairwise moments also agree. A supported matched G calibration cluster with pre-choice eligibility, full original buyer target offers/no-offers, QG, FG, job arrivals, paid accepted recipe, rights and inclusion propensities would identify the linked overlap under valid exchangeability/positivity. Zero G assignment supplies none of those causal outcomes. If offline G calibration creates no worker jobs, FG=1 is a different physically justified restriction and the original bounds change.
6. Enlarging an admissible action or data set while retaining old feasible policies cannot lower a maximum over policy values. Its gain may be zero if no new qualified customer production is reachable. Forced training or extra costs/rights restrictions can shrink net value, so no monotonicity claim applies to those different problems.
