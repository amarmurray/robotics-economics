# Student exercises — shared procurement budgets and economic data value
**Separate from instructor solutions. Conditional synthetic numbers only.**

1. In a qualified two-division market, each division will pay at most6, both request jointly with probability q, and one parent has common budget B∈{7,8}. Menus may quote price4,5 or6, and no buyer pays for more than their parent can afford. X has one safely qualified operator slot, G has two and fixed cost1/2. Derive each policy's best expected net contribution conditional on B. For q=1/2 compare X/G/E (new second slot cost2)/R (independent verified contribution2). Explain why benchmark skill count does not rank investments.
2. Let q=Pr(H), b=Pr(B8), t=Pr(H,B8). Starting from the complete four-cell state table, derive necessary and sufficient joint-probability bounds on t. Find G−X for (a) budget observed before quote with permitted contingent pricing and (b) one uniform precommitted quote. Find their crossing thresholds at q=1/2.
3. With q=1/2 and t=3/10, calculate the gross G-specific benefit of timely parent-budget information and the incremental value of that information to the best investment across X,G,E,R. Why do these differ? Where should paid audit costs enter?
4. A researcher independently observes exact full-policy mean economic values E V_X=5 and E V_G=4 in the SAME eligible population, but no matched cross-policy potential outcomes. Is their average comparison unidentified? Would knowing only customer demand marginals and operator slots suffice to identify each full-policy mean? Give the distinct reasons.
5. Derive an upper certificate for the integer original buyer service allocation using dual prices for operator slots, parent budget and one-sale-per-group constraints. State when the certificate fails to be an achievable lower guarantee.
