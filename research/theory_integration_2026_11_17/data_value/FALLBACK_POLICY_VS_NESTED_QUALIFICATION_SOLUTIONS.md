**Keep separate from the student file.**

1. If Pi_R subset Pi_new and payoff/world/resources are identical, sup over the larger class is at least sup over the smaller. After a compulsory nonrefundable cost K, net comparison loses K; if law/rights/capacity shift, the same objective is not being optimized.
2. Both X policies qualify 3/4 and R 1/2. X_N loses no R success and its OR success is 3/4. X_C loses A (1/4), gains C and D (1/2), and its hypothetical OR is 1. Neither separate arm marginal identifies the OR or supplies its physical resources. Choosing global R gives 1/2, global X 3/4.
3. Lower loss max(0,1/2-5/8)=0; upper loss min(1/2,3/4-5/8)=1/8. The zero-loss claim needs joint restriction, not separate averages.
4. By conditional independence, E[BQ_T|z] = (1-t_z)h_Rz+t_z h_Xz, so gain sum p_z t_z(h_Xz-h_Rz). Casewise Q_T>=Q_R requires t_z Pr(Q_R=1,Q_X=0|z)=0 on every supported z; a positive average can coexist with individual losses.
5. A's weighted net contribution is 3/4 and worst-case regression 1/8; B's are 1/2 and 1/8. Highest benefit/risk is A, so set t_A=1/2, t_B=0 for regression 1/16 and net gain 3/8 (before any fixed acquisition K). This is only a statistical expected-harm allowance with strong independence/support assumptions, not a controller safety signoff.
