# Matched validation for robust data-value policy choice

**Prepared design, 18 November 2026; no robot runs, no customer access or new permissions.**

**Unit and comparator.** Same qualified baseline controller/body, site × end-to-end accepted-service bundle × customer demand/price/contract window. Compare routine production with retained byproduct data; deliberate teleoperation or safe target-grounding diagnostic; redesigned embodiment; fully qualified specialist or retained old policy. Count current accepted output, first-action opportunity cost, compute/curation, rights, downtime, net held-out customer value and time-to-acceptance exactly once via the production envelope. Keep operator and safety resources equal and *pathwise*, not average.

**Identification order.** First establish operator-approved terminal actions, zero-correction exposure denominator, independent gate audit, recovery tail upper support/reserves and fallback menu. Second randomize or defensibly match full customer qualification **pairs** (R vs E), recording per-recipe 00/10/01/11 and a confidence interval for the *paired economic difference* \(q_E-q_R\) on the same service window. Include common failures and selection effects; a site-level parallel design needs exchangeability/positivity. Third independently challenge a target diagnostic on labelled transfer regimes in **both** directions with held-out bodies/sites and compare conditional regime-report confusion, policy-induced exposure and actual follow-on full joint qualification. Non-corrections and unvisited states require explicit denominators.

**Decision thresholds (hypothetical, not calibrated).** For the synthetic marginal-bundle demonstration, one current accepted-unit of R production and incremental accepted-bundle contribution \(\Delta\) make R robustly beat E when a credible matched upper bound on \(q_E-q_R\) is below \(1/\Delta\); the competent specialist must also be compared. For the one-slot diagnostic demonstration, a per-regime lower accuracy bound must exceed \((4+c)/6\), *conditional on verified safe branch costs*. Different tariffs, noncomplementary recipes or customer windows change the envelope and these thresholds. A diagnostic that is already a free optional productive byproduct may have a different opportunity cost; do not classify it as costly acquisition.

**Safe validation bound illustration.** Under independent labelled Bernoulli reports within each of two fixed regimes, n=400 per regime and hypothetical correct fraction 0.8, a union-bounded Hoeffding per-regime lower accuracy bound is about 0.726 with simultaneous confidence >=95%. This calculation has no authority to approve a mismatch recovery branch; bounded independent samples cannot prove an impossible rare state. Real confidence methods should account for site/body clustering, changing regimes, selection, calibration multiplicity and imperfect proxy labels.

**Falsifiers.** A registered paired acceptance difference above the customer-window threshold invalidates the R certificate. An independently audited mismatch using >2.1 operators invalidates D's assumed safe menu. Evidence that productive routine data already identifies the relevant H at lower opportunity cost removes the diagnostic timing gain. If customer demand for joint B,C is absent, the illustrative bundle advantage disappears. If complete transfer regimes change during operation, the fixed-world robust comparison must be redesigned. A source of safe synthetic or simulation probes might replace human recovery in one qualified kernel, but it must be validated against actual accepted service.

**Official quantitative backbone.** The official production/value-chain matrix remains missing. No corporate rollout announcement, unlabeled benchmark, imagined accepted order, or estimated demo price substitutes for quantitative BEA/BLS and matched-site acceptance evidence. No new primary official production source was obtained in this tranche.
