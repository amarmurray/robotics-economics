# Prospective evidence and official-atlas source audit — order-loss support

**18 November 2026 | no customer access or physical safety authorization.** This extends the preceding [policy-closed protocol](POLICY_CLOSED_NETWORKS_EMPIRICAL.md); it does not propose unsafe assignments.

## Minimum real-world fields before claiming a bound

1. **Immutable opportunity census**: customer/market IDs and original enrollment, order window and zero-order outcomes; full accepted-order bundle IDs under frozen qualified F; observed net captured contribution per accepted *whole-service* order; order/value revisions, lost opportunities and counterfactual baseline dispatch assumptions. The *entire* original order registry must be accounted for, not only new-controller accepted jobs.
2. **Source-to-qualification provenance**: distinct field trajectories, recoveries, teleop, simulator, synthetic and pretrained sources with rights, selection/gating, compute/curation and controller/version lineage; matched held-out **joint** target qualification; safe fallback, specialist and new embodiment variants evaluated on their own merits.
3. **Policy-specific whole-network causal paths**: signed controller checkpoint hashes/broadcast and forced retraining rules, dispatch bidder/adapters/competitors and customer repricing/substitution links, operator reservations including simultaneous recovery peaks, physical admission, and route-induced new demand. Construct the policy-closed graph over *all* candidate policies. A graph cut absent in baseline logs is not proof of a future cut.
4. **Per-loss-cap provenance**: each row of \(H\ell\leq d\) requires a *horizon-wide*, verified logical/physical basis: what exact original-order bundles are protected, what path might remove each, whether one initial preemption can cascade, and whether a customer can switch providers after slowdown. Record specific coverage failures. A one-slot dispatcher setting does not certify a one-order lost-service limit.
5. **Customer and price residual**: any induced demand, order churn, new competitor orders, price changes or nonadditive human/overtime costs excluded from the direct B-loss sum must be bounded in \(e_p\). If not, the certificate is explicitly **unavailable**, not zero residual.

## What is identified, and what would falsify it

A protected set of baseline orders can yield loss bounds even if one connected market prevents independent complete-policy randomization, *but only with credible system restrictions*. Observed positive A service under G and a single dispatch event do **not** establish the certificate. Verify candidate A under candidate-specific isolation and training (the alternative might alter transfer); measure accepted orders and operator load on the same original-window registry. If the B high-value job can be displaced under G despite claimed protection, reject the corresponding H row immediately. A competent specialist and retained F set real outside options, not weakened controls.

Use an additive order-accounting diagnostic: if net contribution really equals the sum of completed service margins, marginal loss rates identify **mean additive loss** even when loss dependence is unknown. Retain full joint qualification and recovery measurement wherever complete customer bundles, common capacity, or nonadditive costs matter. No pathwise safety inference from mean loss.
