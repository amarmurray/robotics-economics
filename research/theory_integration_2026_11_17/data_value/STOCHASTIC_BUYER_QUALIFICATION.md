# Stochastic buyer choice, qualified second service, and the economic return to grounding
**20 November 2026 UTC | continuation of *When Capabilities Become Production* | conditional theory, not a field estimate**

## Intellectual question
A physically qualified second robotic service is not a purchased second service. The previous parent-budget chapter allowed parents to buy the maximum count of services they could afford; it expressly warned that affordability is not purchase. Here the parent's **choice**, the grounded controller's **joint qualification**, and the **right to dispatch safely** are separated. The decision remains X = more transferable source learning, G = target grounding and operator/recovery reduction, E = new approved embodiment, R = actually retained competent policy. No source type is privileged: field trajectories, recoveries, teleoperation, simulation, synthetic/pretrained/public sources enter a versioned learner and its qualified joint-service outcomes, not directly a dollar price.

## Common production-value object and exact slot tail
Fix original buyer registry, a precommitted policy p and legal offer menu m, the parent's *actual* potential accepted/paid orders (including refusal, outside choice, and its shared budget), versioned qualification, rights and operator constraints. A capacity promise can change buyer choice: denote the random number of **admissible, paid, complete-service orders** under h offered operator slots by N_h, where 0<=N_h<=2. Counting quotes or benchmark passes as N_h would be invalid. Net contribution is J_h=r E[min(N_h,h)]-K_h for uniform captured margin r, provided no other bottleneck and no downstream continuation value is counted twice.

For h=1 versus h=2 (and N_1<=1,N_2<=2),
  J_2-J_1 = r{Pr(N_2>=2)+Pr(N_2>=1)-Pr(N_1>=1)}-(K_2-K_1).
If demand and menu are truly **invariant** to slots, N_h=min(N,h) for a common pre-admission request N, and the formula collapses to r Pr(N>=2)-ΔK. The same tail sum yields r Pr(N>=h+1) for a stable N and uniform r, implying diminishing capacity returns in that restrictive setting. When buyers react to advertised safety, quality, prices, or shared procurement, the *first-purchase response drift* can be negative; the second-slot tail by itself does not measure economic value. With multiple services, contributions, budgets or O-ring bundles, a knapsack/production optimization replaces the simple tail and even discrete capacity concavity can fail.

**Important optionality:** if a larger physical capacity leaves the smaller admission/price policy genuinely available, optimizing operations cannot reduce *gross* value. That fact does not make a paid irreversible investment profitable, or make a forced training recipe monotone. If the incumbent is no longer safely/legalistically feasible, do not insert R as a fallback.

## Complete rational buyer counterworld: same budget and observed high-price purchases
One original parent with two eligible divisions, verified budget B=8, high-demand probability q=1/2, low state with no purchases. High state has quasi-linear willingness-to-pay 7 for the first service and either 3 (world L) or 5 (world H) for the second; both worlds are allowed original-price menus {4,5,6}. The parent maximizes utility including no purchase subject to its budget. This is **not** an assumption it buys everything affordable. A low-price two-unit purchase is chosen only if its marginal second-unit value covers price4; at price6 two units are not affordable. X has one safe slot, G two when qualified at cost 1/2, E an independently safe second body at cost2, and R is a separate verified contribution2; all monetary magnitudes are invented normalized units.

At price6 both worlds produce exactly one sale when high, zero when low; accepted-only records and **the true parent budget** agree. X optimizes price6, J_X=3. In L, G optimizes price6: J_G=5/2, E=1, R=2; X wins. In H, G optimizes price4 and sells both in the high state: J_G=7/2, E=2, R=2; G wins. The only difference is the previously unsupported original buyer's second-unit choice at price4. Both utility types obey stable preferences and budget feasibility. This is a stronger challenge to the old maximal-affordability assumption than changing B alone.

Let a denote conditional probability the parent buys its second original service at price4 in high state, with the first reliably purchased, verified B8, two G slots **qualified** and no response spillover. The supported menus give
  J_X=6q,  J_G=max(6q,4q(1+a))-1/2.
For q=1/2, G exceeds X iff a>3/4; G is no better at a=3/4. Only a lower bound a>3/4 from actual applicable choice support could certify G. A generic "more demand" story or a per-task benchmark improvement cannot.

## More important: buyer willingness and incremental qualification must occur together
Drop the assumption that G's *second slot* is always qualified. Let H denote a real first high-demand order, C a **second actual purchase commitment at price4** within H, and Q a valid joint-robot/operator/rights qualification allowing two accepted services in that same parent-period network state. Let q=Pr(H), c=Pr(C), g=Pr(Q), and s=Pr(C AND Q). The first service always has a competent safe path. At price4 G produces 4(q+s) gross; at price6 it retains its first service and produces 6q gross. Thus
  J_G=max{6q,4(q+s)}-K_G,    J_X=6q,
  J_G-J_X=max{0,4s-2q}-K_G.
No assumption of independence between qualification, budgeted buyer choice or operator availability is used. With separately observed c and g, the **sharp Fréchet support conditional on this response family** is max(0,c+g-1)<=s<=min(c,g). It can be wide; the learner success rate is not an estimate of *paid joint deployment*. This couples heterogeneous source transfer to downstream demand: more transferable data and more target-specific alignment can be economically complementary when they alter s, even if each marginal technical or buyer success is unchanged. This uses standard joint-probability algebra, not a novel general fact.

Exact two-world countermodel with q=1/2,c=2/5,g=1/2,K_G=1/2 and B8: (i) Q and C disjoint, s=0: G=5/2 < X=3; (ii) all C states admitted by Q, s=2/5: G=31/10 > X=3. Both have the *same* buyer and qualification marginals, original budget, safe first service, X/R/E economics (E=8/5, R=2), and legal menus. E's second body does not rely on G's Q but still requires customer purchase. Thus the missing object is matched **joint qualification × original buyer acceptance × captured payment**, not another isolated transfer percentage or data-count metric. If real training changes original buyer population or prices, even this support must be reidentified.

## Partial evidence, decision and data value
An original-buyer price4 intervention with actual safe and legally supported choice/receipts can identify c; an independently measured qualification program identifies g. Without a justified coupling or accepted same-network paired observations, these alone do not yield s. For q=1/2 and K_G=1/2, a lower-bound s>3/8 is necessary to certify G over X in the exact model. If paired validated data establish s>=2/5, G>=31/10 and X=3 (before any additional audit expense), while E<=8/5 and R=2 under the same synthetic physical/customer restrictions. Off-support or shadow *intention* to buy is not itself a paid accepted order.

Under the equal prior on the two utility worlds (second-unit value3 versus5), an optional perfectly informative pre-investment buyer report has whole-policy expected decision value 1/4: blind choose X with value3 (G's prior mean also3), with accurate signal choose X in L and G in H for (3+7/2)/2=13/4. A symmetrically wrong report with error e<=1/2 has gross EVSI (1-2e)/4; subtract its actual data/curation/legal cost ONCE. This is a standard Bayesian value-of-information example, not a price assigned to human data or a separate bonus atop the already optimized production value.

## Alternatives, network support, and broader program
If buyer choice is capacity/policy/menu dependent, never assert the fixed-N positive marginal slot tail. If paid offers cannot be made, no buyer/learning qualification inference follows from informal quotes. If model updates, operators or demand are shared across sites, H,C,Q are **whole-network** response variables; independent site replication is invalid. Valid certified whole-network exposure error epsilon_p can widen each supported net-policy interval, but no epsilon is asserted here. Rights, legal pricing, curation/compute and physical approval bind the policies; do not let financing dominate theory. New embodiments may permit different Q and customer services, not merely another identical slot. Full multilayer demand/manufacturing/coordination/value-chain and empirical BLS–BEA backbone remain in the main roadmap.
