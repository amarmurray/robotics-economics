# Prospective interference-closure and shared-operator identification registry
**Design only | 19 November 2026. No physical intervention, customer access, purchasing or outreach authorized.**

**One unit of economic accounting.** Pre-list all original offered/rejected/unserved customer orders for a fixed site-market cohort and horizon, including competent alternative, negotiated or measured *captured* contribution, labor/recovery hours, displacements, failed attempted orders, and policy-induced new demand. A controlled source training arm X, targeted grounding G, independent nonactuating report A, and physically qualified second embodiment/slot E are *distinct treatments*. Never treat raw source-data acquisition as identical to controller retraining. Every accepted service must satisfy its full recipe, not a task benchmark. Keep versioned field, failure/recovery, teleoperation, simulation, synthetic and public/pretrained source streams and permissions separate.

**Pre-policy interference graph.** Vertices: site, customer, work cell, controller version, model-training job, dispatch queue, recovery/teleoperation human, robot body, physical battery/tool and service contract. Mark directed edges for pooled operators, live learner updates, transferred source data, product substitutes or buyer referral, shared price/capacity, and cross-site recovery. Register potential *time-lagged* edges: a failure or source-derived gradient on date 0 may alter another site's availability/qualification on date 1. Closure of a policy assignment unit must contain every materially affected descendant over the declared horizon; two sites are not independent clusters because they have different addresses. Rights and opt-out constraints govern whether edges actually operate.

**Three distinguishable identification strategies, conditional on independent approvals.**
1. **Closed-pool market regimes:** independently randomized physically isolated operator/model/customer markets, assignment at the entire closure to an approved X or G regime. The previous whole-network \(\hat J_X-\hat J_G\) is valid only if there are genuinely independent closures and fixed pre-policy cohorts. Report the number of independent closures, not robot count, as design replication.
2. **Within-pool saturation/factorial capacity:** within each independently closed pool, randomize exactly 0/1/2 source admissions and separately E∈{0,1}, under safe qualified protocols. With positive assignment probabilities and intact original-order accounting, identify \(F_0(0),F_0(1),F_0(2)\) and \(F_1(0),F_1(1),F_1(2)\) for that population. Test the source cross-difference with/without capacity; **only** if the body changes capacity, not learning, can their contrast be interpreted as congestion v. Changing the E body can also change demand, qualification, operator needs, or transfer, invalidating this separation.
3. **Exposure mapping without closure:** version-pin the full policy trajectory; record assignments and actual shared exposures; prespecify \(e_i(\mathbf z)\) and positive \(\pi_i^a\). Use the standard exposure weighted comparison only for **approved** policies with correct consistency. Log exposure event counts and effective support; do not interpret a near-zero probability all-neighbor assignment as high-quality evidence. If exposure graph incomplete or positivity absent, report structural envelopes or a clearly labeled \(\epsilon\)-sensitivity interval, not an identified X/G market contrast.

**Matched technical interventions and source-to-production attribution.** To distinguish the source-data learning \(\ell\) from physical congestion v, freeze a baseline controller and complete-service target qualification panel; compare independent source-only updates with old and new embodied capacity, carefully matching target conditions and original customer opportunity shares. Log model weights/version, operator contacts, service failures/recoveries, customer demand at the pre-action decision point, report timing/selection, and controller rollback. For a customer order, separately identify qualification of *each component* and success of the **joint accepted recipe**. If pooled training crosses cluster boundaries, exposure is not isolated. Deliberate pre-action audits may identify missing demand/qualification cells but cannot be equated with profitable physical learning.

**Falsification and support tests.** Test whether the per-other-site outcome change is in fact bounded by candidate \(\epsilon_{ij}^a\); check operator calendar/cross-site delays, customer referrals, changes in billable customer service, source lineage leakage and shifted market prices. Test version-switch carryover and selection of recorded failures. Pre-register all-site treatment saturation support and raw original-order denominators, including never-admitted and lost orders. Where only accepted-order observations exist, retain partial identification. E's safety approval must be independent; no on-robot experiment is inferred from this registry.

**Official production atlas.** Prior numerical BEA use/supply and BLS occupational wage/duty crosswalk remains **missing**; no new valid official extraction was obtained in this continuation. These sources would inform market exposure and resource-share plausibility, *not* determine robotic qualification from industry input-output or wage shares.

**Decision consequence, conditional only:** if robustly \(D(p)>0\) yet the interference bound includes \(T<0\), avoid a blanket source-training rollout inference; distinguish targeted partial rollout, target grounding, and a new qualified embodied slot under the *same* operating budget. No real controller, dataset or deployment has been assessed here.
