# Exercises — source transfer and operator-pool interference (19 November)

1. Starting from the original qualified-production LP with two qualified X incremental recipes sharing one recovery slot, derive \(F_e(q)\). Explain the accepted-service and retained-incumbent assumptions needed for a fixed positive \(\ell\) bonus.
2. Prove that \(\Delta_{12}F_0=\ell-v\), \(\Delta_{12}F_1=\ell\), and the factorial capacity interaction equals v. Construct a reason E might change \(\ell\), invalidating that attribution.
3. Derive the local Bernoulli(p) own-X effect and full-market effect. Under \(b=3,v=4,k=5/2,\ell=0\), demonstrate opposite signs at p=1/2; identify the estimands and *why unbiased randomization at the site level cannot repair the mismatch*.
4. Enumerate \((q,e)\in\{0,1,2\}\times\{0,1\}\) with K=1. Which feasible portfolio is best? Compare it to independently randomizing each site at the optimal p with e=0, and explain the role of coordination.
5. Prove the interference sensitivity certificate (I9) via a path changing each neighbor's assignment. Show sharpness in the synthetic shared-slot example and explain why its \(\epsilon\) cannot be inferred from accepted-only logs.
6. Give the exact exposure-HT estimator for all-X versus all-G and its positivity assumptions. Why can a shared online learner that is retrained from both treatment/control sites invalidate exposure mapping and the earlier closed-network design?
7. Distinguish a free optional corpus, a forced costly source retraining, a nonactuating target report, target grounding and a qualified new embodiment. Which kinds of evidence could separate pooled learning \(\ell\) from operator congestion v without mapping a benchmark percentage directly to dollars?
