# Endogenous-transfer exercises

Attempt independently before opening TRANSFER_SOLUTIONS.md. All numeric units and experiments are hypothetical.

1. With independent Gaussian parameters \(\theta,\phi\) and prior precisions \(a,b\), show why source exposure \(x\) and target grounding \(g\) yield \(S=A/(a+x)+C/(b+g)+\rho\). Identify the implication that fails with a misspecified source-to-target representation.
2. Prove the fixed-expenditure allocation formula using Cauchy–Schwarz and then explain the economically important difference between maximizing qualification probability at fixed learning expense and maximizing qualification value *net* of learning expense.
3. Differentiate \(q(S)=2\Phi(\tau/\sqrt S)-1\) twice. Obtain the sign of \(q_{xg}\) under separable posterior variance and explain why it does not establish a universal complementarity theorem for full accepted services.
4. Evaluate the old/new body premium at source stocks \(X=0\) and \(X=2\), using \((a,b,A,C_{\rm old},C_{\rm new},\rho,\tau,W,F)=(.2,1,1,4,.25,0,1,30,8)\). Does this prove that redesign advantage increases monotonically in source data?
5. The source-reuse action pays \(7\) in a high-transfer regime and \(-1\) in a low-transfer regime. The competent target-only alternative pays \(5\); the prior is \(1/2\). An \(80\%\)-accurate binary signal can be purchased. Compute EVSI, and evaluate a diagnostic cost of \(0.1\) and \(0.3\). Which investment changes technical capability?
6. If two service qualifications have marginal success \(p\) but depend on the same latent Gaussian error, compare their joint pass probability in the perfectly shared-error case with the product \(p^2\). Explain the identification consequence for a customer order requiring both.
7. Design a matched experimental panel separating source-data volume, paired target grounding, a behavior-aligned representation, and embodiment redesign. Include two service-level quantities that cannot be inferred from per-trajectory technical scores.
