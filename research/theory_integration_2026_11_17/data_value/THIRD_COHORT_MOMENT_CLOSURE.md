# When successive learning moments close

23 November 2026 — same *When Capabilities Become Production* argument, extending [successive source-created eligibility](SUCCESSIVE_LEARNING_RISKSET_VALUE.md). Conditional finite theory; not measured robotics economics.

## 1. Forward economic moment closure, not a technical transfer scalar

A lawful policy changes the distribution of subsequent economically eligible histories, not merely a skill score. Let X_t be the finite history state sufficient for the chosen common structural law, including original buyer budgets/orders, typed usable source lineage, safety qualifications, actual queues and worker capacities, and rights. Actions depend only on information legally observed before decision; a policy is a mapping from observable history to feasible actions, not from hidden structural types. Include time and all needed memory in X_t. For a fixed feasible decision rule d (possibly independently randomized conditional on observed reports), let P_t^d be the stochastic matrix mapping full-state distributions into next distributions and r_t^d the vector of expected net accepted service contributions, including acquisition/verification costs once. Write the row distribution μ_t, feature matrix F_t (one row per full state, constant column included), and economic moments m_t=μ_t F_t.

**Exact closure proposition.** For a fixed common law and all permitted rules in a stated finite policy menu, m_t determines both the next feature moments and current expected net production for every initial distribution on the relevant full-state set iff, for each rule d,

    P_t^d F_(t+1) = F_t A_t^d,      r_t^d = F_t b_t^d.

Then m_(t+1)=m_t A_t^d and expected reward=m_t b_t^d. If terminal payoff lies in the terminal feature span, recursive value for every predeclared feasible rule sequence is identified from m_1 and these coefficients. Proof: sufficiency follows by multiplication. For necessity, any vector v in the left nullspace of F_t has zero sum (constant included). If nonzero, split v into positive and negative parts of equal total mass and normalize to get two distributions with identical m_t. If their next moments or pay differ, closure fails. Otherwise every column of P F and r is orthogonal to the left nullspace, hence belongs to col(F_t). Restricted initial laws may support weaker closure; the iff is for all distributions, not only a single incumbent occupancy. If a common-law set is only partially identified, the coefficients must also be identified or jointly bounded; closure of each law separately does not identify them. Unknown law cannot choose a policy.

**Minimal construction and error bound.** Given a terminal feature span S_T containing 1 and the terminal payoff, recursively set S_t=span(1, all desired economic audit features at t, all r_t^d, all P_t^d f for f in S_(t+1) and permitted rules d). This is the smallest family of spans containing the required features and closed under every listed pullback, by backward induction. Equivalently for only fixed policy values one may keep just their full continuation payoff vectors; full moment propagation is a stronger requirement. The span can grow to the entire history space: no claim of universal low dimension. Closure for an open-loop action set does not automatically extend to report-dependent dispatch: multiplying a continuation contrast by a report indicator can generate a missing interaction feature. Likewise maximizing across actions statewise is nonlinear; the linear recursion evaluates predeclared feasible rules, not an oracle able to observe hidden types. Comparing all rules in an explicitly enumerated menu is valid, not a proof of computational tractability for arbitrary policy classes.

For an approximate candidate rule sequence, let approximate continuation vectors vhat_t=F_t c_t satisfy sup norm residual ε_t=||r_t^d+β P_t^d vhat_(t+1)-vhat_t||∞; terminal residual ε_T bounds the payoff approximation. Subtract true Bellman evaluation and apply the stochastic matrix contraction to obtain |J(d)-m_1 c_1|≤Σ_t β^(t-1) ε_t (terminal included), by backward induction. A lower bound on one rule exceeding the upper bound on another certifies the rank; with uncertain common laws take valid uniform residual bounds, or optimize across jointly feasible coefficients. Do not treat independent stage-specific error minima as a sharp common-law bound. Residuals must be bounded on candidate-reachable histories, not just observed incumbent samples. This complements the previous occupancy-weighted advantage identity: closure gives a compact way to propagate the *candidate* occupancy. It cannot replace lawful action support or paid outcomes.

## 2. Third cohort: worker release, a new source, and a newly authorized order

Extend the previous early K recovery-data and second G grounding menu. Use the original-cluster denominator. At t1 A contributes 3; early K costs 1. Half the clusters have a licensed eligible report (F failure-origin or K completed-recovery origin, split by a fair independent label if retained). At t2 ineligible clusters or those skipping G deliver existing C for 2; G on eligible clusters uses that slot, pays 8 for an accepted B iff S=1, else pays zero. S is binary with conditional probability 1/2 given eligibility. Failed validation is not unsafe dispatch. The t3 worker is occupied by an already owed recovery obligation unless G succeeded; success clears it early, releasing exactly one t3 slot and creating a distinct licensed recovery trajectory. No terminal value is assigned to raw data. Released capacity can deliver an existing separately authorized L order for 2, or attempt new source M validation and a different original buyer's D order for 10 upon safe qualification. The new order has a standing contingent authorization Y, observed before M; M is lawful only on released and authorized histories. If M fails qualification the slot has been spent, and neither order is delivered. This is not free optional data availability: it is a resource-consuming training/validation choice. All costs in this witness are the explicit K cost and slot opportunity cost; no additional cost is assumed. No customer order is paid twice.

Z is a binary latent target-compatibility attribute; in the stipulated model a lawful separate test with preexisting test inputs could measure Z, and if the newly generated source exists, its M qualification equals Z. This transport equality is a structural assumption, not established from that test. Z is not revealed at the live dispatch decision; it is observed after validation. Y denotes contingent authorization even on unrealized histories (not a payment or actual offered job when release fails). Thus the three attributes can be coherently assigned at original cluster entry; actual M is never performed where S=0. Keep the same field and recovery provenance distinction, plus separately tagged routine, teleoperation, simulation, synthetic and pretrained alternatives.

On eligible clusters let (S,Y,Z) be uniform over even-parity triples in world L and odd-parity triples in world H. Each single attribute has mean 1/2, each pair has product mean 1/4, and each pair's entire binary distribution is identical across worlds. Yet Pr(S=Y=Z=1|eligible) is zero in L and 1/4 in H. Mixtures of these two laws preserve all pairwise observations. On the original denominator set x=Pr(eligible,SYZ=1), so x∈[0,1/8], and Pr(eligible,SY=1)=1/8. This witnesses failure even if every separate test and every pairwise joint moment of these attributes were legitimately obtained; in practice the proxy transport itself needs evidence.

The retained no-K policy yields 5. Early K followed by no G yields 4. G on all eligible histories followed by L on every released slot has contribution

    J_G,L = 3-1+2(1/2)+8(1/4)+2(1/4)=11/2.

The policy M on every released authorized history has

    J_G,M = J_G,L - 2 Pr(eligible,SY=1) + 10 x = 21/4 + 10x.

The sharp range is [21/4,13/2] for the stipulated mixture family. A genuinely independently staffed new embodiment E contributes 6 (old A,C plus new order contribution 3 less launch cost 2). Thus the pairwise-matched worlds reverse G,M versus E. Since the preliminary F/K report is independent of the latent triple, selecting G by this report supplies only an independent G probability g; the M decision sees only release/authorization and not Z or world. Let q be the conditional independent probability of choosing M on such histories (an independent lineage-specific q can be averaged among G selections). Then

    J(K,g,q) = 4 + g[3/2 + q(10x-1/4)].

World L implies any such rule contributes at most 11/2, attained by all G then L; no K-family policy in this stated menu can robustly beat E. The optional original policy remains available. In world H, all G then M yields 13/2. One additional economically relevant original-denominator moment x determines the policy comparison under these assumptions: all G,M beats E iff x>3/40. Equivalently conditional qualified M success among eligible released authorized histories must exceed 3/5. Such conditional success alone does not suffice if the original exposure 1/8 is not identified. A joint audit x≥1/10 certifies at least 25/4; this is an illustrative sufficient threshold, not a proposed universal benchmark.

The feature basis {1,S,Y,Z,SY,SZ,YZ} has no SYZ component on the full binary cube: the alternating parity vector annihilates all degree≤2 terms but not SYZ (its dot product with SYZ equals 1). The terminal paid-event feature pulls back to the missing triple when actual t3 eligibility requires both release and authorization. Including that one feature closes this particular valuation, given known costs and the other moments; it is not a claim that all later learning cycles close on third-order moments. A different structural restriction, such as Z conditionally independent of Y given S and eligibility, implies x=1/16 from the stipulated pairs, so J_G,M=47/8 and E=6 wins. That conditional independence is falsifiable with a supported joint audit; pairwise independence alone does not imply it.

Free routine P countermodel: if already produced licensed routine records recreate the same K report and all qualification, resource and buyer transitions at zero incremental cost, all G then L contributes 13/2, exceeding E even without knowing x. This is not an equivalence based on technical scores alone. If E instead shares the t2 C slot and cannot release t3 capacity, committing it yields 3+3-2=4; retaining R=5 dominates it. Staffing independence is economically constitutive.

## 3. Prior foundations and empirical implications

This is a conditional application of linear algebra, fixed-rule Markov reward evaluation and established predictive-state/linear approximation ideas, not a claim of original RL mathematics. Jin, Yang, Wang and Jordan, [COLT 2020 version, abstract](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v125/jin20a.html) (checked Nov 23 2026), studies RL with linear dynamics and rewards; [Littman, Sutton and Singh, NeurIPS 2001 abstract](https://mlanthology.org/neurips/2001/littman2001neurips-predictive/) studies action-conditional predictive state representations. No full paper review claimed this tranche. DDA §5 and Appendix B remain prior art for endogenous future data and aggregation. Our candidate synthesis is the economically admissible feature span generated by qualified customer-production payoffs and resource/rights-induced eligibility, with a common-law causal audit rather than a benchmark-derived monetary mapping.
