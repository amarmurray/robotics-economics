# Student exercises — dynamic source exposure and whole customer production
**Use the synthetic assumptions only. Do not claim empirical bounds. Instructor answers are separate.**

1. State the exact difference between a Bernoulli(p) own-X direct effect and a complete-market all-X versus all-G production contrast with shared operators. Explain why either can be estimated without identifying source-to-target technical transfer, and under what restrictions.
2. With b=(1/10,1/5,0), Q/O/D A matrix from the theory chapter, and U_0=0, calculate U_1, U_2 and U_3. Explain what an operator→customer-demand edge means economically at t+2.
3. With L=(2,1,4) and four dates, calculate the whole-service influence certificate epsilon. Then delete A_D,O=1/2; compare answers and state which can be justified by accepted-only logs.
4. Under symmetric two-site exposure, p=1/2, epsilon^1=521/500, epsilon^0=0, and hypothetical D=2/5, give a valid interval for complete-market T. Does it rank a blanket X rollout?
5. For w_i(z_i,z_j)=3+(3/4)z_i−z_i z_j, calculate the 0,1,2 source admission market values and the own-X direct effect at p=1/2. Explain why the policy recommendation can reverse.
6. Construct two laws on joint binary qualification Q and operator availability O with equal Bernoulli(1/2) marginals but unequal Pr(Q=O=1). Which assumption of the coordinatewise influence theorem must then be checked or strengthened? Suggest a versioned experimental falsifier.
