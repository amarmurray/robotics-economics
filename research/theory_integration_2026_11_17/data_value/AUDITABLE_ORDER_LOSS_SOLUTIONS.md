# Instructor solutions — auditable service-loss bounds

**Private teacher edition | 18 Nov 2026; not student handout.** All normalized amounts are synthetic.

1. A service is accepted only after the *joint* qualification (grasp, navigation, safety/recovery and customer-specific completion) succeeds. Marginal benchmark rates do not determine the copula of skills or actual complete accepted orders. Measure joint held-out successful service, safe recovery support, offered customer orders, completions accepted, resource utilization and captured margin under each complete policy. A 20% grasp score movement could coexist with no new accepted recipe or no demand.
2. Worst B lost contribution is 1.2 because the most valuable single order can disappear. Complete lower gain is \(2.4-0.4-1.2=0.8\). If B1 is certainly preserved in every reachable approved history, worst loss from B2/B3 is 0.6; the lower bound rises to 1.4. This is *bound tightening*, not additional orders; a new engineering cut entails cost and may change A competence.
3. A feasible loss vector is \(\ell=(1,0,1)\), paying \(1.2+0.2=1.4\). Dual \(\lambda=(1.2,0.2),\mu=(0,0,0)\) costs \(1.2+0.2=1.4\) and meets \(H^\top\lambda=(1.2,1.4,0.2)\geq b\). Weak duality and matching primal/dual attainments prove optimality. Feasibility requires actual horizon-wide disruption constraints, not speculative network diagrams.
4. Specialist gain \(1-0.1=0.9\), routine gain \(1.1-0.3=0.8\), shared grounding worst gain \(0.8\), pinned grounding worst gain \(2.4-0.6=1.8\). If pinned grounding is actually feasible and retains that A gain, it has the highest of these guaranteed contributions. Without it, specialist has the strongest guarantee. Isolation can remove training information, change dispatch and demand, or alter the feasible state support, so its A outcome is not logically identical to shared grounding. Pinned G beats specialist at zero B loss exactly when actual A gain exceeds 1.5 at the stated cost.
5. In case (i), both possibilities \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\) occur with probability \(1/2\); expected additive lost contribution is \((1.2+0.6)/2=0.9\). In case (ii), \((1,1)\) and \((0,0)\) occur with equal probability; the expected total is again 0.9. However, a two-simultaneous-recovery state is impossible in case (i) and occurs with probability 1/2 in case (ii), violating a hard one-operator capacity rule. If losses differ from operator recovery events, the example must explicitly equate them for that physical conclusion.
6. One preemption on day 1 can delay an original order, trigger customer cancellation, stop subsequent subscriptions and reduce later accepted orders even if dispatch never preempts another job concurrently. Alternatively, a delayed recovery can reduce later capacity. A versioned original-order ledger, controller checkpoint/broadcast trace, dispatch/queue and customer-demand transitions, operator reservations and safe-stop histories must rule out all such paths or bound their additional contribution in residual \(e_p\). Without that, set no finite k from the single contemporaneous dispatcher rule.
