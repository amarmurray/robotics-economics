# Post-service demand, target grounding and the value of a lawful buyer report

**23 November 2026 · Continuation of When Capabilities Become Production.** Models P (productive source learning), G (legally admitted grounding of retained evidence), E (separately embodied and staffed late capacity) and R (retained safe policy) retain the same optimized, accepted-and-paid production objective. November 22 scientific acceptance is still OPEN. All numbers below are deliberately synthetic. The result is an explicit application of dynamic production, g-formula transport, total-variation sensitivity and statistical treatment decisions, not a claim of independently original mathematics or real robotic returns.

## Exact intellectual question

The last [original-offer theorem](ENDOGENOUS_BUYER_RESPONSE_AND_ORIGINAL_OFFERS.md) permits transport of an R buyer ledger to G_skip only if orders arrive before any G-induced buyer response. If G actually supplies early A service, acceptance, quality and recovery can change both the customer's remaining purchasing budget and later original offers. Can we still connect licensed target qualification to paid work *without* pretending historical G assignments or observed customer records exist?

**Result:** conditional on the complete early accepted-quality and conserved worker state H, an R original-offer kernel can be reweighted under a G intervention if and only if a *defended conditional mechanism invariance* is available; support of the R early strata, a justified G early-state law and a verified Q_G are separately needed. Direct G-dependent buyer response can instead be bounded within state-specific, customer-value-weighted neighborhoods. Those bounds are sharp in a small common-law witness with two repair jobs, while accepted-invoice-only R histories fail even under exact conditional invariance.

## 1. The full production law

At t0 choose a physically and legally feasible program a and a precommitted early A service rule u. At t1, let Y_a in {0,1} indicate early **accepted and paid** A service, not just a technical pass. Its measured quality, promise, price, delivered output, and report status join the history H_a. Let J_{A,a},J_{X,a} in {0,1} be two distinct repair jobs induced by the early attempt or a shared fault; the sole worker processes **one** repair unit before t2. The inherited backlog is

\[
L_{2,a}=\max\{0,J_{A,a}+J_{X,a}-1\},\qquad W_{2,a}=\mathbf 1\{L_{2,a}=0\}.
\]

This is an explicit capacity identity. A has a **one-unit lifetime purchasing budget** across early and late periods, so B_{A,2}=1-Y_a. When Y_a=1, later A interest is not an actually purchasable second order. The other buyer C has independent purchasing capacity. Let D_a and Z_a denote potential later A interest and C's actual original offer, respectively, before routing, and Q_a(h) in {0,1} target C's independently qualified **paid recipe**, subject to genuine rights and safety constraints. Only Q_a Z_a creates eligible C service.

With one common late robot-time slot and one qualifying worker, and unit captured contributions b,v_C >= 0, the optimized terminal action after offers are visible earns

\[
f_a(h,d,z)=W(h)\max\{b(1-Y(h))d,\;v_C Q_a(h)z\}.
\]

Equivalently, writing m=min(b,v_C),

\[
f_a=W\{b(1-Y)D+v_C QZ-m(1-Y)DQZ\}. \tag{1}
\]

The overlap term is the *actual displaced paid order* from one shared service slot, not an arbitrary data discount. The full fixed-policy program value is

\[
V_a=\mathbb E_a[r_{1,a}(H_a)+f_a(H_a,D_a,Z_a)]-K_a, \tag{2}
\]

where r_1 is truly collected early contribution and K_a contains actual training, compute, curation, hardware, licensing and coordination costs **once**. Old policies remain in the feasible menu wherever rights/safety and resources permit. The option to use extra free data cannot lower the maximum attainable value when the old menu remains and training can be declined; forced training can. A separate E robot and independent worker may serve C concurrently, but cannot make A purchase twice. Replacing this terminal formula by a multistage Bellman problem is necessary if t2 service itself creates future demand, learner records or recovery jobs.

### Buyer histories and the exact decomposition

Let H contain initial buyer covariates, paid/quality early service, public signals, incident jobs, backlog and C-qualification-relevant conditions. Write p_a(h)=Pr(H_a=h), K_a(d,z|h)=Pr(D_a=d,Z_a=z|H_a=h). For an arbitrary fixed target qualification q(h) set

\[
\mu_a(h;q)=\sum_{d,z} K_a(d,z\mid h)\,W(h)\max\{b(1-Y(h))d,\;v_Cq(h)z\}.
\]

Assume a common, comparable history alphabet, including R-positive strata wherever G can reach; Q_G and Q_R on the same histories are meaningful independently validated recipes, not benchmark percentages. Then the following telescoping identity is **exact**:

\[
\begin{aligned}
V_G-V_R={}&(\bar r_{1,G}-\bar r_{1,R})-(K_G-K_R)\\
&+\sum_h p_R(h)[\mu_R(h;Q_G)-\mu_R(h;Q_R)] &&\text{qualification on R histories}\\
&+\sum_h [p_G(h)-p_R(h)]\mu_R(h;Q_G) &&\text{early service/quality/queue mediation}\\
&+\sum_h p_G(h)[\mu_G(h;Q_G)-\mu_R(h;Q_G)]. &&\text{direct conditional order response}
\end{aligned}\tag{3}
\]

**Proof.** Expand (2) for both arms, then add and subtract \(\sum_h p_G(h)\mu_R(h;Q_G)\) and \(\sum_h p_R(h)\mu_R(h;Q_G)\). No independence of buyers, learner, service, quality or recovery is assumed. These are bookkeeping terms with an explicitly chosen order of replacement, **not uniquely identified natural direct and indirect causal effects**. Do not infer the counterfactual terms from R invoices.

## 2. A constructive but demanding conditional transport certificate

Suppose (i) the full t1 G history distribution p_G(h) is learned from an approved, service-matched intervention or a valid external transport design, including **buyer acceptance and recovery**, not merely Q_G lab scores; (ii) the R original A/C offer/no-offer panel measures K_R(d,z|h), with Pr_R(H=h)>0 for every G-reachable h; (iii) matched target qualification Q_G(h) is independently verified on the same accepted-service recipe, hardware, safety gate, deadline and legal lineage; (iv) after conditioning on the complete pre-order H, G changes no further customer price, knowledge, demand or reporting mechanism, and both clusters have a consistent market/no-interference boundary; (v) the same dispatch, opportunity-cost and worker rules apply.

Then the conditional exclusion K_G(.|h)=K_R(.|h) makes the final term of (3) zero, and

\[
\boxed{V_G=\bar r_{1,G}+\sum_h p_G(h)\mu_R(h;Q_G)-K_G.} \tag{4}
\]

This is a *mechanism-transport formula*: actual G-induced demand feedback **through** changed quality, accepted service, budget and recovery H is retained by p_G(h), even though the post-history buyer response kernel is transported. It is more permissive than unconditional no-response and much harder to justify than a simple technical performance test. If G is forbidden, cannot be approved, or has no credible way to identify p_G, formula (4) is **not an estimate**. Conditioning on an incomplete selected H can itself induce bias. The t1 G trial need not be a commercial fleet deployment in name, but any trial that claims to identify buyer-paid Y_G must genuinely support the buyer and outcome it purports to measure. G-specific conditioning in an unsupported region cannot be filled by R reweighting.

## 3. State-dependent sharp bounds when direct G buyer response is unknown

Let \(d_{\mathrm{TV}}(K_G(.|h),K_R(.|h))\le\delta_h\) be an externally defended *conditional* uncertainty restriction, not an estimate from R-only observations. For terminal f in (1) define \(\operatorname{osc}_h f=\max_{d,z}f(h,d,z)-\min_{d,z}f(h,d,z)\). The standard finite-law coupling inequality gives

\[
\boxed{\left|V_G-V_G^{\mathrm{transport}}\right|
\le\sum_h p_G(h)\delta_h\,\operatorname{osc}_h f.}\tag{5}
\]

**Proof:** for each h couple the two buyer-order distributions with disagreement probability at most \(\delta_h\); the realized paid difference is at most the within-history range of f, and sum with p_G(h). This is a valid *outer* bound; it is sharp only when sufficient mass can move between the extreme-payoff histories. A more exact bound solves a four-atom transportation LP independently at each h, subject to nonnegative mass, unit sum and total variation. It is essential that no G action-support claim is derived from a small R/G marginal total-variation coefficient. A TV restriction on within-arm laws does **not** identify same-unit causal discordance between two programs.

**Physical screening:** if L2(h)>0, W=0 and \(\operatorname{osc}_h f=0\). A late buyer report in that permanently blocked state cannot change **this terminal paid production**, despite possible value in a longer-horizon model. If Y=1, A's budget is exhausted; observing D cannot change this late dispatch, whereas C's original offer still can. This identifies a prospective, rights-feasible audit hierarchy instead of counting all customer records equally.

## 4. Globally coherent exact synthetic witness

Take b=2, v_C=3, Q_G=1 under a stipulated, licensed, independently verified recipe. The historical R buyer offer law conditional on every early h is an independent pair of fair binary offers, represented by four atom probabilities (D,Z)=(00,01,10,11), each 1/4. R qualification for C is zero, so accepted R invoices omit C original offers. R reaches each of the three histories below with positive probability (e.g. p_R=(1/4,1/2,1/4)), but the program-specific, **hypothetical and not R-identified** G-early history distribution is:

| H after G early attempt | p_G(h) | Early A paid? | t1 repair jobs / processed | Late A budget / worker | Conditional R late payoff under Q_G |
|:--|--:|:--|:--|:--|--:|
| good | 1/2 | Y=1, receipt 4/5 | 0 / 0 | 0 / free | 3 Pr_R(Z=1)=3/2 |
| idle | 1/4 | Y=0 | 0 / 0 | 1 / free | E_R[max(2D,3Z)]=2 |
| blocked | 1/4 | Y=0 | 2 distinct jobs / 1 cleared | 1 / blocked | 0 |

Here an unsuccessful early attempt can produce two distinct repair obligations, and only one clears in time. A's one unit of buyer budget is not consumed unless accepted/paid. Holding all G early histories, price, rights, and P/R/E programs constant, **only** K_G(.|h) varies. A single common probability space can generate each alternative potential G order kernel jointly with identical observed R/P/E histories using independent uniforms; no cross-world copies of an actual invoice are manufactured.

The transported fixed G-early policy has

\[
V_{G,\mathrm{early}}^{\mathrm{transport}}
=\tfrac12(\tfrac45)+\tfrac12(\tfrac32)+\tfrac14(2)
=\boxed{\tfrac{33}{20}}.
\]

For each of the two free-worker histories, impose the hypothetical conditional response radius \(\delta_h=1/4\); in blocked H any radius has zero terminal value. Each free history's baseline four-atom buyer table is uniform. In good H, moving 1/4 of mass from Z=0 to Z=1 (or back) changes conditional payoff by 3/4. In idle H, moving 1/4 from (D,Z)=(0,0) to (0,1) (or back) changes it by 3/4. Thus the transport inequality is **sharp** here:

\[
\boxed{
V_{G,\mathrm{early}}\in
[\tfrac{33}{20}-\tfrac9{16},\;\tfrac{33}{20}+\tfrac9{16}]
=[\tfrac{87}{80},\tfrac{177}{80}]. }\tag{6}
\]

The same construction works for common \(\delta\le1/4\), giving \(33/20\pm(9/4)\delta\). These are **conditional sensitivity intervals**, not sampling confidence intervals. Four-atom integer enumeration in the companion script realizes both endpoints across 169 paired synthetic conditional laws.

For comparability, retain prior *assumed* fixed P value 2, safe G-skip value 2 (legally retaining the old no-early-service action with R-conditioned offers), and truly independently staffed E value 21/10, with its independent equipment, rights and costs already accounted once. These values are a coherent hypothetical terminal menu, not empirical calibration. At the low endpoint E beats G-early; at the high endpoint G-early (177/80) beats E (168/80). G owns the **option to skip** under every world. Under a *known* world its optimized value is \(\max\{2,V_{G,\mathrm{early}}\}\), so the addition of optional G early service cannot decrease optimal G value below 2.

**No oracle for the uninformed firm.** If its program and early policy must be selected before any G-response signal, it cannot use the worldwise maximum as a realizable state-contingent policy. With E=168/80, low G-early=87/80 and high G-early=177/80, a fixed ex ante lottery allocating probability q to G-early and 1-q to E has endpoint regrets \(q(81/80)\) and \((1-q)(9/80)\). Equality gives

\[
\boxed{q^*=\tfrac1{10},\quad \max\mathrm{regret}=\tfrac{81}{800}.}\tag{7}
\]

The safe G-skip and P policies, each worth 2, are dominated by E in this **particular** stipulated choice menu, but remain retained alternatives. Maximin selects E. The G-early upside can first exceed E only when a *defended* common response radius exceeds 1/5: \(33/20+(9/4)\delta>21/10\). This is not a derived empirical threshold for any real buyer market.

## 5. A second, sharper nonidentification result: paid-only invoices can defeat transport even if direct G response is zero

Keep the same R early histories and R qualification Q_R=0. Take two common-law worlds with identical actual R services, accepted A invoices, worker transitions, prices, t1 quality, reporting and P/E histories. In both, R C orders are never served and **never appear in R's invoice system**. In world L, Pr_R(Z=1|good)=0; in world H it is 1, with all other R and G mechanisms unchanged. Assume the stringent conditional invariance K_G=K_R, including C, and identical hypothetical G history distribution. Both worlds have *the same selected R invoice dataset* even though G-early's paid value differs by \((1/2)\cdot3=\boxed{3/2}\). R invoice equality does not imply original-buyer-law equality. This is a structural reporting/support failure, not random statistical imprecision.

If actual full original A/C offers **including no-offers and declined/unserved C** can be legally enumerated under R, the two worlds become distinguishable. Otherwise even imposing zero direct G order response leaves G-value uncertainty. A missing-at-random weight is legitimate only with an independently defensible positive known report probability in each relevant history and no undisclosed outcome-conditioned selection. If rights forbid observing C's original offers, more R invoices will not repair the missingness.

## 6. Empirical discriminants, closest art and the next proof gap

The most valuable prospective comparison records (i) actual t1 accepted paid Y and quality, (ii) separately clocked two-worker-job obligations and clearance, (iii) independently original A/C buyer offers, no-offers, refused and unserved orders, (iv) admitted Q_G, same-context technical control and cost, (v) observed buyer publicity/price/offer deadlines, and (vi) reporting, data-retention and deployment rights for every causal arm. For R-positive histories, an independent lawful original-offer audit tests the buyer kernel. A genuinely approved G early-service trial is necessary to learn G's altered H distribution unless a separate valid mechanism transport exists. A staggered disclosure experiment *before* offers can test direct market response without claiming to validate robot production. A trial that cannot actually deliver the service cannot establish Y_G.

A finite-horizon accepted-production model should eventually allow G's t2 dispatch to create new repair jobs and t3 customer demand; the current terminal W=0 audit irrelevance then need not survive. The next mathematical action is a policy-dependent two-step buyer-response/recovery Bellman with selected observation masks and an exact reachable-state identified set, rather than another arbitrary choice of parameters.
