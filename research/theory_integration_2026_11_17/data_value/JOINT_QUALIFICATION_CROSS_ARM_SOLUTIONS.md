# Instructor solutions — cross-arm qualification and demand

1. With binary monotone Q, HSG≥HS,HG pointwise. Also HSG−HS≤(QA_SG−QA_S)+(QB_SG−QB_S): the jointly newly qualified event requires at least one task upgrade. Take expectations, repeat G, and cap joint success by each task marginal. Old checkpoint availability enlarges *menu value*, not a forced trained checkpoint's task success.
2. H_S ∩ H_G=H_R because each task's baseline qualification is the meet of its S and G qualifications, and H_S∪H_G⊆H_SG. Inclusion–exclusion proves hSG≥hS+hG−hR. Mean Δp=0 can mix individual AND (positive) and OR (negative) task interactions and therefore does not enforce taskwise meet/join equality.
3. Fréchet: hSG∈[max(0,3/5+3/5−1),min(3/5,3/5)]=[1/5,3/5]. Monotone: lower max(1/4,1/4)=1/4; upper 1/4+2(3/5−1/2)=9/20. Modular lower 1/4+1/4−1/5=3/10, same upper 9/20. All endpoints are attained by the four exact sparse joint response-type count tables in joint_qualification_cross_arm.js; pointwise inequalities prove no intermediate-outside values.
4. SG net = (2pA+2pB+8hSG)−1=7/5+8hSG. Thus Fréchet gives [3,31/5], monotone [17/5,5], modular [19/5,5]. E-only net 4 wins at the lower endpoint of each, SG wins at each upper endpoint; no robust winner. Note [3,31/5] comes from h=[1/5,3/5] under the stipulated fixed F, not a prediction.
5. Bundle gross 2(3/5)+2(3/5)+8(9/20)=6. One-slot gross=2(3/5)+2(3/5)−2(9/20)=3/2. New SG net=(3/4)6+(1/4)(3/2)−1=31/8<4. E wins in this constructed market *only if* its separately qualified, customer-signed alternative remains net4 when order composition changes. If E's demand/feasibility also moves, recompute its full value.
6. Write Σ(π−π0)m = Σδ^+m−Σδ^-m with equal total positive/negative masses equal TV, so absolute deviation ≤(max m−min m)TV. When order mass Λ moves, Σ(Λπ−Λ0π0)m has an additional count/intensity component not controlled by TV of normalized π alone.
