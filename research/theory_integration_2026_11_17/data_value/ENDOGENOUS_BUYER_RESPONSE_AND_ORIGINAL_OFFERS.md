# Buyer response as a production input: when a grounding program changes who orders and what can be observed

**23 November 2026; continuation of the single _When Capabilities Become Production_ argument, not a new model family.** Scientific Nov22 acceptance remains OPEN. This is conditional mathematics and a fully synthetic economic witness, neither a field estimate nor an independently established novel theorem. Prior: [three-date buyer-budget/worker theorem](THREE_DATE_BUYER_BUDGET_QUEUE_AND_GROUNDING.md), [component-kernel correction](COMPONENT_KERNEL_IDENTIFICATION_AND_ECONOMIC_TRANSFER.md).

## The continuing question

A full-stack firm chooses among productive source service **P** (current accepted work and retained field learning), **G** (rights-permitted target grounding of already-held evidence), genuinely independent embodied/staffed capacity **E**, and safe retained **R**. When the customer hears about the resulting service, original orders may change before any robot is deployed. The last chapter held G's marginal original offers at one-half but left their cross-buyer overlap unknown. We now distinguish a baseline order registry from **policy-induced buyer response** and prove exactly when that registry can and cannot value G. This advances the same source-to-qualification-to-paid-production function, including the lost original A sale and conserved recovery queue.

Let U be a date-1 early A service decision; the initial A buyer can purchase at most one unit over dates 1 and 2. Let A_1 be qualified accepted-and-paid early service, J_1 the incident it creates, and B_{A,2}=1-UA_1 the remaining purchasing capacity. One worker clears at most one of the two repair units created by an incident before date 2, hence with zero legacy backlog L_2=UA_1J_1. This is a physical, not financial, identity. Let W_2=1{L_2=0}. For each legally feasible program a and history H_1, define original later buyer offers D_2^a(H_1) for A and C_2^a(H_1) for distinct customer C; C qualification is Q_2^a(H_1), not a benchmark score or automatically an invoice. With a single t2 robot-time and b,c>=0, conditional late paid contribution is

\[
r_2^a(H_1)=W_2^a \max\{b B_{A,2}D_2^a,\ c Q_2^a C_2^a\}.
\]

For the admissible t1 rule U chosen from what was observable at its decision date, and real program cost K_a counted once,

\[
V_a(U)=\mathbb E[a_1UA_1+r_2^a(H_1)]-K_a.
\]

A physically distinct E worker/embodiment may provide a second concurrent C slot, but may not create a second A budget. More complicated queues replace W_2 by an actual backlog/eligibility Bellman transition. The buyer kernels may depend on the program, announcement, early quality, accepted output, price, deadline and realized recovery: C_2^a=f_C(a,H_1,\eta_C). Thus field production is permitted to generate demand feedback as well as model evidence. For R/P, their own observed original-buyer laws are never relabeled as the unobserved G response.

## Economic qualification and market response are different effects

For the G_skip policy, U=0, no new worker job, A budget preserved. If grounding qualifies target C deterministically, Q_G=1, and customer offers occur before final dispatch, put f(d,c)=max{bd,cc} with the second c in the argument denoting the binary C offer (write it as z below). For a fixed, common distribution of D and C,

\[
f(d,z)=bd+cz-\min(b,c)dz,\qquad d,z\in\{0,1\}.
\]

In the current witness b=2,c=3. Let x_G=Pr(D_G=1,C_G=1). Then

\[
V_{G,\mathrm{skip}}=2Pr(D_G=1)+3Pr(C_G=1)-2x_G-K_G.
\]

This is an exact buyer-budget/robot-time overlap identity, not an approximation or a technical transfer coefficient. Even if the two marginal order rates remain constant, a reordering of buyer demand over common scarce capacity changes the contribution from G. Successful service cannot be counted twice when A and C simultaneously request the one slot.

More generally, with a fixed buyer law and a binary qualified target that expands from Q_R to Q_G, the *pure technical qualification effect*, for c>=b, is

\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{qual}}=\mathbb E[(c-bD)C(Q_G-Q_R)].
\]

This identity holds for arbitrarily dependent D,C,Q, provided the original offers and physical menu remain fixed and Q_G>=Q_R on every compared history. When the program changes orders, the exact decomposition is

\[
V_G-V_R=
\underbrace{\mathbb E_R[f(D_R,C_R;Q_G)-f(D_R,C_R;Q_R)]}_{\text{qualification at baseline buyer opportunities}}
+\underbrace{\mathbb E_G[f(D_G,C_G;Q_G)]-\mathbb E_R[f(D_R,C_R;Q_G)]}_{\text{original-buyer response}}
-K_G.
\]

Here f(d,z;q)=max{bd,cqz}; Q_G is a pre-offer certified potential target recipe, evaluated on common contexts. If Q_G itself depends on post-offer case selection, this formula requires a richer full-history counterfactual law and baseline transport is unavailable. No data premium is added after already counting the entire optimized service payoff.

**Constructive transport certificate, not free identification.** Suppose a real original A/C order/no-order registry is complete under retained R; G's target recipe is independently qualified for the same contexts, embodiment, safety gate and deadline; its license permits this use; G is deliberately *not revealed or advertised until after both original orders are irrevocably submitted*; buyer-facing price, SLA, supply information, market spillovers and offers are otherwise identical; and G_skip creates no early worker job. If those exclusions are causally valid, (D_G,C_G)=(D_R,C_R) before the order cutoff. Therefore the R joint original-offer distribution plus the independently certified Q_G conditional outcomes determine G_skip's expected paid value by reoptimizing one standard t2 dispatch. Historical G production exposure is not mathematically required **under these stronger premises**. Without a certified Q_G and an original *unserved-offer* ledger, even this restricted certificate fails. A buyer who anticipates G or a quote that changes the expected delivered service invalidates the exclusion. This is a potential experimental design, not a claim those conditions currently hold.

## Two coherent response laws and a failure of complete R audits

Use the prior chapter's normalized, hypothetical economic values and physical resources. A qualified early paid A service contributes 4/5, source P learns C but carries an independent incident chance 1/5 after service, while C's order chance is 1/2: V_P=4/5+3(1/2)(4/5)=2. R_skip retains only the later A service, V_R=2(1/2)=1. G's early-service option has fixed value 4/5+3(1/2)(1/2)=31/20 under its stipulated job/demand independence. E_skip has a genuinely separate late C slot and worker, C order chance 1/2 and a once-charged 2/5 cost: V_E=2(1/2)+3(1/2)-2/5=21/10. These independent resources are assumed for the witness, not established for any real embodiment. P's and E's full potential histories, including original buyer records, are identical across worlds.

Let D be a shared fair binary original A order. Let C_R be an *independent* fair original C order under retained R. Thus even a **complete paired original R buyer registry** finds x_R=Pr(D=1,C_R=1)=1/4 in every world. For G_skip suppose target qualification is independently and correctly certified, Q_G=1. Let C_G be a potential original C offer that may react to the existence, timing or disclosure of a grounded service. Conditional on D, define

\[
Pr(C_G=1\mid D=1)=2x,\qquad
Pr(C_G=1\mid D=0)=1-2x,\qquad 0\le x\le 1/2.
\]

A common independent uniform innovation generates C_G from these conditional probabilities; C_R has its own uniform innovation. This defines one common potential-outcome world, not separate arbitrary arm tables. For every x, D and C_G are fair, all observed R and P histories are identical, and C_G's **marginal** original order propensity remains 1/2. Yet

\[
Pr(D=1,C_G=1)=x,\qquad
V_{G,\mathrm{skip}}(x)=\frac52-2x.
\]

| World or mechanism | G buyer overlap x | G_skip paid contribution | Best of P,G,E |
|:--|--:|--:|:--|
| Grounding orders arrive in different A windows | 0 | 5/2 | G |
| No announcement/offer response, same R joint law | 1/4 | 2 | E |
| Grounding orders coincide with A windows | 1/2 | 3/2 | E |

The G_serve option is 31/20 in all three stipulated worlds and is dominated by E; P is 2. In particular, the complete R buyer registry, every P history, G's qualification indicator, and even the two hypothetical **G individual buyer marginals** are identical, yet the optimal investment reverses. The missing object is the policy-dependent joint original A/C demand response under G. Even knowing both G individual order probabilities would not identify this overlap. An original customer order is economically different from an observed accepted invoice: only one customer may be served when both request the same scarce robot-time.

The pair is an explicit *negative identification result*, not empirical evidence of a demand effect. Holding x_R=1/4 and G's marginal buyer rates fixed, it is exactly the customer-buyer covariance, not a change in robot capability, that creates the reversal. Comparing only successful C invoices would confound original orders with qualification, dispatch, worker availability and payment selection.

## A sharp, actionable sensitivity certificate

Suppose a separate causal/operating design **defends**, rather than pretends to estimate from old records, the restriction |x_G-x_R|<=delta, with the two G buyer marginals fixed at 1/2, the certified G qualification and the physical menu above unchanged. Then Frechet feasibility gives

\[
x_G\in[\max(0,x_R-\delta),\min(1/2,x_R+\delta)]
\]

and therefore the sharp interval

\[
\boxed{
V_G\in[5/2-2\min(1/2,x_R+\delta),\quad5/2-2\max(0,x_R-\delta)].
}
\]

**Sharpness:** each feasible x is realized by the conditional C_G construction above while preserving the *entire* R joint law and other arm histories. This restriction is a sensitivity assumption, not a causal estimate or statistical confidence band. Marginal service-rate equivalence alone does not imply it. Worker and E independence, qualification and rights assumptions remain indispensable.

At x_R=1/4 and the illustrative bound delta=3/20, G is in [17/10,23/10]. P=2, E=21/10. Under no lawful informative signal before investment, maximum guaranteed contribution selects E. For **minimax regret** among fixed nonanticipative G_skip/E lotteries, write q for the probability of G. At the upper G endpoint the oracle earns 23/10 and regret is (1-q)(1/5); at the lower endpoint the oracle earns E=21/10 and regret is q(2/5). Equating gives

\[
\boxed{q^*=1/3,\qquad \max regret=2/15.}
\]

No optimal-in-each-world hindsight dispatcher has been substituted. E is a guaranteed winner throughout this interval only if x_R-\delta>=1/5 (strictly if >); with x_R=1/4 the certificate requires delta<=1/20. That number is **derived from the real normalized opportunity cost relative to E** but is not a measured empirical response bound. If G changes its marginal order rates or its qualification/recovery, the interval and threshold do not transport.

An authentic precommitment report that perfectly separates the two interval endpoints (x=1/10 versus x=2/5), with a stipulated 50/50 prior and no extra price, is worth 1/10 *gross*: the no-report Bayesian choice is E=21/10; the informed policy chooses G when its value is 23/10 and E otherwise, averaging 22/10. This report must be lawful, actionable before commitment and predictive of the G response; P/R telemetry in this construction provides no such information. A postcommitment original-order audit cannot retroactively exchange an embodiment for grounding.

## Reporting selection: what can and cannot be learned

An invoice conditional on accepted service is not an original demand event. Under R, C is unqualified, so a paid-invoice-only dataset never records C's latent original order; it can record the successful A service and give Pr(D=1 | invoice for A)=1 even though actual A original order propensity is 1/2. Under G, observing only the *winning* buyer's invoice still does not reveal whether both A and C offered an order. In a lawful, randomized, complete-policy comparison, however, **complete revenue, resource and payment records can identify total policy value without reconstructing every counterfactual joint demand event**. The missing joint offers matter for the mechanism and transport to other policies, not because invoices can never value a randomized whole program.

A prospective audit must independently enumerate original buyer offers *and no-offers*, rejected/unserved offers, qualification, worker backlog, outcome and payment, by connected cluster and predecision history. If G and R are lawfully randomized with a positive known action probability and the original-order audit is independently sampled at a positive known rate in each observed preaction stratum, standard inverse weighting identifies the corresponding arm-specific joint x under exchangeability and no relevant unmodeled interference. Outcome-selected missingness without a justified observation law, or zero permitted G assignment, defeats that route. A G-specific independent test of target skill measures Q_G; it does not by itself measure changed customer orders. A legally authorized pre-offer paired buyer registry plus independently qualified recipe is sufficient **only under the timing/exclusion certificate above**. Safety-forbidden G is not a candidate with an unknown numeric payoff.
