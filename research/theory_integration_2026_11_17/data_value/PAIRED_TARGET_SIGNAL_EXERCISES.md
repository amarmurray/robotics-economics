# Student exercises — paired report support and economically actionable grounding

Use the hypothetical original-buyer R2/matching E4 economy and the linked [chapter](PAIRED_TARGET_SIGNAL_SUPPORT_AND_VALUE.md). No exercise assumes a physical safety certificate or field measurement.

1. With C=P(Q_A=Q_B=1), derive its four (T,Y) cells from q,x,m,z. Show that z is sharply between max(0,x+m-q) and min(x,m), without invoking independent transfer or report calibration.
2. Derive F's and L's optimal contribution on C, retaining the R fallback, and prove the distance-to-interval expression for D. Explain when source-produced qualification itself makes Y useless for switching.
3. Suppose q=1/2, x=m=1/4; for the complement q_0=1/2, x_0=m_0=1/4. Global report agreement is 3/4. Find the sharp z interval and D bounds; give two full distinct joint tables with equal pairwise statistics and global accuracy but opposite decisions at extra flexibility cost 1/4.
4. A researcher observes only unconditional report accuracy, another records Q×Y with symmetric marginal accuracy, and a third collects held-out same-parent Q×T×Y. Which can validly test qualification-conditional kernel stability? Under what conditions can an approved whole-network randomized experiment rank complete policies without identifying all three-way structural terms?
5. Under equal prior probability on the two endpoint worlds in Exercise 3, calculate the gross perfect shadow-audit decision value if information arrives before an irreversible hardware choice. State what changes if its timing is after lock, and why the result is not a universal dataset price.
