# November 22 integration: physical admission, policy overlap, and economic data value

**Prepared 20 November 2026 UTC, private scientific working paper.** Continues *When Capabilities Become Production*, its source X / grounding G / embodiment E comparison, the finite observation-closed recovery game and the same November 22 Models A–B review. **No physical field authorization, original customer experimental panel or identified industry robotics return is asserted.** The results are existing causal identification/partial-identification mathematics composed with an already developed economic production framework, not independently demonstrated novelty.

## One decision, three different information requirements

A production system needs three distinct forms of closure before converting technical evidence into *identified* economic data value:

1. **Physical admission.** The actual customer/robot/operator state, observation, disturbance support, deadline and rights admit the whole intended action/contingency policy. The earlier information-support winning set C_t determines this *conditionally on a sound, complete and signed model*. Retaining code or attaining a high mean benchmark success rate is not certification.
2. **Original-market accounting.** The unit begins with original eligible customer orders, including refusals, unserved orders, completed-and-rejected orders, penalties, human/specialist displacement and receipts—not just successful robot clips. Price, quality, procurement budget, policy-dependent demand and cross-order operator interference must be included in the whole-policy outcome.
3. **Causal policy support.** An observed distribution of logged on-policy trajectories cannot reveal outcomes of a different source-trained, grounded, embodied or contracted policy if that whole policy never has positive assignment probability on the relevant original-network customer stratum. Extra sample size inside the existing logged distribution does not repair missing support.

The first condition concerns **hard physical feasibility** and is logically separate from the third condition, which concerns **statistical identification** of average economic value. A randomized complete-policy study can identify an average *if it is approved and performed*, but randomization does not retrospectively prove pathwise safety. Conversely an externally certified safe alternative may have no logged original-order market support and still have unknown economic value.

## A single feasible-program model

Let g be an independently schedulable original-customer/order–shift–operator–procurement cluster with preassignment characteristics Z_g, and let a denote a **complete** network policy: incumbent R, a source-trained X, target grounding G, new embodiment E, or specified bundles/menus. A policy includes admissible pricing, dispatch, retained human alternatives, failover actions, data rights and fixed physical resources. Let F(z) be the actually admitted, legally available set of such policies under physical/observation and rights conditions, not an optimistic set of controllers appearing in a benchmark.

Write Y_g(a) for the *net capturable provider contribution* from all originally eligible orders within g if complete policy a were used, including refused orders, unqualified attempts, contracted credits, dispatch displacement, operator opportunity cost and policy costs **once**. If the object is gross feasible production instead, explicitly change Y and keep sunk investment costs separate. Interference inside g is part of Y_g(a). Assume bounded L_a(z)<=Y_g(a)<=U_a(z) for a stated common target population, and no interference across independently assigned clusters. Let mu_a=E[Y_g(a)], and let V=max_{a in F}mu_a for the fixed policy class. A dynamic version evaluates the same feasible actions using immediate captured production plus delta E[J(next state)] **once**, so learning-generated future data does not become a second add-on premium.

**Proposition A (approved whole-policy mean identification).** Suppose Z is measured before assignment, clusters are exchangeable relative to the declared eligible original-order population, complete-network potential outcomes satisfy consistency, assignment A is conditionally independent of all relevant Y(a) given Z, conditional propensities e_a(z)=P(A=a|Z=z) are known, and e_a(z)>0 on every positive-probability target stratum. Assume accounting records each cluster's original-order contribution even if no service is purchased or completed. Then

    mu_a = E[ 1{A=a} Y_obs / e_a(Z) ].

**Proof.** Condition on Z: E[1{A=a}Y_obs | Z]=e_a(Z)E[Y(a)|Z] by consistency and conditional exchangeability. Divide by positive e_a(Z), integrate over the declared original population. No independence of individual robot picks within a cluster is required. The conclusion identifies the population mean of each *specified complete* policy, not its casewise comparison with R, its hard safety guarantee, the value of a never-offered price menu, nor an untried policy outside F. This is standard inverse-probability weighting, not a new estimator.

**Proposition B (sharp missing-overlap contribution interval).** Keep consistency/exchangeability on the strata S_a={z:e_a(z)>0}, but allow zero treatment support on M_a={z:e_a(z)=0}. Let m_a(z)=E[Y(a)|Z=z] be identified only on S_a, and let valid conditional gross/net bounds L_a(z),U_a(z) be available for M_a. Without restrictions linking potential outcomes across the missing strata, the sharp identified interval is

    lower(mu_a) = E[1{Z in S_a} m_a(Z) + 1{Z in M_a} L_a(Z)],
    upper(mu_a) = E[1{Z in S_a} m_a(Z) + 1{Z in M_a} U_a(Z)].

**Proof.** Decompose E[Y(a)] over S_a and M_a. On M_a no observed unit received a; either endpoint can be attained by assigning all unobserved potential outcomes there the respective permitted bound without changing any observed joint law. Intermediate means follow by mixing the endpoint completions. The sharpness is conditional on unconstrained missing potential outcomes, known population weights and valid bounds; physical cross-policy restrictions or shared demand could tighten it. An investment a is robustly preferred to b under these separate identified sets only if lower(mu_a)>upper(mu_b); otherwise *this certificate* cannot rank them. Shared restrictions may narrow joint differences; do not infer an independent joint counterfactual law from endpoint arithmetic.

**Proposition C (more selectively produced data need not identify economic transfer).** If production under logged policy R generates data only from service/exposure strata R visits and a distinct physically admissible policy G has zero logging probability in an original-order stratum H, unlimited additional R-generated clips and receipts do not identify m_G(H) without a transport restriction. Two worlds that agree on every observed R and G-on-L record but differ in the unobserved G-on-H potential contribution are observationally indistinguishable at any dataset size. The sharp bounds in Proposition B do not shrink. A source or pretrained model may *predict* G-on-H and still fail to establish its original-order economic contribution without a valid source-to-target transport argument. This is the classic selective-label/off-policy-support problem.

## An exact synthetic choice: more incumbent logs, a target audit, or an embodiment

Two equally prevalent original eligible customer strata L and H have a known incumbent whole-network value R=2 normalized contribution units per cluster. A separately physically admitted embodiment alternative E gives E=9/4. A grounded alternative G has measured full-original-order net contribution G(L)=3, but was **never assigned** in H. Suppose a defensible, explicitly hypothetical bounded outcome G(H) in [0,5]. Then Proposition B gives mu_G in [3/2,4]. Neither infinitely many extra accepted L clips nor more R logs in H separates G from E: the cost of missing overlap is not estimation variance.

Two observationally identical synthetic worlds set G(H)=0 or G(H)=5. In the first, optimal admitted production is E=9/4 (G=3/2); in the second, G=4. An illustrative **prior**, not data, gives these worlds equal probability and assumes a perfectly revealing, lawful, *safe* target-H audit that does not alter physical states, customer demand, policy costs or eventual options. Before the audit E[mu_G]=(3/2+4)/2=11/4, so G is chosen over E or R. After the audit the optimal policy is E in the first world and G in the second; expected optimized contribution is (9/4+4)/2=25/8. The gross expected decision improvement is

    25/8 - 11/4 = 3/8 normalized units per original cluster.

A real audit's value is net of its actual cost, expiry, opportunity cost and effects on the source-to-qualification process. If the audit costs c>=3/8 in **consistent units** and changes nothing else, it is not attractive on this illustrative prior. If the audit is only a technical shadow benchmark rather than an original-order economically predictive signal, the 3/8 computation is inapplicable. If signal-driven conditioning is infeasible at policy-selection time, the information does not create the stated choice option.

Now suppose X is a source-trained proposal that still fails the preceding observed-state recovery admission because its possible alpha/beta outcomes are observationally indistinguishable. **X is excluded from F**, regardless of how promising a shadow benchmark appears. A certified observation refinement G or independently resourced rescue embodiment E may alter F and the subsequent economic ranking, but that is an intervention altering feasibility and resources, not a free Blackwell-information comparison. Never run a physically unadmitted X to cure an off-policy-support problem. Grounding can thus serve two different functions, physical admissibility and economic information, each needing a separate justified mechanism.

## Coherent learning/data interpretation

For heterogeneous field, failure/recovery, routine accepted production, teleoperation, simulation, synthetic and public/pretrained inputs, keep different source lineage, preprocessing/compute, rights, safety and target opportunity. A source-to-qualification map changes the law of jointly qualified original service; the resulting production policy changes the distribution of future data exposures. Production data is generally **policy selected**. A carefully designed target probe could add missing information; more existing-policy data can instead improve measurement precision only where it already has support. Neither trajectory counts nor benchmark percentages are economic units.

At date t, write J_t(s)=max_{a in F_t(s)} {r_t(s,a)-k_t(a)+delta E[J_(t+1)(s')|s,a]}. Future production-generated data is in the state transition to s', and any future productivity is counted through J_(t+1). An informative, freely ignorable signal weakly enlarges a fixed feasible choice problem's value because the decision-maker can ignore it. Forced training, sensor installation that consumes operator time, changed markets or a new embodiment fail the required fixed-feasible-set conditions.

The constructive empirical implication is *not* "randomize unsafe controllers." First certify feasible observation/recovery and operator/market boundaries using appropriately authorized independent evidence. Then, among approved complete policy arms and independently schedulable customer clusters, measure original-order treatment coverage and outcomes; separately test target-stratum transport. An objective cannot be declared economically identified before both gates are crossed.

## November 22 acceptance / Models A–B integration

**Model A (current qualified market production).** Admissible policies and original-buyer eligible clusters must be specified; observed policy overlap plus complete-market outcomes identifies means of those approved complete policies. Without overlap, report sharp bounded identified sets instead of pseudo-rankings. A genuinely certified old fallback is stronger than preserving a software binary; do not substitute one for the other.

**Model B (learning and data-generated future options).** Exposure data selection is endogenous to admitted action and customer response. Any subsequent learning, target audit, source dataset or new embodiment must affect the same J recursion via a measured or explicitly hypothetical transition and a valid feasible set. The audit's value of information enters the optimized continuation once. The exact prior-illustration is a conditional standard decision result, not a calibrated robotics data price.

The largest open *scientific acceptance* gap is still a real signed observation/transition-recovery network and original customer-panel support for at least one complete buyer–robot–operator comparison, including legal dispatch rights and policy/menu response. No such evidence was obtained here. This chapter improves the identification criterion, not the field evidence. November 22 remains OPEN; December 25, 2026, 23:59 Eastern unchanged.
