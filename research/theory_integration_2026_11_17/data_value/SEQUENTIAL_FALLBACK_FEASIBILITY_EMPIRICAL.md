# Prospective original-order and physical retry feasibility contract

**20 November 2026 UTC. Documentation/design only; no private data access, outreach, physical intervention, recruitment or purchases.**

## Audit the architecture before statistical extrapolation

Record the **complete original customer–robot–learner–operator network**, not an accepted-only robotic motion set: original eligible and refused buyer/order IDs, site and common parent procurement budget, requested physical service and competing specialist, rights/SLA/payment/refunds, pre-treatment clock and task state, which bodies/operators share resources, controller/source snapshot and human fallback permissions.

For every proposed sequential X-first then R-on-failure path, require a signed feasible intervention specification: versioned X action and early-stop logic; exact definition of an X failure versus unsafe fault; post-X object/environment/body state; safe reachability of the original R recipe; verified time remaining against contracted SLA; operator schedule after all other work; human rights and available recovery capacity; data retention rights. Physical safety review must address unobserved rare state tails. A historical zero failure rate is not a universal guarantee.

**Distinct, noninterchangeable estimands:** `Pr(X=1)` technical first-attempt qualification, `b=Pr(X=0,H=0)` sequential retry failure, `Pr(R0=1)` original R qualification, `ell=Pr(R0=1,X=0,H=0)` old qualified regressions (potential-outcome pair requiring validated design), `E[B_F Q_F]` paid original-service completion, operator hours/displaced jobs and actual collected/retained margin. The audit's coarse Fréchet bound uses r and b; a more informative conditional bound additionally requires physically valid `a=Pr(R0=1,X=0)`. Do not infer a from separate R and X pilot means.

### Designs that could distinguish the mechanisms, conditional on separate approval

- A non-actuating shadow simulation and engineering state reachability proof can support **structural physical implications**, but success in simulation alone is not a production safety certificate.
- If physically approved and legal, randomly assign **entire independent network units** to complete R, X-only, X-then-R fallback, G and E configurations where available, and observe every original customer opportunity with zeros, failed orders, displaced production, resource use, pricing and collected cash. Randomized whole-arm economic means do not require observing `ell`; they do not prove no casewise loss.
- Where an independent signed nondestructive common-initial-state qualification assessment exists, record the joint R/X state and post-X retry support, rather than invent counterfactual replay on the same irreversible customer task.
- Separate deliberate targeted collection, incident/failure recovery logs, production-generated field trajectories, teleoperation, simulation, synthetic and public/pretrained data lineages. Log training/compute/curation/rights costs, approved old controllers and source-specific changes in safe visited-state composition.
- Match original buyer quality terms and offered prices, refusals, receipts, refunds and SLA timeliness. The physical nested-Q claim and the legally paid BQ claim are distinct tests. An eligible customer who declines to purchase remains in the denominator.

**Decision rule.** If casewise physical retry feasibility cannot be certified, do not deploy F on the strength of population averages. If independently approved whole-network trial and commercial registry exist, estimate the complete R/X/G/E policy value under actual customer/market response and operator scarcity, without claiming mechanical Q nesting. If neither exists, retain structural bounds and an inconclusive applied ranking.

The verified official BLS 2025–2035 code-tagged employment/output slice is useful for the broader sector opportunity atlas, but **does not** replace an original-order robot network panel or provide a numerical BEA input-output edge.
