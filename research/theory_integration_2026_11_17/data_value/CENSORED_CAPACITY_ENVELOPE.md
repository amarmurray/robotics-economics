# Censored qualification: economic capacity value from accepted-order ledgers
25 November 2026 Eastern. Extension of the shared-capacity bridge within the compact first paper, not a new paper. The question is which evidence distinguishes a data investment from additional physical or organizational capacity when settlement records censor qualified but unserved orders.

## Information distinction
Fix an original simultaneously eligible cohort with strictly positive known net contribution weights w1>=...>=wn and a common deterministic tie order, K>=1 interchangeable dated slots, no other constraints, and latent binary readiness A (qualified, authorized, and certain to deliver the specified paid service in this restricted terminal model). The dispatcher observes A and deterministically serves the top K ready orders S, or all ready if fewer than K. The analyst observes original opportunity IDs, S, K and weights but not A for unserved orders; all observed S are actual settled complete service. Let f_K(A) be the weighted top-K objective. Distinguish deterministic optimal dispatch as a maintained empirical restriction from actual routing or acceptance. If the dispatcher cannot observe readiness or the selected order can fail, these implications need not hold: compare realized whole-program receipts and report the separate oracle gap; never impute an unserved order's qualification from settlement alone.

The log identifies f_K(A) and min(K,N_j) for each weight-ranked prefix j (N_j is its number of ready orders). It does NOT identify N_j, and hence not the return to expanding the fleet. If independent, same-window-population marginal qualification probabilities p_i are available, the *total weighted overflow* E[sum_i w_i A_i - f_K(A)] is identified by subtraction. Yet even that statistic is generally insufficient to value the next slot: it includes all currently unserved ready orders, which may be simultaneous and mutually competing. This resolves an important distinction in the preceding [shared capacity result](SHARED_CAPACITY_ENVELOPE.md): ordinary paid output may identify the current terminal objective, not the value of an untried resource intervention or a differently trained policy.

## Sharp accepted-log envelope for capacity expansion
For a logged selection s, let q_s=Pr(S=s), and let E_s be the set of strictly later-ranked unserved orders than the worst served order if |s|=K; if |s|<K let E_s be empty. Every selected order must be ready; every unselected order outside E_s must be unready. Conversely, *any* readiness subset of E_s appended to s yields exactly the logged selection. Thus given independent marginal p_i, define residual r_i=p_i-Pr(i in S). Let x_si=Pr(S=s,A_i=1) for i in E_s, and set it to zero otherwise. The feasible allocations X obey 0<=x_si<=q_s and sum_s x_si=r_i. They exist iff 0<=r_i<=sum_{s:i in E_s}q_s for each i. These conditions test the maintained dispatch restriction and population comparability, not necessarily optimality if they hold.

Add ell>=1 interchangeable slots in the same window, holding A, prices, demand, rights and other costs fixed. For j=1,...,n let d_j=w_j-w_{j+1}, w_{n+1}=0. Conditional residual prefix ready counts M_sj=sum_{i<=j,i in E_s} A_i. The incremental gross capacity value is sum_s q_s sum_j d_j E[min(ell,M_sj)|S=s]. Define T_ell(x_{s,1:j}) as the sum of the ell largest entries (pad with zeros). Then the sharp identified interval conditional on S distribution and p is
L = min_{x in X} sum_s sum_j d_j T_ell(x_{s,1:j}),
U = max_{x in X} sum_s sum_j d_j min(ell q_s,sum_{i<=j} x_si).
Proof: conditional on s, apply the previous uniform-matroid prefix envelope to residual readiness with marginals x_si/q_s for q_s>0. Multiply by q_s. The comonotone lower coupling and cumulative-interval cyclic upper coupling attain all prefix endpoints simultaneously for each s. Feasible allocations X impose only the column marginal and row eligibility constraints; constructing these conditional laws separately and mixing by q_s preserves exactly both the original log and p. Thus any feasible x attains its conditional endpoints, and compact X yields global endpoints. Mixing the two optimizing joint laws fills the interval. No assumption of independent latent qualifications. Both are linear programs: for L, epigraph variables bound each sum of up to ell entries in a prefix (or use the standard top-ell hinge representation); for U, hypograph variables bound ell q_s and prefix sum. Nonnegative d_j justifies the optimization direction. The discrete empirical frequency model with indivisible windows may restrict endpoints; on an equally weighted finite panel, the executable checker enumerates feasible integer conditional marginal allocations separately from realized latent matrices, and verifies attainability with the finite grid analogue of the two couplings. The population theorem is not inferred from that check.

With no independent p, simply use x=0 for the lower endpoint and x_si=q_s on E_s for the upper. With ell>=n-K and optimal dispatch, additional capacity removes all overflow, so its gross return equals total weighted overflow when p is observed. These formulas value a conditional terminal expansion under unchanged readiness; if extra slots themselves change demand or data production, or if grounding alters the readiness law across sites, use the common full-history Model B. For fixed cost k in common units, subtract it once from both endpoints; compare to the best rival's net interval, not just zero.

## A capacity decision that the usual logs cannot identify
Three orders worth 12, 8, 6 share K=1. A is always qualified and served. B and C each qualify in half of windows but are unserved. If they are coincident, one extra slot produces mean 4; if disjoint, it produces mean 7. Both economies have identical initial accepted-order logs, identical technical marginals (1,.5,.5), identical weighted potential 19, and identical weighted overflow 7. A slot costing 5 yields net -1 versus +2. Two extra slots yield mean 7 in both. In the coincident world, one extra slot releases only the more valuable of the two jointly ready tasks; in the disjoint world, one slot covers all. This is a synthetic capacity counterfactual, not customer field data. A deliberate grounding action that only improves lower-ranked qualifications can be redundant with current capacity but become valuable when extra slots are installed. Whether that joint intervention dominates the best independently evolving C/P/X/E alternative still depends on its own readiness law, costs and continuation. A shared learning update can also change A and the distribution of S; never splice stratum-specific policies from incompatible histories.

## Decision-specific evidence and empirical protocol
For the one-slot or multi-slot restricted expansion, the sufficient additional statistic is the economically weighted *clipped* residual prefix count, E[min(ell,(N_j-K)_+)] (unconditional over original windows). It differs from untruncated overflow; on idle windows it is zero under the maintained rule. Log all original offered orders with values and deadlines, selected recipe IDs and accepted settlements, capacity K, dispatch-time authorization and rank/tie rule, and learner/checkpoint lineage. Record predispatch readiness for original unscheduled orders through independent safe qualification evidence when lawful; do not actually dispatch unsafe jobs to measure it. Randomly sampled saturated windows with *joint* readiness observation identify clipped residual counts via inverse inclusion weights under known positive window-level inclusion probabilities and valid independent observation; independent per-order marginals alone do not estimate the union/count truncation. If only partial readiness is ascertainable, retain the conditional envelope with known positive/negative statuses as extra row restrictions or use full latent-state LP. An audit should target windows and order prefixes whose economic weights can change the rival investment ranking. Treat sampling intervals separately from sharp population identification. Observed realized C and CG contributions identify their fixed-program terminal contrast directly when fully followed and validly assigned; the capacity envelope answers a different counterfactual and is not an extra data premium. If dispatcher readiness is latent too, selected service is not an oracle; keep fixed-policy ITT and separately bound oracle regret rather than applying this theorem mechanically.
