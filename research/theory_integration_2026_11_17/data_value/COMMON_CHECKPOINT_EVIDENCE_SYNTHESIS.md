## Why resetting both experimental arms to the winning checkpoint matters

Use the existing controlled law m. At date t state s contains actual controller, source stock, rights, operator capacity, demand and legally available reports. Let fixed programs C (mixed curated reuse) and H (HITL only) have stage contribution r_t^a and kernel K_t^a, including acquisition costs once; terminal value phi is distinct net accepted production. A common-checkpoint contrast at state s identifies a conditional difference in the selected measured outcome at s, not the closed sequence C versus H. Even if a study measured net economic r rather than a proxy, the exact recursion for a fixed-policy contrast would be

    D_t(s) = r_t^C(s)-r_t^H(s)
             + beta (K_t^C-K_t^H)V_{t+1}^H(s)
             + beta K_t^C D_{t+1}(s).

Here operators act on continuation functions at the next state; D=V^C-V^H, with common terminal function D_T=0. Subtract Bellman evaluation equations and add/subtract K_C V_H to prove. This is standard finite-horizon policy evaluation, not novel RL. The cross-kernel term cannot be computed merely from observed technical improvements at selected C checkpoints. If a genuine economic one-step advantage against H continuation is nonnegative at every C-reachable state, its expected discounted sum under C is nonnegative (iterate the recursion). A technical outcome improvement at a single checkpoint is not that advantage. Shared learners and changing buyer law belong inside s and K.

### Sharp unsupported-branch countermodel

Two learning rounds, zero interim contribution and beta=1. Common starting state s0. C first reaches c1, H reaches h1; observed first-round technical scores 1 and 0. At c1, second-round C and H yield technical scores 2 and 1. Both worlds have those same observations. No study follows H from h1 in round two. Suppose terminal contribution from C is v=2, H from h1 is either 0 or 4; common-checkpoint H from c1 yields 1. Normalize costs equal; all recipes safe, feasible, lawful and purchased only when authorized. Even making technical score equal to terminal contribution in this synthetic example (NOT a conversion of actual benchmark results), C−H is +2 or −2. For independently bounded H continuation h in [L,U] and known C terminal v, fixed-arm contrast with total committed costs kC,kH lies sharply in [v−U−kC+kH, v−L−kC+kH]. Proof: affine monotonicity, with both endpoints achievable by altering only the unsupported h1 transition. No restrictions on hidden transitions beyond stated bounds. The intervals are conditional, not empirical. In the general recursion, known economic occupancy, uniform validated kernel bounds or matched independent program rollouts can tighten this interval; a forced C arm is not an optimized menu with H retained. If all H policies remain feasible without incremental cost, optional access to C cannot lower the optimum.
