# Matched protocol — diagnostic transport and pool-level learning interference

**Prospective only; no experiment, private access or robot deployment conducted.** Continues [COUPLED_IDENTIFICATION_EMPIRICAL.md](COUPLED_IDENTIFICATION_EMPIRICAL.md) and the integrated manuscript. Policy effects are measured on *accepted customer service and full opportunity denominators*, not estimated from benchmark percentages.

## Identification unit and eligibility

Pre-register a site × customer demand stratum D **before** route assignment, source–target embodiment combination, controller checkpoint lineage, policy rights, customer-order registry (including zero and canceled follow-on orders), safe alternative specialist and actual operator/recovery budgets. Freeze starting controllers and define the full qualification recipe: tasks bundled, throughput, error/recovery, assisted service, required human/organizational resources, end-to-end customer acceptance, service period and price/capture. Separate actual customer business from internal test trajectories.

**Pool is the assignment unit** whenever robots share training/checkpoints, constrained teleoperators, coordination or customer demand. A robot-level randomization would violate the no-interference assumption. For eligible independent pools, pre-specify pure R, pure G, mixed R/G, separately isolated R/G and available embodiment/specialist/no-training alternatives. Use two-stage/cluster assignment or explicit randomized saturation to learn pool direct/indirect effects, keeping access/rights and *per-pool* resource constraints commensurate. A valid static treatment contrast is not automatically an adaptive multi-stage policy estimand.

## Diagnostic calibration test

Record Z and D on the same complete pre-action population, then obtain an independently justified safe H-related label if feasible; evaluate \(P(Z=1\mid H=h,D=d)\), not merely pooled sensitivity/false-positive rate. An H label based only on the same simulator or controller confidence score is circular. If latent H cannot be safely audited, direct randomized route-level accepted-service outcome contrasts can identify expected route economics under appropriate exchangeability and follow-up. Preserve the two counterexample worlds in the theory as *non-identification absent this test*, not as claims that different industries actually have these regimes.

Report joint \(D,Z\), joint validated \(H,D,Z\) where possible, full order denominator, service acceptance, recovery peak and cumulative operator use. Analyze D-by-report interaction; validate on held-out sites/embodiments/dates and allow sensitivity/false-positive intervals. Do not condition on a customer order that is generated *after* route selection when defining the pretreatment population.

## Pool interference test

Compare pure and route-mixed **shared-controller pools** and mixed **isolated-controller pools** using frozen starting policies and matched site opportunity. Log updates, conflict/catastrophic-forgetting rates, genuinely new jointly accepted recipes, compute/curation costs, full source rights, body hardware and qualification delays. Test whether the randomized discordant pair rate equals \(2x(1-x)\), whether an observed conditional accepted-service difference has a stable sign L, and whether third-robot or cross-site network effects invalidate pairs. Observe independent/cluster bootstrap or design-based uncertainty; do not naively count within-pool robots as independent observations.

Measure actual separate-controller replication and validation overhead c; never assign a software cost just to create a theoretical information premium. If retention makes old qualified policies available at negligible cost, include the free isolation benchmark. If a mixed pool needs a rare recovery > the approved cap, exclude it or use a *different preattempt-abort policy*; no mean-risk shortcut. Field trajectories, intervention recoveries, teleop, simulation, synthetic and pretrained inputs remain separate in provenance and what they physically identify.

## Direct falsification and economics

- **DC1/2:** Subgroup calibration invariant enough to narrow t across the R–G threshold? Alternatively, do randomized complete-route outcomes rank policies without latent t? Fail if reported H does not track target qualification.
- **PI1/2:** Is mixed-pool accepted-service production additive after actual overhead, positively complementary, or destructively interfering? Fail if frozen old policies eliminate any incremental loss or treatment spillovers invalidate random pairing.
- **Customer demand:** Does follow-on order flow differ by route and D even after pre-registration? Estimate effect on total potential service and chosen outside options, not just technical success among observed orders.
- **Safety:** Are all supported histories within hard approved recovery and operator limits, including peaks? Finite absence of high-tail events is not safety certification.
- **Atlas:** Link verified industry/occupation codes to the specific **required service recipes**, with human/robot coordination and customer accepted units. No occupation is automatically robot-addressable and aggregate wage bills are not deployment value.

### Measurement falsifiers and next smallest safe action

A matched design should be evaluated for feasibility **before** assigning experimental work. The highest-value safe observational upgrade is a D-stratified joint report/whole-service acceptance panel and controller lineage, including every eligible opportunity and absent/zero customer order. If cross-route learning is authorized, use independent whole-controller-pool assignments and independently audit recovery support; otherwise preserve the partial-identification interval and document why experiments are not feasible. The November 22 synthesis remains an open gate, not an automatic paper.
