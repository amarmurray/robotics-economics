# Instructor solutions — sequential original-buyer and worker audit

For [student exercises](LONGITUDINAL_AUDIT_EXERCISES.md). **Conditional synthetic deductions only.** Set safe common contribution 6 aside; costs are already charged once.

**1 — Full-information dynamic policy.** Under the connected-cluster intervention and sequential exchangeability, conditional on a full potential scenario and target legal actions, the logged assignment (A_0=a), audit (S_1=1), and subsequent matching action have probability (p_0(a)rho_1e_1). Terminal audit (S_2=1) contributes conditional probability (rho_2) *after* (A_1). Hence the complete-case inverse weight is their reciprocal. Multiplying the observed entire paid contribution by the event indicator and integrating these choices sequentially returns the full target-policy value for every potential scenario, hence in expectation under the common scenario law. Late (S_2), original terminal buyer order and paid settlement cannot be used to choose the earlier (A_1).

**2 — Queue.** Under a conserved worker resource, (J_1le1) is discharged at the intermediate date; a newly admitted (J_2=1) occupies the only shared operator in the terminal service window. Thus its terminal qualified buyer reward is exactly zero. If demand on this blocked service affects posthorizon demand, updates the learner, changes the next job duration, or affects a contract, the buyer variable is not payoff irrelevant to a longer horizon and the reduced audit is generally invalid.

**3 — Paired program ranking.** For G/probe with uniform latent ((z,w)in{00,01,10,11}), the four normalized net contributions are (4/5,3/10,-1/5,13/10), averaging (11/20). E/adaptive averages (3/4). G-probe selected positive-record probabilities are proportional to (1/16,3/32,1/16,3/16), respectively; thus its matched complete-case mean is (99/130>19/28). The actual full-program ranking favors E, although the selected G-probe mean exceeds the selected E-adaptive mean 19/28. P's legally feasible best policy is 1/2, so full **true** menu E>G>P; the corresponding selected naive best is G>E>P. The example stipulates causal effects, licensed menu restrictions, sampling rates and normalized costs; it offers no evidence of any real deployment return or ranking.

**4 — Minimal audit.** For a pre-terminal audit history with (J_2=1), future buyer reward is known zero and the remaining whole-program reward is determined by earlier observed events plus costs. Its random terminal audit factor (S_2/rho_2) has conditional expectation one. Replacing this factor with one is conditional expectation of the former complete-case estimator, reducing its second moment through the law of total variance. The exact G/probe second moment falls (391/25 -151/10=27/50). This need not be globally optimal: deterministic costs can be subtracted outside the random term, nuisance-model information can improve estimation, and an extended horizon may make the buyer data relevant.

**5 — Unsupported action.** Let a reachable history (h) have (e_1(Tmid h)=0). Set the logged (R) transition and receipt the same in two common potential-history worlds. In world 1, (T) would generate a qualified, accepted original buyer service; in world 2, (T) would fail qualification and create an unserviceable recovery job. No logged (T) event in (h) distinguishes these laws; the target (pi(Tmid h)>0) has different value. Even perfect (Y) auditing on observed (R) cannot repair the missing treatment. Only restrictions justified independently, authorized assignment or honest partial bounds may narrow the gap.

**6 — Rights, future information and measurement.** Record unserved original demand on the blocked (J_2=1) path if the learner or later buyer transition depends on it; in an extended Bellman value, the term (V_{t+1}(L',D',B')) is then a function of that previously omitted Y, so the terminal-zero shortcut fails. An action propensity is a probability of *executed lawful treatment* conditional on observed history, not a licence, physical worker, independent embodiment, or proxy for an unexecuted causal outcome. Sequential support and consistent connected-cluster exposure are independently necessary.

Closest methodological prior art: Robins–Hernán–Brumback (2000) marginal structural models; Jiang–Li (2016) off-policy evaluation; Farboodi–Koh–Xia (2026) *Data-Driven Automation* §5/Appendix B. None of the conditional weighting or Rao–Blackwell identities is claimed new.
