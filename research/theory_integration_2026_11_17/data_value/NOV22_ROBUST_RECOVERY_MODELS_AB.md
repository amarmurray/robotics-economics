# November 22 Models A–B adjudication: what finite recovery can establish

**20 November 2026 UTC; November 22 scientific gate OPEN.** This is one continuing argument, not a new first-paper claim.

**Shared economic environment.** Enumerate original eligible customer demands and refusals; qualified physical recipes for the *entire* robot–operator–buyer network; the customer's actual accepted menu and net receipts; rights, training, operator, manufacturing and coordination costs; and any retained specialist/legacy alternatives. A versioned physical game can establish a *necessary/sufficient conditional admission criterion* for one X-first/R-retry recipe only if all transitions, timing, and rights are sound and conservatively represented. The original R policy's physical robustness is a winning set, not a buyer order.

**Model A: market production.** For each physically admitted complete R/X/G/E recipe, optimize original market's net accepted production subject to shared operator, body, customer budget and buyer-choice constraints. An extra safe robot recipe can be of zero value where buyers do not purchase its service or its operator use displaces a higher-contribution order. A preaction gate changes which routes qualify; it does not by itself identify the market value of X data versus G grounding or new E bodies. If approved complete-network original-order randomized arms are available, design-based mean comparisons can be identified without identifying individual R/X coupling. This is distinct from obtaining a universal all-cases safety certificate.

**Model B: endogenous data and organization.** An admitted policy changes where service is attempted and which field failures, recoveries, teleoperated trajectories and production byproduct data are obtained; learning updates the next target support. Model B's continuation accounts for this production-data option once. Novelty is not the fact that future data has value (DDA §5) or that data aggregate complementarily (DDA Appendix B); closest mechanisms include controlled invariance/shielding, dynamic treatment policies and partial identification. Technical source transfer and economic value need not share a sign under changing buyer menus and resource bottlenecks.

**Key negative test.** In the finite example, allowing one possible operator-blocking X failure eliminates worst-case safe admission even if average X qualification rises. Pricing the X intervention at the same robot benchmark percentage as a qualified order would be invalid. A stricter support model may sacrifice near-term X action exposure—and thereby reduce routine production-generated training data—while preserving old service. The economic choice is among permitted *full policies*, including deliberate target audit, R, X with the actual gate, G, E and specialist, and never includes unsafe hypothetical counterfactual service.

**Closest prior-art objection.** Finite winning regions and safe shielding are known mathematical control results, original-arm causal randomization is standard design-based inference, and DDA explicitly values dynamic data/spillovers. The integrated qualified original-buyer production treatment is a **candidate explanatory synthesis** only. To advance toward a paper contribution, show empirically that the physics admission/target transfer constraints materially reorder production investment relative to credible old/specialist and matched source-training/grounding alternatives. Without observed policy-closed buyer/robot evidence, Nov22 should not be marked passed. Future milestone gates, including December 25, 23:59 Eastern, stay unchanged.

**Next exact action:** audit one physically and commercially definable whole-network intervention class and the minimum original-buyer measurement unit; distinguish verified safe-state support from selected successful robot logs. If not available, retain open identification and proceed with mathematical sensitivity/field design instead of fabricated data.
