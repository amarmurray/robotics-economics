# November 22 Model A/B reconciliation — matched buyer/qualification support

**20 November 2026 UTC. A scientific review input, NOT a passed November 22 gate.**

**Model A: qualified production with menu/parent/customer closure.** An original eligible order becomes capturable value only if actually bought, safely delivered, rights-valid and payable under the whole operator/robot network. The production LP and complete-service resource-price certificates survive as conditional upper bounds when customer acceptance/budget and true operator capacity are independently bounded; a physical slot does not imply a sale. Optimized production V selects legal pricing and preserves the incumbent only when retaining it remains physically and contractually feasible.

**Model B: endogenous source/grounding/embodiment choice.** X learns from heterogeneous source modalities, G pays to align/ground and potentially save recovery, E purchases or qualifies a new body, R retains a verified old system. Learning changes joint qualification Q; menus, buyer outside alternatives and shared budgets change purchase C. The product C AND Q is economically decisive. Existing static and two-date learning state transitions are inherited; nothing adds a separate future-data bonus to optimized continuation value. A finite baseline (C0,Q0) paired law together with externally defended upper cross-policy switch fractions u_C,u_Q gives exact two-bit transport bounds on P(C1 AND Q1). This is a decision-support *special case*, not a substitute for the general source-to-qualification joint distribution or model of the full economy.

**Sharp diagnostic and rejected hypothesis.** With first order q=1/2, legal prices4/6 and G cost1/2, the threshold for G over X is global paired s_G>3/8. The old crude joint drift upper 2/5 fails to decide; actual baseline cell support gives s_G<=7/20 and certifies X>G in synthetic case A. A different baseline and tighter switch budgets gives s_G>=2/5 and certifies G>X in case B. These opposite results are not robot measurements. Rejected claim: small observed policy marginal changes imply small individual potential-response switch budgets. They do not without structural coupling.
