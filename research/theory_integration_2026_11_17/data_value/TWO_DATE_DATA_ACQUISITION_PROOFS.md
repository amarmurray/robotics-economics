# Proof ledger — endogenous two-date data acquisition and original-order production

**19 November 2026; synthetic conditional mathematics, not physical evidence or scientific originality.** Chapter: [TWO_DATE_DATA_ACQUISITION.md](TWO_DATE_DATA_ACQUISITION.md).

1. **Bellman reduction (D1).** Condition on the full date-0 safe action and subsequent observed history, then optimize remaining accepted-service production over feasible stage-1 rules. The tower property and a finite action/history model give D1; costs appear exactly at the acquisition stage. If the stage-1 training uses a shared resource, represent it in the transition/feasibility rather than subtracting it twice. Joint qualification outcomes can be arbitrarily dependent because D1 integrates full histories, not products of marginals.

2. **Routine information dominance.** For every A policy, use a state-independent garbling of R's observation to simulate the same A signal. Equality of physical state and remaining feasible menus implies equality of continuation value, while date-0 contribution is no smaller under R. Optimal R dominates. The common-state coupling and greater information are separately necessary to this sufficient condition; **100 natural traces** are not by themselves a Blackwell certificate.

3. **The selected report is uninformative conditional on original regime.** Latent q_H=3/5, q_L=2/5; P(O=h|H)=(3/5)(2/3)=2/5, P(O=h|L)=(2/5)(1)=2/5, and low reports never recorded. Thus P(O=missing|H)=P(O=missing|L)=3/5. For any n conditionally independent selected events the likelihood ratio is one for every event history, and posterior remains prior 1/2. More data does not fix lack of information support. The data may still have physical-learning value in an alternative model; only the frozen diagnostic inference is proved.

4. **Optimization over complete qualified terminal menu.** In twentieths, I=(40,40), Z=(120,−80), X=(130,−70), G=(140,−120), E=(80,60). Their no-report equal-prior means are 40,20,30,10,70 twentieths respectively. Thus best is E=70/20=7/2. R earns 20/20 now, so total 90/20=9/2. Perfect report selects G high=140/20 and E low=60/20; minus audit cost 4/20 gives (140+60)/40−4/20=96/20=24/5. Difference 6/20=3/10. Under a perfect free R report, 20/20+(140+60)/40=6.

5. **Derivation of (D2).** A symmetric binary audit with P(correct)=α and equal H/L prior produces P(report=h)=P(report=l)=1/2, and posteriors α and 1−α. On the high report, X improves on E iff 10α−7/2≥3+α, equivalent to α≥13/18. G improves on X iff 13α−6≥10α−7/2, equivalent to α≥5/6. G versus E crosses at α=3/4, but X lies above G until 5/6; I and Z are dominated in the resulting upper envelope. At low report, E is best for α≥1/2. Average the two conditional maxima and subtract the audit 1/5 to obtain (D2). The high-report upper envelope and low-report E values are continuous at both boundaries. On final segment D2 exceeds 9/2 iff α>19/20; lower segments are below. This is global optimization because feasible randomized mixtures cannot exceed the best pure terminal action for each report under risk-neutral linear expectation and no shared terminal coupling.

6. **Budget feasibility and timing.** Acquisition cap B=6/5. Auditing 1/5 and subsequently choosing G or E at cost 1 is feasible, just as routine and any one terminal action are feasible. The gross date-0 routine 1 is not inserted as an extra explicit cost into A: it is the foregone positive opportunity captured by the between-policy comparison. Source X is hypothetical deterministic *validated* post-training qualification; a stochastic physical qualification law would require expanded Bellman histories and retained failure branches, and no real intervention is authorized.

7. **Nonmonotonic marginal information value.** The old menu I/Z gives no-report max 2 and perfect-report average (6+2)/2=4. Adding E gross (5,4) raises no-report to 9/2 and perfect-report to (6+4)/2=5; its information premium falls to 1/2. Adding G gross (8,−5) instead leaves no-report optimum at 2 and raises perfect-report to (8+2)/2=5; its premium rises to 3. Blackwell monotonicity of gross value for a fixed menu remains valid throughout; only the *incremental report premium across different menus* is nonmonotone.

8. **Proof boundaries.** The statewise payoff table presupposes compatible accepted original-order service and no shared terminal resource conflict across the single chosen recipe. Fixed equal priors, binary symmetric signal and terminal qualification completion are assumptions, not estimates. More heterogeneous source, teleoperation, simulated, synthetic and field data must have separate measured transition kernels, and dynamically changing customer demand invalidates the frozen example. If the physical action set differs across information arms, direct qualified whole-network policy comparison replaces the simple Blackwell theorem.
