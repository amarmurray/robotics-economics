## Priors: production is a network of feasible transformations, not a list of automatable tasks

A robot is a dated, rival physical resource. Its controller, qualifying evidence, retained source data and authorization are different economic objects: each affects the feasible transformations, and only some are reusable across bodies or locations. A qualified transformation can enable another downstream service, or compete with an existing path to the same final customer. The productive return to a body therefore depends on the entire network of *alternative and complementary* transformations, not its standalone task success or utilization. In an end-market setting, a warehouse receiving, routing, pick and pack chain is a candidate illustration; its robotics investment is constrained by the other stages, and by how many customer orders can actually be fulfilled. The same applies to a manufacturing chain with downstream inspection. The unit of output here is a complete accepted customer service; intermediate activity is not another sale.

Task-allocation theory (Acemoglu and Restrepo, prior scaffold), data and learning complementarity (Data-Driven Automation, section 5 and Appendix B, prior audit), and economic production-network aggregation are prior foundations. Baqaee and Farhi, *The Microeconomic Foundations of Aggregate Production Functions* (NBER WP25293, November 2018/revised November 2019, https://www.nber.org/papers/w25293) explicitly derive aggregate responses from heterogeneous producers and linkages. Their *Nonlinear Production Networks* (WP27281, May 2020, https://www.nber.org/papers/w27281) studies complementary input bottlenecks and heterogeneous shocks. Abstracts inspected 25 November. Ford and Fulkerson, *Maximal Flow Through a Network* (1956, https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1956-045-5), publisher extract inspected, is the classical source of the flow/cut result. The arguments below specialize this established mathematics; neither the cut theorem nor learning-by-doing is our originality claim. The candidate synthesis is to make the economically relevant topology endogenous to qualification, source reuse and deployment programs, and to specify what must be measured to choose between learning and embodied capital. No general-equilibrium aggregation or measured production function is claimed.

## 1. Restricted Model A: dated accepted flow

At a lawful predispatch history, construct a finite directed acyclic dated graph (the cut result also holds with cycles). Each arc consumes or transfers one homogeneous unit of the same service token; each unit traverses a complete path from source to final accepted sink. Include an explicit final demand cap and any independent time/qualification/rights gating as arcs. Capacities c_e(g,k,h) are nonnegative and encode qualified service per dated arc under learning/coordination program g and body vector k. A missing qualified recipe is represented by a zero arc. A positive final per-unit net contribution v is common across complete paths, with genuine variable costs already accounted once. This exact restriction excludes path-specific costs, yields, joint-input assembly, multicommodity competition and shared bodies/operators unless their shared resource can be represented faithfully by a single common arc without introducing infeasible routes. Otherwise use the general recipe LP or lawful integer schedules in Model A. A time-expanded graph can be used only with valid carryover and conservation. Extra source/target/embodiment interventions may alter c differently; the new body need not be one unit of every arc. For an indivisible body define its supported simultaneous net capacity change vector d, not its nameplate rate.

Let F(c) be max accepted throughput, and let C index source-sink cuts, C(c) their capacity sums. By max-flow/min-cut, F(c)=min_C C(c). With fixed v, gross one-window value W=v F. Define slack s_C(c)=C(c)-F(c). For any signed feasible capacity change d with c+d>=0, the exact finite contrast is

    W(c+d)-W(c) = v min_C [s_C(c)+sum_(e in C) d_e].      (1)

Proof: substitute the cut representation at c+d and subtract F(c). This works even when the identity of the binding cut changes. A feasible incremental d>=0 with an unchanged old menu gives a nonnegative gross return; an actual training program that retires old policies can have signed d. Changes in demand, rights, prices or allowable paths must be represented, not silently held constant. If a new qualified path is possible in the union graph, use zero initial capacities, and check that adding arcs does not falsely allow mixing incompatible recipes.

For a dedicated extra body on one arc e, let a_e=min capacity of a cut containing e, b_e=min capacity of one excluding e, with min empty set = infinity. The incremental value of capacity delta>=0 is

    MPK_e(delta | g,h) = v[min(a_e+delta,b_e)-min(a_e,b_e)]
                       = v min(delta,(b_e-a_e)_+) if a_e is finite.     (2)

If a_e=infinity, no cut contains e and the return is zero. The proof partitions the cuts into the two classes; if b_e is infinite, the gain equals v delta. With integer capacities and delta=1, the marginal return is v exactly when every minimum cut contains e, otherwise zero. For vector d>=0 the right directional derivative is v times the smallest sum of d_e across current minimum cuts; a finite intervention must still check *all* near-minimum cuts. For positive increments, the economic marginal product is a nonnegative but generally non-additive vector of body-specific finite differences. One isolated task's return must not be summed with another that draws the same body.

For a random joint law of observed histories, common v (or lawful history-specific v), and fixed delta, the one-window expected single-edge gross return is E[v min(delta,S_e)], S_e=(b_e-a_e)_+ (zero if a_e is infinite). Equivalently for constant delta, integrate E[v 1{S_e>z}] over z from 0 to delta. For an integer one-unit body it is an economically weighted probability of being in all minimum cuts. This is not the probability of being individually utilized, nor a mean success rate. The distribution of **cut slack**, conditional on program, demand, qualification and resource incidence, is a sufficient statistic for this restricted terminal contrast. When latent readiness is only known after action this is a full-information oracle, not automatically an implementable policy.

## 2. Propagating learning through the value chain

Holding the body's dedicated capacity increment fixed, a remote learning intervention changes a_e and b_e. Its interaction with body capital is the change in the truncated slack S_e. It is positive when remote changes create more economically usable slack for that body and negative when alternative paths or tighter complements erase it. No fixed sign follows from positive technical transfer. Even learning that does not increase current accepted output can raise the MPK of the next robot. In a serial chain with upstream capacity 1 and downstream capacity 1, a remote downstream improvement to 2 leaves F=1 but increases the upstream next-unit return from zero to v. In a parallel chain with two routes of capacity (1,0) feeding final demand 2, learning on the other route raises its capacity to 1. The existing route's next-unit return falls from v to zero, even as current production rises from 1 to 2. Both use nonnegative capacity changes without negative transfer.

This yields a distinction between capacity-expanding data and bottleneck-shifting data. The latter may have little immediate revenue effect yet change the best allocation of the next capital dollar. Conversely improved utilization or an extra technically feasible route can make more hardware redundant. With a body that can change the capacity of several arcs, use (1); no assumption of independent edge gains or equal capital intensity. Under general fixed-coefficient production LP, the analog is a dual resource price and finite-change transport certificate, not necessarily a cut. A production-network planner's v is final-output net surplus; a deployer's v is its captured contractual net contribution, not downstream revenue attributed to it. A structural economic return per unit of robot, per hour of productive capital, and per dollar invested require different denominators. Capital velocity depends on qualified dated completed throughput and turnaround; an extra body that sits behind a cut adds no terminal throughput.

## 3. Restricted Model B: capital changes the evidence that changes the cut

Consider two dates with a fixed lawful deterministic homogeneous flow network at date 0, current body stock k, and feasible output q in [0,F0(k)] (integers if capacities integral). A program g fixes the rights, reuse, learning and curation rule. Paid production generates retained exposure q under g, then the final network has capacities c1(g,k,q); for simplicity each component is nondecreasing in q and all data collection costs are already in the period net margin v0>=0 or fixed program cost. No additional choice of data composition, other resources, or demand occurs in this restriction. For a fixed discount beta>=0 and final margin v1>=0,

    W_g(k) = max_q [v0 q + beta v1 F(c1(g,k,q))] = v0 q_k + beta v1 F(c1(g,k,q_k)), q_k=F0(k).

Monotonicity proves the optimum at q_k: raising q weakly increases both terms. Let k' be the extra-body stock and q'=F0(k'). Subtract W_g(k) and add/subtract the final flow at (k',q_k):

    Delta W_g = v0(q'-q_k)
      + beta v1 [F(c1(g,k',q_k))-F(c1(g,k,q_k))]
      + beta v1 [F(c1(g,k',q'))-F(c1(g,k',q_k))].       (3)

The second term is the frozen-exposure direct capacity effect; the third is the induced experience effect on the next dated topology, evaluated at the expanded body stock. Each difference is computed by (1) with its own cut slack. The decomposition is exact and path/order specific, not a unique causal mediation decomposition; choosing another telescoping order changes the labels but not total. If q_k is not feasible under the new stock, the middle term is a defined model counterfactual only if supported; it is not a lawful policy guarantee. Committed incremental capital cost and any different learning costs are subtracted once from the whole-program contrast. There is no added data premium beyond future production.

Example: two periods, unit margins, beta=1. k=1 or 2. Date 0 downstream capacity=2 and q=k. At date 1 the upstream body capacity is k, and P has a fixed downstream cap of 1 (the relevant evidence is not usable); G has downstream cap q, through lawful reusable production experience. At the incumbent exposure q=1, both programs have a zero direct date-1 extra-body return. Date-0 gain=1 in each. Yet with q' = 2 the G program earns an additional future unit, giving total gross marginal returns P=1, G=2, so an incremental installed cost 1.5 reverses the investment ranking. The model does not claim every paid job has equal data value. In the general problem exposure is a source-specific state, not scalar q: two production policies with the same q can generate different target coverage, failure/recovery records and rights. They can produce different future cut slack and demand. Then the scalar monotonicity argument fails and Model B must choose the full policy, preserving feasible old options. A higher early body count may generate less useful data if it substitutes autonomous routine paths for rare cases.

## 4. Feasible-policy versus oracle evidence and aggregation

At each supported history an actual qualified feasible complete flow gives a lower throughput bound, any valid cut gives an upper. If arm i has feasible fixed-policy value l_i and a program-wide valid upper u_i, its optimized value is in [l_i,u_i]. Therefore for a body change under g, l_g(k')-u_g(k) <= V_g(k')-V_g(k) <= u_g(k')-l_g(k). Applying the same common-law bounds to both P and G gives a safe cross-difference interval by subtraction (not generally sharp; joint law and shared constraints can tighten it). To certify positive complementarity require the lower G marginal to exceed the upper P marginal; adoption against all competing programs requires complete net J comparisons, not this cross-difference alone. The upper bound cannot sum logged-policy cut gaps over time: new bodies or controllers change exposure and downstream history. Instead either solve the lawful history program, or use a supersolution U_t(h) >= r_t(h,a)+beta E[U_(t+1)(H')|h,a] for every lawful reachable action under each program; terminal U_T bounds salvage. Iterating conditional expectation proves the program-wide bound. A cut envelope can enter r_t or the feasible-action relaxation at each *reachable* state, including counterfactual states, with explicit extension of transitions and constraints; no extra revenue from intermediate nodes. A fixed feasible policy evaluated on its own law remains the lower witness. This uses the existing [dynamic occupancy certificate](DYNAMIC_OCCUPANCY_AUDIT_DUAL.md), not a new general dynamic dual theorem.

Prospective test: predeclare final accepted unit, eligible demand including unserved orders, path and each dated stage's independently qualified capacity, physical occupancy and recovery/inspection slots, valid paths and authorized substitution. For each independently evolving P, X, G, C and E (and relevant combinations), log actual settled final orders and retention/lineage, estimate conditional cut slacks or bound them using certified feasible schedules and resource cut ceilings; vary capacity only where lawfully possible. If shared bodies or multi-input recipes invalidate the single-commodity graph, preserve the full resource incidence/recipe LP instead. The most informative falsifier is a remote learning intervention that changes a measured cut slack and the realized incremental paid capacity response on matched original opportunity windows, while controlling demand and resource incidence. Test fixed-checkpoint direct effects and separately actual induced evidence on later held-out qualifications; compare against an independently evolving cheaper coordination/routine-learning arm. Measure contributions and real incremental costs separately from throughput. No source benchmark score becomes a dollar amount. No matched full program causal return was retrieved.
