# Student exercises — lawful robot-production information

The model is finite, synthetic normalized accepted-and-finally-paid customer value. Use the previous chapters' full-horizon Bellman Q; never interpret it as a robotics dollar forecast.

1. A licensed sensor groups h1 and h2 even though their optimal legal dispatch actions differ. Prove that increasing the *postprocessing* alphabet without acquiring any new lawful observation can never deliver full-information production. Is an unrestricted two-symbol action oracle necessarily feasible?

2. For three equally likely contexts with specialist paid-Q vectors (1,0), (0,1), (1,0), compute W* and W(z) for z=(0,0,1). Find a two-symbol licensed report achieving W* and explain its legal/physical preconditions. Then compare against a fully priced embodiment E worth 3/4 per original opportunity.

3. Assume a new legal report costs 1/8 normalized value and does not affect demand, training or action menus. Compute its net improvement and the SG−E comparison before/after the report. Explain why this cannot be read as a field-estimated license price.

4. Prove the exact action-subset formula for W_L(z). Explain why its sets consist of legally usable **atomic blocks**, rather than histories treated as perfectly observable. Give an example of an atomic block with empty lawful action intersection.

5. A single hidden demand regime takes θ∈{A,B}; action a pays 1 under A and 0 under B; action b pays 0 under A and 1 under B. The parameter is not initially revealed. Compute deterministic and randomized minimax regret, and distinguish per-regime optimum from a single deployable policy. When does a signal make the regret zero?

6. A teleoperation data run produces a report but takes up the only operator slot until a buyer's order expires. Can Proposition 2 prove net value increases? Discuss which Bellman state transitions are needed.
