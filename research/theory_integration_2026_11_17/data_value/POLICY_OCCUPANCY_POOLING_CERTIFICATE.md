# Policy-occupancy support and the value of pooled robotics source data

**November 21, 2026; same When Capabilities Become Production / November 22 Models A–B question. Scientific gate OPEN.** The preceding [uniform complete-event pooling certificate](POOLING_INFLUENCE_CERTIFICATE.md) bounds SG-versus-embodiment (E) production value only if learner/operator/buyer/settlement kernels have small error at *every* common history/action. This is frequently a stronger premise than approved experiments and telemetry can establish. This chapter derives a policy-occupancy alternative, its exact support limitation and a synthetic counterworld. The intended object remains qualified original-buyer **accepted and finally paid production**, not benchmark accuracy and not another premium added to future productive data.

## Economic process and the question

For any a in {SG,E} and availability set A subset of H={F,T,M}, P_(a,A) denotes the actual pooled learner, robot, operator and original-buyer network over a fixed horizon h; Q_(a,A) is a proposed same-market economic reference. F denotes field production/failure/recovery, T licensed teleoperation, M simulation/synthetic data. Public/pretrained inputs, deliberate purchased experiments, and later *production-generated* data remain separately tagged by rights, lineage and time. Coalition membership denotes **optional availability**, never compulsory mixing or replacement of old viable policies. An embodiment can change feasible robot capabilities and legal controllers. A complete history records checkpoint/source ancestry, joint task qualification and failure, robot/operator slots and fallback, buyer orders including unserved/refused, price/demand, refunds, cleared settlement, rights/costs, and new learning. The entire realized contribution Y(history) is bounded in [L,U], width R, with opportunity costs, acquisition, coordination and production resources included once.

Fix identical initial buyer cohorts and feasible, nonanticipating policy class Pi **within a program** P versus Q. E and SG need not share a policy class. Let V_K(pi)=E_(K,pi)[Y] and J_K=sup_(pi in Pi) V_K(pi). Define delta_t(z,u)=TV(P_t(.|z,u),Q_t(.|z,u)) for the **complete** next-event law, including the economic descendants and rights-relevant state. For any fixed pi, let mu_(Q,pi,t) denote Q's history-action occupancy immediately before date t.

## The occupancy-weighted controlled-process lemma

For every such pi,

    |V_P(pi)-V_Q(pi)| <= R min{1, Σ_(t=1)^h E_(mu_(Q,pi,t))[delta_t]}.    (1)

**Proof.** Construct h+1 hybrids, with the first k kernels from Q and remaining kernels from P, using the same history-conditioned policy. Hybrids k−1 and k share the Q distribution of their first k−1 histories and actions; only their next-event kernels differ. Conditional continuation after that event is generated by the *same* P and policy, so the expectation of the complete bounded payoff changes by at most R delta_k. Summing the hybrid differences proves (1); the trivial overall payoff span yields the minimum. Future data learning, buyer price changes and settlement already live in the subsequent events. This is a standard coupling/hybrid simulation inequality, not a novel theorem.

Let pi_P and pi_Q be optimal (or use epsilon-optimal limits), with B(pi)=min{1,Σ_t E_(mu_Q,pi,t)delta_t}. An important asymmetry survives optimization:

    −R B(pi_Q) <= J_P − J_Q <= R B(pi_P).                          (2)

For the upper inequality, J_P−J_Q <= V_P(pi_P)−V_Q(pi_P); for the lower use pi_Q. **The behavior policy used to log data is generally neither policy.** Replacing the right side by a small mean error on the observed policy is invalid.

### Observable support and coverage corollary

Suppose a signed, lawful reference-regime logging policy beta induces nu_(Q,beta,t). If the **Q-reference** history-action occupancy measures of BOTH pi_P and pi_Q admit densities bounded by C_t relative to nu at every required date, let m_t=E_(nu)[delta_t]. Then

    |J_P−J_Q| <= R min{1, Σ_t C_t m_t}.                            (3)

This follows by change of measure in (2). More sharply, keep different C for each optimal policy and the directional signs. If support is incomplete, use the covered integral plus an explicit bound on the **uncovered policy occupancy**, with delta<=1 on that part; setting missing histories to delta=0 is not permissible. One signed failure/recovery event at a rare checkpoint can dominate an apparently excellent aggregate estimate. Estimating m_t or C_t from finite samples requires simultaneous coverage and valid stage-specific causal kernel comparisons in the *target pooled market*, not just simulation replay or cut-off independent robot islands. A single on-policy error rate, marginal technical test, or data license receipt is not a usable kernel estimate.

If the admissible P and Q menus differ, retain a genuinely shared sub-menu Pi0 and define nonnegative economic losses e_K=J_K−sup_(pi in Pi0)V_K(pi), K=P,Q. Then

    |J_P−J_Q| <= b_(Pi0)+max(e_P,e_Q),                             (4)

where b_(Pi0) bounds the common-menu optimized difference. A novel embodiment E may have a large *extra* feasible-policy value e_E; old-controller logs do not certify it. When a customer population differs, there is a separate buyer-market transport error rather than silent equality of initial measures.

## Synthetic original-buyer paid-production counterworld

A hypothetical original customer has one qualified service with a normalized paid contribution of 1 upon final settlement, 0 otherwise, and one scarce robot/operator slot. All other investment costs are stipulated equal only for illustration. SG's legally allowed choices are a safe recipe, paid with probability .40 in both P and Q, and a risky recipe, paid with probability 0 in Q. If **all F,T,M** are optionally available, P's risky recipe instead pays with probability 1, while the safe fallback remains. The new embodiment E has an eligible recipe paying .55 under both P and Q and all source coalitions. For every singleton, pair and empty source coalition P=Q and SG optimum is .40.

The lower-coalition original-buyer paid SG−E difference is −.15. Therefore the three-source order-two Möbius extrapolation is D2=−.15, but full pooled D_all=1−.55=+.45, leaving an economically unobserved third dividend **+.60**. The source-availability option set is nested; SG's own optimized paid value rises from .40 to 1, and E's remains .55. Thus free optional data monotonicity does **not** identify the high-order investment comparison.

A safe-only Q logging policy sees **zero** P/Q next-event error on every supported action, yet SG wins the full-mixture investment in P and loses it in Q. Even 1% Q logging exploration of risky observes mean TV only .01. The P-optimal policy chooses risky with probability 1; its occupancy density ratio is 100. The valid coverage certificate is R min(1,100×.01)=1, not .01; it does not settle SG-versus-E. With zero exploration C is infinite. The false small-error shortcut would incorrectly choose E. These are synthetic paid-service probabilities, **not** empirical robotics rates or conversions of benchmark scores into revenue.

Occupancy can also be genuinely more informative than a uniform model bound. In a two-date accepted-service path, Q and P share first-stage qualification probability .1; conditional paid success after qualification is 0 under Q and 1 under P. Their paid-value gap is .1. Only 10% of Q paths reach the later discrepant kernel, giving a *sharp* Q-occupancy bound .1, whereas applying a horizon-wide maximum delta2=1 would be vacuous. This is a simple exact example, not an observed transition.

## Putting the result back into the same integrated production comparison

For each program and source coalition, let b_a(A) be a defensible bound from (1)–(4) for the target pooled **optimized paid** value difference between P and Q. Suppose in addition that the reference's *economic* function J_Q(a,A), including shared operator scarcity, customer substitution and actual costs, is genuinely two-additive as a set function of available source types **in that same target market**. Let D_P=J_P(SG)−J_P(E) and D2_P be the signed lower-coalition extrapolation. Then Möbius inversion and the triangle inequality give

    |D_P(FTM)−D2_P| <= Σ_(A subset H)[ b_SG(A)+b_E(A) ].           (5)

If physical architecture genuinely gives P=Q under all lower coalitions, only the two full-source b terms remain. Mere observed equality of lower-coalition means is not that architectural statement. Lower-coalition sampling error and nonzero economic third interactions in the reference must enter the certificate separately. An interval that contains zero is an honest **not-yet-identified** SG/E decision, not failure of arithmetic. The combined theorem does not demand fixed customer demand, additive technical accuracy or source independence; it does demand a supported economic reference, outcome and policy support. Real new embodiments must be considered with their manufacturing and rights feasibility, not assumed costless like the toy.
