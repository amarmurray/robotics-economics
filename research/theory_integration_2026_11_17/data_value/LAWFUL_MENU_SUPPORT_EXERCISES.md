# Exercises — lawful menus and partial identification of economic information value

1. With two equally likely buyers and two lawfully available actions, prove the absolute-value representation of one-decision buyer-information regret in terms of the state-specific paid-continuation differences d1 and d2. State which full-horizon information is allowed after the one blind dispatch decision.
2. Derive the exact maximum and minimum regret over Cartesian intervals d1∈[l1,u1], d2∈[l2,u2], checking the cases where zero is feasible in either interval. Explain why a pair of separately estimated whole-program value intervals need not give a sharp difference interval.
3. Old A reservation has paid contributions (1,0); a lawfully qualified source-trained B reservation has contributions (0,q), q∈[0,1]. Under equal offered-order probabilities, prove that the buyer report's economic value lies in [0,1/2]. Explain what *technical* B qualification alone does not establish.
4. Add a hypothetical generalist embodiment worth 3/4 in either original buyer state and acquisition costs 1/10 for S and E, 1/20 for the buyer report. Derive the paid SG versus E boundary, and say what separate target grounding/alignment might change.
5. A logged historical controller only observed original-buyer A. Buyer B is in the population but absent from the logs. Explain the difference between (i) legally unapproved action B and (ii) certified legal but never paid-tested action B. What can be inferred from no B receipts in the A-only log?
6. Suppose one action a* is certified feasible in every history and its supported lower Q bound dominates every legal competitor's full-horizon upper Q bound state by state. Prove zero one-decision regret without observing other actions. Why is this not a full recursive network quotient?
