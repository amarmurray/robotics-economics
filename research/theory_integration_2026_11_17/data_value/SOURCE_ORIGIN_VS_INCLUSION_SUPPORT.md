# Two support problems, not one: source generation versus source inclusion

**20 November 2026; a continuation of When Capabilities Become Production and the existing November 22 Models A–B decision gate.** This is a conditional causal-identification argument applied to verified public source metadata, not measured robot learning, new causal inference, an independently novel theorem or a claim that any learned robot policy is physically approved.

## The economically meaningful intervention must be specified before the comparison

Let \(\pi\) specify original-customer offers, rights-cleared data acquisition, frozen initial controller, shared learner updates, held-out whole-service qualification, installed embodiment, grounding signal and decision clock, signed human/robot/dispatcher operating policies, resource use, safe fallback, receipts, and continuation learning. Its value \(J(\pi)\) remains the existing expected optimized, net, **original-customer accepted-and-paid contribution**. The old qualified policy remains an actual available option only if its controller, operator and budget resources genuinely remain feasible. The fixed continuation value is inside \(J\), never added again as a data premium.

A source "modality" is not one treatment. Define two different assignments, with units formed by **entire connected learner/update/customer/operator networks** when shared weights or resources cause interference:

1. **Fixed-archive inclusion \(Z\):** conditional on an immutable, lawfully accessible archive \(D_0=(D_\mathrm{teleop},D_\mathrm{script})\), assign a specified training algorithm \(L\) to use either an explicitly priced, size/compute-matched subset or the control subset, keeping initial weights, update budget, data filters, target evaluation and assigned networks invariant. Its potential target qualification vector is \(Q_t(Z;D_0,L)\). With randomized full-network inclusion, held-out target measurement and nonzero *inclusion* assignment probability, the matched \(\Delta^{inc}_t=E[Q_t(1)-Q_t(0)\mid D_0]\) is experimentally identified for any **evaluated** target \(t\) (subject to randomization, interference control and outcome measurement), even if the scripted archive contains no episodes generated *at* task \(t\). Source-to-target cross-task transfer is the very effect this design can measure. No actual such experiment was performed here.
2. **Source generation \(M\):** at a predeclared acquisition context \(c\) (task, environment, robot, state and budget), intervene on how a new episode or connected acquisition program is generated (e.g. teleoperator versus scripted controller), before selecting data. A fixed downstream learner and qualified production policy yield potential bounded technical outcome \(Y_c(M)\). The source-generation contrast \(\tau(c)=E[Y_c(S)-Y_c(T)]\) is not identified without a well-defined feasible source-generation mechanism for each arm, relevant support and assignment/exchangeability at \(c\). Existing source labels in a pooled observational archive do not constitute such randomization.

These effects are not synonyms. Even a perfectly identified inclusion contrast conditional on one available collection does not identify the expected economic return to *commissioning future data* from each collection method. That requires the distribution of generated episodes, actual rights/collection costs, policy updating and customer production. An offline transfer result may be a sufficient mechanism to prioritize a future signed field test, but not a currency conversion.

## Conditional proposition: two different positivity requirements

For **archive inclusion**, if whole network \(Z\) is randomized with positive probability on both arms, held-out \(Q_t\) is measured for the same target version \(t\), training recipe/compute/seeds are controlled or randomized as designed, and no cross-network interference breaks assignments, then the mean difference in target qualification outcomes identifies the fixed-archive causal inclusion effect at \(t\). **Proof:** randomization makes each potential qualified outcome mean equal to its arm-conditional observed mean, irrespective of whether \(D_\mathrm{script}\) contains an episode labelled \(t\). The latter only describes the acquisition distribution, not where the trained controller can be evaluated. Generalizing to a new dataset or customer population requires additional transport assumptions. The induced controller/body *economic* value remains unidentified without complete signed paid-service outcomes.

For **source generation**, let \(\mu(c)\) be a stipulated distribution over mutually well-defined connected acquisition contexts, let \(S\) be contexts with both admissible source-origin potential outcomes identified, and suppose \(Y_c(m)\in[0,1]\) for each \(m\). Write \(\rho=\mu(S)\) and \(\bar\tau_S=E_\mu[\tau(c)\mid c\in S]\). If no restrictions couple missing context effects across \(S^c\), the whole-target source-generation effect has the **sharp identified interval**
\[
\Delta^{gen}_\mu\in
[\rho\bar\tau_S-(1-\rho),\ \rho\bar\tau_S+(1-\rho)].
\]
**Proof:** partition the \(\mu\)-average into observed-support and missing-support terms. The latter is in \([-(1-\rho),1-\rho]\) because the contrast of two [0,1] potential outcomes is in [-1,1]. To attain both endpoints, assign \(Y(S)=0,Y(T)=1\), or the reverse, on every unsupported context, preserving supported observations. Thus the interval is sharp under the stated **unrestricted missing cells** assumption. Correlated shared learners, cross-task complementarities, physical-admission rules or stronger structural restrictions may shrink or invalidate this simple context decomposition; then use an explicitly joint complete-network policy model instead.

Source-generation positivity and target-evaluation positivity are **orthogonal**. A missing scripted episode on a target task blocks a same-task *generator-replacement* comparison but does not establish zero or negative transfer from available scripted data to that target. Conversely, a brilliant pretrained benchmark evaluation supplies no counterfactual source-generation comparison and no original buyer paid-return estimate.

## A specific real archive and an exact conditional illustration

The **publisher's** BridgeData V2 page (inspected 20 Nov 2026) explicitly reports **60,096** trajectories: **50,365** teleoperated demonstrations and **9,731** *scripted pick-and-place* rollouts, from 24 environments and 13 skill categories. The publisher states **all data** is under **CC BY 4.0**, while its accompanying code repository has a separate **MIT software** license. The raw demos and scripted rollouts are separate archives, and the project documents offline dataset loaders and held-out learning evaluations. These are genuine candidate technical data sources with visibly different origins, **not** randomized modality arms or original-customer receipts. There was no download, archive checksum, episode-level audit, training or robot execution in this tranche. [Publisher dataset and data license](https://rail-berkeley.github.io/bridgedata/); [official code and archive split](https://github.com/rail-berkeley/bridge_data_v2); [code LICENSE](https://github.com/rail-berkeley/bridge_data_v2/blob/main/LICENSE).

Suppose **hypothetically**, not as a claim about the actual task mix, that we want an equally weighted effect of *generating* alternative datasets for 13 independent skill-context versions; both modes are admitted and causally comparable on exactly one version, with supported contrast \(+1\). Then \(\rho=1/13\) and the sharp interval is \([-11/13,1]\). Two indistinguishable worlds assign all missing contexts contrast \(-1\) versus \(+1\), reversing the sign. This uses a *hypothetical* unit contrast, not a measured outcome, and its uniform target weights are not BridgeData's task frequencies. Since the publisher specifies scripted **pick-and-place** data, a same-task source-generation comparison for its other skills lacks a disclosed scripted collection arm; this does **not** mean those other skills cannot benefit from off-task scripted training. A task/scene state crosswalk could make even the one assumed overlapping version unavailable; hence \(\rho=1/13\) is an illustrative condition, not verified actual support.

The [stdlib exact JS check](bridge_source_support_audit.js) and [record](bridge_source_support_results.json) validate only the published counts, rational shares, algebraic interval and counterworld witnesses. They explicitly label the conditional illustration **NOT measured**. Independent Python Fraction arithmetic checked the same rational endpoints; neither check is a proof of external source fidelity or technical efficacy.

## How this changes the original X/G/E/R decision, without detaching a new problem

A useful progression is now **licensed archive → randomized inclusion experiment → matched held-out full joint qualification → separately signed controller/body/grounding policy → original unchanged customer offered/refused/accepted/completed/paid net production**. One may learn transfer from a fixed scripted archive to unseen target skills even when learning the economic value of commissioning more scripted demonstrations at those skills is impossible. For future collection, distinguish marginal deliberate collection, production-generated field trajectories, failure/recovery, teleoperation, simulation, synthetic and public/pretrained datasets; their data-generating policies, legal permissions, curation, costs and causal support differ. Neither a scripted-to-teleop contrast nor a human-teleop master ontology applies universally.

The immediate fork in the *existing* source versus target grounding versus learned embodiment decision is therefore **not** "which dataset has more demonstrations?" It is which *implementable, decision-timed program* has sufficient supported source inclusion or generation evidence, independently validated qualification, and a legally/physically admitted path to paid customer service. Source inclusion on a compatible embodiment may be worth testing offline without touching hardware. A newly trained embodiment that makes formerly unsupported task recipes available is a separate signed policy and needs its own learning-qualification and market-service support. Customer grounding may unlock reuse of archived data while avoiding new generation, but only if it changes a real predecision report or deployment action. Free optional data availability cannot lower the optimized value when the old policy set truly remains nested; forced updates, expenses and unsafe hybrid controllers remove that nesting.

**Closest foundations and limits.** Standard randomized experiments, causal positivity/overlap, partial identification and finite-population bounds precede this construction. The original [Data-Driven Automation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10127) already prices future data and complementary aggregation. Our present contribution is a disciplined empirical intervention distinction embedded in the existing customer-production model, not independent methodological novelty. Public source rights and source data formats do not imply model artifact, face/third-party, downstream customer, environment-access or robot-safety permissions for a particular use; these must be recorded before acquisition.
