# Focused first paper — sensitivity to lost readiness and joint-only transfer
24 November 2026 Eastern. Direct extension of [Result C](FOCUSED_CONTRIBUTION_MISSING_COMBINATION.md); no new paper or field estimate.

## The critical assumption is not monotone average performance
A retained executable controller is not proof that the same original order survives intervention. Nor is OR-readiness guaranteed: source/grounding can interfere with routine training, and coordinated target trajectories can unlock qualifications neither program supplies separately. The compact paper should distinguish these failures rather than make an all-or-nothing structural claim.

Keep the common original-order law, policy-invariant nonnegative contribution measure mu, capacity/authorization sets F0 subset F1, and single accepted service per window as in Result C. Let P,G subset F0 and C,Z subset F1 denote paid service events under routine, grounding, coordination, combined programs respectively. Their known masses are p,g,c; unknown z; f0,f1 and d=f1-f0 as before. Drop exact OR-composition and casewise nesting. Instead impose five independently justified **upper bounds** (all in the same weighted measure):
- a >= mu(P minus G), b >= mu(P minus C): loss of incumbent production in each standalone program.
- r >= mu(G minus Z), s >= mu(C minus Z): combined program regressions relative to either standalone.
- h >= mu((Z intersect F0) minus (G union C)): genuinely joint-only qualification/acceptance inside baseline usable capacity.

The last quantity excludes service enabled solely by new capacity outside F0; it isolates the possibility that coordination changes the marginal productivity of grounding, not merely opens a new slot. It does not identify which technical channel causes that difference. Values may be upper bounds, not exact losses. Feasibility requires 0<=p,g<=f0<=f1, 0<=c<=f1, g>=p-a, c>=p-b and nonnegative budgets. These conditions are sufficient in the divisible common-law model. No budgets are implied by arm means or controller retention. Unrestricted demand and contribution changes require complete program J.

## Sharp sensitivity certificate
Define t=max(0,p-a,p+g-f0), m=max(0,t-b). Then the sharp identified interval is

    max(0,g-r,c-s) <= z <= d + min(f0,g+c-m+h).       (S)

Proof of lower bound: Z must contain at least g-r of G and c-s of C. Attainment: choose P,G,C nested by size in F1, with P,G in F0 (initial segments in a measure interval suffice). Then both incumbent loss bounds hold; choose Z as an initial segment of length max(0,g-r,c-s). It is contained in G union C so h is respected.

Proof of upper bound: P and G lie in F0, so their intersection has mass >=t. At most b of P is absent from C; hence at least m of G intersects C inside F0. Consequently Z intersect F0 has mass <= min(f0,g+c-m+h), while Z outside F0 has mass <=d.

Attainment: arrange G with exactly t of P (possible by g>=p-a and finite capacity). Put u=min(c,f0) of C in F0, including at least max(0,p-b) of P, and c-u outside F0. The minimal possible overlap of this C portion with G is j=max(0,g+u-f0,t-b). To construct it, first select max(0,p-b) mass from P, preferentially from P minus G; the forced overlap is max(0,t-b). Fill the remaining u mass preferentially outside G, then within G if necessary. This reaches j; there is sufficient room because u>=max(0,p-b), and g,u<=f0. Choose Z to contain G union C plus up to h additional mass inside F0 and all of F1 minus F0. Its inside mass is min(f0,g+u-j+h). Substitution yields min(f0,g+c-m+h) (if c>=f0, j=g and the whole F0 is covered). Both combined-regression budgets hold since Z contains G union C. Mixtures of extremal measure laws preserve all marginal equalities and budget inequalities and fill the interval. Equal-unit integer count examples attain all integer points; heterogeneous indivisible weights can yield a narrower feasible set. With all five budgets zero, p<=g,c and t=p, m=p, reproducing Result C.

The upper bound is independent of combined-regression budgets; the lower is independent of incumbent-loss and joint-only budgets, conditional on feasibility. This is useful for evidence acquisition: **rejecting** an incremental grounding investment after coordination primarily requires bounding incumbent overlap losses and joint-only gains; **adopting** it conservatively requires bounding combined-program regressions. The two questions call for different audits. With incremental cost k, no additional changes in other J terms, net increment lies in [lower-c-k, upper-c-k]. A full-menu choice still requires valid comparisons with X, E and other alternatives. Unknown cost or continuation differences require separate bounds; no data premium is added.

If a measured combined program has z>d+min(f0,g+c-m), its support requires positive joint-only mass at least max(0,z-d-g-c+m), provided the remaining assumptions and incumbent-loss bounds hold. If z<g or c, corresponding regression is at least g-z or c-z. These are diagnostic lower bounds, not attribution to transfer. Conversely ordinary technical cross-validation cannot upper-bound these quantities without the joint service/rights/capacity bridge.

## Example and discriminating evidence
Use 12 equal-mass normalized contribution units, f0=10,f1=12,p=6,g=7,c=6; additive grounding cost k=4. Exact OR/nesting yields z in [7,9], hence incremental grounding after C is strictly unattractive (at best -1). Allow one incumbent-loss unit in G, b=0, and no other violation: z in [7,10], so strict rejection disappears. Allow also one G-to-Z regression unit and one unit of joint-only transfer: z in [6,11], so the sign is unidentified. These are not estimates of real dollars or violation rates. With vacuous budgets z in [0,12]. Distinguish support-based bounds (above) from sampling confidence intervals.

In the [whole-program protocol](COMPACT_WHOLE_PROGRAM_VALIDATION.md), a credible loss budget requires supported original-order cases with matched deadline, authorization and fallback resource occupation, not a shared benchmark. A joint-only upper bound may be supplied by an independently validated exclusion restriction on the original customer opportunity set: outside a specified subset of baseline-capacity orders, at least one standalone approved controller/recipe suffices whenever the combined policy succeeds. Measure the *economic mass* of the exception set, not merely number of tasks. Such a restriction needs independent verification including shared worker/compute constraints, changed trajectory mix and actual customer acceptance. An observed successful combined-only technical case may falsify OR but cannot by itself upper-bound h. Four isolated evolving program arms directly identify fixed-program interaction in expectation without these cross-world assumptions, if all original opportunities and settlements are followed. Pairwise certification audits can support budgets; independent arm marginals generally cannot. No authorized private data or actual P/G/C/CG panel is available in the evidence assembled to date.
