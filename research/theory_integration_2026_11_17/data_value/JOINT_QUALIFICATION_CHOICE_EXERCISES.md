# Student exercises — joint qualification and investment choice

1. For binary qualifications with \(p_A=0.6,p_B=0.6\), prove the sharp bound on \(h=\Pr(Q_A=Q_B=1)\). Construct the four qualification state probabilities at each endpoint and explain why a separate task-success dashboard cannot identify \(h\).

2. Assume one signed original customer network values \((Q_A,Q_B)\) as \(F(0,0)=0,F(1,0)=F(0,1)=2,F(1,1)=12\). Derive \(a,b,\kappa\) and the exact source×grounding interaction. Using p and h in the chapter, calculate gross outcomes and the cost-inclusive decision in both worlds, including E.

3. Replace bundled demand with one shared operator slot, \(F(1,1)=2\). Compute \(\kappa\) and the two source×grounding economic interactions. Explain why no source learning mechanism changed.

4. Show that with \(\kappa>0\), only marginal qualification probabilities deliver sharp Fréchet bounds on \(\Delta_{sg}V\). Why could common whole-learner seeds, targeted data rights, or old-checkpoint retention make independent endpoint choices incompatible?

5. Prove (or give a counterexample to) the statement: giving the learner additional free optional licensed data reduces maximized dynamic production value. Contrast this with mandatory retraining at nonzero compute cost.

6. Design a prospective legal and customer-validated experiment to distinguish genuinely new independent recovery episodes from resamples; explain why it must collect paired complete-service qualification and original offered-order receipts, and why it does not directly identify a BEA sector-flow coefficient.
