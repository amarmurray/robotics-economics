# Student exercises — protected service, pinned transfer and economic value

Use the full original customer window, not per-motion success. All figures are invented normalized captured contributions. No physical safety premise is automatically satisfied.

1. Prove by finite induction that an invariant B sub-state with identical exogenous customer demand and no incoming influence path gives identical B accepted orders under two otherwise different policies. Supply a demand-rerouting counterexample when only model weights are isolated.
2. With B=(1.2,0.6,0.2), k=1, and shared grounding a=2.4,c=0.4, calculate its worst B loss and net lower certificate. If protecting B1 costs 0.2 and reduces A by 0.7, recalculate and interpret.
3. For protection subsets P⊆{1,2,3}, define W(P)=top1(B)−top1(B\P). Compute W(∅), W({1}), W({2}), W({1,2}), and explain why this does not establish complementarity of *actual* robot output.
4. Starting with the prior ideal pinned grounding gain 2.4 and incremental cost 0.6, derive a condition on loss of accepted A gain x for pinned deployment to strictly beat a competent specialist with upper net gain 0.9. What if x=1.0?
5. Suppose uniform full-service TV δ and a resource reduction r yield an A transfer loss ≤Mδ+Kr. Let r=0.25, K=2, Mδ=0.2; compute the pinned lower bound. Explain why a marginal benchmark-success TV would not suffice.
6. Construct an indivisible two-qualification production system in which removing just 0.01 of validation capacity destroys all new A service; explain why optional old-policy availability does not reimburse sunk training costs.
