# Exercises: endogenous source, buyer and worker decisions

1. From the common structural environment \(D_G=D_{GK}=B\vee S,\;W_G=C,\;W_{GK}=CM,\;Q_{GK}=H\vee T\), derive the *incremental whole-program* value of acquiring K in addition to G. Identify the three economically distinct terms and the exact assumptions needed for the OR qualification.

2. Using eight equiprobable failed-ticket histories with \(B=1\) on 0–3, \(M=0\) on 0 and 4, \(T=1\) on 1,2,5,6 and \(H=1\) on one atom in \(\{0,3\}\) and one in \(\{4,7\}\), find the sharp interval for \(\theta=\Pr(H(1-T)M=1)\). Explain why having all three separately supported R, G and K arm outcome distributions **and** paired technical H/T tests is insufficient to distinguish the endpoints.

3. For \(p=1/2,r_1=m_0=1,\Delta=4,c_G=c_K=1/4,D_E=1,m_E=4,C_E=5/8\), derive all five whole-program contributions. Find the rank switch and the minimax-regret mixture against E under the specified ambiguity family. Explain why this is not a recommendation to buy or deploy a robot.

4. What happens to the sign of the data option value if K is only *available* at no additional cost versus if K worker training must be precommitted and displaces one service slot? Give a mathematically valid argument.

5. Propose a lawful, non-selected original buyer/worker and complete customer-recipe evaluation capable of distinguishing more K-qualified services from lost worker availability. Name a specific way in which the eight-atom identification result would fail to transport to two firms sharing a mobile maintenance crew.
