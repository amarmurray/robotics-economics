# Student exercises — intervention support and economic data value

Read [source origin versus inclusion](SOURCE_ORIGIN_VS_INCLUSION_SUPPORT.md) and the independently dated [public evidence ledger](SOURCE_ORIGIN_BRIDGE_EVIDENCE_AND_DESIGN.md). Do not consult the separate solutions before attempting these problems.

1. **Two experiments, two causal quantities.** Define an experiment randomizing training inclusion of a fixed scripted archive and another randomizing how the next episode is generated at a fixed task/scene. State explicitly why the first can measure cross-task transfer to a held-out skill absent from the scripted source archive without identifying the second.
2. **Sharpness.** Let the target distribution put weight \(\rho\) on source-generation contexts where \(\bar\tau_S=d\), with outcome potentials in [0,1]. Derive the sharp interval for the average effect. Construct both endpoint worlds, and state an assumption that would make the interval *not* sharp.
3. **Concrete but conditional calculation.** The BridgeData V2 publisher lists 13 skills, but scripted rollouts only for pick/place. *Assume* uniformly weighted independent skill-specific source-generation contexts, valid joint support at exactly one skill, and a unit supported contrast. Calculate the sharp bounds. Explain why your answer says nothing by itself about transfer from pick/place data into unseen skills.
4. **Qualified economic value.** A held-out offline technical evaluation detects a positive inclusion effect. Write a minimal chain of additional evidence needed before ranking the archive inclusion policy against target grounding or manufacturing a new embodiment by original customer captured value. Explain why customer-paid contribution cannot be mechanically inferred from success percentages.
5. **Rights and measurement.** Explain the difference between the lab's stated CC BY 4.0 dataset license and the code repository's MIT software license; suggest two validation fields that need independent verification even if both notices are correct.
