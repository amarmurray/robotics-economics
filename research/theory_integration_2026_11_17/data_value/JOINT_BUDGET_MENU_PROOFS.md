1. **Resource/budget/choice certificate.** For each original service j, let offered price P_j, captured per-sale contribution r_j, group g(j), parent b(j), resource vector a_j and a_j's demand-and-qualification eligibility A_j be nonnegative and bounded. For nonnegative duals lambda,eta,gamma,delta satisfying lambda·a_j+eta_{b(j)}P_j+gamma_{g(j)}+delta_j>=r_j, multiply by z_j, sum, then apply each primal resource bound to prove the displayed upper certificate. This is valid weak duality without buyer independence. Because z is integer, LP strong duality concerns the relaxation, not necessarily the actual dispatch integer optimum. If delta≥0 and E A_j<=pbar_j under the fixed p,m, substituting pbar_j supplies an expected upper certificate. A priori payment and eligibility/qualification envelopes must come from original buyers; a robot benchmark is insufficient.

5. **Mean policy comparison versus potential-outcome coupling.** For full policy outcomes V_X,V_G on the same target population with both expectations observed, E[V_G−V_X]=E[V_G]−E[V_X] by linearity, regardless their cross-world joint law. A same-world joint response restriction can sharpen partial-ID bounds, allow adaptive policy conditioning or control interference, but is not needed to subtract two identified means. By contrast the *within-policy* coupling between original buyer demands and budget or operator slots determines E[v_p(theta)] before the full mean is known.
