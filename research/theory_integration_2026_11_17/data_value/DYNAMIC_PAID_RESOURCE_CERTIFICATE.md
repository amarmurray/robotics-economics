# Dynamic original-buyer production certificates with endogenous data and fallback

**21 November 2026; continuation of the same November 22 Models A–B source-data/target-grounding versus embodiment comparison. Scientific acceptance remains OPEN.** This is not another parameter sweep or financing theory. It makes the previous [supported-policy paid-production envelope](SUPPORT_FRONTIER_PAID_VALUE.md) operational for a *whole horizon*: a valid upper certificate must account for adaptive recovery, new customer orders created by successful deployment, permitted source-data reuse, changes in robot embodiment, scarce operator/robot capacity, and actual accepted-and-finally-paid service. The mathematical device is a classical dynamic-programming super-solution and optional resource-price Lagrangian, applied to the integrated production system. It is not claimed as a new robust-MDP theorem or a measured robotics return.

## 1. Economic environment and the missing inference

Fix one target original-buyer population and horizon H. A program a in {R,S,G,SG,E}, with optional data availability D, has a finite *legal* nonanticipating strategy set Pi(a,D). A state z_t is sufficiently rich to recover full joint customer orders/declines/prices, joint qualification and service acceptance, cleared payments and refunds, fleet embodiment, robot/teleoperator/coordination resource inventory, manufacturing capacity, owned/licensed training rights, learner checkpoint and provenance, and learned future qualified service recipes. A true complete-event kernel K_t^a(dz',dy,dg|z,u) maps legal action u to the next state, stage net accepted-and-paid contribution y and resource use g. The state can be an entire observable history if a Markov summary is not defensible. Field success, failure/recovery, licensed teleoperation, simulation, synthetic and public/pretrained data are distinct origins. Deliberate experiments and *production-generated* new data enter state only at the permitted date and under applicable rights. Other buyers in a network must enter the same state and objective whenever the learner, procurement, or operator pool couples them.

Define J_a(D) = sup_{pi in Pi(a,D)} E_pi[sum_{t=0}^{H-1} y_t + y_H], with all horizon investment, acquisition, curation, operation and settlement costs included once. Net payoff is not a benchmark percentage. The old no-training/fallback policy remains available under genuinely free optional data; forced pooling, changing rights, or new manufacturing obligations need not preserve the old menu or law.

The prior [occupancy certificate](POLICY_OCCUPANCY_POOLING_CERTIFICATE.md) requires coverage of policies that might optimize the true pooled network. The prior [support envelope](SUPPORT_FRONTIER_PAID_VALUE.md) instead permits lower bounds from observed *complete legal policies* and upper bounds on unobserved policies. The proof gap is how to obtain an upper bound that does not mistakenly discard endogenous future services or contingent fallback. A first-period robot or buyer capacity estimate is not such a bound.

## 2. Conditional dynamic paid-value upper certificate

For each program, fix outer conditional ambiguity sets Kbar_t(z,u) containing the **true complete** conditional event law for every reachable legal history and action. These sets must include demand, training-generated services, learner updates, joint qualification, payments and future rights—not merely accuracy on logged actions. Let B_H(z) >= terminal y_H. If, for every legal u and every K in Kbar_t(z,u),

    B_t(z) >= E_K[y_t + B_{t+1}(z') | z,u],                     (1)

then J_a(D) <= E_{z_0} B_0(z_0). This is a *certificate*: a numerical candidate B with checked inequalities suffices even when the ambiguity sets are correlated across histories and B is not the smallest possible super-solution.

**Proof.** For any legal adaptive pi, condition on its actual z_t and u_t. True K lies in the claimed outer set, so (1) bounds the conditional expected present net paid contribution plus continuation. Iterated conditional expectation telescopes; the terminal bound closes the induction. Supremize over pi. Neither independent qualification nor independent buyer demand is needed. Rectangularity of Kbar is required only for interpreting the *optimized* stagewise robust Bellman recursion as an exact robust value. Taking a rectangular hull of correlated real-world hypotheses remains an upper bound but can be wastefully loose. If B ignores a legally reachable data/right/embodiment state, (1) has not been certified.

### Resource-price generalization

Suppose every lawful complete policy has E[sum_t g_{kt}] <= b_k (a stronger pathwise guarantee is sufficient), g_k >= 0, and lambda_k >= 0. For each stage replace y_t by y_t - lambda.g_t in (1), giving a super-solution B_t^lambda. Algebra and the resource inequalities yield

    J_a(D) <= inf_{lambda >= 0} [lambda.b + E B_0^lambda].    (2)

A single feasible lambda and B provide a checked *weak-duality* bound; optimizing the RHS requires additional work and does not establish empirical validity. These lambda are shadow certificates, not additional actual robot costs; the latter already enter y once. When the remaining resource inventory is in z, exact dynamic optimization can be sharper. The earlier static resource LP is recovered when transitions have no economically relevant intertemporal state and full-horizon constraints close the service catalog.

**Supermartingale interpretation.** Under the true law, sum_{s<t}(y_s-lambda.g_s)+B_t^lambda(z_t) is a supermartingale. Thus an apparent opportunity ceiling fails exactly when a permitted state/action or transition can raise this potential above its asserted bound. This is useful for audit: it forces enumeration of failure rescue, learner upgrades, induced new buyer orders, and E-specific menu expansion, not merely confidence bands on isolated task scores.

## 3. A two-date source-to-grounding-to-paid-service example

These are **synthetic normalized service-equivalent units**, not dollars, robot reliability estimates or customer receipts. One incumbent original-buyer service is finally paid with probability p=2/5. Conditional on success, and *only if the training data are licensed and the qualified source-plus-grounding program is physically permitted*, a distinct future buyer service can be attempted. Its end-to-end conditional paid probability is q; its incremental training/grounding/operation cost is c=3/20. Conditional on first-service failure, an independently approved robot/operator rescue can complete that *same* original order with paid probability r=1/2 at cost d=1/20. The success and failure branches are mutually exclusive, so the second date has at most one additional service opportunity. The first service could in a different model create useful failure telemetry on a failed attempt: that channel is not silently collapsed into this special case. A new embodiment E has its own independently eligible complete policy and hypothetical paid net contribution 19/20.

With free choice to skip either contingent action, and actual rights to the future service, the exact dynamic value is

    J_SG(p,q,r) = p[1+(q-c)_+] + (1-p)(r-d)_+,                 (3)

where x_+=max{x,0}. Proof: conditional on first success, choose train/serve or skip; conditional on failure, choose rescue or skip. The two conditional optimizations do not consume simultaneously used resources; summing branchwise maximum *unweighted* values would double count mutually exclusive opportunities. Equation (3) is both a Bellman upper certificate and achieved by the feasible contingent strategy. A change in training source, grounding, embodiment, customer demand or rights can alter the conditional q, the available menu and the costs, so (3) is not a universal transfer law.

At q=4/5, (3) is 93/100, below E=95/100. A crude additive ceiling counting the initial service plus both mutually exclusive branch maxima is 150/100; it cannot rank the investments. The *valid* dynamic certificate of 93/100 would rank E over SG **only if** p,r,q,c,d, the full lawful action menu, target buyer eligibility/settlement, and E's lower paid value were actually established. They are not. If the eligible future-buyer paid probability can instead be 9/10, with identical p,r,c,d and observed first-service outcomes, SG earns 97/100 and wins. The exact customer-conditional threshold is q=17/20. The q=4/5 restriction therefore cannot be imputed from first-service qualification, a simulator, or production logs that exclude the second buyer's acceptance/settlement.

If reuse rights are absent, the learned future service is *not a legal action*: the same first-stage and rescue data yield 67/100. Optional licensed data can enlarge the legal menu and never reduce optimized value when old policies, kernels and resource opportunities are retained. Forced training replacing an old policy is a different intervention and may show negative transfer. The cross-period source effect above a positive (q-c) threshold has dJ/dq=p, and otherwise zero; technical transfer affects economic value only after the paid-service opportunity, conditioning and eligibility gates. This derivative is *not* a price conversion from a benchmark score.

### Economic complementarity from endogenous production occupancy

The example yields a stronger, generalizable conditional result than the threshold calculation alone. Let an upstream source-data intervention x change *first-date accepted-and-paid occupancy* from p_0 to p_1, while a target-grounding intervention g separately changes conditional future paid-service probability from q_0 to q_1. Hold legally permitted menus, conditional recovery f=(r-d)_+, per-success learning cost c and the original buyer market fixed. If both q_g >= c, the optimized two-date objective is J(x,g)=f+p_x[1+q_g-c-f]. Therefore its exact finite cross-difference is

    J(1,1)-J(1,0)-J(0,1)+J(0,0)
        = (p_1-p_0)(q_1-q_0).                                (4)

With optional training thresholds, replace q_g in (4) by (q_g-c)_+. This is a *dynamic economic* complementarity even when x and g do not interact within a technical transfer map: x changes how often the system reaches a state in which g becomes productive. In a synthetic check, p rises 8/20 to 12/20 and q rises 10/20 to 16/20; the cross-difference is 24/400=0.06 paid-service-equivalent units. It is an occupancy product, not an independently estimated neural scaling law. The analogous general finite-horizon mechanism is the effect of sources on the reachability/occupancy of future qualified customer-production states, interacted with how grounding changes their attainable paid value.

The sign is **not generally positive**. If x and g both improve the *same* conditional q and substitute under saturation, take p=8/20 and q_00=0, q_10=q_01=q_11=16/20 with zero marginal training cost. Every intervention weakly increases optional paid production, yet the cross-difference equals -128/400=-0.32. A shared operator queue or induced buyer switching can likewise reverse the sign without negative technical transfer. Equation (4) requires the separable p_x/q_g information structure, unchanged contracts/resources and realized downstream eligibility. This both supplies an interpretable sufficient condition for positive source×grounding economic complementarity and a matched empirical falsifier: estimate p conditional on x and later paid q conditional on g in authorized exposure-closed buyer cohorts. Do not infer the sign from independent task-benchmark marginals or from tests lacking the relevant joint histories.

### Correlated-world warning

Consider two possible world-level transition regimes with no costs/recovery: world A has p=1/2 and q=0; world B has p=0 and q=1. The greatest feasible whole-world paid value is 1/2. A stagewise rectangular ambiguity hull allows p=1/2 *and* q=1 in a fictitious recombination and reports an upper bound of 1. It remains valid but not tight. Shared learner, buyer and data-rights histories can make such nonrectangular coupling economically important. This is a caution about calibration and inference, not a reason to drop conservative outer bounds.

### Impossible bound without an economic opportunity envelope

An otherwise identical first-service log cannot bound future expected paid production if a legally reachable learned service admits unbounded eligible buyer value or unbounded new capacity. Add a permitted future service yielding an arbitrary settled amount M on a positive-probability licensed branch, leaving all first-date observations unchanged. For any proposed finite first-date-only ceiling C, choose M sufficiently large: E[Y]>C. Hence a finite actionable certificate requires an externally defensible horizon cap on served orders, capturable prices, capacities and rights, or valid payoff bounds; technical learning saturation alone is insufficient. This counterexample does *not* assert that real robot demand is unbounded.

## 4. What changes in Models A and B, and what remains unproved

**Model A (fixed qualified production):** a finite original-buyer service list, accepted/paid outcome law and resource inventory may be closed under legal recovery, making B checkable with conditional demand and settlement envelopes. Its dual is a dynamic counterpart to the existing LP resource-price certificate.

**Model B (recursive pooled firm):** transitions must endogenize learned qualified recipes, source/embodiment transfer, digital and physical agent coordination, production-created data, pooled fleet/operator interference, target re-grounding and buyer order/pricing responses. New capabilities alter *the graph of future available production*, not merely task success percentages. An economically closed finite horizon may be established through certified future-state reachable sets; otherwise do not assert the upper bound. The Nov22 same-market SG/E comparison cannot be transported from isolated robot islands without the earlier network-exposure/pooled-learning bridge.

**Proposition on testing.** An admissible matched study that measures only first-service qualification or historical trained-controller reward cannot distinguish q=4/5 from q=9/10 here. A legally supported second-buyer order-and-paid-settlement observation conditional on first-source provenance, qualification and rights—or a defensible externally signed upper q—can. This is a falsifiable *identification requirement*, not a claim about how many field trials suffice. An E comparison separately needs approved embodiment policy support and paid original-buyer outcomes, or a valid E value interval. Multiple robots, firms, sectors and source origins require the same closed economic-event ontology rather than a common data-modality label.

**Prior-art boundary.** The super-solution and resource-price argument follows classical dynamic programming and weak duality. Robust finite MDP conditional-measure/rectangularity precedents: Iyengar, *Mathematics of Operations Research* 30(2), 2005, https://doi.org/10.1287/moor.1040.0129; Nilim and El Ghaoui, *Operations Research* 53(5), 2005, https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.1050.0216 (bibliographic dates checked 21 November 2026). *Data-Driven Automation* (arXiv:2606.10127) §5 already values production-generated future data and Appendix B already treats complementary sources. The contribution being developed is a **candidate synthesis** linking data genealogy, endogenously feasible production networks and ultimately settled original-buyer value; no independently demonstrated theorem novelty or empirical robot performance is claimed.
