## Proposed central claim at the right level of abstraction

When robot capability improvements become economically productive is jointly determined by: (i) which **complete** services become qualified and safely supported, (ii) whether those services complement existing production recipes and match actual customers, (iii) which physical, human, data and organizational resources remain bottlenecks, (iv) how deployment produces both accepted service **and** valuable observations, and (v) which future customer orders and embodied capabilities the current deployment itself brings into reach. Raw cross-embodiment benchmark accuracy is not a measure of captured customer value. This is a **candidate economic synthesis**, not an established novel general theorem or evidence that robotics adoption follows a particular measured curve.

The canonical sequence is:
\[
\underbrace{(x_t,g_t,h_t,\theta)}_{\text{source, target alignment, body, regime}}
\ \xrightarrow[\text{intervention, safe support, rights}]{\text{learning/validation}}
\ Q_{t+1}
\ \xrightarrow[\text{scarce resources, qualification bundles}]{V(Q;\omega)}
\ \text{accepted customer production}
\ \xrightarrow[\text{market response}]{}
\ \omega_{t+1}
\ \xrightarrow[\text{physical/financial constraints}]{}
\ \text{next acquisition choice}.
\]
This is a causal/economic architecture, not a plug-in data premium. Reports and corrections affect both inference about \(\theta\) and actual controller competence. Demand and visited state support depend on the chosen policy; post-treatment states cannot serve as unconditional transport strata.
