## Premises

A deployed machine can serve as a coordinate reference for evidence acquired on a different body. A sequence of redesigns changes the correspondence between the archived action and today's eligible procedure. A firm can preserve intermediate bodies, retain a portable representation of the relevant relationships, or pay for a new measurement. The last two may dominate owning equipment. The relevant object is neither the number of reference links nor their individual average accuracy: it is the joint distribution of those relationships conditional on the observations actually retained. One reference test can inform more than one subsequent decision, yet each recipient still has to meet its own acceptance and capacity constraints.

Technical motivation: Zhou et al., [Simultaneously Calibration of Multi Hand-Eye Robot System Based on Graph](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02518), May 2023, abstract consulted November 25, reports graph-based calibration including closed-loop constraints and physical robot experiments. Chen and Schwertfeger, [Heterogeneous Multi-sensor Calibration based on Graph Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11167), May 2019, abstract consulted November 25, reports inconsistent pairwise sensor-frame calibrations and joint refinement. These motivate retaining relations and dependence; neither establishes the parity model, portability economics, qualification costs or market structure. Chen and Waggoner, [Informational Substitutes](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.08636), March 2017 abstract, already develops decision-dependent information complements/substitutes. The following binary algebra is standard Fourier/Bayesian conditioning; shared-input and procurement economics are prior foundations. DDA §5 and Appendix B already incorporate production-generated data and complementarity. The candidate synthesis is dated physical calibration, service qualification and rival access as one constrained investment problem, not a claim of mathematical novelty.

## 1. Transfer over a chain is a joint moment

Write the convention of body j as A_j in {-1,+1}. Let D_j=A_(j-1) A_j be the relative change, and R_i=X_i A_i the archived task relation for task i. The target procedure at generation n is T_i=R_i product_(j=i+1)^n D_j. Local qualification selects a sign and tests it before release; an unsuccessful test never enters paid production. At a given inherited belief b, the maximum probability of one successful test is (1+|E_b T_i|)/2. With several tests, the results also update beliefs about other tasks. Historical task relations may themselves be uncertain and correlated with drift.

Suppose a shared change Z affects several links, D_j=Z U_j, and Z and the U_j are mutually independent conditional on the inherited history, with means z and u_j. For a chain of k consecutive links,

    E[product D_j] = z^(k mod 2) product u_j.

Proof: each sign squares to one; factor the remaining independent variables. Marginal link means are z u_j, so multiplying those means incorrectly inserts z^k. For a balanced common change, the marginal means vanish but the even-length composed bridge can be highly informative. An independently redrawn common factor per link instead produces the usual product. The sign of the error is not universal: if adjacent changes are balanced but constrained to be opposite, the composed bridge is known negative. With arbitrary correlation use the joint product, not a fixed sign or a universal decay exponent. This is a model of symbolic compatibility, not a claim that actual 3D transformations commute.

## 2. Which representation can substitute for the physical reference?

Represent all retained latent signs (including archive relations and persistent measurement error if relevant) by a binary vector x in F_2^d. Let chi_u(x)=(-1)^(u dot x), and m_u=E[chi_u]. A permitted symmetric noisy observation y of contrast chi_v has accuracy q and conditional likelihood (1+y l chi_v)/2, with l=2q-1. For any positive-probability report,

    m'_u = (m_u + y l m_(u xor v))/(1+y l m_v).

Proof: multiply the old density by the likelihood and normalize, using chi_u chi_v=chi_(u xor v). The moments on the span S generated by economically relevant target parities and all available future observation parities suffice for these updates. They determine the full joint law of those parities by finite Fourier inversion; hence they also determine joint qualification outcomes and any reward depending on that law. If state evolution is independent additive binary drift, moments multiply by the corresponding joint drift moment, even when drift coordinates are correlated. For a general action-dependent transition, require that the conditional expectation of each relevant character be a function of the represented state, e.g. belong to the span of retained characters. Add the resource/rights/time and original-order state separately. With these closure conditions, finite-horizon backward induction on the actual action menu makes the representation decision-sufficient. A fixed state-contingent policy can implement it; no claim of advantage over an equally capable fixed rule.

Universal sufficiency for arbitrary payoffs on the relevant joint outcomes requires determining their joint law: if two representations identify different joint laws, there is a joint event with differing probability and an event-contingent payoff that discriminates them. This does NOT imply that all span moments are necessary for a particular economic menu or that a costly exact representation is optimal. If a diagnostic is never affordable or an order expires, those distinctions may be irrelevant. Correlated errors or informative missingness require their own likelihood, not the symmetric formula applied repeatedly as if noise were independent.

A portable representation that retains only target-wise success probabilities can thus destroy the conditional return to a later diagnostic without changing any current single-target score. Conversely, storing sufficient joint moments with valid update and transition rules can substitute for the old body *for this menu*, provided timely lawful access, validation occupancy and cost are also matched.

## 3. Finite qualification capacity: identical marginals, opposite reference purchases

Three links D1,D2,D3 have balanced marginals. D3 is independent of (D1,D2), whose joint product has mean d. Two archives with known original relations +1 yield T_L=D1 D2 D3 and T_N=D3. Both targets are individually balanced for every d; their product mean is d. Hence a perfect safe test of either reveals the other with optimal probability (1+|d|)/2, though the latter must still pass a separate test before production. A local test reveals the tested binary sign on success or failure; this is a deliberately restrictive informative local qualification technology.

Two distinct original orders have net contributions 4 and 10 relative to ordinary fallback, each admits at most one new robot. H is the number of local validation slots, P is the number of separately funded installations; testing one order spends one H. Both programs retain ordinary fallback and can test either order, accept or decline after the result, and use learned information in all later decisions. The reference option buys a perfect external observation of T_N before the local slots open, at cost 3.5 including real outside measurement resources. No extra local slot is required for that report, but the T_N test still is. This is a calibrated contrast assumption only.

For H=P=2, optimal ordinary value is 2+5(1+|d|): test the lower-value L first, then use its result to choose N's candidate. The reverse ordering earns 5+2(1+|d|); test both because contributions are nonnegative and resources fit. With external report value before fee is 10+2(1+|d|). Thus the marginal external value is 5-3|d|. At fee 3.5 it is bought for |d|<.5 and declined for |d|>.5. At H=1, baseline is 5 for every d, external report supports N with certainty, and the same fee is worth paying irrespective of correlation. Scarcity changes which informational distinctions matter economically. In the two-slot case:

| Conditional joint law | Ordinary optimized contribution | External net | Chosen expected L,N installations |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| d=0 | 7 | 8.5 | .5, 1 |
| d=.8 | 11 | 10.1 | .5, .9 |

Both laws have the same individual test priors; the first site's paid qualification procedure changes the second site's attainable production. Note the higher total expected installations in the d=0 *chosen* program than in d=.8, despite lower optimized contribution: the firm is optimizing net contribution rather than maximizing unit count. (Counts here are 1.5 versus 1.4.) A perfect report of N is not itself an accepted service; no benchmark-to-dollar mapping is made. H, P, demand and the fee are synthetic. More general correlated test errors, shared original orders, expensive retries and continuation generated by actual installed routes remain in the earlier renewal recursion, not independently counted here.

For the common induced-deployment matrix, compare a specified extra source robot or reference purchase against its reoptimized ordinary alternative before counting descendants. The finite response above is the conditional installation menu at a given date, not an installations-per-installation coefficient. Endogenize the report likelihood and joint law using source/embodiment choices to obtain that coefficient; when an extra source changes shared support or rival access, it may alter both signs and recipients. Physical MPK remains a fixed-controller incremental accepted-service vector, not the net information premium.

## 4. Shared reference infrastructure and rivalry

A common calibration result can be reused across rivals; the resource to create it can remain scarce. Consider one original buyer in market 1, with a conventional fallback costing C. Two suppliers' ordinary qualification events are independent, each with probability p. Qualified suppliers each have real cost C-D, D>0; a reverse second-price procurement with fallback at C awards a qualified supplier the cost saving D as margin only if it is the sole qualified supplier. In both regimes a buyer pays a small qualification bonus b>0 to each verified qualified bidder, independent of award, to make seeking qualification strictly worthwhile whenever success can occur; assume prepaid validation slots and zero incremental test cost beyond the reference fee. Bonuses are transfers, not real benefits. Both bidders retain their ordinary programs. One infrastructure purchase F yields a usable report with probability q, independent of ordinary events. On that event the designated eligible recipients can qualify with certainty after their local tests. Otherwise ordinary qualifications persist. q can be generated by the joint reference-link technology above; the all-or-nothing perfect usable report is a restriction, not an inference from marginal test scores.

A separate market 2 can be served robotically only by firm B; its ordinary qualification probability is p2, real saving D2, and captured share alpha. It has a separate prepaid validation and installation opportunity. Shared access also allows this buyer to benefit from a usable report. Exclusive access by A does not. This distinct original order is counted once.

For any two joint qualification probabilities in market 1, p_A,p_B and joint j, gross real saving is D(p_A+p_B-j); private gross margins before bonuses are D(p_A-j), D(p_B-j). Thus for symmetric marginal improvement Δp, both competitive margins decline while gross real saving rises if Δp<Δj<2Δp. Shared success can create exactly that covariance shift: Δp=q(1-p), Δj=q(1-p^2). Under open access the changes from no infrastructure, before its sponsor fee, are

    private A: q(1-p)(b-Dp)
    private B: q(1-p)(b-Dp) + q(1-p2) alpha D2
    aggregate real: q[D(1-p)^2 + D2(1-p2)] - F.
    qualified robotic orders: q[(1-p)^2 + 1-p2].

Under A-exclusive access, A's gross increment is q(1-p)[D(1-p)+b]; B loses q D p(1-p), and aggregate real increment is q D(1-p)^2-F. These follow by enumerating the useful-report and ordinary states. The qualification bonuses are common transfers in the social account. With conventional service available, the robotic-order response measures substitution and associated resource savings, not additional final-service volume.

A firm may choose whether to fund the common reference; if either sponsors, both obtain access and the sponsor pays F once. If both gross private increments are negative, neither sponsors in the unique equilibrium for F>0 (when the other sponsors, abstention saves the fee; when the other abstains, sponsoring lowers payoff). Exclusive A purchase can nevertheless be privately profitable. Shared infrastructure is jointly efficient relative to no purchase when its real gain is positive, and superior to exclusive A access when market 2 has additional reachable savings. A buyer consortium, side payments or common funding can implement the shared route if it can capture enough of the real gains; no universal claim that it will. A cheaper licensed equivalent, scarce local slots, a different price mechanism or costly duplicate validation can remove the result.

Synthetic example p=.5, q=.8, D=10, b=.2, p2=.4, D2=6, alpha=.1, F=1. Shared gross private changes -1.92 and -1.632 imply neither sponsors; its funded real gain is 3.88 and robotic-order gain .68. Exclusive A gross gain 2.08 exceeds F; real gain 1, robotic-order gain .2. Thus the same technical report becomes *more* useful across the industry yet neither private firm would finance open access. The distinction follows from joint qualification and capture, not a generic assertion of underinvestment in data. If the second market lacks timely commissioning or rights, its gain disappears; if shared access is too costly, or a rival benefits enough to pay, equilibrium changes. If testing has positive marginal cost or availability responds strategically, one must solve the testing game; we have not assumed these quantities away in a general claim.

## 5. Evidence and scope

A matched panel would retain dated source/target lineage and all pairwise and loop-closure measurements across successive hardware generations, including ordinary test results and failures. Compare a portable representation, physical reference and independent external diagnostic on joint held-out acceptance, matching crew time, rights, available orders and installed hardware. Test whether the retained joint correlations predict the value of observing an additional test and whether the induced investment changes when one versus two validation slots are available. In a material-handling setting routine repetitive testing may substitute for a calibration visit; in multistage manufacturing common metrology may couple otherwise separate workcells; in sparse inspection limited repeat encounters may make an outside reference more valuable. These are discriminating hypotheses, not observations of their economic magnitude.

For shared access, observe original buyer opportunities, the joint set of qualified suppliers, actual fees, fallback, dated distinct local tests, and who funds and can reuse each reference. An average supplier qualification increase cannot identify real gains or lost private rents. No field joint economic panel is claimed. The single-market qualification/competition model in the common production-of-capability work remains the prior base; the added mechanism joins correlated cross-vintage representations and bounded access to a separate recipient market. Further work should endogenize the number and price of reference measurements, allocation of a shared metrology crew across firms and dates, and the resulting source/recipient response when qualified suppliers strategically choose whether to incur real marginal test costs. Do not claim a general welfare ranking.
