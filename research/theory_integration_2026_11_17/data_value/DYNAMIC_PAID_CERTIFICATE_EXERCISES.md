# Exercises: dynamic original-buyer economic opportunity

Do not read the solutions file until attempting the proofs. All numbers are hypothetical and normalized.

1. Prove the conditional super-solution bound B_0 for arbitrary finite-horizon adaptive complete policies when the true kernel is contained in each declared outer set. Identify precisely whether rectangularity is needed for validity or only robust tightness.
2. Introduce an expected resource budget b and nonnegative multipliers lambda. Derive the supermartingale and bound lambda.b + B_0^lambda. Explain why adding lambda.b as a separate accounting expense is a mistake.
3. For first paid p=2/5, conditional future paid q=4/5 after licensed data and grounding, conditional recovery paid r=1/2, costs c=3/20,d=1/20, compute the optimal conditional whole-horizon SG value. Compare with an E complete value of 19/20 and with the additive nonexclusive upper bound.
4. Find the q at which SG ties E in problem 3. Construct a distinct future-buyer-demand world with opposite SG/E ranking yet identical first-buyer outcome laws; name the missing identifiable event.
5. Remove the right to train on first-service data. Calculate the SG value if failure recovery remains lawful. Explain why this is not an instance of optimally free optional data lowering value.
6. Give a two-world example where independently maximizing stagewise transition bounds overstates the highest value attainable in any coherent world. Explain why the bound remains safe and which joint source/market evidence could tighten it.

7. Suppose source x changes initial paid-service probability from p_0 to p_1 and grounding g alone changes conditional future paid probability from q_0 to q_1, with common recovery and costs. Prove the source×grounding paid-value cross-difference and give a substitutive counterexample with every individual optional intervention nonnegative.
