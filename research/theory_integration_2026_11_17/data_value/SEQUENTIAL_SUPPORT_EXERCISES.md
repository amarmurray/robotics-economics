# Sequential support — student exercises
Do not consult SEQUENTIAL_SUPPORT_SOLUTIONS.md before attempting the proofs. All payments are hypothetical optimized service contributions.

**1. Selected qualification.** Two equally likely regimes have first-correction rates 3/4 and 1/4. The first correction unlocks a safe second B evaluation with exposure one. Show that the posterior regime probability is 3/4 after correction. Compute joint B qualification for second-episode qualification chances (3/4,1/4) and (1/4,3/4). Why is the product of marginal rates wrong, despite the same unconditional mean second qualification chance?

**2. Competent specialist.** A qualified A service exists. B would add 8 of captured complete-service contribution if it validates. A B validation displaces 3, while a separate specialist can earn 1 incremental in the same second slot. Use the post-correction conditional qualifications from Exercise 1. Which second action is chosen in either world? If the first supervised probe forgoes 1/4 today and is the only first action that creates a correction, compare the globally optimal two-episode actions.

**3. Observational equivalence.** Prove that unlimited first-episode correction and exposure logs cannot distinguish the two worlds when the observed first kernels are identical. Then prove a stronger zero-support impossibility by changing the B transition only on states that none of the logged policies visit. What additional safe measurement or assumption could distinguish the worlds?

**4. Option value under a better robot.** A newly trained policy produces no binary corrections but delivers more accepted service. Does the binary signal's information ordering prove this new policy economically inferior? What if old policies remain usable for free? What if the new policy forcibly replaces the old one?

**5. Customer demand and bundles.** Assume the customer is fully served by the incumbent, so f(A+B)-f(A)=0, but B's held-out technical qualification is high. Recalculate the B-versus-specialist comparison. Next suppose a complementary qualification C is required to make the customer accept B; explain why the conditional chance of B alone need not imply an immediate gain and which joint event must instead be estimated.
