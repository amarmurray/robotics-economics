## Candidate contribution and next falsifying test

The candidate is a measurement-linked explanation of how customer production structure transforms capability learning into economic value. It requires economically grounded qualification units, correct joint acquisition laws, competent alternatives and a dynamic/organizational extension. It is not yet an accepted original paper contribution.

A decisive test changes customer demand or a conventional fallback while leaving technical learning fixed, and checks whether the economically productive qualification bundles change as predicted. A second compares independent versus shared acquisition with the same marginal technical performance. A third observes progress and retention to distinguish adaptive stopping from batch commitment. These tests need qualified complete-service and real cost/payment observations; standalone benchmark scores do not supply them.

## Demand/contract integration (17 November 2026)

**Derived, conditional, not externally validated:** Under fixed qualified B incremental receipt flow, independent exponential qualification, stationary known concave accepted-A demand, and development that displaces incumbent capacity, the private option premium of shared-hardware qualification is nonincreasing in incumbent demand. Under matched separate specialist hardware, its premium need not be. Proof and exact synthetic reversal: [DEMAND_CONTRACT_THEORY.md](DEMAND_CONTRACT_THEORY.md), equations D6–D8. The finite service-floor and penalized acceptance cases use stated hypothetical contract terms. They are NOT reports of DHL/GXO/Formic contracts.

**Verified public observations:** DHL's January 31, 2023 commercial unloading description and May 13, 2025 future case-picking expansion plan; GXO's June 27, 2024 formal multi-year RaaS contract and October 26, 2026 report of accumulated tote movements and 2027 plans; Formic's February 2024 description of billing only for productive robot time. URLs, dates, and interpretation are in the detailed theory note.

**Not identified:** complete-order willingness to pay, output-demand curvature, concurrent A production while collecting B data, site/order-level acceptance, customer SLA floor/penalty, provider net payments and collection dates, or matched specialist-vs-shared learning hazard. Issuer claims do not calibrate these.

**Prior art challenge:** Production pricing and network revenue management already jointly model demand and capacity (Deng–Yano 2006; Maglaras–Meissner 2006), and joint demand learning/price/finite resources exists (Chen–Jasin–Duenyas 2021). Distinct candidate synthesis is timed qualification/production/rights/payments, not invented concavity or Bellman analysis. Additional prior-art audit is still required before Dec6 claim selection.
