## What is established in this block

1. A set of additional qualified production recipes has no operating value exactly when one common old optimal dual resource-price vector supports every new recipe. This is an application of LP duality, not a novel optimization theorem.
2. A production chain can require an arbitrarily large bundle of qualifications before any incremental operating value appears. Other networks make qualifications substitutes. Neither diminishing nor increasing marginal returns to qualification edges is universal.
3. Under independent small-probability acquisitions, the smallest economically productive qualification bundles determine the leading order of expected gain. Customer demand can change that order without changing robots or their learning technology.
4. Concave individual technical learning can induce an S-shaped economic return and a discontinuous investment decision under batch commitment. Numerical thresholds depend strongly on the technical response function.
5. The economic sign of joint qualification depends on the complementarity of the production opportunities. Shared acquisition events can change the leading order; per-capability averages alone do not settle the comparison.
6. Adaptive observation and retention preserve the minimal-event gross order under bounded independent acquisition rates, but sharply alter net investment policies. The homogeneous cost-aware chain is now solved recursively. A budget of 1 can support positive adaptive value where the batch model prescribes no investment. Do not perpetuate the rejected universal large-upfront-bet claim.

These are conditional derivations, not observed robot returns.
