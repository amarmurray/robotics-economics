# Contracted service can outlast an entry advantage

Edition 0.24, continuation of the same Deployment and Entry Decision campaign. The accepted input is edition 0.23 at `133903316c3710fbfb288934baa05722a4f8cc03`. All results below are deliberately synthetic cleaning-service executions. They neither estimate a target customer's contracts nor calibrate robots from the recent commercial disclosures.

Equal access to robots leaves three distinct questions: can the entrant win a recurring service, can it keep delivering it, and do actual collections recover its recorded operating outlays? The [renewal extension](../model/COMPETITIVE_RENEWALS.md) now derives assignments from funded capacity, locked prices, expiry and prior service. Earlier scheduled customer moves remain preserved in their original cases. This bounded addition advances **DE-CF05**, without claiming an equilibrium or a general investment ranking.

## A price-response window is not a cash-recovery window

The common reference has 500 recurring zones per month, ten identified fifty-zone contracts, two robots requested by each business, paid staff and the same financed supply. The incumbent's default $29 bid becomes $25 when qualified supported robots are available; the entrant bids $27. Switching work, onboarding, formation, collections, support and fixed obligations remain. The four primary timing worlds have identical physical plans, installed unit identities, qualified cohorts and support allocations. Assigned demand and actual human/robot use may differ. They change only the incumbent response rule or its earliest month.

| Twelve-month world | Entrant accepted zones | First cash break-even | Entrant operating cash after recorded bills at close |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Prompt supported-robot response, first month 2 | 200 | Not observed | −$87,884.51 |
| Response first in month 7 | 2,400 | Not observed | −$34,684.51 |
| Response first in month 10 | 3,900 | Month 8; maintained through month 11 | $4,315.49 |
| No incumbent response | 4,900 | Month 8; maintained through month 11 | $16,815.49 |

All four deliver 6,000 market zones in total; customer transfers never add market activity. The date convention is zero based. These are outcomes of declared prices and commitments, not a predicted competitive margin or a universal response deadline. The nonresponsive incumbent still delivers residual work; it is not removed from the market.

The no-response world observed only through month seven delivers 2,900 entrant zones but ends at **−$5,054.19**, with uncollected invoices. Extending actual execution to month eleven yields the positive value above. Keeping twelve months but delaying entrant receipts from one to three months preserves 4,900 accepted entrant zones and changes terminal operating cash after bills to **−$10,184.51**. Booked revenue and accepted output cannot finance an earlier obligation before settlement. No annualization or additional observed period is invented.

Delayed procurement is a separate world because it changes shared unit allocation and the entrant's own commissioning timing. With incumbent procurement delayed to month seven, response occurs in month eight. The entrant first crosses zero in month eight, then ends at **−$21,425.80**. A first cash crossing is not durable recovery. The initial incumbent reference similarly crosses in month one, falls below zero again and only remains nonnegative from month five. Report both dates rather than choosing the favorable one.

## Customer protection and capacity protection are different

A six-month contract prevents unilateral repricing. The entrant keeps its initial 100 recurring zones through month five, delivering 600 entrant zones overall, versus 200 under monthly renewals. Yet it ends at **−$77,484.51**. The same lock also prevents it from competing for the incumbent's later-expiring lots when new capacity arrives. Term length is not an automatic one-way advantage for entry.

One month's price difference on a fifty-zone lot is exactly $100. At that switching-cost tie the incumbent retains the customer; at $99.99 the entrant can win its two initially fundable lots. The one-cent witness tests a declared contract rule, not the empirical precision of a buyer preference. Onboarding must also be affordable. An unfunded onboarding bid pays neither fictitious onboarding nor switching resources and leaves the customer with the next funded eligible provider. Separate tests make buyer funding fail after recipient onboarding would otherwise have been affordable; atomic admission prevents a stranded resource payment.

With $9,000 entrant opening cash and six-month terms, the entrant funds its initial awards but misses service in month one. The permitted prior-service exit allows reassignment in month two: market service totals **5,900**. Removing that exit keeps unserved contracts locked until expiry and totals **5,500**. The 400-zone difference is restored future service; neither policy recovers the first 100 missed zones. Both preserve the failed entrant's fixed obligations and end with the same **−$37,600** operating cash after recorded bills. A customer lock cannot cure an underfunded operator.

A human-led incumbent that forgoes its robot order and responds in month two still delivers the complete 6,000 market zones with its funded staff. The entrant again delivers 200 zones and ends at **−$90,225.80**. This is a separate physical/procurement comparator, not the pure timing contrast. Its purpose is to challenge the assumption that buying robots is necessary to defend service. It is not an optimized staffing or shutdown strategy, and no digital uplift is estimated from a benchmark.

## Implications for deployment, entry and the three packs

For an organic cleaning entrant, proceed to target measurement only with a defined accepted service, an executable onboarding/capacity plan, durable customer access and dated collections that survive a plausible incumbent offer. Wait where a temporary price gap or robot procurement lead is the only advantage. Reject an asserted cash-recovery claim that substitutes booked sales, financing inflows or locked but undelivered work for actual funded service and collections. These are evaluation gates, not a recommendation to make a financial transaction.

A customer-side platform choosing build, acquire or hybrid must compare the actual retention and authority it receives. [The acquisition cases](entry_transitions.md) preserve inherited obligations and separate purchase price from working equity. The new renewal cases demonstrate a mechanism that may change the value of those customer relationships, but no combined valuation, acquisition premium or automatic synergy is calculated. Provider/operator/integrator/financier obligations remain separate. Cheap equipment and an apparently attractive customer contract can coexist with operating cash failure.

For hospital logistics, a commercial renewal cannot establish clinical continuity, handoff acceptance or the available fallback after a failed supplier. For utility inspection, continuity concerns accepted coverage, retained visits and review obligations rather than this model's cleaning zones. For automotive workcells, changing the buyer or provider must preserve qualified configuration, takt, handling and retained support. Transfer the renewal, breach, working-capital and exit questions to these packs; do not transfer synthetic prices, demand or response months. Their existing proceed/wait/reject recommendations and empirical cost gaps remain.

This is substantial H through the conventional operational counterargument: a simple transparent renewal rule, paid human capacity and an improving incumbent can defeat an undifferentiated technology-access thesis. No LLM execution benefit, data exclusivity or automatic capability transfer is awarded. Existing intelligence-role fees, learning/qualification and digital-human evidence retain their separate demonstrated scopes.

## Falsifiers and next work

The entry interpretation changes with measured durable contracts, superior whole-service production, genuinely underserved demand, a funded staffing redesign, slower feasible incumbent response or stronger customer value beyond the chosen price rule. It also changes when realistic termination penalties, qualification lead times, failure guarantees, financing and shutdown/redeployment options alter obligations. None is estimated here. Stable lot order and current-capacity admission are declared conservative policy choices, not optimal behavior.

The [seventeen complete worlds](../model/results/competitive_renewals.json) include qualification delay, thin capital, support scarcity and exact switching boundaries. Bounded coverage becomes thirteen families and 128 worlds; **DE-CF10 remains partial**.
