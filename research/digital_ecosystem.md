## What changes the robotics judgment

Digital AI changes both the candidate robotic system and the incumbent against which it must compete. A robot operator that beats yesterday's human process can still lose to a human operation with better scheduling, documentation, purchasing, exception handling, or engineering support. These digital improvements can also complement robots. The correct comparison therefore needs four matched operating configurations, with an explicit interaction and full implementation costs.

The strongest source evidence in this wave does not support a universal digital productivity coefficient. A large customer-support deployment increased resolved issues per worker-hour, particularly for less experienced workers. A later study of experienced software developers found its current uplift estimate undermined by participant selection, task selection and concurrent-agent time accounting. These are informative differences in production systems, rather than competing estimates of one economy-wide effect. Likewise, the current peer-reviewed agent-coordination study supports conditional architecture choices; it does not establish that adding agents always increases production.

Direct digital contributions to physical systems are real but occur at distinct interfaces. AutoRT physically used foundation models to select and organize data collection. DrEureka used GPT-4 to generate training configurations subsequently tested on hardware. Astranomy evaluated language-model control and oversight in simulation. Open-RMF documents conventional traffic and building interfaces. SAP announces enterprise agents. Combining those sources into a diagram does **not** establish that a working LLM-to-ERP-to-fleet production stack has been deployed or measured.

Three immediate decisions follow. First, select markets using accepted output, timing, integration and support requirements rather than benchmark success alone. Second, keep the customer process owner, deployment integrator, robot controller, model provider and capital provider distinct even when one entity fills several roles. Third, measure learning and R&D gains through validated improvements and critical-path reduction, not raw episodes, generated code or announced customer access.

## The connected ecosystem dossier

The chain is physical inputs and capital → model research, training, post-training and evaluation → inference and cloud distribution → agent/tool runtimes → integration and change management → operating customers → accepted customer output. Each arrow has a different unit and evidentiary status. Training FLOPs are not usable inference capacity; capital commitments are not commissioned capacity; model access is not permission to write business records; a portfolio relationship is not a deployment; an accepted work product is not eliminated payroll.

The separate HI chapter covers power, qualified compute/memory, capacity and feedback to construction, maintenance and infrastructure. This H chapter covers how capability travels through that chain into work. Its upstream development evidence is deliberately bounded: [Hoffmann et al.'s compute-optimal training paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.15556v1) studies allocation between parameters and training tokens under a specified training-compute budget. The domain is text-model training loss and downstream evaluation. Its fitted allocation exponents are neither robot reliability exponents nor coefficients for adoption, manufacturing cost or firm output. Deployment lifetime inference costs and physical experience collection introduce objectives that differ from this fixed-training-budget optimization. Post-training, data licensing and research-talent markets remain incomplete in this wave.

The [FTC's January 2025 partnership report summary](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-issues-staff-report-ai-partnerships-investments-study) identifies equity/revenue-sharing, cloud-spend commitments, distribution, information access and some control/exclusivity rights in selected Microsoft–OpenAI, Amazon–Anthropic and Alphabet–Anthropic relationships. Its information boundary is September 2024 plus public material through January 2025. It supports a mechanism in which finance can be bundled with compute purchases and switching costs; it does not establish the October 2026 terms of every relationship or realized competitive harm. The implication for robotics is to maintain separate financial, compute, data, operational-control and customer-access edges. An investment cannot be counted again as independent cloud revenue and additional free deployment capital in a consolidated scenario.

Two current deployment-company disclosures illustrate distinct organizational mechanisms:

| [OpenAI Deployment Company, 11 May 2026](https://openai.com/index/openai-launches-the-deployment-company/) | An OpenAI-majority-owned and controlled deployment business, more than $4 billion of announced initial investment, partner channels, and an agreement to acquire Tomoro. These arrangements support studying implementation as an economic activity. They do not yet prove that an integrator earns durable rents, that ownership causes faster adoption, or that a customer-side physical newco can finance its ramp. A services company helping existing businesses adopt AI differs from a new operating company selling manufactured products, inspections, deliveries or cleaning. The physical entrant needs customer qualification, facilities, assets, working capital and retained contribution; a lab relationship supplies only some possible complements. A model improvement can strengthen an integrator by expanding useful applications, or weaken it by making previously scarce work easier for customers or platforms to perform. Both mechanisms remain open.

The [Census CES 26-25 full paper](https://www2.census.gov/library/working-papers/2026/adrm/ces/CES-WP-26-25.pdf), dated April 2026, supplies a better deployment denominator than announcements. For the November 2025–January 2026 reference period, 18% of firms reported AI use in a business function; the employment-weighted figure was 32%. Among functional adopters, 57% used AI in three or fewer functions. These are broad-AI measures, not LLM-only use or physical automation. The revised core question took effect on 17 November 2025, so a discontinuity from earlier wording is not clean adoption growth. Firm use refers to two weeks, worker-task use to six months, and firms report worker behavior. The study distinguishes adoption, breadth, task use and investment; it does not identify causal productivity from their correlations with commercial performance.

For robotics this favors an adoption record with separate capability, process integration, operating authority, workforce use, economic attractiveness and delivered output. Low adoption need not mean technological incapability, but high benchmark capability does not establish the size of an economically viable deployment overhang. That quantity remains unknown.

## Two contrasting operating dossiers

### Business-software customer support: assistance inside a repeated production process

[Generative AI at Work](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/140/2/889/7990658), published in the QJE on 4 February 2025, examines 5,172 customer-support agents at a software firm and its subcontractors. The rollout primarily occurred in fall 2020 and winter 2021. The tool used GPT-3-based real-time suggestions; agents remained responsible for conversations and could edit or ignore suggestions. The relevant product is a resolved customer issue, not generated text.

Access increased resolved issues per agent-hour by 15% on average. This combined changes in handling time, parallel chat handling and resolution. Benefits were heterogeneous: less experienced/lower-skilled workers gained more, while highly skilled workers showed little productivity improvement and some quality deterioration. Evidence during outages is consistent with some persistent worker learning, but those outages were rare and estimates noisier. The paper's staggered-adoption/event-study design handles observed worker/time/tenure factors and examines alternative estimators; interpretation still requires parallel-trend and adoption assumptions. It is not simply a randomized trial of the full observed rollout.

The mechanism is concrete: knowledge and communication practices are delivered at the point of decision inside an existing workflow, with a human retaining final authority. This suggests a research question for physical end markets: can digital assistance improve dispatch, maintenance diagnosis, operator training or customer-service work around a physical process? It does not supply a 15% uplift for assembly, floor cleaning, nursing, inspection or robot supervision. Even in the source population, more resolved issues per hour does not identify removed payroll, wage changes or equilibrium employment. At fixed work and quality, time can be released; its realized value depends on staffing, demand and redeployment.

For adoption strategy, the relevant complement is not just model quality. It includes historical examples, an interface fitted to live work, human judgment, feedback and training capacity. It also warns that copying the average effect across different worker cohorts can overstate expert benefits. A digitally improved incumbent may become more competitive before installing any robot.

### Software development: changed task selection makes uplift difficult to identify

[METR's 24 February 2026 update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/) explicitly rejects treating its new experiment as a reliable current productivity estimate. The original February–June 2025 study found that AI-allowed tasks took 19% longer, with a reported interval of +2% to +39%. The later experiment began in August 2025 and included 57 developers across 143 repositories and more than 800 tasks. Among ten returning developers, the raw task-time estimate was −18% with an interval from −38% to +9%; among new developers it was −4%, interval −15% to +9%. Negative figures mean less time, not negative productivity.

The crucial finding is methodological. Developers increasingly declined participation because they did not want to work without AI. Participants withheld tasks they expected AI to accelerate. Pay also fell from $150 to $50 per hour between studies, creating another selection mechanism. Some work changed in scope or quality; some developers worked on another task while an agent ran, making task-time attribution difficult. Randomizing submitted tasks does not remove selection into submission. The authors considered the data weak evidence about the magnitude of current speedup.

This does not establish that AI slows developers in October 2026, nor that large current gains are proven. It establishes that a dated average task-time result can lose validity as tools, work choice and organization change. For robotics R&D, the unit should be validated engineering output at a fixed objective, with active human time, elapsed critical-path time, model/training compute, failed proposals and physical testing recorded separately. Faster code generation is not equivalent to a shorter hardware qualification campaign. Concurrent agents may improve total throughput while complicating per-task measurement; that is a reason to improve the accounting, not to assume a gain.

Together these dossiers motivate heterogeneous digital effects, an explicit human baseline, and outcome-specific measurement. They cannot be pooled into one “AI productivity” parameter.

## Benchmark cards and a consequential version correction

The linked JSON retains configuration, task population, budget, assistance, costs, acceptance and missing fields. An unknown budget is explicitly unknown rather than silently comparable.

Added SWE-bench and Terminal-Bench cells have only 20 instances each. |
| [Astranomy](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/srl/projects/astranomy/) | Direct, persistent-agent and orchestrated Astra policies on 120 RoboLab tasks, five fixed-seed episodes each, with simulated DROID. | Simulation pauses during inference; no walltime or retry limit. Medium reasoning and action-step limits are part of the configuration. Success does not measure real-time productive throughput. |
| [DrEureka v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.01967v1) | GPT-4-generated rewards/domain randomization, RL training and real-world quadruped/manipulation tests. | Existing simulation infrastructure, multiple candidates and hardware validation are required. Best candidate performance is not one-shot engineering success or a total R&D cost saving. |

**The July coordination paper materially revises the January Google blog.** The earlier blog describes 180 configurations, four benchmarks and cross-validated R² = 0.513. The peer-reviewed article expands to 260 configurations and six benchmarks, with R² = 0.373, or 0.413 with a task-grounded capability measure. It explicitly labels its approximately 45% single-agent capability threshold a practical selection rule rather than a universal scaling principle. Most descriptive patterns do not survive cluster-robust inference; six benchmark clusters limit precision. The model selects the best architecture in 87% of held-out **within-domain** configurations. A separate validation predicts the sign of multi-agent gain in 94% of 16 model-by-benchmark configurations.

The direction remains useful: structured financial reasoning showed large benefits from coordination, sequential planning deteriorated, and verification architecture affected error propagation. The reported 17.2× versus 4.4× amplification comparisons are benchmark trace measures, not robot accident rates. They motivate comparing a strong single agent, conventional solver and alternative coordination structures with equal information and budgets. They do not set a numerical fleet uplift or a 45% robot-autonomy threshold.

Astranomy makes the economic distinction especially visible. Its page reports Agentic Policy success of 74.8% and 13.37 seconds average inference latency per call. Continued full-page retrieval resolved the initial headline ambiguity: Section 4.2 and Table A11 state that cost values cover **successful episodes only**, priced using October 1, 2026 API rates. The Agentic mean is $36.40 over 447 usage-covered successes out of 449 successful episodes. This is not total expenditure on successful and failed attempts divided by delivered successes. Failure costs, resets and physical support would still be needed for commercial cost per accepted output.

Section 4.1 reports that inference accounts for 97.8% of Agentic estimated episode duration, 97.0% for Direct EEF and 94.9% for Orchestrated Policy. Estimated duration adds rollout time and inference time while the original simulation pauses during inference. It is not an observed real-world operating cycle or proof that the same success would persist while the scene changes. Section 7 explicitly says statistical significance of configuration differences has not been established and excludes contact-rich insertion/assembly and highly dynamic tasks. These qualifications constrain both adoption and architecture claims. A stronger simulated score can coexist with a poor time-constrained production proposition.

## Three direct-contribution maps

### Robot reasoning and control

AutoRT's interface is image/scene understanding → language task proposal → affordance and constitution filtering → chosen scripted, learned or teleoperated policy → observed episode outcome. The physical collection spans four office buildings and seven months. It used 53 robots in total and more than 20 simultaneously; those are different denominators. One human supervised three to five mobile manipulators, and sometimes reset the environment.

Table 1 is important counterevidence to reading “77,000 episodes” as productive autonomous output: 73,293 scripted-policy trials had 21% success, 3,060 teleoperated trials 82%, and 936 RT-2 trials 4.7%. The authors attribute low RT-2 performance to differences between collection environments and its training/inference conditions. Their stated objective is diverse data collection, with a preliminary downstream-learning evaluation. Successful data generation, successful task execution, accepted customer output and profitable operation are four different outcomes.

For the six shared market cells, a proposed digital planner needs explicit state, command authority, validation and fallback. A low-level controller remains separate. The measurable contribution might be improved task selection, fewer interventions, faster recovery or better held-out performance. Each requires a target test; it cannot be inferred from model naming. Astranomy supplies a second, simulated control configuration and highlights why inference cost and timing must be separate from physical-policy quality.

### Fleet, site, and enterprise operations

[Open-RMF interfacing](https://openrmf.readthedocs.io/en/latest/interfacing/index.html) documents fleet state, destination, pause/resume and path messages, plus door state and requests. A fleet that only reports state gives an orchestrator different options from one accepting paths or pause commands. Coordination therefore depends on negotiated interfaces and control rights, not just planning intelligence. The retrieved documentation is a mutable `latest` page, not a pinned deployment release.

The proposed full interface is ERP/WMS/MES orders, inventory and priorities → validated allocation or schedule → fleet manager → robot/building execution → acknowledged state and business-record reconciliation. The process owner defines priorities; the fleet manager controls movement; human staff resolve exceptions. Stale information, an unacknowledged instruction or duplicate transaction can break the chain. Timeouts, idempotency, reconciliation and escalation are proposed design requirements here, not claimed observations of a deployed LLM stack.

[SAP's May 2026 announcement](https://news.sap.com/2026/05/more-autonomous-supply-chain/) supplies intended planning, manufacturing, logistics, asset/service and ERP roles, with phased general availability through 2026. It supports identifying interfaces to investigate. It does not establish which named agent is available at a selected customer or a measured robotics productivity contribution.

The target-side boundary is stronger with [ChristianaCare's operator-authored account](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13048758/). Its grant-funded 2022–25 pilot used three cobots across more than 80 patient areas, reported more than 48,600 deliveries and tested EHR/elevator integrations. Operational value concentrated in nonurgent medication and equipment flows. Complex, time-sensitive nursing workflows were not meaningfully augmented. No LLM effect is isolated. For `CELL-M10-hospital-logistics-US-2025`, this argues for separate urgent and nonurgent cohorts and an explicit request/dispatch/infrastructure interface, rather than treating all hospital physical support as one eligible pool. A large delivery count is insufficient to infer nurse payroll savings.

### Robotics research and development

DrEureka takes simulator code, an objective and safety instructions, generates candidate rewards, builds a reward-aware physics prior, proposes domain-randomization settings, trains policies and tests transfer. It used GPT-4, sampled 16 domain-randomization configurations and trained three seeds per configuration. The authors evaluated candidate policies in the real world. The comparisons show useful technical improvements in bounded locomotion and manipulation settings; they do not remove training or hardware-test needs.

The paper reports a three-hour runtime for its bounded domain-randomization comparison versus ten hours for CEM and twenty for BayRn. That is a method comparison, not a measured reduction in total humanoid engineering labor or development cost. It also explicitly lacks a systematic mechanism to select the most promising policy before deployment. Some ablations generated jerky, unsafe behavior that triggered controller protection. Candidate generation has value only when verification and release constraints are included.

The model consequence is to let an R&D intervention change a validated release's cost or delay only through an explicit critical path. Coding, reward search, training and physical validation can constrain different projects. If hardware testing remains binding, faster candidate generation may improve exploration but not calendar time. METR's measurement concerns reinforce recording total accepted engineering output and concurrent resource use rather than adding an unexplained “research acceleration” multiplier.

## Analogy ledger and permitted use

Eight full cards in the JSON preserve source evidence, target evidence, necessary conditions, material differences, predictions, falsifiers and alternative explanations. None authorizes copying a numerical digital coefficient into robotics.

| Test | Current judgment | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H-AN01: training scale → delivered physical productivity | Source-domain relationship supported; numerical target transfer unsupported. | Model compute, coverage, validation and operating outcomes separately. |
| H-AN02: cheap software replication → fast fleet rollout | Naive transfer rejected. Digital infrastructure itself is physical; robots additionally need qualified units, sites and support. | Keep manufacturing, commissioning and service constraints after capability gates. |
| H-AN03: model availability → integrator rents | Complementary activity is observed; durable rents unproven. | Test repeat deployment cost, customer control, platform bundling and portability. |
| H-AN04: deployment access → data moat | Conditional, with explicit rights and transfer validation. | Raw fleet size or customer count cannot create a separate data-value premium. |
| H-AN05: benchmark performance → autonomous job/payroll replacement | Direct conversion rejected. | Restore workflow boundaries, intervention, timing, quality and demand. |
| H-AN06: more agents → better fleets | Universal claim rejected; architecture-specific hypothesis retained. | Permit positive, zero and negative coordination effects; compare simpler controllers. |
| H-AN07: lab-linked deployco → financeable physical newco | Organizational comparator only. | Separate service integrator, customer operator and financier; fund enterprise ramp. |
| H-AN08: AI improves source workers → improved physical incumbent | Source effects heterogeneous; target coefficient unknown. | Keep digital-only human baseline in every joint scenario. |

An informative falsifier is not simply a failed technology demonstration. For the learning analogy it is deployment expansion that adds little accessible, relevant experience or fails to improve held-out target performance. For integrator value it is an OEM/platform bundle or modular interface that removes the scarce service while retaining customer outcomes. For the newco analogy it is an incumbent adopting the same available technology before the entrant recovers formation and ramp costs.

## Contract for the executable joint scenario

Use the authoritative CPU engine, not a second calculation implementation in this chapter. At matched output, quality, demand and period, let costs be:

| | Existing digital operations | Specified additional digital intervention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Human-led process | C00 | C01 |
| Specified robot-assisted process | C10 | C11 |

The robot's incremental saving under the improved digital baseline is `C01 − C11`; total joint saving is `C00 − C11`. The cost interaction is `C11 − C10 − C01 + C00`, with a negative value indicating complementary cost reduction. Output and service-quality contrasts must be displayed separately. A cell that cannot operate without required embedded robot software is not a valid “robot without software” counterfactual; hold that mandatory software fixed across the robot row and vary only the additional intervention.

Digital assistance to humans, robot planning latency, inference expense, integration amortization, intervention labor and coordination effects need separate controls. Evidence observations remain fixed when those scenario controls change. Shared scheduling or data improvements appear once. Demand expansion and altered service scope require a separate scenario. No source in this wave calibrates the six physical cells' digital-only gain, joint interaction or whole-enterprise labor saving; those parameters remain explicit assumptions or nulls.

The main sensitivities should include a successful digital-only incumbent, little physical transfer, negative coordination overhead, a binding supervision/integration constraint, and an R&D improvement that fails to shorten physical validation. These are informative tests of the strategic mechanism rather than forecasts.

These priorities connect directly to market sequencing, deployment architecture, learning investment, provider boundaries and same-technology incumbent/newco comparisons. They require public research first; unavailable field measurements remain explicit gaps rather than invented calibration.
