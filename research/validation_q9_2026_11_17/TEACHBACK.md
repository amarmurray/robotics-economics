# Teach-back 08 — information versus capability

Assume a hidden target regime with complete-service success g_H=.95 or g_L=.85 and prior 1/2; target net payoffs are V_H=1.16 and V_L=-.76. A qualified incumbent expansion yields J=.30. Each nonbillable complete-order trial costs .01; the new target investment requires 2 liquid units.

1. Derive the likelihood and posterior after n iid full-order trials with s complete successes. Why are eight successful grasps not eight complete-order successes?
2. Derive the posterior high-regime cutoff for choosing the target over the incumbent, and its likelihood-ratio form under equal priors.
3. At n=8, which observed outcomes lead to target entry? What initial cash level makes that conditional choice possible?
4. Why can perfect information be valuable while positive-cost evidence gathering is not, given a budget of exactly 2?
5. What records distinguish investing to **test** a fixed policy from investing to **improve** it? Explain how the two can otherwise be double-counted.
