# Annual commitments can change competition without increasing service

Edition 0.30 adds an enforceable annual support/integration allocation to the existing monthly competitive cleaning case. It addresses a gap in the earlier bridge: proving that twelve monthly ceilings fit inside an annual reference pool does not establish that the pool remains available after other commitments. This is a synthetic mechanism and a decision test, not an observed annual staffing plan or calibrated estimate of robot returns.

The [complete executable contract](../model/ANNUAL_COMMITMENTS.md) holds physical manufacture, unit identities, payer accounts, paid staffing and customer demand in the existing loop. A resource reservation changes actual admissible support or commissioning. It does not create another market's output or another resource bill. Fourteen named challenge families now include eight additional resource worlds, deepening the competitive and scarcity mechanisms rather than adding a new empirical claim.

## The important reversal is in attribution

In the unrestricted control, 14 supported robot-months occur during the first six observed months, July–December 2026. They consume exactly 9,100 support minutes. Reserving all but 3,900 minutes permits September's two bookings and October's four, then blocks every November and December robot booking. January uses its own new-year allocation and restores all four units. It does not restore the eight omitted robot-months.

Under the declared robot-triggered incumbent pricing rule, that interruption changes November and December's incumbent offer from $25 to $29 per accepted zone. The entrant gains 100 recurring zones in each of those months. Its total accepted service rises from 200 to 400 zones over the twelve-month window. Buyer switching resources rise from $200 to $400. This is a conditional consequence of the explicit response rule, not evidence that an incumbent would actually withdraw its price response when robots stop.

**Total accepted market service remains 6,000 zones in both cases.** Already funded human capacity covers the work. The entrant's funding-adjusted operating cash after recorded bills remains negative: −$87,884.51 in the unrestricted case and −$80,319.35 with the support interruption. Neither amount is a complete lifetime return: opening assets, residual value and future unissued obligations have their existing explicit boundaries. Released task hours still do not identify payroll removed.

The human-led response counterfactual makes that distinction stronger. The incumbent buys no robots and follows a declared calendar response. It keeps its $25 offer during a month with no supported robot in the entire market, preserves 6,000 delivered zones and limits entrant service to 200 zones. Procurement and policy intentionally differ from the equal-technology control. A robot-dependent competitive moat cannot be inferred when a feasible paid human operation can preserve service and the same price response.

## Exact resource and calendar boundaries

An allocation of exactly 9,100 first-year support minutes admits all 14 bookings. At 9,099 minutes, the final 650-minute booking is refused and 649 minutes remain unused. The model admits complete paid unit-month bookings; it does not quietly give a robot 649/650 of its required support. Total market output can still stay unchanged through the human alternative. Thus a resource threshold, a robot-output threshold and a customer-service threshold are different claims.

Reserving all but 8,400 first-year integration minutes admits two new units. The other two retain their physical identities and wait for January commissioning, while the first pair continues supported operation. Two 70-hour commissioning bookings use the full available integration allocation. The remaining annual support pool cannot substitute for missing integration capacity. Similarly, ample annual availability cannot waive a 20-hour monthly support ceiling.

The opening-balance control splits the same unavailable support amount between 300,000 minutes of assumed pre-window use and a smaller outside reservation. It preserves all physical, cash and commercial execution exactly. This tests the accounting boundary: reclassifying unavailable capacity must not create new costs, work or benefits. Prior use is not reconstructed history; its historical funding and output remain outside the observation window.

All reservations are synthetic. Their large share of the reference pool is a chosen stress condition, not a finding about a real employer's utilization or supplier commitments. Each year reconciles prior use, outside reservations, actual bookings and unbooked balance to the same reference pool. January never lends capacity backward. Software qualification competes in the support pool, and a one-cent software payment shortfall rolls back both contingent physical payment and annual booking.

## Consequences for the three deployment packs

For **hospital logistics**, require a dated support and integration commitment for the actual cohort and site alongside the complete delivery and fallback obligation. A supplier's annual staffing total cannot establish the hospital's reserved capacity. If competent funded porters preserve the complete service, compare robot task changes and actual avoidable paid commitments separately.

For **utility inspection**, bind access, review, qualification and residual physical visits to the same calendar. An annual survey capacity number does not guarantee the specialist window or remove a required visit. The cleaning minutes and prices do not transfer; only the allocation and service-accounting question does.

For **automotive presentation**, reserve commissioning and old-generation support before relying on a ramp date. A physically received unit can wait for an integration allocation while existing work remains funded elsewhere. Identify whether the stronger conventional workcell or human baseline already preserves accepted kits. The separate component-vector and plant scheduling contracts remain unresolved.

Proceed to a scoped target evaluation when the chosen service, dated usable capacity, paid fallback, unit configuration and each payer's obligations reconcile. Wait when an aggregate annual budget hides competing commitments or the required calendar is unknown. Reject a claimed service or payroll benefit that depends on unsupported robot-hours, backward borrowing of future capacity, imported outside-market output or an untested human baseline. These are evaluation gates, not purchasing or investment instructions.

## What remains unresolved

The annual six-cell model and monthly physical loop use different component representations. This tranche deliberately establishes no conversion between annual actuation/compute/memory vectors and the monthly generic module. Simultaneously executing other markets would require explicit system-compatible bills of materials, qualified receipt dates, shared queues and actual payer obligations. Reservations alone cannot prove joint portfolio feasibility.

The wrapper also does not optimize allocation, investment, exit or bidding, and does not make separate acquisition/recovery/expansion scenarios jointly feasible by adding their results. A next consequential integration must choose one of these missing contracts and test a decision reversal on conserved stocks and cash. More favorable scenario variants would not close that gap. Actual annual resource commitments, response behavior, service outcomes, rights and full costs remain empirical acceptance needs.
