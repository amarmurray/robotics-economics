## Physical optionality versus digital coordination

Robotic firms invest in *qualified accepted customer production*, not skill demonstrations alone. A fully qualified generalist platform may transfer scarce physical capacity between workflows as demand varies. A digitally coordinated specialized fleet may instead release supervisor time by intelligently routing work among distinct hardware and retained workers. The physical and digital choices must be compared against identical customer service criteria and a common available budget.

For two concave customer services, a flexible fleet with total capacity K earns an additional reallocation premium proportional to (1-lambda)Delta²/(4b), where lambda is the synchrony of task demand and Delta the spread in marginal task value. Subtract embodiment cost and any capital premium to compare against a competent coordinated specialist fleet. This is classical process flexibility mathematics. When shared human attention binds, however, the digital specialists can outperform flexible hardware even in states favorable to physical reallocation. In an exact synthetic example, F wins by 6/25 at attention H=1 under opposed task demand, yet loses by 54/125 at H=4/5 because the digital alternative produces accepted service with less supervisor attention.

The empirical object is therefore a typed **production/qualification/coordination network**, not a ranked catalog of robots. LocusONE's heterogeneous Pick & Pass workflow and Amazon's Sequoia illustrate organizationally joined robotic production, but neither calibrates this comparison. The necessary panel joins simultaneous task demand, complete accepted service, qualification/rights, human involvement, customer outside options and actual payer commitments.
