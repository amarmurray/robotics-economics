# Separate solutions

1. For failure events defined on one complete-service distribution, P(any failure)<=sum P(failure_r), irrespective of dependence. Means over heterogeneous tasks, conditional step scores and utility grades are not automatically those marginals. The sum is a sufficient upper bound, not generally an equality or necessary condition.
2. Its inexpensive component has failure .002 without further adaptation. The expensive component can use .018, requiring x=log(.12/.018)=log(20/3). Cost is 8 log(20/3). Verification adds 2. The KKT inactive-component inequality confirms that marginal savings elsewhere do not justify training the already-strong component.
3. Both components are active, so residuals are 1/lambda and 8/lambda. Their sum .02 gives lambda=450. Cost is log(36)+8 log(45/16), about 11.856109. Equal .01 budgets cost about 14.954945. The more expensive component optimally receives a larger failure allowance when the service permits that substitution.
4. The floors sum to .03, ruling out the stated sufficient certificate, not every possible dependence structure or real service. Joint-failure data could justify a tighter aggregation rule. Changed hardware, process or support could change floors, but neither effect is assumed free or proven by this calculation.
5. Under fixed n and true success .99, all-success probability .99^299 is below .05. Repeatedly selecting a passing batch changes the sampling procedure and false-acceptance probability. The simple fixed-n inference is no longer valid without adjustment. Deployment-distribution change is another separate failure of the assumptions.
6. Qualification saving matters only through the alternatives it enables. If the destination's net capturable surplus does not recover entry cost and displaced incumbent value, cheaper qualification does not justify sacrificing current cash. A technically superior transfer path can therefore have lower enterprise value.

A useful empirical design holds the service, configuration, target distribution and quality requirement fixed; varies source experience without changing other training inputs silently; measures adaptation/verification resources and uncertainty; then joins dated costs to customer output, alternatives, prices and contractual capture. No exercise here supplies those observations.
