# A better average policy can be a worse investment

Read Sections 2-5 of RESEARCH_NOTE.md without first reading the solutions.

1. Explain why a mean component success score does not establish a whole-service success rate. Identify precisely what the union bound permits and what it does not identify.
2. Derive the least adaptation cost for the score-rich profile. Why is spending on its inexpensive component unnecessary in this example?
3. Recover the bottleneck-rich profile's optimal failure allocation using a Lagrange multiplier. Compare it with assigning each component exactly .01 failure allowance.
4. Suppose both irreducible failure floors are .015 while epsilon=.02. What has been ruled out: all physically successful production, or a finite solution under this specified certificate? What additional information could change that assessment?
5. Explain why 299 successful iid tests can support the stated one-sided .99 bound, while 10 successive retests until one batch passes cannot be interpreted in the same way?
6. In the joined investment model, why can weaker destination demand overturn the preferred source even when the qualification-cost ranking is unchanged?

The key empirical design task: list the minimum observations needed to distinguish an improvement in mean benchmark score, a reduction in cost to meet a service standard, and an increase in capturable customer value. Those are three different objects.
