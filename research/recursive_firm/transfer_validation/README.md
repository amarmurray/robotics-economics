# What is a transfer audit worth?

A digital disposition model supplies a shared rule to a desk agent, fixed arm and mobile robot. After an upstream release or embodiment change, the old rule may still work at one destination and interfere at another. A firm can buy evidence, disconnect a recipient, keep a validated release, or follow the current donor. Disconnecting also changes which representation gets the next scarce correction. This last feedback accounted for more operating value than selectively prioritizing the audits in this experiment.

## Experiment

Three orders arrive per shift for 60 shifts, one per lane. A complete paid order requires digital release and a correct local action. Margins are 4, 7 and 10. Twelve case families share a donor convention and partially differing native conventions. A trained donor starts with 77% average conditional correctness against its initial rule. Heads implement stochastic binary decisions in canonical action coordinates. On failure the model may acquire a correction. Retention probabilities are 1, .8 and .45 by lane. One post-training slot costing .30 is available to eligible incidents per shift. A disconnected lane spends its selected slot on a native correction, an open lane on the donor representation. Every fifth shift all three heads also receive native calibration costing .90 in total. Labeled updates put weight .60 on new incident evidence and .65 on new scheduled native evidence. Changes in performance therefore change the supply, source and destination of subsequent corrections.

A separately priced audit draws eight case families with replacement from the current operating mixture at one target. Its labels score the local, live and stored models on identical cases. Labels used for validation cannot train the heads. An audit costs 2 and its choice settles after two or seven shifts. The selective reviewer prioritizes margin times time since last inspection times model disagreement (plus a small exploration floor). It can switch to native execution, reopen sharing, or install the exact donor release that was tested. The live comparator may follow later donor updates. A rotation checks the same number of targets on the same dates with the same labels available per audit and the same ability to install checkpoints. The compared policies can produce different future training records even on matched external histories.

We selected the fixed policy from all 27 combinations of local, live and initial-snapshot modes and two incident allocation rules on 48 independent calibration worlds. It froze the desk snapshot, followed the live donor at both robots and prioritized high-margin incidents. We also report wholly separate heads that can learn directly from incidents, the best fixed policy excluding snapshots, and live and pinned audit schemes. Review every two, four or eight shifts was calibrated separately by method. Evaluation uses 192 fresh independent worlds, paired across policies and regimes. Training seeds 200–247 and evaluation seeds 7200–7391 reproduce the split. Default drift changes donor and physical conventions on overlapping subsets of families at staggered shifts and moves demand toward different families after shift 30. Stable, extra-reversal and late-controller regimes and the longer lag test the frozen rules outside calibration. The public task-transfer literature motivates measuring directional links. Its estimates do not set these synthetic parameters. ## Results

Differences below compare selective checkpoint validation every four shifts to the independently selected fixed policy. Net contribution includes all review and training charges. Counts are per 60-shift world. Parentheses give paired 95% Monte Carlo intervals across worlds for contribution. The last column prices one eight-case audit.

| Evaluation | Extra paid orders | Net contribution difference | Break-even audit fee |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Stable, two-shift delay | +3.07 | −18.12 (−23.13, −13.12) | 0.79 |
| Drift, two-shift delay | +6.54 | +17.05 (8.92, 25.18) | 3.14 |
| Additional reversal, two-shift delay | +5.89 | +11.82 (5.05, 18.59) | 2.79 |
| Same reversal, seven-shift delay | +3.22 | −7.93 (−14.39, −1.47) | 1.43 |
| Later controller drift, two-shift delay | +2.00 | −24.81 (−30.52, −19.09) | 0.35 |

For a frozen operating and review policy, the break-even fee against a comparator with zero audits equals incremental contribution before validation charges divided by expected audits. This is a policy-specific shadow price. The fleet can choose a different review schedule if fees change. The two delay treatments admit 15 and 14 audits respectively because reports arriving after the horizon have no in-window use.

In ordinary drift mobile accuracy on the uniform terminal family panel rose from 46.56% to 66.70% against the fixed policy. The adaptive firm opened a previously local link 0.63 times per world, retired 2.48 links and installed a newly audited donor snapshot 6.67 times on average. It consumed 38.14 incident updates against 40.23 for fixed sharing. Its advantage over the matched audit rotation was only 0.96 contribution units (−3.44, 5.36). The wholly local learner actually earned 12.01 more than selective validation (paired interval 4.00 to 20.03). A rule selected on 48 calibration worlds can lose to another member of its candidate set on new histories.

## Which decision did validation improve?

We held the selective audit algorithm, candidate releases, prices and external histories constant, then prevented a disconnected lane from redirecting its selected incident update into its local head. The correction continued training the donor. Enabling redirection added **23.84** contribution units in ordinary drift (19.95 to 27.72) and **16.73** after a second reversal (12.89 to 20.56). In ordinary drift 9.55 incident corrections went to local heads. Each fork evolves on its own ensuing data, so the estimate includes the downstream consequences of changing which failures recur. The hierarchy gains something from letting validation change future training allocation. The measured advantage of choosing the inspection target over rotating it was much smaller and uncertain.

To follow up, a calendar policy could pay for checks in six candidate windows including never. Calibration favored shifts 20–59, around the usual drift. Freezing that choice gave +23.15 against fixed sharing in ordinary drift (15.09 to 31.21) and −17.36 with later drift (−22.59 to −12.13). Knowledge of the typical change date is an economically valuable assumption. Terminal capability and in-window cash are distinct outcomes, and the comparison assigns no resale value to the terminal learner.

## Evidence limits and falsification

These are simulations of symbolic agents. No robot or pretrained LLM was evaluated in this extension. Transfer structure, prices, family support, retention and drift are imposed. No empirical scaling exponent was fitted. The audit has clean labels, sees only sampled present cases and treats the release as reproducible. The experiment omits retraining overhead for restoring weights, settlement beyond the horizon and a learned embodiment adapter. Paired intervals quantify sampling uncertainty within these worlds. Policies share tapes across regimes, so the five rows are not five independent populations.

For a deployment test, fork product releases before an adapter change and compare a rotating sample with priority-weighted inspections at identical spend and report latency. Within both arms independently randomize whether a diagnosed mismatch can redirect the next training slot. Log candidate version, family, record lineage, cash after costs, paid complete orders and fixed-panel skill. If redirecting has little effect on future case arrivals and contribution once the training budget is held fixed, the proposed mechanism loses support. The next unresolved question is whether a fleet can discover a lasting replacement adapter cheaply enough to beat persistent local heads under unannounced changes in drift rate. A local head is a serious alternative to repairing a transfer link.
