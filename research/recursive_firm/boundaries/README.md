# When should a recursive firm own the stack?

The economic question is which decisions a firm must bring inside today to retain the ability to make a better decision tomorrow. A deployment can reveal that an error comes from the training mixture, an unobserved contact condition, a fixture, or the machine design itself. Before seeing the error the firm may be unable to specify which party needs to do what. After seeing it there may be only one shift before the next training run or factory design freeze. Leaving a function outside carries the opportunity cost of discoveries the firm can no longer test or use in time. Bringing it inside can sacrifice supplier expertise, factory utilization, access to other customers’ evidence and scarce internal attention.

Our [companion paper](https://github.com/amarmurray/robotics-economics/blob/b784b262d7503a8baae573d4ff49efcaba48193c/papers/autonomous_firm/FULL_STACK_FIRM.md) takes a manufacturer, model developer and deployment operator under one owner and derives what it will build and learn. Here the **boundary itself is a choice**. Three results follow. A set of functions can be worth internalizing together although none pays in isolation. The premium for faster internal response can peak at an intermediate rate of technical change. A firm can integrate to produce a portable specification that subsequently makes outsourcing economical.

## The economic premises

1. **The job and its claimant.** A customer needs an accepted delivery by a deadline. A digital agent controls admission and escalation, an arm picks, a mobile carrier completes the handoff, and a person can recover some failures. Each can succeed on its own task while the order misses the cutoff. Value the best feasible mixed team’s output, labor by role, and the cash accruing to the party choosing the boundary.
2. **What can be lost.** Only some opportunities enter the robot’s queue. Only some observed cases distinguish a problem the ordinary team would otherwise miss. Only some changes beat the incumbent release before it catches up. Record the dates of the original case, diagnosis, decision, qualification, first accepted work and first eligible hardware batch. A lost option can show up as a change that was never proposed. Measuring only completed engineering tickets selects away that loss.
3. **What the firm can move.** At the hardware layer distinguish design and instrumentation authority, prototype capacity and volume fabrication. At the model layer distinguish training, evaluation and release authority. At the deployment layer distinguish integration, dispatch, teleoperation and observation of customer acceptance. Each can be bought, performed internally or delegated under an agreement. Full stack here means internal manufacturing, model development and deployment/integration. In all arrangements component markets, customers and their retention rights can remain external.
4. **The feasible rival.** A contract manufacturer can build to a design the firm can revise. An external model provider can reserve a validation slot. A customer can grant access to a pre-routing reference panel. Compare integration with the best feasible package of these alternatives, including its cost, rights on unfamiliar cases, partner investment and actual response time. Common ownership can also leave the internal teams separated by queues or incentives.

Grossman and Hart explain why residual control over unspecified uses of assets can matter and why moving it can reduce the other party’s incentives. Langlois asks how the cost of acquiring or transmitting capabilities changes as firms and markets learn. The specific mechanism here is that controlling a deployment changes the evidence available to change another layer, and the resulting invention can itself change which decisions can subsequently be delegated. [Grossman and Hart](https://doi.org/10.1086/261404) · [Langlois](https://doi.org/10.1093/icc/1.1.99)

## The opportunity cost of leaving a loop outside

For each original job and date $t$, let $w_t$ be the incremental operating contribution available if a particular reusable method has qualified in time. Let $X_{g,t}$ indicate that the method is available under arrangement $g$. Each arrangement gets its best ordinary fallback. For the restricted comparison in which recipient work, per-use benefits and fallback are common, the extra learning benefit from integrated arrangement $I$ over partner arrangement $P$ is

$$
L_{I,P}=\sum_t\beta^t\,\mathbb E[w_t(X_{I,t}-X_{P,t})].
$$

Sum over original uses and count the same benefit once even if it requires several layers. The expectation allows difficult, high-value cases to take longer to qualify. Add differences in direct production margins and subtract extra engineering, management, capital and switching costs. To measure the focal firm's willingness to invest, use its own captured margins. To assess real value, include effects on suppliers and customers and remove pure transfers. A higher captured margin from repricing alone supplies no evidence of improved inquiry. When governance also changes offered jobs, equipment or funding, compare the full optimized paths of dated net contributions. In that case mechanically holding $w_t$ fixed would miss displacement and entry.

**Why a stack can be worth more than its layers.** Suppose a class of unresolved failures requires an instrumented hardware revision, retraining and release on a customer site. The hardware change exposes a previously invisible contact condition. Retraining can then distinguish the condition. The site's acceptance test determines whether the new response actually earns anything. In a restriction where every externally controlled step for this class would miss the design window, only the joint bundle creates a timely option of value $L$. Suppose other operations are equally served by partners and internalizing each function carries a positive net cost $k_s$. The full bundle wins precisely when $L>\sum_s k_s$. Every isolated addition to an entirely external stack loses money. A sequence that admits only individually profitable layers can therefore stop too early.

The same logic can favor a narrower boundary. If a supplier can manufacture the revised part before the freeze under a reserved-slot agreement, compare that hybrid against the full bundle. The incremental learning reason to own fabrication then vanishes for this class. A dedicated prototype line can coexist with outsourced volume production. Conversely, process changes that can only be tested on the volume line put manufacturing back in the comparison. In general full ownership must beat **every** feasible hybrid, including the option to wait. The pivotal object is the set of decisions needed to close an economically important loop before its opportunity expires.

## A faster-changing frontier can favor either boundary

Hold the source opportunities, reachable users and captured payoff $w>0$ fixed. An otherwise identical qualification takes $d_I$ periods internally and $d_P>d_I>0$ with the best partner. The method remains useful with probability $\rho$ per period, independent across periods until expiry. In this restriction each arrangement receives one payoff at qualification if the method survives. With $x=\beta\rho$, the gross return to internal speed is

$$
w(x^{d_I}-x^{d_P}).
$$

Its interior maximum as a function of $x$ is at $x^*=(d_I/d_P)^{1/(d_P-d_I)}$, if that point is reachable. Differentiate the two powers to obtain this threshold. At very short lifetimes both teams usually miss. With very long lifetimes the partner also usually qualifies and the remaining premium includes discounting the delay. For a one-period internal and two-period partner lag the premium is $wx(1-x)$. If persistence crosses the peak and internal overhead is sufficiently large relative to the gains at both ends, the firm integrates only in the middle. If internal action is immediate, the short-lifetime conclusion changes. If an external specialist responds faster, the speed premium can favor the specialist. Repeated work requires integrating the dated lead over eligible uses. [Prior work on the location of the audit](../inquiry/audit_location/README.md)

## Integration can produce the means to outsource

Consider a new embodiment with an **unresolved** interface. Engineers can ship through it, but a new supplier cannot reliably reproduce how the incumbent diagnoses and qualifies changes. A **portable** interface exists when an independent team can use a retained test, action specification and acceptance criterion on an unfamiliar lot. Codification is an output of engineering work. The firm's weekly choice is to serve current work or to divert resources to matched field, model and hardware experiments that may produce that interface. A retained record alone does not make this transition.

For an unresolved interface under arrangement $g$, effort choice $e$ gives net flow $\pi_g(e)$ and chance $r_g(e)$ of producing a portable interface by next period. Operationally $r$ is the joint chance that an informative case is encountered, the necessary layers can test it, and an independent team reproduces the resulting procedure. Matched cases, reservation of test capacity and response authority can each change that joint probability. There is no imposed ranking of $r$ across owners. Hold the distribution stationary within each state. For this solved problem portability persists for the remaining horizon, and no financing constraint prevents a privately worthwhile switch.

Suppose mature partner production earns $m_P>m_I$ per period. Entry into integration costs $F$ and handing the function back costs $D$. Mature values are

$$
W_P=\frac{m_P}{1-\beta},\qquad
W_I=\max\!\left\{\frac{m_I}{1-\beta},W_P-D\right\}.
$$

For each boundary compute the best value of staying with it while unresolved, allowing a change at maturity,

$$
Q_g=\max_e\frac{\pi_g(e)+\beta r_g(e)W_g}{1-\beta(1-r_g(e))}.
$$

This solves the one-state continuation equation. For example, starting with effort zero, an alternative experiment that costs $k$ in current flow and raises the transition probability by $\Delta r$ improves the policy precisely when $\beta\Delta r(W_g-Q_g(0))>k$, where $Q_g(0)$ is the value of maintaining effort zero until maturity. A better way to investigate has a price because it changes when the firm can adopt a cheaper way to produce.

**The integrate-then-delegate path.** Starting with the partner, integrate while unresolved if $Q_I-F>Q_P$. Once a portable interface exists, delegate if $(m_P-m_I)/(1-\beta)>D$. Both inequalities can hold at once. With time-homogeneous unresolved opportunities, waiting one period solely to pay the same entry cost in the same unresolved state cannot improve the optimum. More formally the two unresolved incumbent values are $\max(Q_P,Q_I-F)$ and $\max(Q_I,Q_P-D)$ for nonnegative switching costs. Substitution into the two switching Bellman equations establishes the comparison. Outside this restriction new evidence about the problem, a scheduled factory opening or a financing constraint can make waiting valuable.

An exact range check illustrates the importance of the comparator. Take $\beta=0.9$, mature weekly contributions 10 for the partner and 8 internally, entry cost 4 and exit cost 2. Under the partner, ordinary and investigative weekly choices give (net flow, portability probability) of (4, .05) and (3, .10). Internally they give (4, .10) and (2, .40). The optimal partner path is worth 63.16 and the integrated path after entry is worth 77.04. During inquiry, the latter earns **less current operating contribution**. It pays because a tested procedure can reach a cheaper production regime sooner. Now admit a joint lab costing 1 to set up and 1 to unwind, earning 3.5 while unresolved and qualifying the portable interface with probability .35 per period. Its value of 82.58 overturns the ownership ranking. These are constructed economies. The [replication](check.py) also verifies both switching equations in 162 parameter combinations. Run **python3 check.py** in this directory.

## What would distinguish feedback capture from ordinary vertical integration?

Amazon’s Robin work documents a pick predictor trained on over 394,000 production picks that guides subsequent choices on physical arms. This establishes a route from production to model to deployment. It leaves open which steps a qualified supplier could have carried out and what owning additional layers would have earned. [Li et al., RSS 2023](https://www.amazon.science/publications/large-scale-package-manipulation-via-learned-metrics-of-pick-success)

For a prospective comparison, start at the same new hardware vintage. Assign comparable release groups to ordinary supplier support, a partner with matched data rights and reserved revision capacity, or internal residual authority where the organizational comparison is feasible. If ownership is predetermined, randomize the portable rights and the partner response capacity and interpret the result as the effect of those rights. Preserve qualified ordinary checkpoints, compute budgets, and the strongest site-level team in every arm. Predefine the freeze date and sample original jobs before dispatch. Track deadlines missed on never-attempted changes as well as completed tickets.

The feedback explanation predicts the largest difference on previously unspecified problems requiring more than one layer to change before expiry. An equal advantage on predictable builds with no learning opportunity favors supplier margins, manufacturing scale, financing or ordinary coordination as explanations. If the matched partner achieves the same timely changes, the ownership premium through this loop is zero at equal cost. To test delegation, give a new supplier the resulting interface and measure time to accepted work on unseen jobs, while holding the executor and input quality fixed. If only the originating engineers can apply it, the predicted portability has not been achieved. A fall in the make share alone could follow falling demand.

Finally follow both paths to the next investment. A gripper redesign may save recovery labor while leaving accepted orders fixed until an additional carrier is available. Alternatively it can eliminate the need for another arm. Record the vector of additional accepted service from an actual increment to each embodiment, upstream component requirements and dated cash by claimant. An organization can improve a customer's physical marginal product and still lose the means to fund the next experiment. [Network marginal product](../network_mpk/README.md)

**Next question.** Can the firm delegate routine production while retaining enough reference coverage and revision authority to detect when a new vintage invalidates the interface? Vary post-handoff coverage independently of production ownership. Measure whether the old procedure signals failure before the first lost order and whether restoring control changes accepted work quickly enough to justify re-entry. This would close a repeated expansion–contraction cycle beyond the single transition solved here.
