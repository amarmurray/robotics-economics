# What the next deployment teaches the fleet

*Recursive Firm Theory · Amar Murray’s independent RoboEcon research · November 25, 2026*

Where should a firm put its next robot if the robot will both do work and change what the rest of the fleet can learn? A parcel arm illustrates the problem. Amazon’s Robin system ranked possible grasps using a predictor trained on more than 394,000 production picks. The ranking helped determine which grasp would be attempted next. That observation establishes a feedback from operations to model to operations. A decision to instrument another workcell requires something further. Which problem could that workcell resolve before anyone else does, and where would resolving it change the work? [Li et al., RSS 2023](https://www.amazon.science/publications/large-scale-package-manipulation-via-learned-metrics-of-pick-success)

A deployment has two distinct economic uses in learning. It can supply evidence others lack. It can also put an improvement found elsewhere to work. **As a fleet acquires more places to use a correction, each of two investigative sites can become more valuable on its own while the premium for combining them falls.** The result can arise with ample compute, no congestion and positive transfer everywhere. When both sites independently discover the same correction, the fleet can put it to work only once at each recipient. The opposite prediction follows when resolving the problem requires evidence from both sites. Distinguishing these technologies matters for where a firm invests.

## What the firm has to organize

*Data-Driven Automation* takes heterogeneous data to be produced in economic activity and useful beyond their task of origin. From those premises follow separate effects on the work machines already do and on the frontier of automatable work. *Organizing Intelligence* distinguishes agents’ competence from their ability to coordinate as the group grows. There, the composition and size of the firm are outcomes. We take the same methodological step with deployment. Specify what occupies a place, what can travel, who can act on it and what an incumbent would do. Then ask which portfolio of sites and procedures a firm has reason to assemble. The economic antecedent for the distinction between producing information and using it in many places is the nonrivalry of data. [Farboodi, Koh and Xia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10127) · [Imas, Koh and Legg](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05417) · [Jones and Tonetti](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20191330)

1. **A job has an outside option and a due date.** A warehouse owes a completed order. Its digital dispatcher, an arm, a mobile carrier and a recovery operator can contribute to the same order. The alternative may be a person completing it on time. A better grasp then saves scarce labor even if paid order volume is flat. If the carrier is full, an extra successful grasp may simply wait. Define both the service vector, in physical units by market, and the dated change in operating contribution for the party making the decision.
2. **A site produces selected experiences.** Which jobs are offered, admitted, attempted, diverted to people or retained depends on the dispatch rule and the site. A supplier may see the pick while the customer alone sees a completed delivery. Two physically distant arms receiving the same packaging lot can supply almost the same evidence. One authorized trace from a recovery operator can distinguish failure modes that neither arm observes. The information in one source is conditional on the other sources already in use.
3. **A discovery takes time to become usable.** A candidate correction needs an appropriate representation, validation on the recipient and authority to release it. Copies can travel while a technician, an audit slot and a commissioning bay each serve one use at a time. The firm may preserve an old checkpoint, open a transfer link for one class of work or retire it. A depot can generate excellent diagnostic evidence even when it cannot install a resulting release. Another can have the reverse capabilities.
4. **Ordinary production already learns.** The comparator can route to people, keep separate heads, pool when justified, use site-specific rules and investigate exceptions. Give it the same rights, capacity and information available before the intervention. Extra releases, site specialization or an affiliate’s margin alone do not establish that production created a better learning procedure. That claim requires carrying a procedure learned in one operating history to a fresh decision where it changes which scarce experiment the firm buys.

These premises imply three objects to measure. **Source value** depends on when this site can change what is known above the ordinary program. **Recipient value** depends on the work that can actually use the change. **Organizational learning** changes how the firm generates, qualifies or allocates that evidence the next time. The first two can make a static portfolio valuable even with a fixed learning rule. The third requires a comparison across operating histories.

## One more site

Consider a correction for one recurring condition before one deadline. Fix the operating and validation rules for this comparison. A qualified correction can be copied to the eligible fleet. Let $C$ be the event that the incumbent sources, including ordinary learning, would qualify it by the deadline. Each candidate site $i$ brings a potential event $E_i$ that it alone could qualify the correction in time. The events may be arbitrarily correlated. In this restriction one qualified discovery suffices, release is beneficial on eligible work, and adding a source does not change the outcomes of the others. We relax these last conditions below.

Let $B$ be the incumbent recipients’ combined incremental operating contribution if they receive the correction in time, and $b_i$ the corresponding contribution on the new site’s own qualified work. Use a common price date and claimant. These quantities can include saved labor or avoided rework at a fixed number of orders. For the next two equations take them as fixed and nonnegative. Define $F=\Pr(C)$ and $u_i=\Pr(C^c\cap E_i)$. Let $\pi_i$ be the site’s discounted incremental value with the focal correction withheld, charging installation, fallback, and the chosen investigation program once. With fixed investigation costs the private incremental value including the correction is

$$
\Delta_i V=\pi_i + F b_i + u_i(B+b_i).
$$

The site can earn on an improvement the incumbent would discover anyway. It can bring forward an improvement for the existing fleet. And it can use its own incremental discovery. This follows by partitioning histories into incumbent success and incumbent failure with new-site success. It prevents charging the full value of a reusable correction to every training source. A site with many interventions but $u_i=0$ can still be a good operating investment or a good recipient.

**The ranking of sites can reverse as a firm grows.** Suppose two candidates have equal $b$, site 1 earns more from immediate operations and site 2 has higher $u$. One commissioning slot is available. Site 2 is worth installing first exactly when

$$
(B+b)(u_2-u_1)>\pi_1-\pi_2 .
$$

Adding recipient-only work can move the firm across that boundary with no change in either candidate’s robot, intervention count or local wages. A deployer with access to unusual failures may rationally start in a lower-margin market after it acquires a sufficiently large compatible business elsewhere. If its rights end at its customer’s site, use only the reachable, captured work in this calculation. A different party may have the stronger reason to fund the same investigation. A profitable ranking results in an installation only when a machine, a crew and funding become available.

## What makes two deployments complements?

Let $V_{ab}$ be value with sites $i,j$ available as indicated by $a,b\in\{0,1\}$. Define $D_{ij}=V_{11}-V_{10}-V_{01}+V_{00}$. It measures how installing one changes the incremental value of the other in the same economy. Comparing which sites earn high margins across economies answers a different question. Hold ordinary operating payoffs additive here to isolate learning.

Partition the histories in which incumbent sources miss the deadline into $i$ alone qualifying, $j$ alone qualifying and both qualifying individually. Call their **unconditional** probabilities $s_i,s_j,d$ respectively. In the first histories, installing $j$ adds a use for $i$’s discovery. In the second, $i$ adds a use for $j$’s. In the third, each would already have served all incumbent recipients on its own. Subtracting the four portfolios gives

$$
\boxed{D_{ij}=b_j s_i + b_i s_j - B d .}
$$

Now add a customer that **only receives** the correction and can earn an extra $\Delta B$ from it. Keep source programs, demand at the two candidate sites and scarce resource access fixed. The individual values of $i$ and $j$ rise by $\Delta B\,u_i$ and $\Delta B\,u_j$. **Their interaction falls by $\Delta B\,d$.** It is negative precisely when $Bd>b_js_i+b_is_j$. Even after $i$ has been installed, $j$ can become more valuable in absolute terms. Its increase is $\Delta B\,s_j$, which omits the discoveries $i$ would already supply. The prediction concerns the premium for combining the sites. Correlation in observed task scores cannot identify $d$. It concerns overlap in *timely, usable discoveries conditional on what the incumbent would miss*.

The calculation also works with site-specific rights, deadlines and uncertain order values. For each recipient $\ell$ and date $t$, let $X_{ab,\ell t}$ indicate whether a usable correction is available under source access $ab$. Let $w^f_{\ell t}$ be firm $f$’s incremental captured contribution on that date’s original work if the correction is available, common across source grants. Write $\beta$ for its per-period discount factor. For additional recipient-only work its contribution to the interaction of the two sources is

$$
\sum_{\ell,t}\beta^t\,\mathbb E\!\left[
w^f_{\ell t}\big(X_{11,\ell t}-X_{10,\ell t}-X_{01,\ell t}+X_{00,\ell t}\big)\right].
$$

Use the joint expectation when high-value orders and discovery events coincide. Each $X$ includes the recipient’s eligibility and validation delay. Under monotone availability there are only two types of histories with a nonzero term. **Either source is sufficient** gives $-1$. **Both sources are necessary** gives $+1$. Thus expanding the same qualified recipient work makes investigations more complementary exactly when the stakes-weighted mass of joint requirements exceeds the stakes-weighted mass of redundant discoveries. An arm trace can identify *when* something went wrong while a carrier trace identifies *which parcel* it affected. That paired diagnosis can give the opposite sign to two arms independently detecting the same fault. This prediction can distinguish mechanisms before choosing any functional form for fleet scaling.

Joint training can also overwrite useful behavior. If adding a source changes scheduling, induces forgetting, or prevents the recipient from keeping an old release, the indicators need not be monotone. If a release changes the value or composition of orders themselves, reprice the resulting feasible production schedules. The four-portfolio difference still measures the total interaction. Separating redundant information from these other channels then requires an intervention on source access at fixed capacity and a second comparison allowing the firm to reallocate.

## From the teleoperation hour to the next allocation

An operator can spend an hour recovering an order, obtaining an unusual demonstration, validating a candidate or teaching a digital agent which cases merit a physical test. Value the recovery on the current order once. For the effect of the hour on learning, compare the ordinary program with the program that buys the hour. Sum the dated contribution on future work times the change in probability that the recipient has the usable method by that date. Charge the additional compute, validation and opportunity cost of the hour. The same procedure prices a week spent changing the learning objective. A week that changes no decision before ordinary learning catches up earns no lead on that work. A week that teaches a reusable diagnostic can pay again after the underlying executor changes. [When Faster Learning Pays](https://github.com/amarmurray/robotics-economics/blob/ed3b9fec57cd87a5d953d06491553d5a3a826a21/papers/autonomous_firm/When_Faster_Learning_Pays.md) · [How firms learn what to test](../README.md)

**When should the growing fleet investigate its own sources?** Suppose the first source is running. A second costs $c>0$ after credit for other uses. Its incremental probability of qualifying the correction is either $a_L$ or $a_H$, with $0<a_L<a_H\leq1$. The ordinary team assigns probability $q\in(0,1)$ to the high state using everything it already knows. Both states can improve expected production. They differ in how often the second source adds something the first would miss. Let $B$ be the discounted eligible work at stake. A timely audit would reveal the state before the firm decides whether to commission the second source.

Write $[x]_+=\max(0,x)$ and $\bar a=(1-q)a_L+qa_H$. The most the firm would pay for that audit on this decision is

$$
G(B)=(1-q)[a_LB-c]_+ + q[a_HB-c]_+ - [\bar a B-c]_+ .
$$

At low stakes ($B\leq c/a_H$) the firm declines in both states. At high stakes ($B\geq c/a_L$) it commissions in both. The audit earns zero at either extreme. Between those thresholds its value first rises as $q(a_HB-c)$, then falls as $(1-q)(c-a_LB)$. It peaks exactly where the ordinary team is indifferent, $B=c/\bar a$. This follows by evaluating the three positive parts in order. An imperfect signal is worth at most this amount for the same timely decision. A priced audit is attractive only to the extent its decision value covers its cost.

This is a second implication of scale. A firm's willingness to pay for the second **source** can rise throughout expansion while its willingness to pay to **learn whether to buy that source** first rises and then vanishes. The hour can move on to testing an embodiment or a different market. The upper cutoff requires a positive return in the low state and a single outstanding decision. If the second source is completely redundant in that state, a timely audit can continue to save its cost at arbitrarily large scale. If the result can govern many later releases, value those decisions too. Observing a large firm cut audits on one mature program can therefore be consistent with its having become better at allocating inquiry, but establishes that claim only with the carried-procedure comparison below.

The next period closes the loop. A release can remove routine failures from the recovery queue, lowering overlap between two remaining sources. It can instead concentrate both on the same difficult supplier lot, raising overlap. It can relieve a human reviewer, raise accepted shipments and move the bottleneck to the carrier. A successful depot might consequently spend its next learning hour on a carrier handoff or put its next machine in another end market. These directions are conditional. To update the allocation, record what happened to the original jobs, how recipient work and residual case overlap changed, which resource now binds and who can fund the next change. A falling exception count alone settles none of them.

This gives an economic interpretation to changing an agent’s training objective. At fixed current service, an extra demonstration has a fleet-wide return only through recipients for which it changes the timing or content of a feasible decision. A standing reward per intervention can therefore point the agent toward the wrong market after a shift in recipient demand or source overlap. Revising the reward has a cost and a validation lag. Its return is the discounted difference in work and real resource use under the allocations the new and incumbent rules will actually choose, net of revision costs. If the feasible allocations stay the same until the opportunity expires, the revision cannot pay on that opportunity. If the recipient is about to change embodiments, testing the sign of transfer can command the next hour even while both source signals remain plentiful. [Derivation of the revision interval](../../learning_dynamics/README.md#proposition-2-the-right-timescale-for-revising-the-objective-can-be-finite-or-infinite)

The [capital paper](https://github.com/amarmurray/robotics-economics/blob/ed3b9fec57cd87a5d953d06491553d5a3a826a21/papers/learning_and_capital/README.md) follows induced orders, transferable methods and reinvestable cash into additional machines. The portfolio result supplies one of its local inputs and specifies when that input changes sign. For each install, keep the vector of additional paid or otherwise accepted physical services by end market, the saved hours by labor role, the change in discounted contribution by claimant and the date cash becomes available. An order filled by a grocery carrier can consume the same routing capacity as a hospital delivery. A release that raises both fleets’ technical capacity can shift actual deliveries between them. An upstream packaging supplier can lose unit demand while supplying the evidence that made the change possible. A claim on the savings, available commissioning capacity and the timing of collections determine whether that supplier can invest again. [Network marginal product study](../../network_mpk/README.md)

### Which kinds of firms emerge?

Classify a business unit by the missing decisions it can supply and the benefits it can use. Firm labels alone conceal substantial differences.

| Operating arrangement | Scarce input and job | What could change its next allocation? |
|---|---|---|
| Recovery contractor at varied parcel sites | Skilled teleoperator time. The host pays for getting a parcel through. | A right to use distinctive demonstrations across sites and a claim on resulting savings can fund more investigation. Repeated incidents on the same supplier lot lower its marginal value. |
| Full-stack operator serving fulfillment and hospital supply | Arms, carriers, digital dispatch, review labor and customer-specific delivery deadlines. | Enlarging the pool of recipients can favor a lower-margin site that distinguishes a new failure. A single remote reviewer can instead make validation the constraint. |
| Model supplier or federation of sites | Compatible traces, integration work and permission to release on customer machines. | Local controllers can signal distinct failure families and customers can report which releases they can actually use. Identical access, prices, timing and decisions give a federation the same portfolio returns as an integrated owner here. Overlapping site reports can lead either organization to buy duplicate checks. |
| Digital service operator | An autonomous agent can attempt a refund. A person can authorize an exception. A rare escalation uses a reviewer and the customer controls settlement. | Replicating the agent can add many places to use a policy correction without generating independent evidence about cases routed to people. A changed admission rule can reverse which team observes the informative cases. |

Electric-utility inspection gives a different unit of analysis. National Grid reports deploying a quadruped with thermal imaging at Sandy Pond. A dated inspection, a maintenance decision, a crew visit and reliable electricity service have different units. The operator could test whether two patrols on similar equipment duplicate the condition they detect, or whether a patrol observation becomes actionable only when combined with a technician’s service record. Those hypotheses imply opposite effects from extending a diagnostic to more eligible equipment. The published deployment establishes the observation technology. Distinguishing the hypotheses would require linked records of checks, action dates, crew use and outcomes. [National Grid, Sandy Pond inspection](https://www.nationalgrid.com/stories/grid-work-stories/spot-robot-dog-carries-out-safety-inspections)

These are choices over control of work, information and release. They do not yet constitute a theory predicting equilibrium ownership shares. The stronger claim that an organization *became better at making those choices through production* would require its learned allocation rule to outperform the best feasible ordinary program on a new set of jobs, after charging for how the rule was developed.

## What existing measurements resolve

The Open X-Embodiment collaboration actually evaluated pretrained policies on robots. In its 55B comparison, adding Bridge data to a large robotics mixture raised reported success on the Google robot’s selected emergent skills from 42.8% to 75.8%. On the generalization suite, it moved from 54% to 61%. The emergent skills were chosen because they appeared in Bridge and were absent from the Google robot’s own dataset. This is evidence of technical portability for those skills and a reason to preserve the evaluation selection in any economic interpretation. [Open X-Embodiment, Table II and §V-B](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.08864v9)

One public-data check shows exactly which claim this evidence leaves open. Treat the reported Google-only, full-except-Bridge and full rows as three corners of a nominal source-mixture comparison. The missing cell is Google plus Bridge without the other added sources. For reported success in percentage points its unknown score $x$ leaves the interaction in the last column below. The score is bounded between 0 and 100. No independence or scaling exponent is used.

| Google robot evaluation | Google only | Full except Bridge | Full | Interaction as a function of missing score | Possible reported-score interaction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Selected emergent skills | 27.3 | 42.8 | 75.8 | $60.3-x$ | $[-39.7,60.3]$ pp |
| Generalization | 62 | 54 | 61 | $69-x$ | $[-31,69]$ pp |

The large conditional Bridge gain is consistent with either sign for the interaction. Even assuming monotone success across these mixtures leaves the emergent-skills interval spanning zero. The generalization point estimates already violate monotonicity. These are bounds on contrasts among reported protocols, conditional on treating the rows as comparable. Training mixture weights and effective exposures can change between rows. Sampling uncertainty, independently funded target validation, paid work and private capture are outside the table. The physical tests were performed by the authors. The accompanying calculation uses their published cells. ## The test that would distinguish the mechanisms

1. **Pick the decision before reading intervention logs.** For a fresh cohort of source sites and an independent recipient, specify one correction family, the outcomes counted as qualified, the original job roster, an economic expiry date and the ordinary alternative. Capture routed-away cases through an authorized random reference sample. Record retention permissions, model versions, fallback labor, audit effort and order settlement. This makes disappeared failures and displaced orders visible.
2. **Cross two source grants with recipient use.** Randomize access to source A, source B, both or neither across independent model-release groups. Keep incumbent learning in all four conditions. Cross those assignments with early or later eligibility to serve an additional recipient-only workload. Reserve its capacity and keep its records out of training during the comparison. Equalize and price the review and compute opportunities, including idle or displaced uses. The difference in source interactions across the two recipient conditions is the test. A negative difference supports redundancy under the restrictions above. A positive difference is predicted by joint diagnosis. Zero can arise from disjoint coverage, offsetting mechanisms, or a recipient where no paid work or labor cost actually changes.
3. **Change the regime and then let controllers act.** In a held-out supplier lot, customer, tool version or embodiment, predict the sign from independent source overlap and recipient qualification before looking at the outcomes. Repeat with ordinary independent controllers and with the retained procedure allowed to move its validation slot between source and recipient. Compare with tuned fixed sharing and allocation, separate heads and a matched audit routine. Retain ordinary checkpoint, review and diagnostic options. A wrong sign under fixed capacity rejects the stated coverage or joint-evidence prediction for that population. To test the audit cutoff, vary eligible workload across both decision thresholds. With one decision left, a timely diagnostic and known positive low-state yield, a positive willingness to pay for that diagnostic when both states dictate the same action rejects the single-decision explanation. A sign reversal only after freeing the controllers points to reallocation, interference or changes in the encountered cases.
4. **Identify what the organization learned.** Transfer just the allocation procedure from independently assigned production histories, holding executor, source rights, recipient work and budget fixed. Then assign the same tests to both histories. An advantage on fresh sites when the procedure chooses tests that disappears under common assigned tests supports learning *which evidence to buy*. Similar outcomes under the strongest ordinary site rules favor established specialization. A test on access to data can identify a learning component of investment value. Identifying the total physical marginal product also requires varying actual capital or feasible machine service and measuring additional accepted work.

**Open question for the pilot.** After a source repair suppresses one family of visible failures, does the organization recognize that its two remaining sources now duplicate each other, or has transfer to a recipient changed sign? Spend the marginal audit on source overlap, recipient qualification or a standing checkpoint at equal cost. Carry forward the earlier result that a precommitted late audit beat adaptive renewal after an unexpected reversal. A larger recipient base raises what is at stake on either side of that decision. It supplies no answer about which test to buy. [Standing reference study](../../reference_coverage/README.md) · [Learning under drift](../../transfer_validation/README.md)
