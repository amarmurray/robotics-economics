# Degrees of the full-stack robotic firm

*Recursive Firm Theory · November 25, 2026*

A robotics firm promises to complete some work under conditions that will change. A machine ties up capital at a site. A trained procedure can travel among compatible machines. The choice of jobs, the dispatch policy and the customer’s permissions determine what experience the firm can use. A change in hardware can invalidate evidence gathered under the previous vintage. These properties lead to a definition of full stack relative to **which work the firm can do, which changes it can originate and qualify, and how long it can keep doing both**.

Three questions follow. **Degree** describes a firm’s present capabilities and control. **Minimum viability** asks which combinations can sustain a specified service and learning obligation with available funds. **Optimal scope** asks which path through those combinations yields the highest discounted return from this firm’s starting position. A specialist can be profitable outside the market for the complete service. A firm capable of delivering the complete service can still find its best investment elsewhere.

## What constitutes a degree of full stack?

Choose the reference population first. Specify end markets, original job opportunities including refusals, embodiments and vintages, customer systems, and the deadline for responding to relevant changes. For a cross-market learning opportunity, specify both the site producing the evidence and the recipient that could use it. A firm can be deeply integrated on packaging lines and control only the model on hospital work. A degree measured on the jobs it elected to accept will partly reflect that election.

| Layer | Capacity and breadth | The control question |
|---|---|---|
| **Hardware and manufacturing (H)** | Conforming units per period at stated yield, changeover and revision lead times, tooling and retrofit capacity. Count distinct qualified embodiments separately from the range of jobs **one** embodiment can cover at a stated changeover cost. | Who can alter sensors or tooling, run a batch, inspect yield and qualify a revision before the next build? Separate design and prototypes from volume manufacturing. |
| **Models and research (M)** | Available training and inference compute, experiments per scarce researcher-week, release and evaluation capacity. Describe which methods the team can execute, including simulation, sim-to-real transfer and post-training. Measure gains, interference and forgetting on independent environments by embodiment and vintage. | Who can inspect and change the model, choose the training allocation, checkpoint a recipient and test or release an alternative? Rented compute can serve an internally directed research program. Purchasing inference can leave all revision authority elsewhere. |
| **Deployment, integration and evidence (D)** | Commissioning and support hours, depth of integration into customer dispatch and acceptance, penetration of eligible work within each market and breadth across markets. Record attempts, human fallback, settled jobs and the cases admitted to training. | Who can change routing, command teleoperation, join an intervention to its final outcome, retain the trace and reuse it at another site? In-house teleoperators and data vendors can each supply rescue, demonstration or validation. These are different deliverables. |
| **Commercial position and finance** | Asset title, funding of work in progress, time to collection, warranty and downtime exposure. Record machine sale, support fees, hourly service and payment per completed job. | Who receives the gain from a better release, who bears a service failure and who has cash when the next build or experiment must start? |

For each function record **installed resources**, **who performs and owns it**, **which actions the firm can direct through partners**, and **what has actually qualified in time**. Research headcount and model breadth enter separately from demonstrated transfer. Likewise penetration is a share with an eligible denominator. A small site footprint can span many independent operating conditions, while a national rollout may reproduce one condition.

One way to report deployment breadth is as a curve. Let $u_{fm}$ be firm $f$’s fraction of the original eligible jobs in market $m$ that reach acceptance through its service. Disaggregate autonomous completions and human recoveries. For fixed market weights $\mu_m$, report $\sum_m\mu_m\,\mathbf 1\{u_{fm}\geq z\}$ across penetration levels $z>0$. This preserves the difference between reaching many markets shallowly and doing most of the work in a few. Use an independently chosen panel to measure the task reach of an embodiment or model. Mark where use is qualified and where the firm merely has an option to attempt it.

For a defined core job and revision, label the internally performed layers by a subset of $\{H,M,D\}$. This gives eight useful starting positions: an externally supplied operator or coordinator, three single-layer origins, three paired origins, and internal HMD. Refine each label by depth, task coverage, decision rights and timing. **Functional full-stack control** means the firm can direct a connected route across hardware, models and deployment on the specified work, including through enforceable partner access. **Manufacturing full stack** additionally requires internal execution of the relevant commercial build or hardware revision, of model revision, and of deployment and qualification. Owning a prototype lab alone does not establish commercial manufacturing scale. An OEM using contract fabrication and an operator owning a volume line have different profiles even when they deliver an identical service.

There is a reason to resist averaging the coordinates. For a prespecified route on a common reference distribution, let $d_H,d_M,d_D$ be the fractions of opportunities on which the relevant work at each layer is internal. The fraction on which all three are internal obeys

$$
\max(0,d_H+d_M+d_D-2)\ \leq\ d_{HMD}\ \leq\ \min(d_H,d_M,d_D).
$$

The upper bound follows by inclusion. The lower bound follows by adding the fractions missing each layer. With two-thirds coverage on each layer, different pairs can cover different thirds of the work and have zero three-way overlap. Alternatively all three can cover the same two-thirds. Both bounds are attainable. Even overlapping internal activities may be unable to exchange a case or get a release accepted before expiry. When several alternative routes exist, test whether at least one **complete** route is feasible. Measure the cost and work at stake on that route. An activity count cannot reconstruct it.

## A minimum viable stack is a frontier

Specify a vector of paid services due over a horizon, a required ability to restore qualified work after specified disturbances, and dated cash obligations. The disturbance might be a new controller convention, an embodiment revision or a change in the customer’s acceptance rule. The required response can be keeping the existing checkpoint when a proposed update would be harmful. Set the standard from the work and its economic deadline. A stable product with no material revision requirement gives little evidence about the firm’s ability to learn.

A structure is **viable for this promise** if at least one jointly feasible plan meets the service and response standards, pays its continuing costs under the stated criterion and can fund each commitment before receipts arrive. Specify available outside financing, human fallback, rights and the tolerated risk of missing the promise. A viable structure is **minimal** if no other viable structure uses a subset of its rights and no more of every scarce resource, with a strict reduction on at least one dimension. Hold the outside supply menu fixed and let each structure choose its best operating plan. Different mixes can be incomparable. The cheapest minimum depends on input prices. The most profitable structure may deliberately buy slack or research depth beyond any minimum. For a strictly owned full-stack minimum, restrict the candidate set to commercial HMD before finding its minimal resource bundles.

A practical minimum for a **recursive** full stack adds a requirement to the production test. The firm can serve and settle a job, observe a permitted consequence of its own action, use it to change a subsequent allocation or investigation across layers, qualify the resulting decision and fund the next cycle. It needs authority over whichever layer binds in the covered contingencies. A given correction may use only a subset. External evidence can make the service viable while the firm’s own production adds no decision-relevant information.

Price this distinction by comparing the best history-responsive allocation with the best feasible ordinary allocation, allowing both programs their existing site rules and external model improvements. Hold the current executor and opportunities fixed when isolating inquiry. The difference, after investigation costs, values discretion to act on the firm’s additional retained history. Compare that difference under internal and partner provision to measure how much of the integration premium is attributable to feedback capture. Evidence that **production improved the firm’s learning procedure** requires a further comparison. Carry the procedure learned under one production history to unfamiliar work, at matched executor, rights and budget, and measure which fresh decision and paid outcome it changes. ### A solvable restriction across end markets

Let $n_j$ be the number of committed environments of type $j$ in a release window. Types distinguish market, labor mix, site and embodiment. For a recurring failure family $h$, one usable source case suffices for this restriction. Each environment of type $j$ independently supplies one with probability $p_{hj}<1$ conditional on no common loss of local coverage. Admission, novelty, retention and teleoperation affect this probability. A common loss of local coverage occurs with probability $c_h$. A separately authorized source supplies the case with probability $p_{0h}$ independently of the local stream. Conditional on an available case the firm can carry a valid change through its remaining layers by the deadline with probability $a_h$. These quantities are specific to the governance arrangement, operating rule and reference case mix.

The probability of a timely qualified response is

$$
Q_h(n)=a_h\left[1-(1-p_{0h})\left(c_h+(1-c_h)\prod_j(1-p_{hj})^{n_j}\right)\right].
$$

The product is the chance every local environment misses when capture is working. This gives a multivariate lower bound on independent exposure. Put $E_{hj}=-\log(1-p_{hj})$ and let $\theta_h$ be the required response probability. When the outside source alone suffices, the required local coverage $b_h$ is zero. When $\theta_h$ lies strictly between outside-source performance and the attainable limit, rearranging $Q_h(n)\geq\theta_h$ gives

$$
\sum_j E_{hj}n_j\geq b_h,\qquad
b_h=-\log\!\left(\frac{a_h[1-(1-p_{0h})c_h]-\theta_h}
{a_h(1-p_{0h})(1-c_h)}\right).
$$

With positive residual miss probability at every finite footprint, a target at or above the limiting response rate has no finite solution unless ordinary outside coverage already suffices. A customer-wide restriction on reuse or a missed factory deadline can thus eliminate the viable set even as the firm adds machines. If environments share additional shocks, use their joint miss probability. If two different traces are jointly necessary, take the probability of obtaining the required combination. In either case the additive log-coverage equation may fail. These are empirical rivals, particularly across sites on the same supplier lot. [Complementary evidence in hardware design](https://github.com/amarmurray/robotics-economics/blob/97c709f527e7312cc4023a3266db7753b0cdc522/papers/autonomous_firm/DEPLOYMENT_PORTFOLIOS_AND_DESIGN.md)

The same commitments create upper bounds. Let $R_{kj}$ measure scarce input $k$ required per environment, including recurring validation labor, integration support or machine capacity. Let $x_k$ be available capacity after fixed release costs. Let $Y$ map supported commitments into service available at the stated standard, $m_j$ be net contribution per site before fixed expense $F$, and $\kappa_j$ the working capital tied up against a financing base $W$. With fixed site economics and a single binding cash date the feasible footprint satisfies

$$
 E n\geq b,\qquad Yn\geq\bar y,\qquad Rn\leq x,\qquad
 m^\top n\geq F,\qquad \kappa^\top n\leq W .
$$

For staged collections, impose the cash constraint at every date. If cash flow or paid service depends on the portfolio’s probability of qualifying, use the corresponding portfolio function. Count a vendor panel, a researcher-week and a teleoperator-hour wherever they draw on the same resource budget. Count the completed job once.

**A firm can have too few independent environments to learn and too many committed sites to support.** In one market with positive unit margin, the evidence, service and cost-recovery inequalities set a minimum site count. The capacity and cash inequalities set a maximum. The interval can be empty. This upper bound concerns obligations to operate and qualify sites. An uncommitted spare machine need not create an obligation. Across markets the feasible object is a set of portfolios: one source-rich deployment can replace several repetitive ones, while a demanding new embodiment can add a whole row of qualification requirements.

**The optimal breadth of the production fleet can differ from the optimal breadth of the evidence it uses.** A common embodiment can save validation hours and increase manufacturing yield. Suppose its sites share a sensing limitation, so $c_h>0$. As local exposure grows and the product of local miss probabilities approaches zero, the gain from one more similar site approaches zero. The marginal effect of an independently instrumented source on timely response instead satisfies $\partial Q_h/\partial p_{0h}\to a_h c_h$. An alternate embodiment, a partner or a small retained reference fleet can supply that observation. The firm can rationally standardize what it builds in volume while maintaining a more varied set of evidence sources. The residual benefit disappears when ordinary sites cover the condition, or when a new source shares their blind spot. Whether keeping an alternate embodiment pays also depends on its cost and how much reachable work the correction can change.

In a divisible approximation, the value of another environment of type $j$ includes its operating margin, its contribution to binding service and evidence requirements, and the labor and finance it draws away from other markets. With nonnegative shadow prices $\omega,\lambda,\nu,\xi$ on those constraints, its marginal contribution to the constrained objective is

$$
m_j+\omega^\top Y_{\cdot j}+\lambda^\top E_{\cdot j}
-\nu^\top R_{\cdot j}-\xi\kappa_j .
$$

Here operating margin is the objective and fixed-cost recovery is checked on the resulting portfolio. This expression prices qualifications required by the promise. Additional option value from an unanticipated future use belongs in continuation value. It explains how the same research team, trained model or teleoperation hour can have different marginal products at two firms facing the same robot purchase price.

## Where the robot wage belongs

A robot wage $w_r$ can denote the price per billable machine-hour paid for a specified service. State the billing clock, service guarantee, maintenance bundle and treatment of downtime. An OEM describes a position in the value chain. An OEM can sell a system, operate it for a customer or charge partly for both. For a buyer comparing ownership with hourly service, divide purchase price plus discounted operating and support expense less discounted residual value by discounted productive hours over the same life. The buyer still needs to compare quality, fallback and risk. The seller’s realized receipts, the buyer’s cost per productive hour, the internal shadow value of a scarce machine-hour and the physical marginal product of another machine are distinct measurements.

Formic’s published materials describe charging for hours its system runs and produces. Symbotic’s fiscal 2025 filing reports systems, software maintenance and support, and operation services separately. Those observations illustrate what a researcher can establish from prices and accounts. Neither disclosure alone identifies who can retain an unfamiliar case or revise the relevant hardware and software before a customer’s deadline. [Formic product guide](https://land.formic.co/product-guide-download-lp) · [Symbotic annual filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1837240/000183724025000278/sym-20250927.htm)

The payment unit can change the optimum even when physical productivity is identical. Suppose a revision saves $s$ billed hours on the same number of completed orders. There is no other demand for the released hours in this window. Let $G$ be the provider’s saved variable costs, including real labor savings, less the revision expense. Under fixed payment per order the provider gains $G$. Under hourly billing at $w_r$ it gains $G-w_rs$. If $0<G<w_rs$, the physical improvement pays privately in only one arrangement. Minimum-hour payments, resale of freed hours, warranty exposure and future orders change this calculation. In a system sale, trace the gain through price, support costs and subsequent sales. This is how commercial structure changes incentives to integrate and invest without changing the description of the underlying technical stack.

## Moving the boundary from different origins

Let $o$ be the starting ownership set. The rest of the state $s$ contains the installed vintages, capital, staff, qualified methods, customer interfaces, rights, outstanding obligations, cash and ordinary alternatives. The firm chooses a boundary $g$ and an operating and investment plan $u$. Deployment under that plan changes the distribution of cases, post-training changes the next release, validation consumes time, and collections finance later commitments. Write this law of motion as $s'\sim P(\,\cdot\mid s,g,u)$. A private optimum satisfies

$$
V(o,s)=\max_{g,u\ \mathrm{feasible}}
\left\{\pi_f(s,g,u)-C(o,g;s)+\beta\,\mathbb E[V(g,s')]\right\}.
$$

New builds, training and commissioning that take several periods enter the state as outstanding commitments. An outside option allows the firm to decline the full-service promise or remain a specialist. Changing which cases are admitted can alter the evidence matrix itself. A portable qualification procedure can raise a partner’s $a_h$ and reduce future integration costs. A new vintage can undo both. The causal question is how production changes this transition, conditional on the initial assets. The related production paper derives how the choice of a deployment changes the next design and how the changed design alters later inquiry. [The full-stack robotic firm](https://github.com/amarmurray/robotics-economics/blob/97c709f527e7312cc4023a3266db7753b0cdc522/papers/autonomous_firm/FULL_STACK_FIRM.md)

For a multi-window promise, work backward from terminal obligations. A state can support $r+1$ windows if it admits a funded plan that meets the current service and response standards and leaves a state capable of supporting $r$ windows under every vintage change the promise covers. The current state includes idle capacity, outstanding commitments and the time needed to exercise partner or re-entry rights. This construction distinguishes a minimum stack that can sign up for one window from a minimum starting structure that can keep serving through a change. A contract or a small internal reference team can alter that second minimum even when it adds no current production.

There is a useful restriction on comparisons across origins. Hold forward opportunities and feasible lead times fixed, so inherited ownership enters only through additive setup and exit costs. Compare the best current-plus-continuation value attainable with layer $k$ internal to the best attainable with it external. Reoptimize the other layers and charge their switching costs in both alternatives. Call the difference $\Delta_k$ before charging the boundary change on $k$ itself. From outside, add the layer when $\Delta_k>F_k$, its setup cost. From inside, retain it when $\Delta_k>-D_k$, the negative of its exit cost. At a tie either decision can be optimal. The interval between thresholds permits two firms with identical forward opportunities and different inherited boundaries to choose differently. A model lab, OEM and integrator can also possess different evidence stocks or compatibility routines, so their $\Delta_k$ can differ even with zero switching costs. Compare both sources of persistence.

**A viable path can be thinner than a viable permanent boundary.** A partner can serve a portable interface today, even though a novel interface would require internal HMD. Delegation can sustain the promise if the firm can detect the change and bring the missing capabilities back before work is due. If re-entry takes longer, retaining a reference cell, a model checkpoint or a reserved build slot can be necessary. In a one-change restriction, keeping this readiness costs $k$ per period until the vintage changes with hazard $\delta$. It yields incremental private value $L$ at the next date on that change, relative to the best cold restart. A constant readiness policy is worth $(\beta\delta L-k)/(1-\beta(1-\delta))$ more than cold delegation, obtained by summing over the periods until change. A payment for reserved access can preserve this option. Deed ownership is only one way to acquire it. [Boundary and portability derivation](../boundaries/README.md)

## What the model checks

The reproducible model solves an infinite-horizon discounted decision problem from **all eight ownership origins**, first with an unresolved interface, then with a portable one. A firm can serve either of two markets, buy a vendor panel, carry a matched test into the next release, change any subset of its boundary or stay idle. Each committed site has one normalized batch of paid work per window. Human fallback completes the batch if qualification fails. A timely correction saves one unit of recovery cost per site. Thus paid volume, release performance and private margin can be tracked separately. Partner and internal probabilities of revision by deadline, source correlation, costs, demand and review capacity are specified in the code. Commissioning a portability test uses a real review slot. Its chance of qualifying an external procedure depends on obtaining a timely correction from the firm’s chosen sites. Vintage changes can invalidate the procedure.

| Constructed regime | Minimal fixed ownership sets that can serve a common positive footprint in either interface state | Optimal first move from different origins with an unresolved interface |
|---|---|---|
| Eight review hours, response target 0.54 | H alone or M alone, with other layers supplied by partners | An OEM keeps H and buys the vendor panel. A model lab adds D. A deployer adds M. An HMD firm releases M. |
| Six review hours, same target | H alone or the pair MD | The OEM adds D. The model lab adds H. An MD incumbent releases D and adds H in the same decision. |
| Eight review hours, target raised to 0.76 | HMD only | All eight origins choose HMD while unresolved. With a portable interface a firm starting at HMD keeps D and delegates H and M. A subsequent unresolved interface induces re-entry. |

In the first regime even the fully internal firm has several minimal site mixes. Two sites of either market or one of each can pass the test. Under the stricter promise its minimal mixes are two of the more informative market or three of the other plus one informative site. In every comparison fixed viability allows affordable partner services and state-specific tests. The minimum ownership sets in the table concern any nonzero service footprint in that example. Specifying jobs owed in both markets would give a different frontier. The dynamic policy values the whole demand menu and can change the footprint at renewal.

These are constructed mechanisms. The model charges switching costs, allows immediate boundary changes between service windows and provides funding for those switches. It restricts working capital for active sites. Delayed reacquisition, scarce research hires, harmful releases and a broader menu of vendor contracts can change the rankings. There are no estimates here of actual firm types, industry integration levels or scaling exponents. ## Carrying the abstraction into RoboEcon

Measure a firm on both a common panel of original opportunities and its self-selected production mix. An increase in measured breadth on the latter can come entirely from abandoning difficult work. Measure manufacturing yield and qualified changeovers, model performance on independent recipient environments, operational acceptance and paid settlement, captured cash and which new tests earlier production made possible. Compare in-house teleoperation with vendor supply at matched opportunity coverage, latency, price and rights. If matched access supports the same correction and accepted service, this comparison yields no extra learning return attributable to owning the teleoperation function.

An out-of-market test can use the source estimates from existing environments and a small independent recipient panel. Predict the change in the probability of missing a deadline when another environment is added, before observing the new portfolio’s releases. Hold the response team, admission protocol, outside corpus and review slots fixed. A systematic miss on linked original jobs rejects the corresponding independence, timeliness or single-case-sufficiency restriction. Additional sites that succeed only when two different records can be joined favor a complementary-evidence model.

For the marginal product of robotic capital, perturb a specific embodiment or machine-service block and record the vector of additional accepted work across end markets under feasible dispatch. Then let the firm revise its allocation and track who can finance the next machine. A supplier can gain an upstream order while a host saves labor and an unrelated customer loses access to a constrained reviewer. Carry the joint distribution of these responses **conditional on initial stack profile, service promise and task environment**. The cross-effect of installing at two sites is a within-world comparison. High returns observed together at similar firms can instead reflect a common demand or technology shock. [Network marginal products](../network_mpk/README.md)

The next discriminating test is the re-entry clock. At comparable initial capabilities and service obligations, vary whether a delegated team retains a reference panel and a reserved revision slot. Predict, on an unfamiliar vintage, which firms recognize the break, which can requalify in time, and which must reacquire a layer. Charge the cost of holding the option. The broader object for Recursive Firm Theory is the **evolution of the feasible combinations of production, inquiry and finance**. Verticalization is one way a firm changes that set. Which direction it moves depends on the work already promised, what the last deployment taught it, and the opportunities its next release makes reachable.
