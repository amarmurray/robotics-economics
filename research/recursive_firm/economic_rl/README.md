# What should the firm tell its agents about the next robot-hour?

*Recursive Firm Theory · Economic reinforcement learning · November 2026*

A dispatcher chooses which orders to admit. A release engineer chooses which controller and embodiment can serve them. An employer decides whether either function will get another engineer, inference slot or robot. If the packaging arm gets faster, dispatch can admit harder orders. If human reviewers are already occupied, the change can instead move the queue from the workcell to review. The firm wants to know which original orders would clear with *one more unit of usable robot service*. Its answer depends on the organization it has built and on who can respond to the change.

This is where the theories of the autonomous firm and the recursive firm meet. Autonomy describes who can take a decision and which observations and resources they can use. Recursion describes how exercising those rights today changes the procedures and means available for future decisions. A firm can delegate a well-specified workflow that never improves. A predominantly human firm can repeatedly improve how it designs experiments. The interesting hypothesis is that particular forms of delegation make that second process faster and change the privately optimal degree of automation. [The Autonomous Firm](https://github.com/amarmurray/robotics-economics/blob/f5eaf0d549a8682923026358a02d45fe0e5de8f5/papers/autonomous_firm/The_Autonomous_Firm.md) · [From recursive self-improvement to the recursive firm](../organizational_recursion/README.md)

## What is being conditioned on?

Denote the inherited state by $s$. It includes manufacturing and qualified vintages, models and evidence, integration depth, labor by skill and date, cash, demand, review and compute slots, pending releases, and ownership and partner rights. A slower firm policy $\Gamma$ maps the information then available into changes in that stack. Its choices include retaining a reference panel, reserving a human release engineer, qualifying a model on a mobile platform, purchasing a data right or delegating an investigation to an agent. Several internal arrangements can support the same service. The full-stack profile is a vector and the initial firm can enter at any feasible point. [Stack profiles and minimum viable structures](../stack_profile/README.md)

Fix an end market $m$, site and embodiment $j$, a service increment $\delta$ with units, a horizon and the rule $\pi$ for operating the resulting stack. For a current shift, an implementable physical marginal product is

$$
J_{mj}^{\pi,\Gamma}(s)=
\frac{\mathbb E[Y_m^{\pi,\Gamma}(s\oplus\delta_j,\xi)-Y_m^{\pi,\Gamma}(s,\xi)\mid s]}{\delta}.
$$

Here $\xi$ is the common external opportunity and shock history. $Y_m$ counts completed and accepted original orders. The intervention adds usable capacity while holding other endowments fixed and allowing the specified decision rules to respond. At the feasible frontier, fix the weights selecting a frontier point in both arms. An operator following a contracted allocation can have a different product. If two stack policies imply identical available actions and responses within the shift, they give the same current $J$ there. They can still give different continuation values. An objective affects physical output through the allocation and qualifications it induces. A change in the metric on a dashboard alone leaves the technology intact. [Network marginal products](../network_mpk/README.md)

There are three different economic accounts. The first records the vector of accepted services. The second prices the current operating change, including saved labor when volume stays fixed. The third, $g^f_j$, is the change in discounted cash flows claimable by firm $f$ when adding the service asset and following its stated continuation policy. Compare that last quantity with the dated acquisition and financing costs on the same basis. The customer can receive more work while its OEM earns less, and a productive use can leave little reusable evidence with the operator. Across firms the object is the joint distribution of the columns of $J$, labor and resource displacements, and claimant returns, stratified by site, end market, embodiment and inherited stack. Compare the same site with and without a tranche to measure causation. Two sectors' returns moving together can arise from the same demand shock.

The timing matters. Condition on the state *before* adding the tranche. If admission, review queues or retained records change as a consequence, let those changes occur in each arm. Fixing their realized post-installation values removes part of the mechanism. Before a decision the organization has an estimate or distribution for $J$. After a decision it can sometimes estimate a causal difference on a reference sample. Neither arm observes both counterfactual outcomes of the same installation.

## What an economic RL agent can use

The firm has a slow policy for stack and objective revisions, a release policy for research, validation and transfer, and operating policies for dispatch, maintenance and customer recovery. The fast decisions generate the work, cash and selected data used by the slower ones. A revised stack then changes what one more robot-hour would accomplish and how much it is worth spending to learn where to use it. Distinct clocks matter whenever a technically correct update arrives after the work it could have changed. [Fleet learning and objective revision](../learning_dynamics/README.md)

This ordering resolves a potential circularity. Score release $t$ with a forecast made before its actions, preserve a sample of the work the new dispatcher can decline, and use dated validation to revise the forecast or objective for release $t+1$. A forecast trained solely on the orders its own policy admitted can appear accurate while losing sight of a productive alternative. A hierarchy running a fixed set of well-designed rules can earn the entire operating gain. An increase in the firm's capacity for recursion requires evidence that experience improved the rule it carries into the next round.

| Decision | Information available before acting | What pays for the decision |
|---|---|---|
| Dispatch an arm, a mobile robot or a person | Estimated additional accepted orders by market, due times, local reserves and fallback | Net work the original orders actually receive, including human recovery |
| Choose the next shared update or audit | Possible changes in those products across tasks, uncertainty about transfer, time to validation, rights and reviewer availability | Decisions changed on fresh work, less displaced service, compute and inspection |
| Change the stack or delegated objective | Distribution of future operating and investment returns under candidate policies, future authority and cash at risk | Discounted incremental receipts the firm can capture after requalification and financing |

Give a local policy the forecast it can lawfully receive, its date and uncertainty, alongside prices, other binding inputs, expected transfer and the option to call a person. An actor receives an economic advantage from the change its *action* causes relative to a feasible alternative. For a pair of departmental actions, let $Q_f(s,a_i,a_{-i})$ be the value of current external net cash plus a discounted continuation under a named policy. An internal credit signal can compare this with $Q_f(s,a_i^0,a_{-i})$, or average over feasible alternatives for $i$. Such credits guide actions. The firm's ledger counts a joint improvement once. When actions require one another, unilateral comparisons can miss the value of coordinated change from an idle state. A shared contingent request or joint decision then has value. Counterfactual multi-agent critics are an established technical precedent. Which cash, time, rights and rejected jobs the critic must include is an economic question. [Foerster et al.](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/11794)

**Why a robot's marginal product is a poor universal reward.** Suppose an order book has $D$ jobs, qualified machine service is $K$, and a substitute procedure supplies $H$ services. Completed work is $\min(D,K+H)$. At $K+H=D-1$, qualifying one inexpensive substitute completes another job and takes the product of an *additional* robot from one to zero. A reward for maintaining a high $MPK_r$ can penalize the very improvement the customer would pay for. Conversely, an idle machine can have a low current marginal product while a small audit about an unfamiliar use has high investment value. Reward paid work, saved resources and credible continuation once. Input a forecast of marginal products where it helps the agent choose.

**When does broadcasting that forecast pay?** For a binary choice, let $D_a$ be the difference in economic action values and let $I$ be a team's existing information. Suppose a dated signal $Z$ derived from the marginal-product forecast can arrive before the decision. With costless discretion to ignore it, its gross value is

$$
\mathbb E[(\mathbb E[D_a\mid I,Z])_+\mid I]-(\mathbb E[D_a\mid I])_+.
$$

This follows by choosing the better of the two actions twice. It is positive precisely when the signal puts positive probability on strictly opposing action rankings conditional on $I$. An accurate report that the arm is scarce everywhere can have zero decision value. A modestly informative report near a dispatch or investment threshold can pay. Charge the resources to measure, transmit and validate the forecast, as well as foregone work during the delay. This result assumes the team understands the signal law. A frozen controller interpreting a new release through an old convention can lose money. If ordinary teams can reconstruct the same decision from their raw queues, the incremental organizational premium from labeling the signal $MPK_r$ is zero.

## In what sense can an agent supply capitalized labor?

A reusable model, tool integration and validated coordination procedure can form a durable productive asset under the firm's control. Each running instance supplies a **flow of task services**. A hosted model can instead be a purchased input. Human experts likewise accumulate skills and routines, and can teach other people. What matters for this hypothesis is the cost and lead time of giving a second worker the same qualified procedure, the dependence of their errors, and the authority to use it. “Capitalized labor” here describes a production technology. Financial-statement recognition is a different question. [Organizing Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05417)

A **reference token-hour** can be defined as one hour of inference service at a declared model version, hardware, context, batching and input/output mix. For heterogeneous runs, benchmark the service time of each workload at that reference configuration and sum the resulting hours. Also record actual input and output tokens, accelerator-hours, elapsed time to accepted work, tool waits and human review. Two concurrent calls can consume two reference hours in one clock hour. A person-hour can substitute for those services only on a specified job at a specified acceptance standard. Human baseline time, agent wall time and a human's time saved measure different things. [METR's task-completion time horizon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14499)

Consider a release window in which the firm can screen $r$ distinct agent proposals in time. Available token service, human or automated verification and the serial depth of the job bound $r$. Each proposal has probability $p_A$ of yielding a candidate that works. With probability $\rho$ all outcomes share one draw with that same marginal probability. Otherwise they are independent. A working candidate has a common probability $q$ that its method can be retained, validated and carried to the next independent use, conditional on at least one success. For $r\geq1$, the chance of delivering a reusable method by the deadline is

$$
P_A(r)=q\{\rho p_A+(1-\rho)[1-(1-p_A)^r]\}.
$$

Set $P_A(0)=0$. For $r\geq1$, the next *screened* proposal adds $q(1-\rho)p_A(1-p_A)^r$. Extra copies outside the review window add zero here. Even unlimited copies approach only $q[1-\rho(1-p_A)]$. These statements follow by conditioning on the common draw and counting the chance that every independent draw fails. They assume proposals are exchangeable, screening finds a working candidate and duplicate successes serve the same use. Correlated hallucinations, common data selection and a shared unqualified fixture can all generate dependence. Their empirical signatures can differ, so $\rho$ here is a restriction to test.

Let the best feasible ordinary or mixed team have timely reusable yield $P_H$, current operating contribution $u_H$ and incremental cost $c_H$. Use $u_A,c_A$ for the proposed agent team, including coordination and all displaced review. For equal deadlines $d$ and the same captured value $S$ conditional on qualification, delegation pays when

$$
u_A-u_H-(c_A-c_H)+\beta^d S[P_A(r)-P_H]>0 .
$$

Thus even a less capable individual agent can be part of a faster-learning team when enough *different* candidate improvements can be screened, copied and exercised in time. For independent repeat windows of equal duration $T$ and constant success probabilities, expected time to first qualified method is $T/P$ for $P>0$. Faster completion of the first method becomes recursive acceleration only if its use improves the production of the next method on fresh work. Training copies to make highly correlated proposals, funding another release engineer, improving a shared representation, or returning tasks to people can each be optimal under different states. A fall in agent inference cost can raise demand for human validation. Track both the share of original work delegated and total human hours by role.

**A tendency with a sign that can be tested.** Holding the two teams' technologies fixed, increasing the future stake $S$ raises the relative return to delegation by exactly $\beta^d(P_A-P_H)$ per unit. A larger eligible fleet therefore favors automating an investigative function when the agent team produces more timely, usable successors. When the ordinary team has the higher yield, the same fleet expansion raises the return to keeping people in charge of that function. Both are forms of recursive investment. Define $\alpha_t$ as the share of decisions delegated on a fixed reference population with fixed job weights. A conditional tendency toward automation means positive expected change in $\alpha_t$ within a state or family of states. An aggregate upward trend can also arise as the economy shifts toward tasks already easy to delegate. Report within-task changes, task composition and new task entry separately. This comparative static gives a testable direction. Estimating a trend requires longitudinal data.

Generalization itself becomes a choice. Open a shared procedure to another embodiment when the expected *joint* change in captured work, evidence and investment opportunities exceeds validation, interference with incumbent skills and displaced support. Compare that with native training and a human or partner routine. The gain can change sign after the next release. Sites share a useful representation to different degrees, and a procedure may transfer even when the trained executor fails to transfer. A test of that distinction keeps the executor fixed. ## Evidence that precedes the model check

A real robot offers a narrow test of the premise that the decision rule conditions productivity. Li et al.'s Robin fleet compared learned pick ranking with a height heuristic. Holding center picks fixed in their Table III, the gain reconstructed from attempts and failures is **0.49 percentage points** when both methods must respect topological ordering and **1.49 points** when both can rank freely. The difference between those improvements is 0.99 points at the unrounded rates. This is the measured success of pick attempts under four configurations. Additional machines, paid final orders, the operator's private return and the production of successor procedures were outside that comparison. The groups' aggregate counts cannot recover site-level uncertainty. [Li et al., Table III](https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss19/p023.pdf)

There is a digital counterpart. In six benchmarks spanning 260 configurations, Kim et al. report multi-agent gains on decomposable financial reasoning and losses on sequential planning. They standardize tools, prompts and compute across architectural comparisons. This is evidence that coordination changes what a model team can produce on a specified task. It leaves the firm's price of review, candidate correlation on its own jobs and downstream learning yield to be measured. [Kim et al., version 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296v3)

## A two-release test of the proposed conditioning

Two customers pay for accepted orders. One uses a fixed arm and one a mobile platform. A dispatch agent can reserve an extra service block for either lane. A qualification agent can release that lane for use. They can also wait, and each active department consumes a priced slot. One routine order per lane clears in either case. An additional order clears when it is ready and has either inherited qualification or matching dispatch and release. Physical marginal products therefore come from intervening on a typed unit of *usable* service. When both lanes have an extra order ready and no inherited capacity, both products are one. Only a successful incremental service on the first release can produce a procedure for the other lane on the second. A cross-use grant is one coordinate of the slow stack choice. The firm can buy it for a fee before the first dispatch. Retention and qualification probabilities can differ by direction.

The experiment fits a two-stage, joint-action tabular Q function from randomized logs of the two departments. One version sees the measured physical products, the date and the relevant grant or inherited capacity. A restricted version sees the calendar and grant or inherited capacity only. A disjoint standing panel randomizes prior qualification and release to measure the products from accepted orders. The predecision classifier recovers the physical finite differences exactly in this small deterministic technology. At the terminal date, passive receipts from an earlier release are common to all current actions. We remove them to learn current action differences and restore them when valuing the earlier choice.

An ordinary dynamic rule learns demand and cross-use frequencies from the same operating records and solves the two-period problem on raw readiness data. A current-margin rule uses the same shared readiness data. A distributed alternative jointly selects the best pair of local decision rules from all 81 feasible pairs at each date, grant and inherited condition. Each department then acts on its own lane instrument and the common calendar without exchanging observations. We also solve the informed planner's optimum separately in each test world. The joint learner represents two production functions under coordinated control. No LLM weights or robot controllers were trained here.

Each of four training seeds supplies 12,000 panel episodes and 48,000 separate operating episodes. Both the fitted Q and the ordinary dynamic rule get the same resources. Anonymous accounting units price extra arm and mobile orders at seven and five, each active slot at one, and discount the second release by 0.9. Forty independently drawn test economies vary load and directional transfer. All policies see the same world for each paired comparison. Within a world we enumerate outcomes exactly. The following means give two-release discounted incremental contribution with cross-use enabled, before the grant fee. The full results also report accepted orders by market, slots consumed, procedure yield and world-level paired standard errors. Upfront research and report infrastructure are common sunk inputs in these comparisons. [Code](study.py) · [Results and source counts](results.json)

| Training family | 9.21 | 6.92 | 8.90 | 9.21 | 7.43 |

With both lanes ready, the learned team chooses the arm under local-only rights. A cross-use grant switches its first decision to the lower-margin mobile lane, which in the training environment often yields usable arm capacity for the next shift. Within the training family, the Q policy and the ordinary dynamic rule tie in all 40 economies. The information and coordinated response have value here. These economies supply no performance premium for the RL implementation over that incumbent. When the direction of transfer changes, allocating to current margin wins in all 40 worlds. When the shared report breaks, a distributed protocol retains its local instruments and overtakes both centrally informed rules. If the corruption also destroys local information, that last comparison must be redone.

The stack choice changes too. With informed reoptimization, the mean break-even fee for cross-use is 1.17 in the training family and 0.25 under thinner joint demand. For a firm making a common ex ante purchase across draws in either family, a posted fee between those numbers changes the favored boundary. Unexpected transfer reversal makes the grant worth 2.02 to a planner that understands the new direction. Following its frozen training allocation, the Q policy earns 0.03 less with the grant than without it in that regime. An additional optional right can be left unused. A firm that systematically acts on obsolete beliefs can nevertheless lose money when exercising it.

## What would establish automation of recursion?

Record the original job population before routing, model and embodiment vintages, capacity offers, actual attempts, human recovery, dated acceptance and settlements. Identify the physical margin through a randomized offer of incremental qualified service with a documented first stage and comparable ordinary alternatives. With one-sided noncompliance and the usual exclusion and monotonicity assumptions, distinguish the response to an offer from the response per induced unit. Randomize across a shared support pool or model its spillover to other sites. A site's own queue alone can omit the orders it displaced elsewhere.

Then cross provision of the measured forecast with decision authority. Let an incumbent human or hybrid team, a single agent and a coordinated agent team choose on the same information, task roster and priced tokens, human review and release slots. Vary review capacity and the independence of candidates separately. Keep a held-out, pre-admission reference panel and assess a new vintage under a reversed or weakened transfer link. Charge producing the forecast to any arrangement that needs it. The conditional prediction is that delegation speeds learning when an extra screened proposal has novel, timely value on another deployment. With saturated review or a common failure, extra instances should add little. If the information-matched incumbent reproduces the gain, attribute it to the signal or allocation procedure. If only the executor improves, attribute the gain to execution.

The strongest open question concerns the *next* generation. After transferring the resulting allocation procedure to a second team at a fixed executor and budget, does that team produce a better successor for a third independent site? Randomize whether that successor is passed on while preserving common access to the original method. A change in token throughput, paid work, local margins or first-generation reuse can all occur with zero effect on this last comparison. This design would connect measured $MPK_r$, delegated production and the firm's ability to change how it improves.
