# Qualified compatible supply is a dated operating condition

The earlier annual model names actuator and onboard-board pools, while the monthly model uses abstract modules. Those quantities cannot be added or converted without a contract. Edition 0.31 supplies an explicit replacement contract for one matched cleaning system: whole compatible manufacturing parts, separately received and qualified inventory, and named discrete replacement requirements on the same paid physical loop. The prior generic mode remains available with unchanged behavior.

This is a synthetic manufacturing/maintenance mechanism, not a product BOM, observed failure rate or measured supply forecast. The annual catalog's actuator/board identities specify compatibility. Actual receipt dates, qualification dates and replacement requirements are separately declared. Full six-cell supply allocation, supplier working capital and the target's actual maintenance process remain unexecuted.

## What the complete cases change

The qualified control begins with no typed stock. In month zero, 100 compatible H actuators and four boards arrive and qualify. Four funded starts consume 96 actuators and all four boards. The remaining four actuators satisfy one assumed replacement requirement per unit in month four. Actual units, Treasury, competition and annual-resource outcomes match the earlier ample generic-material control. This is a controlled change in the stock contract, not an estimate that the two inventories have equal economic value.

An announcement with no actual receipt creates no usable or quarantined inventory. With physically received but unqualified boards, 100 actuators can be usable while four boards remain held and manufacture still cannot proceed. With 100 Q actuators and four boards, the H system cannot manufacture at all. Summing these stocks as undifferentiated “components” would erase the binding incompatibility.

If board qualification occurs in month one after the sole month-zero manufacturing attempt, no robot is manufactured automatically. The inputs then contain usable stock but no later order. An explicitly rescheduled month-one start creates four units, moves the first supported incumbent response from month two to month three, and reduces first-year supported exposure from 14 to 10 robot-months. A promise, receipt, qualification and authorized funded production are four different milestones.

The spare-delay case delivers four replacement actuators in month four's phase 4. All four installed robots already missed their phase-3 service bookings that month. The parts remain protected and are issued when service succeeds in month five. No replacement or annual support booking is counted in the failed month. First-year exposure falls from 14 to 10 robot-months, and the declared response rule temporarily changes the incumbent's offer. Service is not backfilled by the later shipment.

Rejecting two of the four paid starts consumes the same 96 manufacturing actuators, four boards and $144,000 of bundled manufacturing cash. The two rejected unit identities remain in the physical ledger. Only the two operating units complete replacement requirements, leaving two qualified spare actuators unused. Applying expected yield as another cost multiplier or returning the rejected parts automatically would violate this execution contract.

## The human alternative limits the economic inference

All eight standard cases still deliver 6,000 accepted market zones through the retained paid human operation and any supported robots. A component shortage therefore changes robot capacity and the specified competitive response, but does not establish lost total service in these cases.

The entrant serves 200 zones in the qualified control, 300 with explicit later manufacture or the spare delay, and 1,200 when no robot manufacture occurs. These differences follow the declared robot-triggered incumbent bid policy; they are not estimated customer preferences. The earlier human-led response remains a direct falsifier of an assumed robot-dependent pricing advantage. The component result does not establish that a real incumbent must abandon a price response when its robot cannot operate.

Entrant operating cash after recorded bills remains negative in every standard case. Avoided robot rent/support or an unexecuted purchase can reduce cash outflow without proving a better long-run business. Missing residual value, future obligations, actual staffing flexibility and full target costs remain separate. No released task-time estimate becomes paid labor removed.

## Decision consequences

**Hospital logistics:** verify compatible, qualified replacements and the provider's actual support entitlement against the delivery obligation. A part shipped later in the day or month does not establish that service was covered before arrival. Preserve funded porters and other fallback; compare their complete service before claiming robot availability adds output or removes payroll. This cleaning timing contract is a question for the hospital, not a measured hospital repair schedule.

**Utility inspection:** identify the configured sensor/compute/actuation stack, usable replacements, qualification and access/review windows. An announced supply quantity or compatible mechanical platform does not prove the installed inspection configuration works. Residual physical visits, review and fixed/conventional alternatives remain in the service boundary. Do not import cleaning parts or prices.

**Automotive presentation:** distinguish whole manufacturing kits, qualified assemblies, site commissioning and generation-specific supported replacements. A rejected funded build retains resources even if no accepted kit is produced. A different actuator or board configuration is not interchangeable by count. Compare the existing qualified conventional workcell, retain its service obligations and require a dated replacement plan before relying on a ramp.

Proceed to a scoped evaluation when the exact configuration, qualified receipt dates, whole-start requirements, installed-service replacements, usable support rights and each payer's obligations reconcile. Wait when qualification or replacement timing remains unknown. Reject an asserted deployment benefit that counts announcements as stock, substitutes incompatible parts, retroactively credits late repair, or ignores the stronger human/conventional alternative. These remain evaluation gates, not purchase instructions.

## Evidence and integration still required

The replacement vector and its inclusion within the fixed support labor/cash bundle are assumptions. The next empirical challenge is a target configuration's actual service, replacement and commercial record: failed versus completed interventions, qualification lead time, technician work, supplier funding, warranty/rights and accepted service during outages. A public primary record that changes one of those assumptions is more valuable than another favorable synthetic variant. Do not repeat exhausted restricted-source searches or infer missing prices and repair obligations from a vendor capability claim.

The annual aggregate spare flow is not yet a discrete failure process, and the declared component receipts do not execute the other annual markets or supplier financing. The model therefore remains partial on joint portfolio feasibility and empirical whole-service economics. Fourteen challenge families now include 174 bounded worlds; their results must not be added as one company plan.
