# Strategy and deployment decisions: first integrated findings

## A completed project does not price the service, edition 0.43

The [utility cost audit](quadruped_operator_followup.md#project-expenditure-replication-and-realized-service-edition-043) supplies historical project expenditure and exposes separately funded site work. This improves the evidence beyond an unpriced demonstration, while leaving current accepted-service costs and realized gains unresolved. The strategic test is which decision useful information changes, after all operating, access and support costs. Innovation funding and project-budget performance cannot substitute for that test.

## Accepted information can be the product, edition 0.42

The [utility operator audit](quadruped_operator_followup.md#specialist-inspection-and-the-wider-service-edition-042) shifts the first purchase question to qualified information and access. An economically useful robot service can retain specialist operators and reviewers; its comparison must include a feasible alternative and all paid duties. Labor removal, production availability and capability value require different evidence. Neither a multi-shift headcount nor a successful component result establishes whole-program savings. The three decision packs now carry that test without transferring source rates or changing synthetic outputs.

The new 7 November decision campaign adds [three provisional deployment and entry packs](deployment_entry_decisions.md), preceded by a reassessment of all six deep cells. The strongest immediate questions are residual paid coverage in hospitals, residual mandatory visits in utilities, and workcell/asset-generation qualification in automotive production. These are conditional research decisions; the integrated execution bridges and challenge cases remain work in progress.

This campaign asks where a humanoid company should deploy, what must improve, and which organizational and commercial arrangements can sustain useful production. The early evidence supports a conditional strategy organized around customer services and their bottlenecks. It does not yet support a calibrated economy-wide penetration forecast or a claim that one morphology will replace a fixed share of physical labor.

The November restaurant comparison sharpens the customer hurdle: model-led folding must compete on the full accepted napkin service against qualified specialist equipment and a measured human baseline. Advertised speed and base wages do not reveal a removable paid shift. [FG-CMP-03](customer_value.md#restaurant-napkin-workflow-comparison) defines loading, acceptance, rework, handoff and support plus separate customer/provider cash and consolidated resources. It narrows the required evidence without establishing an ROI or winner.

The US labor atlas establishes a broad economic frame. The six deeper cells are automotive assembly/material presentation, third-party logistics handoffs, contract floor cleaning, hospital internal logistics, construction installation and utility inspection. These are deliberately contrasting production systems. They differ in access, quality standards, process authority, environmental variation, customer relationships and the alternatives already available. Cases outside the United States inform mechanisms but do not become US labor observations.

A production system is valuable when it delivers the required service, at the required quality and time, within the available resources and customer demand. Robot capability is one part of that system. A successful route, attractive demonstration, larger fleet, financing announcement or open repository can be relevant evidence without establishing the completed service or its economics. The dossier chapters retain those distinctions so that a commercial decision can be traced back to its actual evidentiary basis.

## A deployment portfolio, rather than a generic robot market

The six cells suggest different bounded entry questions. For automotive assembly, the question is whether a configured system can reliably complete a defined sequence within the production process, and how that compares with fixed or specialized automation. Public pilot evidence can help establish the workflow; it does not disclose a plant-wide labor substitution rate. For contract logistics, the end customer, site operator and robotics supplier may be different organizations. A commercial RaaS agreement is important evidence of an arrangement, while undisclosed price, support and intervention terms still prevent a complete investment case.

Floor cleaning illustrates why simpler embodiments and contract duration matter. The useful outcome is an accepted cleaned area under the site's service standard. A specialized machine can be a stronger competitor than a more general robot. A facilities-service contract may end sooner than the robot asset's economic life, creating utilization and residual-value risks even if a steady-state task comparison looks attractive. Transferring the asset to a service provider reallocates those risks; it does not remove them from the production system.

Hospital logistics tests the difference between a bounded delivery service and an occupation. Useful nonurgent transport can coexist with limited ability to perform time-sensitive or complex nursing workflows. Doors, elevators, handoffs and support work can decide the value of a mobile system. The public evidence should therefore change the definition of addressable work before it changes a productivity coefficient.

Construction installation involves changing sites, access, sequencing and multiple contractors. A bounded technical demonstration cannot establish a general site deployment rate. The next question is which workflow can be repeated with an explicit acceptance standard and sufficient authority over the surrounding process. Utility inspection similarly requires more than access: measurements must be usable, reviewed and linked to an appropriate decision or action. Fixed sensing, wheeled platforms, quadrupeds, drones and human teams may substitute for one part of the chain or complement different parts.

These distinctions prevent a false aggregation. A robot supplier's potential sales, a contractor's labor bill, the customer's value added and the final product's revenue are not additive measures of the same opportunity. The atlas identifies employment coverage and residuals; it does not turn headcounts into physical-work hours by assuming a universal time share. Competing systems share the same underlying work, and service providers can appear in several value chains without creating several labor pools.

## Capability progress changes a constrained production function

The capability evidence makes hardware generation, policy version, task distribution, assistance and observation period explicit. The relevant improvement is a change in accepted output, intervention, adaptation or cost under comparable conditions. Neither a growing demonstration dataset nor a benchmark score establishes a general improvement exponent across markets. Transfer can be useful within a tested setting and fail in another, including when a larger mixed dataset performs worse on a particular target.

The modeling consequence is to keep base capability, directed learning, coordination, hardware qualification and customer readiness separately inspectable. A policy improvement may widen technical eligibility while leaving manufacturing, commissioning, support or demand as the binding constraint. A new generation does not automatically upgrade installed fleets. A replacement consumes supply without increasing net active stock. A component commitment is not a finished robot; a finished robot is not a commissioned customer service.

This is also why learning value needs a counterfactual. If useful experience changes future operating cash flows, its benefit belongs in the comparison of policies with and without that learning, including collection and validation costs. Adding a separate data premium would count the same gain again. Information that changes a later investment decision is another channel and should not be confused with a measured policy improvement.

## The digital counterfactual moves too

Workstream H materially changes the robotics comparison. Human-led and incumbent operations can improve through software, models, routing, planning and better information before or alongside a robot deployment. Comparing a future robot with a permanently frozen human baseline overstates the incremental case. The report therefore uses the four-way logic of current operations, additional digital improvement, additional robotics, and their combination, with matched output and quality.

A combined system can outperform the sum of isolated changes, underperform it, or merely count the same removed bottleneck twice. The common engine exposes a synthetic interaction rather than copying an effect size from a digital study. Evidence from customer support, software development, professional-task benchmarks, simulated enterprises and embodied control remains tied to its own population, system and evaluation conditions.

Coordination is similarly conditional. The updated peer-reviewed agent study narrows the earlier blog's claims and explicitly treats its capability threshold as a within-domain selection rule. That finding supports testing architectures against a strong simpler baseline. It does not prove a universal benefit from adding agents, and it does not transfer a numerical coefficient into a physical fleet. A digital planner, conventional scheduler, enterprise transaction system and robot controller also operate at different timescales and authority boundaries. Connecting them is an engineering and operating claim that requires more than citing each component separately.

## Customer formation and provider strategy are different decisions

A customer-side AI-native newco sells an end-market product or service. Its advantage could come from a different operating design, an earlier adoption window, or access to capacity when human labor is constrained. Those are distinct hypotheses. None follows simply from access to the same robot that an incumbent can also buy. The comparison must include a prompt-adopting incumbent, customer acquisition, ramp losses, working capital, demand that can actually be won and delivered, and the possibility of price or retention responses.

A holding company that creates operating businesses, one that acquires existing firms, a standalone entrant and a hybrid face different constraints. Acquisition can bring customers and operating access while importing process and contractual commitments. Greenfield formation can allow redesign while requiring new customers and complementary assets. Thrive's disclosed structure is a useful organizational comparator, but its financing and digital deployment claims do not establish the economics or financeability of a humanoid-enabled industrial entrant.

The provider decision is separate: sell equipment, finance assets, license software, integrate, manage operations, form partnerships or operate part of the customer's value chain. These choices move cash requirements, operating risks and control rights between actors. They do not create free capital or necessarily change total production value. The published BrainOS terms make a further distinction concrete: ownership of operational data is different from access, improvement-use rights and exclusivity. A customer cannot assume it retains an exclusive learning advantage merely because it owns the data.

## Qualified supply and supported operation limit scale

The physical and digital supply analyses converge on a practical rule: qualify the resource and the workflow before pooling a constraint. Embedded LPDDR memory and datacenter HBM have different configurations and applications. Actuator specifications, environmental qualification, spare compatibility and field support likewise cannot be replaced with an undifferentiated robot count. Product specifications establish an architecture; they do not disclose manufacturing cost, yield or available qualified capacity.

Supplier investment and customer commitments can support expansion but have different accounting meanings. Micron's take-or-pay arrangements illustrate how supply assurance and supplier financing can coexist with customer commitments and restricted uncommitted availability. TSMC's reported capital expenditure illustrates investment already made, without identifying a robot OEM's allocation. Deposits, expenditure, revenue and delivered output must remain separate. A supply backcast gives necessary conditions for a scale target; it does not establish demand or adoption.

For a humanoid company, these findings favor a sequence of testable service propositions with explicit alternatives, measurable acceptance, known process authority and visible support obligations. They do not establish a universal sector ranking. The next investment-worthy evidence is a matched cohort showing accepted output, all human support, retention, full costs and rights. Public disclosures often establish only some of those links. Where they stop, the report preserves a bounded conclusion and a precise next question rather than inventing a margin or penetration rate.

## What the deeper investigations change

The hospital follow-up strengthens the case for defining service eligibility before discussing automation rates. The Guro study excludes urgent requests from its evaluated mission population, while routine handling and a trained accompanying safety operator remain part of the observed arrangement. Its reported success percentage disagrees with the ratio implied by its eligible and completed counts. The report retains both and does not select a convenient reliability coefficient. The Qilu comparison supplies distribution and retrieval phase timings; those shorter phases cannot establish whole-shift payroll removal. The operational question becomes whether a proposed service can satisfy its acceptance standard after counting all residual handling, oversight, failure and recovery work. The source-specific denominators and unresolved economic methods appear in the hospital follow-up in the customer chapter.

The non-Spot quadruped investigation reaches a similarly useful but narrower conclusion than a broad inspection-adoption claim. TenneT's operator-coauthored offshore study shows that a legged platform can traverse and inspect portions of a difficult environment. Instrument visibility varies by room; inaccessible doors, prohibited areas, safety rules and unfinished integration constrain what service the platform can complete. Later onshore pilot material shows additional inspection development, but project duration and a programmed mission repertoire do not disclose productive exposure, retained fleet use or realized avoided labor. The evidence supports a configured inspection proposition with explicit exclusions. It does not establish abandonment, successful scale, or the economics of replacing a human visit. Both possibilities remain open where the operational record stops.

The learning-data follow-up changes the denominator for a capability-investment decision. Cobalt's cloud-cost comparison concerns one collection-stage resource, and its simulated collection and separate physical evaluation have different populations. DexWild demonstrates useful human-data collection and robot-grounding experiments, while changes in dataset size and within-batch sampling mix are different interventions. Neither paper establishes a general price per unit of commercially validated capability. A robotics company choosing between teleoperation, portable human capture, simulation and additional robot deployment needs the full incremental collection, filtering, training, recovery-coverage and held-out validation cost at a fixed target. Lowering one input burden may be valuable; the value depends on which bottleneck actually limits the target policy.

The benchmark reconstruction also makes a material comparison less ambiguous. TheAgentCompany's archived displayed costs include non-perfect attempts, whereas Astranomy's reported conditional cost covers a successful subset. A cheaper-looking number can therefore measure a different event. The reconstructed table improves source-side accounting without resolving omitted infrastructure, other model use, human work or the executed monetary-limit configuration. Astranomy's paused simulation further separates action-clock success from elapsed-time service in a changing environment. Those distinctions constrain any attempt to price physical orchestration from an agent benchmark.

Finally, the pinned coordination interfaces locate a concrete systems question: what is known after a request is accepted, a response times out, or cancellation is acknowledged? Admission, assignment, execution and reconciliation have distinct authorities. A retry may repeat an action whose first acknowledgement was lost; cancellation acceptance need not reverse partial effects or establish a physical stop. A durable business identifier helps maintain intent, but it cannot alone make an external action and a local record one atomic transaction. A credible coordination contribution needs explicit failure handling and a strong deterministic baseline before a language model is added. Local fault tests can establish software behavior under declared assumptions; a compatible installed stack and target operating data are still needed for production claims.

The onboard-compute follow-up gives that timing boundary a concrete implementation example. NVIDIA's published Thor/GR00T optimization recipe is inspectable and reports a large reduction in its component-sum timing. The released measurement code, repeated observation, warm-up rules and conflicting source tables constrain the claim: it does not establish sustained sensor-to-actuator deadlines, runtime memory headroom or the target robot's accepted service. Module specifications and a faster benchmark remain useful inputs to qualification. A deployment decision still needs the exact workload, concurrent perception, action horizon, thermal/power conditions and closed-loop outcomes. The [onboard qualification analysis](onboard_workload_qualification.md) records the missing run artifacts and preserves the distinct task populations.

## Decisions to make next, and the evidence that could reverse them

The next deployment decision should begin with one accepted service and a credible alternative, chosen from the broader atlas. Define the customer, process authority, output and quality standard, timing, exclusions and residual human work. Then identify the mechanism that could make the proposed configuration better: a different operating design, improved technical coverage, lower total service cost, additional useful capacity, or a temporary adoption lead. Each mechanism has a different counterexample. A simpler machine may defeat a general embodiment on stable work; a digitally improved human process may remove the apparent savings; a prompt incumbent may eliminate an entrant's timing advantage; unsupported operating work may consume the released labor.

Next, separate economic value from commercial terms and liquidity. Match all alternatives on the same delivered service before allocating price, title, maintenance, control and data rights. Test the cash dates as well as lifetime margins. A refundable deposit, asset lease or supplier commitment changes who bears funding and performance exposure, and may change whether execution is possible. It is not an additional resource saving. The current annual fleet and separate monthly entrant reference illustrate parts of this logic, but their separate resource pools still prevent a joint financeability conclusion.

Research should then target the observation most likely to change that decision. For an inspection proposition, retained measurement quality and avoided follow-up visits may matter more than another mission count. For hospital logistics, complete handling and recovery exposure may dominate a faster transport phase. For an entrant, customer qualification, collections, retention and funded commissioning may matter more than the next robot-price announcement. For a coordination contribution, a reconciled adverse trace may be more informative than another agent score. Public-source work should stop at a documented missing artifact when additional searches merely repeat the same disclosure. Target-site measurement or practitioner validation can follow when separately authorized.

The accepted learning sequence can begin from this report while the campaign continues. Amar's first task is to explain these boundaries, challenge the six initial theses and identify the smallest observation that would change a preferred course. Understanding is not inferred from having a dashboard or reading a report. The thesis register and guided walkthrough supply prompts; they do not predetermine a startup, employer, investment or technical contribution.
