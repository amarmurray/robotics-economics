# Teach-back 4 — When coordination increases the return to another robot

**Level:** applied microeconomics / queueing and comparative statics. Try before reading `TEACH_BACK_SOLUTIONS.md`.

1. A fleet has N active-capable robots, one remote exception responder, arrival intensity λ per working robot-hour and mean intervention service rate μ. Construct the finite-state transition graph indexed by the number waiting/under repair. Why does it matter that interrupted robots cannot generate another request?
2. Derive the ratio `pi_k/pi_(k-1)` and the exact fleet's expected productive units. Show using flow balance that, for N sufficiently large and λ>0, adding robots without expanding the attention capacity eventually yields very little additional productive output.
3. A proposed digital investment halves blocking request intensity without reducing safety standards. Explain why that is *not* the same thing as doubling the robot's physical speed. Write the conditional comparison between the digital investment and one more robot after allowing customer output demand to respond.
4. For the four system configurations 0, H, D, H+D, derive the second cross-difference of the provider's profit under quadratic gross profit, a fixed capture share and all capacities binding. Explain when its sign differs from the cross-difference of expected productive capacity.
5. Suppose the producer's output market saturates at Q* below the baseline supported capacity. Would reducing interruptions generate additional customer cash under the specified output-share tariff? What other benefit, contract, or end market would need to be modeled to change the answer?
6. Contrast Serve's remote sidewalk intervention workflow with Agility Digit's routine autonomous industrial workflow. What data must exist before converting either description into an average number of robot-units supported per human supervisor?
7. Find one serious violation of the model's assumptions and propose a test that would reveal it. Would the failure necessarily favor new hardware, digital coordination or more supervisors?
