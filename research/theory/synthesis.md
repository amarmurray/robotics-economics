# Theory synthesis: from productive intelligence to funded physical service

Initial integrated synthesis, November 16, 2026 UTC. This is a living research explanation, not a finished literature review or a selected first-paper contribution. Primary-source versions, hashes and reading boundaries are in the linked mechanism cards. Numerical examples are synthetic unless explicitly attributed to an inspected source.

The program's central distinction is between a productive opportunity and an implementable sequence of investments. A learned policy can improve a task without supplying hardware, customer access, validation, cash or authority. Conversely, a profitable current deployment can fund investments whose learning comes from a different source. The production, learning and payment relationships therefore need separate meanings before they can explain one decision.

## What each seed theory supplies

*Organizing Intelligence* separates competence from coordination and lets firms select composition and scale. In its worked technology, better fixed-composition competence scales productivity without changing the productivity-maximizing size; coordination changes the size term. In its large-market theorem, reproducible machine inputs with suitable increasing returns can concentrate production. The [reconstructed concentration proof](organizing_intelligence.md) depends on linear rental costs, a large machine-only alternative, fixed human supply, sharp cost bounds and the paper's equilibrium efficiency concept. A physical support-cost counterexample shows why the output exponent alone does not determine operating-unit size. Neither result establishes which legal entity should own the robots.

*Data-Driven Automation* makes production generate task-specific data, with directed spillovers into capital productivity. Endogenous output therefore changes later automation incentives. The [Proposition 5 proof reconstruction](data_driven_automation.md) connects a uniform positive bound on spillover paths to comparable effective data, then uses factor-market clearing to rule out a positive limiting measure of labor-produced tasks. Ordinary graph reachability does not verify its uniform hypothesis. A permanently unpaid physical qualification obligation is outside that model; technological contagion does not itself finance entry. The result is asymptotic, not a date at which a particular enterprise can deploy.

*Capability Accumulation and Conglomeratization in the Information Age* already models markets as sets of capabilities and studies the consequences of acquisition and recombination. The [source audit](capability_accumulation.md) reconstructs its component upper-bound argument. Capability hypergraphs are prior art. A market's valued capability set should not automatically be treated as an all-required engineering qualification checklist; substitutability, complementarity and ownership need their actual source meanings.

Stein's [internal-capital-market model](internal_capital_markets.md) already explains resource reallocation under scarce finance and attaches ownership/control and incentive conditions to headquarters' advantage. Moving a fixed budget toward a better project is not a new robotics contribution. Our allowed-payment graph is a much thinner object than that theory of control. It cannot establish vertical integration merely because a consolidated budget looks easier to allocate.

The [contracting audit](contracting_and_pledgeability.md) adds Holmström–Tirole’s incentive-compatible pledgeable-income threshold and Bergemann–Hege’s Bayesian learning/stopping problem. The latter already couples dynamic funding and experimentation; its terminal contracting wedge is reconstructed rather than presented as a new robotics result. A perfect-contract benchmark shows that separate payers can implement pooling when total initial cash covers prerequisites and later operating profit covers total investment.

Together these sources supply endogenous organization, directed data accumulation, complementary capability markets and scarce-finance allocation. Combining their vocabulary is not enough to establish novelty. The research question is whether the particular timing and alignment of physical qualification, learning and capture creates a defensible additional result.

## The model ladder and what survived

The [static and two-period model](deployment_investment.md) derives customer surplus from responsive output and a feasible alternative, then prices provider capture explicitly. Current cash and future opportunity value differ. A bridge loan changes the deployment ranking in the exact example. This is a diagnostic instance of constrained dynamic investment, not an original contribution.

The [qualification/payment model](qualification_finance.md) requires a complete bundle of payer-specific prerequisites. A standard funding-cut characterization shows why total cash is insufficient when a subset of payers cannot obtain the funds it must spend. The demand partition shows when repairing the payment constraint changes investment only and when it changes the first deployment as well. Its contracting counterexample prevents an inference that common ownership is necessary.

The [directed qualification-savings extension](learning_funding_alignment.md) makes source experience reduce particular bills. Equal aggregate savings can have different deployment values because only one relieves a binding payer cut. Its exact threshold is a corollary of the funding cuts. The empirical claim that experience actually reduces those bills is a separate, currently unidentified premise.

The [eligibility and optional-policy extension](eligibility_and_free_disposal.md) replaces cost reduction with a policy that first meets a service threshold but requires increasing validation expenditure. Eligible funded versions can form a bounded interval. A forced new version can lose value; the option to retain an earlier qualified version restores weak monotonicity. Thus the unrestricted claim that more freely available experience reduces optimized private value is rejected. Costly acquisition, lost rights, mandatory replacement and changed liabilities can alter the feasible set, but must be modeled and evidenced explicitly.

The [hourly-receipts diagnostic](raas_receipts.md) derives responsive customer output and an exact financing interval under committed hourly billing. Faster service can raise output while reducing billable receipts; fixed output pricing or repricing reverses the cash effect in the example. Policy retention again defeats a claim that free learning reduces optimized value. Pre-qualification pledgeability and pricing rights are explicit scenario boundaries, not inferred from press releases.

These results identify assumptions and defeat careless rankings. They do not yet select the paper's contribution. The strongest prior-art objection remains that the present examples combine familiar constrained investment, flow feasibility and option preservation.

## What the technical evidence permits

The [DexWild/DreamGen audit](qualification_evidence.md) preserves the actual joint training recipes rather than attributing an entire policy improvement to one outgoing data edge. Human target-task data, robot grounding, processing, pseudo-actions, adaptation and evaluation are distinct inputs. A benchmark partial-credit mean is not necessarily a binary fraction of completed service. Conditional rubric bounds can limit full-score fractions, but are not industrial reliability estimates or sampling confidence intervals.

Neither inspected study identifies the sign of total qualification-cost savings by payer. Faster demonstration collection can be offset by processing, validation, integration and retained support. Simulation generation resources cannot be allocated to a different physical-policy experiment without evidence. The [sparse evidence records](evidence_transitions.json) therefore preserve unknown costs, commercial rights, customer demand and permitted early payments as null values. They are not calibrated model inputs or atlas v1.

The [RaaS assignment-finance slice](raas_assignment_evidence.md) documents a lender-described route from customer receivables to upfront vendor cash, a Formic financing partnership and issuer-reported hourly billing. It does not establish that a Formic contract was assigned, that an advance preceded qualification, or how underwriting treats productivity changes. Technical transfer and financial relationships remain separate observations, with their joining edges unknown.

The priority measurement is cost to meet the *same* target acceptance criterion under matched source-experience alternatives, with full failures, human/compute effort, physical validation, recurring support, payer and payment dates. Policy retention and rights must be observed to test whether a forced-update example applies. Sector employment data cannot fill these local transition and contract gaps.

## Analogy and non-analogy

| Mechanism | Useful mapping | Boundary requiring evidence or a new model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Digital replication | A policy can be copied across compatible units | Qualified hardware, supported capacity, rights and cash are not copied with it |
| Competence | More accepted output or lower resource use under a matched service | A benchmark score may omit interventions, quality tolerances or customer demand |
| Coordination | Shared scheduling, supervision and exception handling | Shared-resource congestion and physical site constraints can bound operating scale |
| Directed learning | One configuration's experience affects another | Similar tasks do not prove transfer; joint grounding/target data must remain explicit |
| Capability bundles | Some services need several prerequisites together | A source hypergraph need not imply a physical all-or-nothing checklist |
| Market depth | Additional service can be absorbed profitably | Willingness to pay, collected receipts and procurement authority differ |
| Internal capital allocation | Resources can move toward a better next project | Legal permissions, pledgeability and incentives determine actual transfers |
| Optional improvement | A firm can preserve a qualified earlier version | Qualification expiry or mandatory update is a separate empirical assumption |

## First-paper discipline and the next test

The proposed first paper remains deployment sequencing and capital allocation. Coordination-driven concentration and general equilibrium automation are foundations and broader-program branches, not requirements to implement in the first kernel. Customer ownership and provider legal organization remain distinct questions.

The [completed perfect-contract test](contracting_and_pledgeability.md) relaxes arbitrary payment prohibitions through a competent contracting alternative. Freely agreed early contributions and future profit shares reproduce the pooled allocation under the stated deterministic benchmark. A payment-network result therefore requires an independently justified restriction: limited pledgeability, noncontractible investment, restricted customer funds or another observed institutional constraint. It should not rely on labelling a valuable feasible transfer “forbidden” without explanation.

Then test whether costly deployment choice, directional learning and finite qualification timing generate a result beyond the existing cash-constraint example. Specify which contract prices and responses are endogenous. If the extension does not add information, discard it as the central claim and pursue a narrower empirical identification or timing result. Do not infer novelty from a larger state space.

Still open: full audits of the remaining source propositions; closest dynamic-investment/contracting literature; measured payment and validation relationships; empirical atlas backbone and contrasts; fair main experiments; a complete working paper; and release replication. The initial synthesis resolves none of those by being written. Its purpose is to make clear what is understood, what follows only conditionally, and which next test could change the direction of the paper.
