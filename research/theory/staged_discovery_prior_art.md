# Staged discovery is already a broad investment theory

Andrew Caplin, *Planning Capital and Discovery-Based Learning-by-Doing: Investment as Staged Discovery in the Hyperscale Era*, NBER Working Paper 35349, June 2026, DOI [10.3386/w35349](https://doi.org/10.3386/w35349). [Inspected primary PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35349/w35349.pdf), retrieved November 16, 2026 UTC, SHA-256 `5b8853600c5c310e3d62966de07b9968381d3ef3728eb38fd333a12aed6f2610`. It is a working paper, not a peer-reviewed publication. Read §§3–4 and §§5.1–5.3 for the formal mechanism; the institutional case narratives and all remaining comparative claims are not independently validated here.

The source is close to the program's broad question. Its staged investments can alter operating capacity, customer access, future feasible actions, beliefs and the cost/quality of later inquiry. Calling such investments “deployments that create future possibilities” cannot itself be our contribution.

**Mechanism card.** The firm faces uncertainty about a payoff state omega and a diagnostic state D specifying how questions and embodied actions generate informative answers. Its state includes a joint belief, the history of inquiry and an operating-capacity vector K. Within a stage it chooses a Bayes-consistent distribution of diagnostic posteriors, observes a realized posterior, chooses a question and a physical/organizational action, then receives an answer. The action has resource cost and can leave a nonnegative durable operating-capacity increment. The history can change subsequent menus, answer kernels and inquiry costs. It can stop and choose terminal implementation rather than continue discovery.

Diagnostic experimentation is a choice over distributions of possible posteriors whose mean equals the prior, not direct selection of a desired belief. The question and action come after the diagnostic posterior, while the answer comes afterward. Confusing that timing would give a policy information it has not yet acquired.

**Reconstructed recursion.** Terminal value is the maximum expected payoff over feasible realization actions. Continuation maximizes expected next-stage value minus diagnostic-orientation and embodiment costs, over the diagnostic experiment and contingent question/action policies. The Bellman value is max(stopping,continuation). Terminal product-market, acquisition and bargaining value is deliberately reduced form. The model imports regularity conditions from a related planning model and does not add a general existence theorem. The selected formalization does not derive payer-specific financing or customer pricing; those could be additional primitives/constraints, not claims that the general framework cannot represent them.

**Selected closed-form check.** In the one-good-path special case, one of n questions preserves the path. A diagnostic experiment attains correct identification probability p in [1/n,1] at cost kappa*I_n(p), where I_n(p)=p*log(n*p)+(1-p)*log(n*(1-p)/(n-1)). For kappa>0, n>=2 and nonnegative continuation value u, differentiating gives I_n'(p)=log(p*(n-1)/(1-p)). The first-order condition yields p*=exp(u/kappa)/(exp(u/kappa)+n-1). Strict convexity of I makes this a global maximum of p*u-kappa*I. At u=0 it is the uninformed boundary p=1/n. The maximized value is kappa*log((exp(u/kappa)+n-1)/n).

The stage recursion subtracts an embodiment cost c and allows stopping at zero. For n=2, kappa=1 and u=log(3), p*=3/4 and the one-stage value before c is log(2). With two such zero-embodiment-cost stages, backward composition gives log(3/2). These are symbolic checks of the source transform, not estimates or a new information theorem. A positive net present value here does not by itself identify how a legal entity funds c today.

**Boundary and contribution implication.** The paper explicitly distinguishes its additive operating-capacity state from an extension in which the capital object itself learns (§4.7). Its broader history-dependent menus/costs already permit favorable and unfavorable transfer, institutional differences and successor opportunities. Our focused paper must identify an additional economic restriction or empirical implication, not redescribe those states. Payer-specific finance and explicit RaaS demand are possible specializations, but neither adding them nor drawing multiple graphs establishes novelty.
