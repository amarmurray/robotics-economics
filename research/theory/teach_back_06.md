# Teach-back 06 — Distinguishing manufacturing, field and customer learning

Work through these independently before consulting the separate solutions.

1. A source deployment creates manufacturing experience X, target-useful field learning L and commercial customer access C. Explain which transition each affects and identify a record that would measure each. Why is a single scalar 'robot learning' inadequate?

2. A hybrid source generates 3 in current collected cash and a future deployable cash budget of 10. The specialist's invoice is 8-3*kappa and its target qualification bill is 7-3*delta; the target customer yields provider operating profit 16 after qualification. Derive the condition to fund its entry and its total provider value. What are the units?

3. The cash-richer factory source yields current provider cash 5 and its best funded expansion yields net gain 22/5. Derive the hybrid's strict preference condition. Compare it with the funding condition; what do the two thresholds represent?

4. Holding delta=1, calculate the smallest pass-through fraction for affordability and the strict preference cutoff. At kappa=81/100, compute the specialist's supplier invoice, actual supplier resource cost, manufacturer margin and total provider value. Explain the consolidated resource-accounting cancellation.

5. Hold kappa=delta=1 but vary the destination customer's base demand intercept a. With capacity K=2 and the two-part tariff, derive the demand-intercept boundary against factory expansion. State the conditions under which the formula fails; does higher capability automatically produce more saleable output?

6. Design a falsifiable observation protocol, rather than an interview opinion poll, for the learning-transfer, manufacturing-invoice and commercial-demand edges. Name three confounders and say why a release announcing 1,000 planned robots is not a causal measurement of learning.

The takeaway should be a causal diagram and a concrete decision comparison, not a recital of model variables.
