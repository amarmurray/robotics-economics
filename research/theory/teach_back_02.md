# Teach-back 2: a connected learning graph is not a funded service

Early packet drawn from the actual source-proof audit and qualification counterexamples. This does not record Amar's completion or replace a dated weekly synthesis.

Read the Proposition 5 continuation in [Data-Driven Automation](data_driven_automation.md), then the [eligibility/free-disposal note](eligibility_and_free_disposal.md). Attempt these before opening the [separate solution](teach_back_02_solution.md).

1. Two equal-mass task blocks have spillover values W=[[0,1],[1,0]]. Both blocks can reach each other. Why does this not verify the paper's uniform strong-connectedness condition? Does that establish that full limit automation fails?
2. Replace only W_11 by d=1/10. Compute the two-step kernel, keeping task mass in the multiplication. Find a valid epsilon for n=2. Explain why matrix powers without block masses give the wrong kernel values.
3. Reconstruct the proof's economic step: why does effective data at least b times aggregate data imply output at least lambda(b)^sigma times aggregate output? Which assumptions make this inference fail for a fixed-capacity robot behind an unpaid qualification gate?
4. A provider has cash five and an operator zero. A target requires one unit of operator qualification expense before receipts. Both receive arbitrarily useful data; neither credit nor incoming payment to the operator is allowed. Is the target funded? Name the smallest financial change that would reverse the answer, holding its engineering fixed.
5. In the eligibility example b=(5,3), k(x)=(2,1+x), acceptance x>=1 and post-investment operating profit ten, compare available experience e=1,2,3 under mandatory and optional incorporation. Why is this not evidence that collecting more free data lowers optimal profit?

Strongest challenge: are an unpaid qualification fee and mandatory version replacement explanatory mechanisms or invented vetoes? Specify the observation that would distinguish each from a firm freely retaining its previous qualified policy. Do not assume that a benchmark full score supplies the customer acceptance criterion.
