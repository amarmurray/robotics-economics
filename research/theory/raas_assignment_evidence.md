## What is observed as a public statement

The lender describes a financial path: eligible vendor/customer service contracts → assignment of payment-stream claims to lender → upfront payment to vendor. This is a route by which future customer receipts may finance present resources. The claim is about an offered mechanism; eligibility, underwriting, discount, advance rate, commitment, recourse and timing relative to installation/acceptance are not disclosed in the inspected pages.

The Formic agreement identifies a lender/provider commercial relationship around manufacturing automation. Formic describes scoping, engineering, procurement, deployment and continuing support, and states an hourly customer billing model. These are distinct service stages and actor roles; they do not establish how every cost is allocated under an executed contract. Equity financing, a commercial partnership and assignment of receivables are distinct relationships. The $27.4 million issuer-reported equity round is **not** the size of an assignment-finance facility or cash available for a particular qualification bill.

These observations contradict any universal premise that legally separate RaaS providers can never turn customer commitments into early cash. They do not prove that every proposed project is financeable, that assignment precedes customer acceptance, or that a specific payer cut in our toy example is relaxed. A positive nominal contract value is not fully pledgeable proceeds.

## The theory-discriminating contrast

The technical research slice documents author-reported policy improvements from joint data/training inputs, but does not identify commercial receipts, rights or qualification-cost savings. This financial slice documents an offered route from contractual receipts to cash, but supplies no measured learning transfer or adaptation-cost coefficient. The two slices must not be joined into an observed end-to-end deployment sequence. The missing relationships are themselves explicit: learning → accepted billable service; service → enforceable receipts; receipts → actual advance; advance → a named payer's prerequisite.

Hourly billing adds a testable tension. For fixed accepted output, a faster robot can use fewer billable hours. With responsive customer demand, output may rise enough to offset that effect—or not. If lending is based on the corresponding payment stream, the contract can change both the provider's operating value and its financing capacity. That is a proposed mechanism, not a finding about these firms. The inspected releases do not disclose fixed rates, minimum commitments, treatment of productivity improvements or underwriting assumptions.

The next model should therefore compare a specified hourly contract with a competent alternative such as output pricing, renegotiable rates or minimum commitments. It must include customer demand and count an advance once: cash received today carries an assigned claim or repayment obligation later. It must also preserve the option to retain an earlier policy unless the contract or service requirement removes it. Otherwise a “learning hurts finance” result may be an artefact of forced adoption or an incompetent pricing baseline.

## Measurement protocol

For a matched service, obtain the contract's billable unit, committed minimum, rate adjustment, acceptance/start date, performance remedies, data rights and termination terms. Separately identify which receivables are assignable, actual proceeds, holdbacks, fees, recourse and who funds ongoing service. Match these to accepted output, robot hours, support work and documented policy/configuration changes. No outreach is initiated.

A discriminating comparison asks whether a validated speed gain changes output and billable hours under otherwise comparable demand, and whether it changes net advance capacity after retained obligations. Demand growth, expanded customer scope, changed rates, easier tasks, contract selection and lender credit reassessment confound the relationship. Announcement dates and aggregate funding amounts cannot identify it.
