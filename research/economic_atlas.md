# Economic atlas and staged deployment pathways

Version 0.1.0 · Evidence retrieved 25 October 2026 · Input revision `campaign launch snapshot (see ignored sync_receipt.json)` · First substantive A/C edition, with unquantified gates retained.

The US employment evidence places much of potentially relevant physical work in large service industries as well as manufacturing and construction. It does **not** yet identify a national number of humanoid-serviceable hours. The most informative deployment evidence instead identifies bounded production systems: correctly placing a known component, transferring a standardized tote, cleaning a navigable floor, delivering a nonurgent hospital item, drilling a planned anchor hole, or collecting a qualified asset observation. Those systems have different economic denominators, alternatives, customer decision rights and bottlenecks.

The strategic implication is to choose deployment sequences using accepted output, avoidable staffing, integration burden and support demand. Employment size supplies context. It cannot rank profitable robot entry points without those additional measurements. The evidence also argues against treating humanoid deployment as a uniform replacement of physical occupations: mature specialized equipment, digital improvements to human workflows, and mixed systems are consequential competitors.

The authoritative evidence snapshot is [market_evidence.json](../data/market_evidence.json). It contains 15 market families, six deeper cells across six markets, an occupation screen, source rows, explicit residuals, conditional waterfalls and unresolved questions. Evidence identifiers below refer to that file. No physical-hour estimate, national automation percentage, calibrated adoption date or robot-market revenue total is claimed. Runtime model selection was requested as GPT-6 Astra/Ultra; independent model/reasoning metadata is not exposed to this lane, so configuration verification belongs to the lead's actual spawn record.

## The US labor anchor and its limits

BLS's latest retrieved National Employment Matrix historical baseline is **2025**, published with 2025–35 projections. Table 2.1 reports **170.2808 million jobs**, including **160.4586 million wage-and-salary jobs** and **9.8223 million unincorporated self-employed jobs**. Components differ by 100 jobs because of published rounding. BLS explicitly defines the matrix as jobs, rather than individual people; a person can hold more than one job. We use the 2025 column only. The 2035 column is BLS's conditional projection, not an observed future and not a robotics forecast. [A-BLS-EP2025; A-BLS-DEFINITIONS]

The occupation lens is a separate accounting view. May 2025 OEWS reports **155,495,730 wage-and-salary jobs**, covering full- and part-time employment in its establishment-survey scope. It excludes most agriculture, the self-employed, private household employees and unpaid family workers. Its estimates combine six survey panels from November 2022 through May 2025 and use 2018 SOC/2022 NAICS. The difference from the employment matrix is therefore substantive, not missing robot opportunity to be filled by subtraction. [A-BLS-OEWS2025; A-BLS-OEWS-SCOPE]

OEWS wage rates exclude benefits and several pay premiums. Its familiar annual-wage convention of multiplying an hourly rate by 2,080 is **not a measurement of hours actually worked**. O*NET task-frequency ratings are percentages of respondents selecting frequency categories, not time shares. These two distinctions prevent an apparently precise but unsupported employment × 2,080 × task-score estimate. [A-BLS-OEWS2025; A-ONET-TASKS]

An analyst-selected screen of eight physically intensive major SOC groups contains **61,320,140 jobs, or 39.4% of OEWS employment**. This is neither exhaustive of physical work nor exclusively physical work within each selected occupation. It includes driving and food service, for example, that a humanoid demonstration does not establish as serviceable. It excludes physically active work inside other groups, including healthcare practitioners. The sum is a reproducible screening statistic, **not automatable employment**.

## Fifteen-family map and accounting boundary

The following quantitative map follows **employer industry**, with ownership adjustments needed to reproduce BLS's sector table. It is a starting taxonomy for end-market discovery, not a claim that a NAICS sector is homogeneous. Every row is quantified for employment and qualitatively screened for deployment. Physical-work hours remain a known evidence gap in all 15 rows.

| ID / market | 2025 employment, thousand jobs | Productive chain and provisional deployment view |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| M01 Agriculture, forestry and fishing | 1,462.4 | Preparation → growing/raising → harvest → grading. Specialized field machinery is the baseline; biological variability, seasonal peaks and crop damage constrain manipulation. |
| M02 Mining and resource extraction | 573.5 | Extraction → handling/processing → inspection. Heavy machines remain central; mobile sensing is a plausible complement, with harsh-environment qualification and remote service costs. |
| M03 Utilities and infrastructure operation | 601.3 | Generation/supply → distribution → inspection → maintenance. Repeatable sensing can precede manipulation; defect detection and repair outcomes must follow mission counts. |
| M04 Construction | 8,266.7 | Design/procurement → structure → MEP/interior work → handover. BIM, prefabrication and specialized tools compete with general robots; project coordination is an economic input. |

M11 comprises NAICS 5612 and 5617; it does not include in-house cleaners employed by hospitals, retailers or manufacturers. M12 is NAICS 562. M14 comprises private education plus federal and state/local government; public schools and hospitals consequently remain there. M10 uses private healthcare to avoid duplicating public hospitals. M13 includes more than household/personal labor, including repair and other services; it is not a pure household-robot market.

The 15 families total **128.9795 million jobs**. Explicit residuals are **25.4608 million** in information, finance, real estate, professional/scientific services and management industries, and **6.0182 million** in other administrative/support services. These residuals contain physical work, including data centers, laboratories, real-estate maintenance and temporary-help assignments. They are not presumed nonphysical. Together they reproduce wage/salary employment to the published 0.1-thousand rounding tolerance. Adding unallocated self-employment reproduces the all-jobs total. The self-employed are deliberately not allocated from conjecture.

A customer's value chain can display outsourced logistics or cleaning, but those are alternative views of the same activity. A 3PL's employees remain in M08 in this view; adding them again to manufacturing, retail or apparel opportunity would double count. Destination allocation requires weights summing to one, including an unallocated remainder. No empirical destination weights have yet been estimated. Unpaid household care/work, military occupational detail and physical activity within residual sectors remain explicit coverage gaps.

## Six deeper market-stage cells

Each cell is anchored to US 2025 activity and includes evidence of other dates and, where specified, foreign comparator deployments. A stable cell ID does not make a foreign result US evidence. Industry employment below is a nonadditive context measure; none is the measured population of the specific workflow.

### Automotive assembly: narrow production evidence, demanding integration

`CELL-M05-auto-assembly-US-2025` covers component presentation/loading into an automotive production process. The 2025 motor-vehicle-manufacturing context is **285,800 jobs**, encompassing much more work than the cell. The relevant denominator is **correctly placed components meeting takt and quality requirements**, per scheduled period. Inbound sequencing, loading, welding and inspection remain connected but distinct stages. [A-BLS-DETAIL2025]

BMW's February 2026 operator account reports that Figure 02 supported production of more than 30,000 X3 vehicles during a ten-month 2025 Spartanburg pilot, moving more than 90,000 components in approximately 1,250 operating hours. Its work was precise removal and positioning of sheet-metal parts for welding. This is meaningful production-linked evidence beyond a laboratory clip. It does not mean that a robot manufactured 30,000 cars or that the entire production line's value is attributable to it. Nor can the reported ten-hour-shift language be substituted for the separately reported operating exposure. [A-BMW-2026]

The choice is between configured cells: humanoid plus fixtures/control, conventional fixed robot, conveyor/fixture redesign, or AMR plus human handling. Shared IT, occupational safety, process management and shop-floor integration are operator-described prerequisites. Economics depend on accepted components, line stoppage risk, retained intervention staffing, scrap, integration downtime and alternatives' cost. Neither a defensible price interval nor a nationally serviceable share is available from the disclosed numbers.

An incumbent OEM has demand, product qualification and process authority. Acquisition changes control but creates no additional work pool. A greenfield operator can redesign a plant, yet must finance the product, supply chain and customer ramp as well as robots. With equal robot access, a claim that the entrant automatically wins is unsupported. The first stage is repeatable bounded loading; broader SKU and multi-step coverage requires verified recovery, fixture/software compatibility and validated support demand. Repeated geometry may support learning within that envelope; cross-market dexterity is an unvalidated transfer hypothesis.

### Contract warehousing: the interface and contract are part of the machine

`CELL-M08-3pl-tote-US-2025` concerns tote handoff in a large warehouse already using AMRs and conveyors. Warehousing/storage employment is **1.8657 million jobs**, not a tote-handling labor count. The useful output is a correct tote transfer reaching the downstream process within its dispatch window. [A-BLS-DETAIL2025]

GXO's June 2024 primary announcement describes a multiyear RaaS agreement with Agility, following a late-2023 proof of concept. At its SPANX operation, Digit takes totes from mobile robots and places them on conveyors, orchestrated through Arc. The significance is an integrated bounded workflow and an operator-backed commercial arrangement. Fees, support staffing, accepted throughput, retained fleet exposure and cash savings are undisclosed. A contract is not an ROI measurement. [A-GXO-2024]

The meaningful counterfactual includes a fixed transfer station, altered conveyor geometry, or human/AMR combination. A humanoid can be attractive where brownfield geometry or product variation makes fixed redesign costly, but this remains conditional on actual throughput and support. Improved WMS scheduling might raise both human and robot productivity; it is not an extra benefit to add twice.

The readiness gate crosses several organizations: the logistics operator implements, the customer supplies demand and may control the site, and the provider supplies hardware/support. RaaS can move initial asset cash requirements but does not remove provider financing or service constraints. An operating newco needs contracted volume, customer acquisition and ramp cash. If an incumbent adopts the same Digit/Arc configuration promptly, any advantage must survive in operating execution, contracts or differentiated service. Common totes may create useful transfer; site interfaces and data rights can block it. The next stage should be earned by sustained peak-period output with observed exception queues, not by multiplying independently chosen eligibility percentages.

### Facilities cleaning: the specialized baseline already moves

`CELL-M11-floor-cleaning-US-2025` covers outsourced floor cleaning. Facilities-support and building/dwelling-services employment totals **2.4741 million jobs**, including landscaping and other work that is not floor cleaning. The denominator should be **area passing specified cleanliness and coverage inspection within the cleaning window**, with total crew and support effort retained. [A-BLS-DETAIL2025]

ISS reports deploying more than 800 sweeping/vacuuming/scrubbing robots across 21 countries. Its March 2026 article says multiple UK customer facilities increased floor-cleaning efficiency by 30%, while humans inspect and perform additional work. It also describes sensor-based occupancy scheduling and a framework checking whether a robot is justified and suitable at a customer site, including privacy/data considerations. This is credible operator evidence of an existing specialized-machine market, with commercial interest in portraying success. The exact efficiency denominator, sample, comparison, labor disposition and US transfer are unknown. **Thirty percent efficiency is not thirty percent payroll removal.** [A-ISS-2026]

A full comparison includes manual walk-behind/ride-on equipment, autonomous machines, and sensor-informed human scheduling at matched quality. The machine's accessible floor area excludes edges, congested spaces or washrooms it cannot finish. Setup, refill, cleaning the cleaner, transport, exception response and manual follow-up can preserve staffing even when autonomous coverage expands.

Customer ownership mechanisms are concrete: a multinational contractor can share engineering and redeploy equipment, while the client retains site access and service requirements; a local contractor may have direct operating authority but less capital and support capacity. Neither label establishes the effect. A robotic-cleaning newco can purchase the same machines as incumbents. Route density, contract retention, implementation skill and customer offer must explain its advantage. Contractor contract duration should be matched to asset payback and redeployment economics; a lease addresses equipment funding, not acquisition costs or losses before route density develops. These mechanisms connect to the customer/value lane's fixed-technology comparisons.

### Hospital logistics: observed deliveries expose a clinical-work boundary

`CELL-M10-hospital-logistics-US-2025` is a large multi-floor hospital's internal delivery system. Private hospital employment is **5.6538 million jobs**; the all-ownership hospital reference is **6.8184 million**, of which public employment is already in M14. Neither count is a delivery-work denominator. The outcome is **correct authorized delivery with chain of custody intact within the relevant urgency-class service limit**. [A-BLS-DETAIL2025]

ChristianaCare's nurse-authored 2026 account describes three cobots serving more than 80 patient areas, completing over 48,600 deliveries during a 2022–25 grant-funded pilot. The program tested EHR and elevator integration. It found operational value in high-volume nonurgent medication/equipment delivery, while concluding that the robots could not meaningfully augment complex and time-sensitive nursing workflows. Nurses infrequently initiated requests when urgency or informal coordination made delivery automation a poor fit. This is material negative evidence for broad clinical-labor substitution, alongside positive evidence for a narrower service. The $1.5 million grant is program funding, not robot acquisition price or a full operating-cost estimate. [A-HOSPITAL-2026]

A separate qualitative study observed 89 deliveries across 23 sessions and 33 observer-hours in November–December 2022. It reports 107 human interventions, including 72 for elevators. The category counts sum to 111, however, without explaining overlap or error. The headline and discrepancy are both preserved. This sample establishes early integration and assistance burdens; it cannot calibrate a current platform-wide intervention rate. Observer-hours are not interchangeable with robot operating hours, and durations per assist were not supplied. [A-HOSPITAL-2024]

Alternatives include pneumatic tubes, AGVs, carts/porters and digital request batching. Compare accepted on-time deliveries and **all** loading, unloading, intervention and coordination effort. Hospital governance distributes authority among nursing, pharmacy, facilities, IT and executives. Protected staff time and support matter even where capital is available. A service newco must work through those interfaces; delivery activity cannot establish removed nursing payroll. The next stage is reliable nonurgent coverage with measured labor and SLA performance, followed by separate evidence for tighter time requirements. Door/route learning can transfer within compatible sites; clinical prioritization is a different mechanism.

### Construction: digital preparation can dominate a narrow robot's gain

`CELL-M04-construction-install-US-2025` covers MEP/interior overhead drilling on projects with coordinated plans and accessible interiors. Building-equipment contractors employ **2.6021 million wage-and-salary jobs**. The cell's denominator is **accepted holes at required coordinates, depth and diameter**, per work package; completed installation is a downstream outcome. [A-BLS-DETAIL2025]

Hilti's US Jaibot material describes semi-automated drilling, with digital plans uploaded through cloud/tablet workflows, total-station positioning, autonomous drilling within reach, and progress feedback. People still operate the machine and set up the total station. A 2021 IAARC paper documents ALEC's One Zaabeel implementation in Dubai and explicitly examines workflow, organization, quality, safety and project-management implications. Only its abstract was retrieved here, so numerical productivity effects are not adopted. The foreign case supports a mechanism to investigate in US projects, not a US penetration estimate. [A-HILTI-JAIBOT; A-JAIBOT-UAE2021]

The economics must include coordinated design, transport/mobilization, setup, obstruction, rebar encounters, operator labor and rework. A high drill rate does not equal a similar improvement in all MEP installation, particularly if the next trade is the bottleneck. Digital layout plus manual drilling and prefabrication are material alternatives; a humanoid must beat the configured alternatives on accepted work and utilization.

Authority is split between the general contractor controlling schedule/site access and subcontractors controlling crews/tools. A robotics subcontractor may aggregate work, but loses utilization when plans or upstream work are late. An acquisition can supply relationships and pipeline without automatically giving control over other trades. Future integrated stages require more reliable work-package preparation and access as well as physical capability. BIM-to-execution/as-built feedback is a direct digital contribution; lower drilling effort and reduced coordination error must not be counted twice when they represent the same avoided rework.

### Utility inspection: sensing is an intermediate product

`CELL-M03-utility-inspection-US-2025` covers generation/substation asset inspection. US electric generation/transmission/distribution employment is **420,500 jobs**, not a count of inspectors or inspections. The useful-output denominator is **observations meeting sensor-quality and defect-detection requirements within the inspection interval**, followed separately by actionable finding, work order, repair and asset outcome. [A-BLS-DETAIL2025]

National Grid's February 2023 operator report describes a three-day Spot trial at its Blyth converter site, with visual, thermal, acoustic and LiDAR functions. It explicitly says results were being reviewed and future investment remained conditional. This supports physical access and data-collection possibilities, not retained routine use, actual outage reduction or a US adoption rate. Broader quadruped/drone operating evidence is owned by the I/J lane; this cell provides the common output boundary. [A-NATIONALGRID-2023]

Fixed sensors, drones, wheeled sensor carts and technicians can be substitutes or complements. A quadruped's mobility only creates value if its payload produces decision-quality data, review distinguishes true faults, and maintenance follows. Repeated missions do not establish lower payroll or avoided outages. Economics include sensor calibration, site mapping, human review, false positives/negatives, support and qualification. A drone failing an inspection-image requirement may be physically capable of flying yet economically unable to deliver the required product; the same distinction applies to ground robots.

Utilities retain asset/security authority; an integrator may assemble payloads, maps and workflows. A new inspection service entrant may compete for contracts while being unable to own or control regulated assets. Scaling therefore needs a service-access and qualification mechanism, not only abundant robots. Repeated routes can improve anomaly baselines; transfer across asset types, failure distributions or into physical repair is unproven.

## Conditional waterfalls and portfolio implications

Each structured cell contains the same logical waterfall: relevant accepted-work population → technical serviceability → full-system economic attractiveness → organizational readiness → deliverable capacity → retained productive output. **All numerical gate values remain null** because a representative matched population and the joint distribution of conditions are not known. This is a substantive limitation, not a zero-adoption result. It preserves the evidence needed to replace a gate later rather than manufacturing percentages now.

The denominator stays the cell's accepted-work unit. Sites, installed robots, robot-hours, observer-hours, human paid hours and currency require separate conversion panels with measured coefficients. A component placed, tote moved or delivery completed is an activity observation. It becomes a productivity result only with matched accepted quality, exposure, human effort and baseline; it becomes savings only if costs are actually avoided.

The supported sequence differs by cell. Cleaning and routine logistics already have specialized operating precedents. Automotive humanoid work has bounded production-linked exposure. Hospital logistics has an explicit urgency boundary. Construction shows digital/site prerequisites. Utility trials require the observation-to-action chain to be established. These are qualitative differences in evidence and mechanism, not a numerical attractiveness ranking.

For a humanoid company, a sensible initial portfolio test allocates scarce integration engineers, local support, qualified hardware and customer ramp funding across **specific cohorts**. Standardized configurations can improve repeatability but also face cheaper fixed alternatives. Higher wages increase potential avoided cost only when staffing can change; healthcare illustrates why wage comparisons alone mislead. A customer with strong capital access but fragmented operating authority can remain less ready than a smaller customer with a simpler process. RaaS changes cash timing and risk allocation while consuming provider capital and support.

Conditional horizons of 0–2, 3–5 and 6–10 years are stored as requirement changes, not extrapolated adoption dates. In the first interval, validate bounded repeated output, assistance and whole-system costs. Expansion requires observed transfer, support capacity and customer demand. Enterprise redesign remains a scenario whose advantage must survive digitally improved human operations, specialized automation and same-technology incumbent response. Announcing a new robot does not upgrade existing fleets or remove qualification lead time.

## What would most change the decision

The highest-priority quantitative gap is matched **accepted output plus total human effort**, rather than another large deployment announcement. For automotive and totes, seek accepted throughput, downtime, intervention duration and complete configured-cell cost. For cleaning, seek inspected area, residual/manual work, support/setup and contract retention at US sites. For hospital delivery, seek urgency-specific SLA performance and post-integration support labor. For construction, seek entire work-package effort, mobilization and utilization across projects. For inspection, link sensor-quality pass rates to actionable findings and completed maintenance.

A separate denominator program should join industry/occupation data to representative hours and measured work shares, retain self-employed and outsourced activity, and validate allocation weights. Until then, the atlas supports a broad map and conditional deployment judgment but cannot support national physical-hour penetration, robot TAM or a market-size-ranked rollout forecast.

Public evidence can improve source coverage, configurations, contract terms and reported outcomes. Several decisive questions require field records: staffing actually avoided, intervention duration, failed attempts, accepted quality, site utilization, commercial pricing and customer-specific authority. Missing access is recorded as a research dependency, not inferred unfavorable economics. The saved questions `A-Q-HOURS`, `A-Q-HOSPITAL-SLA`, `A-Q-CLEANING-CONTRACT` and `A-Q-PORTFOLIO` define bounded next actions and completion criteria.
