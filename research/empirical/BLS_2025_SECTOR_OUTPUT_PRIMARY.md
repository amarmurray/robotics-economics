# Original BLS 2025 major-sector gross output: verified primary-source context (NOT an I–O requirements matrix)

**Retrieved/checked 22 November 2026.** Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, *Table 2.2. Output by major industry sector, 2025 and projected 2035*, page last modified **1 September 2026**, https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/output-by-major-industry-sector.htm . Values are **billions of chained 2017 dollars**; 2025 is the base year for the 2025–2035 BLS projections and 2035 is **projected**, not realized. The underlying linked [Industry Output and Employment](https://www.bls.gov/emp/data/industry-out-and-emp.htm) page was updated 27 August 2026. Industry categories use the BLS 2022 NAICS major-sector designations below. This is an actual, primary-source **macro production context** slice, not actual robot deployments, replacement wages, interindustry purchase coefficients or addressable robotic revenue.

Source records are preserved exactly as displayed in BLS HTML, excluding unrelated sectors. **BLS explicitly cautions that chained subcategories do not necessarily add to higher categories** because of chain-weighting; do not reconstruct a total by summing these. Publicly owned schools and healthcare are included under state/local government rather than the private sector rows. The 2025 table is economic output, not service-type qualified robotic demand. It cannot be multiplied by benchmark completion rates to obtain robot revenue. Numeric interindustry coefficient (input commodity by output industry), official BEA industry–commodity concordance and version-matched nominal value matrix remain **missing**. BLS's [interindustry data](https://www.bls.gov/emp/data/input-output-matrix.htm) includes 1997–2025 historical and 2035 projected matrices but its 16MB ZIP could not be retrieved via the available tools (binary unsupported / container DNS), so no alleged I–O edge was inserted.

## Where this fits Model A and Model B, without a false TAM

A future **qualifying service recipe** must state its actual original buyer process (not just NAICS), unit of accepted paid output, operator/robot/tooling resource demands, physical safety rights and counterfactual alternative. Model A's value table can attach those recipes to observed buyer offers and resource shadow prices. Model B then adds endogenous capacity investment, data generation, transfer to other recipes, multiple embodiments and induced demand across value chains. These BLS sector aggregates may help prioritize *where to seek defensible task-by-task service observations*, but they cannot identify penetration, willingness to pay, value capture or inter-industry spillovers without further BEA/firm/buyer evidence. No robotics market-size estimate is produced.
