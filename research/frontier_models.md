# Frontier robotics models: where intelligence becomes an operating input

Current extension: [the regional source audit](regional_frontier_models.md) adds RLWRLD, Unitree, AgiBot and RynnBrain dossiers, artifact-specific rights, operator references and a verified source-arithmetic discrepancy. This chapter retains the first K evidence wave; the new packet now has eight dossiers and preserves all earlier unresolved boundaries.

Workstream K, source edition 0.6, as of 25 October 2026. The question is when a model supplier can create and retain value independently of the robot maker and the customer operating the service. The evidence supports several coexisting structures. It does not establish a universal model-lab margin, a general robot-policy winner or a calibrated cost advantage for one architecture.

Four contrasting dossiers examine Physical Intelligence, Skild, Figure and OpenVLA. A broader role map includes FieldAI, RLWRLD, Google DeepMind and NVIDIA, with Zebra recorded as a separate transaction counterparty. The [evidence packet](../data/frontier_model_economics.json) preserves fourteen primary-source claims, exact artifact pins where available, release/access/rights cards and a four-architecture comparison design. The [source summary](../model/results/frontier_model_summary.json) reproduces Zebra's disclosed transaction arithmetic. A separate [conditional architecture scenario](intelligence_architecture_scenarios.md) is now implemented; real target costs and qualification remain unestablished. Synthetic assumptions do not fill those evidence gaps.

Regional partner examples are not a measured installed footprint. An actor can occupy several rows of a value chain without creating several robots, customer payments or units of useful output. ## Physical Intelligence: buying model infrastructure while retaining the deployment problem

[Pi's October 13 beta announcement](https://www.pi.website/blog/api-beta) describes uploaded data, managed fine-tuning and hosted inference. Selected partners use the service to iterate from demonstrations and failures. The post also reports connectivity difficulties in some applications. It does not name an immutable latest hosted checkpoint or supply a complete price, hardware, service-level and data-rights contract. Public pi0/pi0.5 research lineage must therefore not be substituted for the unspecified beta model. [K-E001]

This structure could reduce the ML infrastructure a robot builder needs to own. The customer still has to specify useful work, collect appropriate examples, integrate its control stack, validate behavior and handle exceptions. The relevant economic comparison is the total cost and time to a repeatable service outcome. An elapsed iteration anecdote cannot establish that cost without failed attempts, demonstration labor, engineering work, inference, monitoring and recovery. A report of no person inside a machine's cab does not establish absence of remote supervision.

The public [openpi repository](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/tree/215abfb217dbac7d5f1273282331b9b1866c0479) is a separate route. Its pinned README offers checkpoint locators and robot examples, with GPU-memory guidance that differs across inference, LoRA and full fine-tuning. Its remote-serving examples move policy compute off the robot; they do not remove network latency, controller interfaces or robot-side qualification. The repository LICENSE is Apache-2.0, while effective rights for every external checkpoint, component dataset and hosted beta agreement were not independently resolved here. No model was downloaded or run. [K-E002]

An independent supplier's advantage depends on reusable adaptation across customers. If each new site still needs extensive custom engineering and support, the supplier may be selling a labor-intensive integration service despite a common model. If model improvements transfer reliably and contractual reuse is permitted, shared research and serving infrastructure could lower the next deployment's cost. Those are alternative hypotheses, not an automatic data-network premium. For a customer-side operating newco, access to such a service may reduce one startup burden while leaving acquisition, site operations, capital and accepted-output risk intact.

The next discriminating evidence is a versioned, repeated service comparison with the same hardware and workload: adaptation hours and failures, all-attempt inference cost, latency tails and fallback, supervision, update/rollback obligations and data/fine-tune rights. A beta access form is neither that evidence nor authorization to submit an application.

## Skild: model development with deployment and acquired distribution

Skild connects general model development to field deployment and integration. Its [S1 source](https://www.skild.ai/blogs/s1) describes a video prompt as task context without a task-time weight update. This is a meaningful alternative to collecting and post-training a specialist for every change. It does not eliminate pretraining, scene setup, qualification or ongoing human work. The publisher's approximate demonstration-equivalence crossing is interpolated, and the same discussion acknowledges that extensive post-training can exceed the one-prompt reference. [K-E003/004]

The headline unseen-task comparison needs its actual measurement boundary. The source describes an average cumulative **per-step** score and uses human interventions to recover rollouts, especially for its language-conditioned baseline. Its 66% and 9% figures are not the probability that a full production task finishes autonomously. Internal tasks, data filtering, compute and prompting conditions matter; no immutable public run manifest or full service-cost ledger was established. A study of data scale under stated controls does not yield a universal learning exponent. [K-E003]

The source's metadata says August 18, 2026, its timeline says August release, issuer promotion was dated August 25, and September accounts use inconsistent relative launch timing. This study preserves those dates and does not manufacture a single exact release date or checkpoint. The named S1 product, a particular experiment and a deployed configuration remain separate records. [K-E003/005/014]

The [September company account](https://skild.ai/blogs/skild-crosses-100m-arr) reports crossing $100 million ARR, $50 million recognized revenue since deployments began ten months earlier and 60+ paying customers. These have different denominators and cannot be added. Its examples also use different maturity descriptions: deploying, working towards deployment and piloting. They do not establish uniform production status, installed units, retention, support burden or margin. A supplier's repetition of the same claims is not an independent audit. [K-E005/014]

The acquisition has stronger transaction-specific evidence. [Zebra's quarterly filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/877212/000162828026034109/zbra-20260404.htm) dates completion to March 27, 2026; the joint announcement followed April 15. Disclosed consideration is $20 million: $9 million cash, $9 million equity-investment fair value and $2 million indemnification-escrow fair value. The filing reports $15 million of net assets sold and a $5 million seller gain. The common source-arithmetic helper reconciles those amounts without treating all consideration as cash or seller gain as Skild operating profit. [K-E006]

Acquired AMR and orchestration capabilities can add distribution, installed relationships, service experience and assets. They also add operating responsibilities. A joint model/deployment business must be evaluated by what it actually supplies, who owns hardware and data, who bears failures and support, and which cash flows belong to each function. Buying a robotics business does not itself establish productive model integration or permission to train on every customer's data. The proposed feedback loop requires permitted capture, useful diversity, filtering, model improvement, compatible upgrades and a measured effect on service.

This route need not displace independent licensing or customer-side newco formation. A model-led provider may serve incumbent operators, support new entrants, acquire deployment assets and license to OEMs simultaneously. The economic record should retain those roles and their transfers rather than assigning the company to a single mutually exclusive category.

## Figure: integrated whole-body learning with a bounded transfer test

[Helix 2.5](https://www.figure.ai/news/helix-2-5-zero-shot-30-home-generalization), dated September 17, reports three behaviors in 30 unseen Bay Area homes. The behaviors were specified by training data elsewhere. Each behavior used a fixed checkpoint across the evaluation homes; this is not evidence that one unchanged checkpoint learned arbitrary new tasks on arrival. Zero-shot refers to the held-out homes and objects. The exact hardware revision and executable artifacts were not established from the inspected article. [K-E007]

The publisher reports 56% versus 9% whole-task success for Index-pretrained and scratch policies under its stated controlled comparison. Safety intervention aborts and fails a rollout, and task rubrics include time limits and completion conditions. This differs materially from S1's per-step study with recovery interventions. Both sources can inform adaptation and evaluation questions, but their percentages cannot rank models or become a common robot-uptime input. [K-E003/007]

The separate scaling claim concerns action-prediction loss over four nested data subsets spanning an eightfold range, with model size and downstream training held fixed. It does not demonstrate a general law for accepted service, human support or manufacturing cost. Figure's reported compute commitment is also an input claim, not proof of expenditure, delivered capacity or deployable robots. [K-E008]

The integrated OEM can co-design embodiment, sensing, low-level control, policy and data collection. That may reduce interface friction and make failures easier to diagnose across the system. It can also restrict portability and require customers to depend on one hardware/software update path. These economic consequences require actual compatibility, servicing and contract evidence. Household evaluation does not establish an industrial assembly case, just as a factory demonstration would not settle household service economics.

The existing Figure 02/03 system records remain related OEM context. They are not silently relabeled as the exact Helix 2.5 evaluation configuration. For Workstream I, model progress therefore joins a specified body and control stack only when the source establishes that link. For F/G, co-design and control rights can affect adoption and value capture without proving a universal preference for vertical integration.

## OpenVLA: an inspectable outside option with adaptation and rights obligations

[Pinned OpenVLA code](https://github.com/openvla/openvla/tree/c8f03f48af692657d3060c19588038c7220e9af9) offers a serving interface and robot/dataset-specific action processing. The [pinned model card](https://huggingface.co/openvla/openvla-7b/blob/47a0ec7fc4ec123775a391911046cf33cf9ed83f/README.md) describes normalized end-effector actions and limits zero-shot use to represented embodiment/domain combinations. New setups require adaptation. A published interface is inspectable and useful; it is not a commissioned controller, safety system or enterprise workflow. [K-E009/010]

The rights record contains a real unresolved conflict. The model card labels checkpoints MIT and identifies Llama-2 ancestry; the repository README warns that model artifacts may inherit Llama Community License conditions. The code-layer MIT license is recorded separately. This research does not resolve effective model rights by selecting the more permissive statement. Dataset components, third-party dependencies and deployment contracts remain distinct. [K-E009/010]

Accessible artifacts can strengthen a builder's outside option, reducing dependence on one supplier if performance and rights are adequate. The relevant cost includes data conversion, coordinate and normalization work, fine-tuning, inference hardware or hosting, validation, integration, support and maintenance. A zero assumed license payment would not make those resources free. Equally, a paid model fee does not establish that the supplier consumes resources equal to the fee; part of the payment may redistribute surplus.

OpenVLA is a research/adaptation comparator, not a claim to be the newest or most capable 2026 policy. Its simulation and physical examples remain within their own versions and environments. We inspected code and metadata, not a trained workload or a physical deployment. The contribution to the program is a concrete alternative architecture with explicit work and rights requirements, rather than an automatic commodity-price assumption.

## Model progress, adaptation and productive service are different comparisons

| Source observation | Held-out or changed dimension | Performance boundary | Permitted economic use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pi beta examples | Partner applications, hardware and iterative adaptation | Selected issuer examples; full repeated outcome/support denominator unavailable | Identify infrastructure, iteration, network and contract questions |
| S1 internal study | Seen/unseen tasks and video-versus-language conditioning | Cumulative per-step scoring with recovery interventions | Test whether prompting can reduce repeated specialization work; require a full-service follow-up |
| Helix 2.5 home study | Homes and objects, with behaviors trained elsewhere | Publisher whole-task grading; safety intervention fails trial | Examine transfer and OEM co-design at the stated task/environment boundary |
| OpenVLA artifacts | Represented versus new robot/domain combinations | Code/model-card contract and bounded examples, not a local executed result | Define adaptation, configuration, dependency and outside-option requirements |

These observations cannot be compressed into one capability score. A model release is not a supported fleet, a fast adaptation anecdote is not a qualified rollout, and a successful clip does not supply the failure distribution. The [physical-requirements crosswalk](physical_requirements_crosswalk.md) adds posture, manipulation and population boundaries, but none of these sources supplies a robot-addressable employment coefficient.

## Four architectures at one matched-service boundary

Use the existing manufacturing material-presentation cell as a supporting comparison: a correct component reaches the designated assembly interface within the required timing and quality window. Keep workload, accepted output, fallback, digitally improved human work and specialized-machine alternatives consistent. This choice does not make assembly or warehousing the program's organizing entry gate.

| Architecture | Required economic boundary | Critical unresolved evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Integrated OEM policy and robot | Model, body, controller, integration and service resources; internal charges consolidated | Actual configured service performance, update/repair obligations and outside-option cost |
| Independent licensed policy | Same or justified matched robot plus adaptation/inference terms; model payments separated from resource consumption | Compatibility, fees, data/fine-tune rights, network/fallback and all support |
| Open policy with adaptation | Code/weight/data rights, engineering, training/serving, qualification and ongoing ownership of failures | Effective rights, target adaptation and maintenance cost; no free-turnkey assumption |
| Model-led deployment provider/operator | Workflow and field responsibility, assets/capital, customer receipts and internal model transfers | Contract scope, service exposure, title/control/data rights, cash timing and retained margin |

No inspected source qualifies all four on the same robot and production service. Actual unit costs and negotiated payments remain unknown. The [four observed records](../data/frontier_model_economics.json) therefore retain unknown real costs. The separate synthetic calculator distinguishes **net resource expense** from **payments between parties**, preserves common output and all human work, and keeps compatibility gates explicit. It does not treat a model license fee as both supplier revenue and an additional consolidated resource cost.

A useful conditional test is how much adaptation and support must be avoided to cover incremental model/inference/validation resources, followed separately by the price interval that allocates gains among the customer, model supplier, OEM and integrator. For an AI-native operating newco, repeat the comparison with an incumbent having equal technology access. Earlier adoption, process redesign, capital and customer acquisition are different possible advantages; a model-access label is not one by itself. The dynamic treasury and entrant models remain separate until their shared capacity and cash are actually reconciled.

## Changes to the existing research questions

1. **B/H: evaluate the metric before transferring a capability claim.** S1 and Helix show why task novelty, environment novelty, recovery intervention, grading and horizon must remain separate. Their headline percentages cannot calibrate the annual model's capability or uptime.
2. **D: data possession is insufficient for a learning network.** Hosted APIs and deployment-led labs create possible feedback routes. Reuse rights, marginal diversity, correction/quality work, validation and deployment of compatible updates still determine whether the route produces useful learning.
3. **E/I/J: place control and compute explicitly.** Pi's hosted route, FieldAI's claimed local stack, DeepMind's control/reasoning split and OpenVLA's remote-serving interface represent different interface and failure obligations. None makes datacenter GPUs interchangeable with onboard memory, or one robot body's dynamics automatically portable to another.
4. **F/G: allocate operating responsibilities and transfers before discussing margins.** Skild's acquisition adds a concrete model/deployment integration pathway. Seller consideration, company ARR, customer savings and model-supplier margins are separate quantities. Pure newco, acquisition and hybrid customer pathways remain available without collapsing them into provider categories.
5. **Open ecosystems: compare effective options, not license labels.** OpenVLA's conflicting declarations and openpi's separate code/checkpoint/API layers show why the artifact and dependency chain must be inspected before claiming commercial substitutability or commoditization.

The next question is now concrete: for a fixed service and qualified configuration, when does a licensed policy's reduction in adaptation and support outweigh the extra resources it requires, and what price/rights allocation leaves both supplier and customer better off? The evidence packet constrains that question. The [conditional quantitative architecture model](intelligence_architecture_scenarios.md) supplies an inspectable mechanism. Broader regional discovery, complete field economics and integrated monthly commercial finance remain continuing work.
