# Initial deployment thesis register

Version 0.1. These are bounded claims and hypotheses for Amar to challenge after the first verified edition, following [POST_RESEARCH_STAGING.md](POST_RESEARCH_STAGING.md). They are not investment recommendations, universal laws or a completed research program. Source findings, synthetic mechanisms and target hypotheses remain distinct. Use the [walkthrough](LEARNING_WALKTHROUGH.md) to refine them. T1 and T6 begin as structural constraints; turn them into target-specific hypotheses with a declared cohort, outcome and decision threshold before treating them as empirical theses.

## T1 — Accepted service is a stricter boundary than successful robot motion

**Mechanism and status.** Navigation or manipulation can succeed while measurement quality, handoff, timeliness or recovery prevents an accepted customer service. This boundary is supported by specific field cases; its prevalence and economic magnitude across markets are not estimated.

**Evidence and scope.** PG&E flight/imagery findings, TenneT instrument visibility and door constraints, and hospital handling/support observations in the [field dossiers](research/physical_supply_field.md), [TenneT follow-up](research/quadruped_operator_followup.md) and [hospital follow-up](research/field_support_denominators.md).

**Strongest objection.** Even partial automation may create valuable monitoring or reduce part of a human route; failure to remove an entire visit does not imply zero value. More capable configurations may close the observed gaps.

**Decision implication.** Specify accepted output, all residual work and the alternative process before pricing a deployment. Separate additional monitoring from avoided labor or maintenance losses.

**What changes the view.** A matched operating cohort demonstrating complete service quality and timing with lower total support and lifecycle cost, including excluded tasks and exceptions. For a target service, stop the public-source branch if only mission totals remain available; preserve unknown economics rather than extrapolate.

## T2 — The relevant human-led alternative improves alongside robotics

**Mechanism and status.** Digital improvement can reduce the incremental savings available to a robot when both address overlapping work; it can also complement the robot. This is an accounting mechanism demonstrated by synthetic contrasts, not a measured industry effect.

**Evidence and scope.** Dated digital adoption studies and bounded transfer tests in [the H dossier](research/digital_ecosystem.md); the common engine's four-way matched-output comparison.

**Strongest objection.** Digital progress can unlock new robotic services or coordination gains that exceed the improvement in human-led work. A static overlap assumption could understate that complementarity.

**Decision implication.** Compare future robotic service with a feasible digital-improved incumbent, retaining required embedded software consistently. Do not freeze the baseline or add overlapping savings twice.

**What changes the view.** Target-side evidence for the separate human and robot channels, with matched output/quality and costs. A time-evolving digital comparator and measured integration effects are still missing; source benchmark gains cannot fill them automatically.

## T3 — Equal robot access leaves commercial execution and cash survival unresolved

**Mechanism and status.** Common technology does not confer equal customer access, process authority, contract durability or working capital. An entrant's temporary adoption lead can disappear under a prompt incumbent response. Evidence supports these mechanisms; the modeled funding effect is synthetic.

**Evidence and scope.** GXO's incumbent deployment and contract disclosures, facilities-contract cancellation mechanisms, BrainOS rights, and the [customer/value dossier](research/customer_value.md). The same-robot entrant comparison isolates conditional price/demand response.

**Strongest objection.** An entrant may genuinely redesign production, secure underserved demand or have superior implementation capacity; the incumbent may lack authority or willingness despite equal technical access.

**Decision implication.** Separate redesign advantage, adoption timing and capacity availability. Test ramp, receivables, retention and prompt competition before interpreting lower robot cost as a financeable newco.

**What changes the view.** Repeated customer commitments, accepted service and collections under comparable technology access, together with funded supply/commissioning. Full shared-resource treasury integration is a next model step, not already established by the entrant reference.

## T4 — Coordination value depends on execution and transaction semantics

**Mechanism and status.** A planner's proposed task, API acceptance, assignment and physical completion are distinct states. Retries and cancellation can leave partial effects requiring reconciliation. Pinned source code supports these interface boundaries; no LLM uplift or observed incident rate is inferred.

**Evidence and scope.** [Coordination reproducibility](research/coordination_reproducibility.md), including Open-RMF request IDs, callbacks and cancellation behavior. Benchmark coordination results retain their own harness and domain limits.

**Strongest objection.** A target installation may already supply durable identity, authorization and reconciliation outside the inspected components. Additional LLM orchestration may be unnecessary, or beneficial only in a narrow exception path.

**Decision implication.** Establish a deterministic baseline and one business-state contract before claiming an end-to-end physical-digital system. Budget failed attempts, human recovery and elapsed time as distinct inputs.

**What changes the view.** A compatible local software fixture showing improved modeled accepted throughput or recovery cost under declared assumptions, while preserving state under duplicate requests, timeout-after-acceptance, restart and partial cancellation. That verifies software/model behavior; physical throughput or cost claims still require target operating evidence. Source pins alone do not establish installed-system correctness.

## T5 — Lower collection-stage cost is not necessarily cheaper validated capability

**Mechanism and status.** Shared compute or portable human capture can lower an input burden while curation, robot grounding, recovery coverage and physical validation remain. The source papers establish bounded stage improvements and target limitations; complete cost shares remain unknown.

**Evidence and scope.** Cobalt and DexWild in [learning-data economics](research/learning_data_economics.md). Simulation cloud charges, human collection rates, dataset sizes and within-batch sampling ratios are separate measurements.

**Strongest objection.** When collection is the dominant bottleneck, a stage improvement can materially lower whole-program cost or unlock useful diversity. The current public evidence does not identify the relevant cost shares for a commercial target.

**Decision implication.** Compare total incremental cost to a fixed held-out capability/quality target, not merely dollars per recorded trajectory or raw robot-hours. Preserve data rights and failed/rejected collection.

**What changes the view.** A complete collection-processing-training-validation ledger and matched policy evaluations across alternate data mixes, including recovery behavior. A larger dataset or faster collection alone is insufficient.

## T6 — Positive unit economics does not guarantee funded deployment

**Mechanism and status.** Payment timing, restrictions, rejected production, receivables and shared resource reservations can block a profitable order. This is a finance/accounting proposition with a reviewed implementation proposal and exact test witnesses, not a measured robot-provider financing constraint.

**Evidence and scope.** Current model limitations and [the treasury extension proposal](model/treasury_extension_design.md), with an [independent design challenge](reviews/treasury_design_review.md). Actual commercial credit terms are not supplied by these synthetic witnesses.

**Strongest objection.** Deposits, supplier credit, asset finance or patient equity may bridge the gap; the assumed manufacturing-payment boundary can dominate the result. Their existence and terms must be explicit, not assumed free.

**Decision implication.** Keep economic feasibility, contractual eligibility, liquidity survival and physical deliverability separate. A lease reallocates funding and risk; it does not remove manufacturing expenditure.

**What changes the view.** A unified dated ledger that passes the stated adverse witnesses, followed by target contracts and observed financing terms. The current separate monthly entrant and annual fleet cannot yet establish joint financeability.

## How this register should evolve

For each thesis, record whether new evidence narrows, supports or contradicts the claim and which deployment decision changes. Prefer a disconfirming observation over another favorable anecdote. Retire a thesis that becomes tautological or cannot distinguish choices; split one whose evidence applies to different populations. Practitioner questions can be prepared from these gaps, but contacting people remains a separate user-authorized step.
