# Initial deployment thesis register

Version 0.14. The active campaign tests fourteen bounded challenge families through service, finance, calendar, architecture, coordination and entry witnesses; empirical acceptance remains partial. These are bounded claims and hypotheses for Amar to challenge after the first verified edition, following [POST_RESEARCH_STAGING.md](POST_RESEARCH_STAGING.md). They are not investment recommendations, universal laws or a completed research program. Source findings, synthetic mechanisms and target hypotheses remain distinct. Use the [walkthrough](LEARNING_WALKTHROUGH.md) to refine them. T1 and T6 begin as structural constraints; turn them into target-specific hypotheses with a declared cohort, outcome and decision threshold before treating them as empirical theses.

## T1 — Accepted service is a stricter boundary than successful robot motion

**Mechanism and status.** Navigation or manipulation can succeed while measurement quality, handoff, timeliness or recovery prevents an accepted customer service. This boundary is supported by specific field cases; its prevalence and economic magnitude across markets are not estimated.

**Evidence and scope.** PG&E flight/imagery findings, TenneT instrument visibility and door constraints, and hospital handling/support observations in the [field dossiers](research/physical_supply_field.md), [TenneT follow-up](research/quadruped_operator_followup.md) and [hospital follow-up](research/field_support_denominators.md).

**Strongest objection.** Even partial automation may create valuable monitoring or reduce part of a human route; failure to remove an entire visit does not imply zero value. More capable configurations may close the observed gaps.

**Decision implication.** Specify accepted output, all residual work and the alternative process before pricing a deployment. Separate additional monitoring from avoided labor or maintenance losses. The [utility service challenge](research/utility_inspection_decision.md) now demonstrates exact residual-point, review and contract switches alongside fixed sensing. It releases task time without automatically cancelling paid capacity; its numerical thresholds are synthetic.

**What changes the view.** A matched operating cohort demonstrating complete service quality and timing with lower total support and lifecycle cost, including excluded tasks and exceptions. For a target service, stop the public-source branch if only mission totals remain available; preserve unknown economics rather than extrapolate.

## T2 — The relevant human-led alternative improves alongside robotics

**Mechanism and status.** Digital improvement can reduce the incremental savings available to a robot when both address overlapping work; it can also complement the robot. This is an accounting mechanism demonstrated by synthetic contrasts, not a measured industry effect.

**Evidence and scope.** Dated digital adoption studies and bounded transfer tests in [the H dossier](research/digital_ecosystem.md); the common engine's four-way matched-output comparison.

**Strongest objection.** Digital progress can unlock new robotic services or coordination gains that exceed the improvement in human-led work. A static overlap assumption could understate that complementarity.

**Decision implication.** Compare future robotic service with a feasible digital-improved incumbent, retaining required embedded software consistently. Do not freeze the baseline or add overlapping savings twice.

**What changes the view.** Target-side evidence for the separate human and robot channels, with matched output/quality and costs. [Annual paths now drive actual monthly cohorts](research/calendar_digital_execution.md) under an explicit synthetic cleaning-zone contract. Fixed staffing survives large task-time reductions, and paid upgrades compete with support. The selected funded-architecture bridge and bounded exact conventional courier comparator now execute; measured target effects and broader real-system qualification remain incomplete. Source benchmark gains cannot fill them automatically.

## T3 — Equal robot access leaves commercial execution and cash survival unresolved

**Mechanism and status.** Common technology does not confer equal customer access, process authority, contract durability or working capital. An entrant's temporary adoption lead can disappear under a prompt incumbent response. Evidence supports these mechanisms; the modeled funding effect is synthetic.

**Evidence and scope.** GXO's incumbent deployment and contract disclosures, facilities-contract cancellation mechanisms, BrainOS rights, and the [customer/value dossier](research/customer_value.md). The same-robot entrant comparison isolates conditional price/demand response.

**Strongest objection.** An entrant may genuinely redesign production, secure underserved demand or have superior implementation capacity; the incumbent may lack authority or willingness despite equal technical access.

**Decision implication.** Separate redesign advantage, adoption timing and capacity availability. Test ramp, receivables, retention and prompt competition before interpreting lower robot cost as a financeable newco. The [support/expansion challenge](research/support_expansion.md) shows that finite requests, current service, announced allocations and the payer’s own cash must also reconcile. Current-capacity caution can delay service, while an unrealized announcement strands a funded unit. A recurring-support reserve can protect old units without funding commissioning.

**What changes the view.** Repeated customer commitments, accepted service and collections under comparable technology access, together with funded supply/commissioning. The [funded renewal challenge](research/competitive_renewals.md) now derives bounded awards from current capacity, price, switching work and prior service. Prompt incumbent response and delayed receipts can erase a synthetic entry cash window; a contract lock can retain unserved customers. First cash crossing may reverse. These are conditional mechanisms, not measured market outcomes. The shared monthly enterprise case now links supported cohorts, customer assignments and Treasury. Dated debt/default/cure now changes delivery in that loop. The annual digital/calendar bridge now executes a matched cleaning-service case. The [funded intelligence-role comparison](research/monthly_intelligence_execution.md) now shows that equal aggregate capital can leave a particular operating entity unable to qualify purchased assets. The [funded share-transition extension](research/entry_transitions.md) now separates purchase consideration, inherited obligations, target working capital and hybrid growth. Price alone changes neither production nor target cash. Real target funding, valuations, transfer rights, asset-purchase/redeployment and measured process improvement remain incomplete.

## T4 — Coordination value depends on execution and transaction semantics

**Mechanism and status.** A planner's proposed task, API acceptance, assignment and physical completion are distinct states. Retries and cancellation can leave partial effects requiring reconciliation. Pinned source code supports these interface boundaries. The local fault fixture demonstrates conditional retry and reconciliation behavior under explicit mock assumptions; no LLM uplift or observed incident rate is inferred.

**Evidence and scope.** [Coordination reproducibility](research/coordination_reproducibility.md), including Open-RMF request IDs, callbacks and cancellation behavior. The [local transaction fixture](model/COORDINATION_TRANSACTION_FIXTURE.md) adds deterministic witnesses with an explicit authoritative lookup/finality assumption. It does not run RMF or verify an installed stack. Benchmark coordination results retain their own harness and domain limits.

**Strongest objection.** A target installation may already supply durable identity, authorization and reconciliation outside the inspected components. Additional LLM orchestration may be unnecessary, or beneficial only in a narrow exception path.

**Decision implication.** The [exact observed-queue comparator](research/conventional_coordination.md) now gives a conventional policy waiting, replanning and shared-resource accounting. Identical labels produce identical outcomes; an artificial proposal delay can miss mandatory service despite available routine throughput. Protect the complete service gate and budget failed attempts, recovery, retained staffing and elapsed time. No LLM performance is measured.

**What changes the view.** The current fixture preserves uncertainty and partial effects under constructed faults, but uses a deliberately naive baseline and unpriced mock lookup. A competent bounded scheduling comparator now exists, but the next empirical test still requires a compatible installation and its actual business-state contract. The exact toy queue cannot establish distributed authority, clinical equivalence or long-horizon investment optimality. Software/model behavior alone cannot establish physical throughput or cost; those claims still require target operating evidence. Source pins alone do not establish installed-system correctness.

## T5 — Lower collection-stage cost is not necessarily cheaper validated capability

**Mechanism and status.** Shared compute or portable human capture can lower an input burden while curation, robot grounding, recovery coverage and physical validation remain. The source papers establish bounded stage improvements and target limitations; complete cost shares remain unknown.

**Evidence and scope.** Cobalt and DexWild in [learning-data economics](research/learning_data_economics.md). Simulation cloud charges, human collection rates, dataset sizes and within-batch sampling ratios are separate measurements.

**Strongest objection.** When collection is the dominant bottleneck, a stage improvement can materially lower whole-program cost or unlock useful diversity. The current public evidence does not identify the relevant cost shares for a commercial target.

**Decision implication.** Compare total incremental cost to a fixed held-out capability/quality target, not merely dollars per recorded trajectory or raw robot-hours. Preserve data rights and failed/rejected collection.

**What changes the view.** A complete collection-processing-training-validation ledger and matched policy evaluations across alternate data mixes, including recovery behavior. A larger dataset or faster collection alone is insufficient. The [R&D qualification follow-up](research/learning_qualification.md) adds physical partial-credit and video-transfer boundaries, inherited grounding/compute, retained-cohort regression and supported-delivery gates. Undated reference workflows and self-assessment scores do not close target operating or supply evidence.

## T6 — Positive unit economics does not guarantee funded deployment

**Mechanism and status.** Payment timing, restrictions, rejected production, receivables and shared resource reservations can block a profitable order. This is a finance/accounting proposition with an isolated settlement core and exact synthetic test witnesses, not a measured robot-provider financing constraint.

**Evidence and scope.** Current model limitations and [the treasury extension proposal](model/treasury_extension_design.md), with an [independent design challenge](reviews/treasury_design_review.md) and [stage 1 financial core](model/TREASURY_CORE.md). The [shared monthly enterprise case](model/SHARED_ENTERPRISE.md) now links paid physical cohorts, commercial cash/title, customer assignment, staffing commitments and equity. [Dated financing](model/ENTERPRISE_FINANCE.md) now adds funded draws, interest, maturity, explicit default/cure, cash recovery and a reserve on declared later starts. One-cent support funding and horizon/maturity witnesses alter delivery without changing demand or creating cash. Annual digital levels now join monthly service and funded upgrades through an explicit unit/cohort/calendar mapping. Four intelligence architectures now pay resource bills and transfer fees in the shared monthly ledger. Asset recovery, autonomous investment and broader portfolio integration remain incomplete. Actual commercial credit terms are not supplied by these synthetic witnesses.

**Strongest objection.** Deposits, supplier credit, asset finance or patient equity may bridge the gap; the assumed manufacturing-payment boundary can dominate the result. Their existence and terms must be explicit, not assumed free.

**Decision implication.** Keep economic feasibility, contractual eligibility, liquidity survival and physical deliverability separate. A lease reallocates funding and risk; it does not remove manufacturing expenditure. Paying all matured debt can still leave support unfunded; moving maturity beyond the observed horizon retains the liability rather than establishing sustainable service.

**What changes the view.** A unified dated ledger that passes the stated adverse witnesses, followed by target contracts and observed financing terms. The monthly ledger now consumes annual digital paths without adding annual fleet stock. The selected four-architecture bridge is implemented with actual entity accounts; equal-resource controls show that labels alone change no productivity. Full annual fleet/component and broader competitive integration remain incomplete, and this synthetic service unit cannot establish target financeability across unlike services.

## T7 — A capability generation is not an indefinite supported asset

**Mechanism and status.** Retirement, incompatible upgrades and scarce technical support can shorten the useful life of a configured deployment even while successor capability improves. This is a testable lifecycle mechanism, not an estimated retirement rate.

**Evidence and scope.** Figure’s dated supplier account says most Figure 02 units have been decommissioned and describes a support-resource tradeoff. Existing BMW records separate the earlier loading deployment from Figure 03 sequencing. See CAP-E024 and [the automotive pack](research/deployment_entry_decisions.md). The [workcell replay](research/automotive_workcell_decision.md) now preserves old owned units while testing a separately paid and qualified replacement; an unqualified purchase adds no service and a late recovery cannot backfill a missed deadline. Customer terms, unit counts, salvage and actual service lives remain unknown.

**Strongest objection.** An integrated provider can fund replacement and protect customer service, and improving designs may justify retirement. A supplier’s development fleet need not have the same lifecycle as a contracted customer fleet.

**Decision implication.** Price the exact cohort’s support, upgrade, title and recovery obligations. Do not assign the successor’s capability to installed hardware or a positive terminal value without a declared policy and evidence/assumption basis.

**What changes the view.** A matched customer asset/support history and contract showing funded replacement, continued service and recovery value. The bounded synthetic support/replacement worlds now execute this challenge; actual contract terms, lifecycle costs and available qualified replacement supply remain missing. Fixed vision/buffering and fast retooling must also be tested before attributing an advantage to humanoid form.

## How this register should evolve

For each thesis, record whether new evidence narrows, supports or contradicts the claim and which deployment decision changes. Prefer a disconfirming observation over another favorable anecdote. Retire a thesis that becomes tautological or cannot distinguish choices; split one whose evidence applies to different populations. Practitioner questions can be prepared from these gaps, but contacting people remains a separate user-authorized step.
