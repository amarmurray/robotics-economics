## How to develop the working papers

Begin each central claim with the production decision, the institutional or technological feature that makes it economically distinct, and the evidence or argument for that feature. Name the payoff unit, the feasible alternative that also learns, and an observation that would favor a competing account. Keep a real deployment in view through the abstraction and return to its original service outcome after the derivation. Numerical economies can then show a sign, a threshold, or the consequence of a particular institution. The [paired manuscript openings and research design](papers/How_Deployment_Changes_Robot_Productivity.md) apply this approach to embodied capital, selected experience and the firm’s capacity to revise its own learning procedure.

The common model in [Learning and Capital in Robotic Production](papers/learning_and_capital/README.md) follows an installed machine into three distinct networks. Goods reach customers, usable methods reach compatible fleets, and reinvestable earnings reach whoever can commit the funds. A matrix counts the first additional installations induced by each source type. Its dated entries distinguish speed from extent, and the changing physical marginal products show where later investment reinforces or offsets the original technical gain.

**Central question.** When does the best current deployment lose to a deployment that changes future profitable and financeable opportunities?

**Linked questions.** (i) How do demand and contract form determine deployer cash versus customer value? (ii) Which directed learning and qualification relationships change the reachable opportunity set? (iii) Which payer can fund the complementary investment, and on what date? (iv) When do hardware design or coordination change these answers beyond ordinary expansion?

**Candidate contribution, not an established result.** Misalignment among production, learning-transfer and payment networks may change deployment order even at the same aggregate resources and engineering potential. The first example isolates that tension, but its reversal is familiar constrained-investment economics. A defensible paper requires an additional structure-specific result or a rigorous narrower/negative finding. Do not manufacture novelty by naming states after robots.

**Dependency map.** Customer output demand and best feasible alternative → contract surplus and collected cash → actor-specific investment feasibility → qualified configuration/capacity → accepted service → customer output/demand. A second directed path runs from deployment → eligible experience and rights → adaptation/validation → future capability and reachable service. Coordination and manufacturing affect those transitions through explicit costs, delays and shared constraints. This is a conceptual dependency network, not observed topology. A joint service requirement is a bundle/hyperedge when its components are complementary.

**Scope ladder.** A: static surplus, pricing and outside option. B: exact two-period cash/learning/access and investment. C: directed transfer and costly new/modified embodiment eligibility; introduce uncertainty only to study a real information decision. D: coordination, organization or competition only if a core result requires it. Do not call a central optimization decentralized competition. Distinguish a changing edge weight from an actually new reachable configuration/customer/link.

## Original prospectus and argument outline

The prospective paper studies deployment sequencing when initial service changes both future production possibilities and the ability of specific actors to fund them. A tractable service benchmark endogenizes customer output against a matched alternative. A two-period provider chooses a current deployment and a subsequent supported-capacity investment. The first exact example shows that a bridge loan changes the preferred deployment without changing its engineering potential, and that access or learning ablations remove the reversal. This is a diagnostic, not a demonstrated contribution beyond constrained dynamic investment. The next model must explain a consequence of directed transfer, complementary qualification, or contractual cash allocation that a generic option-value narrative would conceal. Empirical cases will restrict feasible edges and parameter bounds, not supply fictional calibrated returns.
