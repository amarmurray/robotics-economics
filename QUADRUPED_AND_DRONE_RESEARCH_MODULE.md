# Quadruped deployment and drone evidence

Version 1.0 · Prepared 25 October 2026 · Research specification; campaign not activated

## 1. Purpose and organizing structure

Workstream J establishes a substantial quadruped research program and uses drones as empirical comparators for selected mechanisms. Investigate quadrupeds as productive systems with their own markets, operating requirements, suppliers, capabilities, learning opportunities, and commercial arrangements. Their role is not limited to demonstrating a stepping stone toward humanoids.

Keep the primary unit **end market × value-chain stage × operating archetype × geography × time**. Within that unit, compare configured embodiments and production systems. A quadruped, humanoid, wheeled platform, aerial system, fixed installation, or human team is a candidate solution, not a separate labor market.

I owns the common supply-chain, component, manufacturability, production-ramp, and layered-openness framework. J owns quadruped deployment evidence, end-market fit, operating economics, and drone comparisons. Reuse the same actor, system, customer, supplier, rights, cohort, scenario, and evidence records. Do not create independent market totals or manufacturing forecasts for each module.

H retains its substantial digital-AI scope and approximately one-third effort default. Research genuinely shared with H or I is counted once. Drone comparisons supplement H's comparison method; they do not replace its required digital-ecosystem coverage or digital-to-robotics tests.

This document prepares research. It does not establish current profitability, calibrated forecasts, a completed deployment census, or an activated campaign. Execution remains public-source research, CPU analysis, and a local dashboard. No hardware purchase, field experiment, paid access, outreach, or training is initiated.

## 2. The quadruped research program

### End markets, outcomes, and operating configurations

Screen productive applications across the existing end-market map. Candidate areas include industrial and infrastructure operations, manufacturing, the built environment, utilities, extraction and field services, and other settings where mobility, sensing, physical access, or intervention matter. Treat this as a discovery map, not a predetermined attractiveness ranking.

For each relevant stage, identify what the customer buys or needs: an accepted inspection, reliable measurement, verified condition assessment, delivered item, completed physical intervention, or another defined service. Distinguish navigating a route, collecting data, producing usable information, and changing a downstream decision.

For inspection, trace the chain from route access through measurement quality, interpretation, review, and maintenance or operating response. A valid finding of no issue may be useful output. Distance traveled, images collected, and mission completion are supporting measures; they do not independently establish avoided labor, asset reliability, or economic value.

Represent the full configuration: locomotion, manipulation attachment, sensing payload, onboard compute and memory, energy and charging, communications, autonomy software, fleet/site interfaces, service arrangements, and necessary human roles. Adding a manipulator or different payload may change reach, stability, endurance, integration, and supported work; record the resulting system version rather than imputing capabilities from its chassis.

### Capability, autonomy, and repeated operation

Distinguish research demonstrations, customer trials, commissioning, repeated productive operation, expansion, and retirement. Record the actual observation window, route population, environment, workload, assistance, and exceptions. An order, announcement, or installed robot is not evidence of recurring autonomous production.

Describe autonomy by role: mapping, route planning, locomotion, measurement, manipulation, interpretation, exception recovery, and business-system updates. Record setup, remote intervention, onsite support, review, and downstream expert work. Avoid a binary autonomous/nonautonomous label that conceals how work is divided.

Investigate terrain, stairs, clearances, surface conditions, weather, visibility, communications, payload demands, charging, and operating access where material. Include mission failure, incomplete observations, false alarms, degraded data, recovery time, and unavailable periods. Evidence from one configuration or route should not silently expand to all sites.

Treat nonadoption and unsuccessful trials as research objects when evidence exists. Identify their reported causes and alternative explanations. Silence after a pilot does not establish failure, and continuation of a trial does not establish scaled operation.

### Learning, coordination, and expansion

Investigate how repeated missions generate informative experience, site knowledge, maintenance evidence, and opportunities for policy improvement. Separate useful data from recording volume. Track collection and reuse rights, compatible system versions, training/validation costs, and whether improvements remain local or transfer.

Study heterogeneous fleets and coordination with people, fixed sensors, wheeled robots, aerial systems, and enterprise software. Identify which platform supplies each part of the service and how handoffs affect reliability and labor. A complementary fleet must not be modeled as several substitutes each claiming the same removed work.

Connect deployment learning to I's manufacturing and lifecycle models without conflating them. Better navigation, more efficient integration, improved assembly yield, longer component life, and lower supplier prices are distinct mechanisms. Software or hardware improvements reach existing fleets only through explicit compatibility, upgrade, and validation assumptions.

### Customer and supplier economics

Build matched-service comparisons against the actual process and a plausible digital-improved human process. Include simpler alternatives where they could meet the requirement. A mobile robot should not be assumed preferable to fixed sensing, a wheeled platform, redesigned access, or a human inspection merely because it can traverse the site.

Account for chassis, payload, software, integration, mapping, infrastructure, charging, spares, maintenance, replacement, transport, connectivity, remote support, human review, and operating downtime. Keep purchase price, manufacturing cost, total deployed investment, recurring expense, and contract payments separate.

Report customer outcomes and provider/operator economics separately. Released time, removed payroll, additional coverage, faster response, and reduced exposure to hazardous work are different benefits. Quantified avoided failures require a defensible counterfactual; do not convert collected anomalies into assumed avoided losses.

Examine ownership, financing, service contracts, inspection-as-a-service, and customer organization through F and G. Investigate whether value rests in the platform, payload, integration, interpretation, operating access, or service network. Test repeatability and retention rather than assuming an initial deployment produces recurring profitable demand.

## 3. Drones as empirical comparators

Study drones through specific end-market services and mechanisms. Useful contrasts include inspection or surveying, delivery/logistics, and agricultural services. They differ in useful output, demand, operating conditions, capital, oversight, and customer economics; do not combine them into one maturity or profitability claim.

For each case, record the configured aircraft, payload, software, communications, operator, customer, service, operating location, period, and evidence status. Separate developmental tests, approvals or permissions, demonstrated operation, completed activity, customer outcomes, and financial performance.

Measure the complete service. A successful flight is not necessarily a completed delivery or adequate inspection; treated acreage is not a net-profit measure. Include loading, dispatch, charging, maintenance, supervision, data processing, travel, and recovery as relevant. Preserve weather, access, operating permissions, and service-area conditions when comparing cases.

Use drone evidence to test bounded hypotheses about:

- Component sourcing, standardization, production learning, repairability, and service capacity.
- Autonomy, operator workload, mission reliability, and remote supervision.
- Payload and software value relative to the platform.
- Customer qualification, repeat demand, geography, and operational integration.
- Open interfaces, proprietary systems, complementary suppliers, changing vertical scope, and value capture.

The drone player map should similarly separate aircraft, payloads/sensors, flight control, mission/data software, operating services, customer channels and infrastructure. Initial case-linked inquiries include Wing/Zipline through Walmart's delivery disclosure, DJI's Agras configuration through the Missouri ownership model, AgEagle/senseFly/MicaSense through the company filing, and mission software/integrators in operator deployments. Include [PX4](https://px4.io/) as a software ecosystem with its own governance and interfaces. Expand the universe from these seeds; an anonymized trial vendor must remain anonymized, and a discontinued business must not appear as a current operating supplier.

Apply H's analogy contract with separate source and target evidence. Flying and ground contact impose different physical requirements; aerial sensing and physical manipulation may produce different outcomes. Permission structures, communication, energy use, failure consequences, and site access may also differ. State which differences break a proposed transfer.

Do not copy a drone learning rate, adoption curve, operator ratio, market share, or profit margin into quadruped or humanoid scenarios without target-specific justification. Historical experience can identify mechanisms and failure modes while remaining an unsuitable numerical forecast.

Matched embodiment comparisons are appropriate only where the alternatives can deliver a defined common service. Otherwise compare complementary roles or bounded mechanisms. A drone that cannot collect the required indoor measurement is not a lower-cost substitute for that measurement merely because it is cheaper per flight.

## 4. Connected models and measurement

Each deeper dossier should join market demand, configured capability, operating workflow, customer structure, commercial terms, and deployment evidence. Use dated site and fleet cohorts rather than extrapolating a single successful mission.

Model a sequence from eligible work through serviceable configurations, qualified sites, commissioning, usable capacity, accepted output, and economic outcomes. Conditional gates must use matched populations. Preserve conversions among sites, assets, routes, missions, robot-hours, human-hours, accepted services, and currency.

Keep inventories and capacities distinct: equipment ordered, delivered, commissioned, active, available, and productively used. Relate accepted output to both usable capacity and served demand. Support staff, charging, spares, interpretation, and downstream action can constrain output even when locomotion works.

Use I's time-indexed component-to-qualified-production-to-commissioned-fleet scenario. J supplies deployment requirements, workload, ramp, utilization, retirement, and service obligations. Reserve components and support for existing fleets as well as new shipments. Shared inputs across quadrupeds, humanoids, and drones are fungible only when specifications and qualification support substitution.

The model should distinguish at least:

- A capability constraint with ample physical supply.
- A manufacturing or component constraint despite adequate technical capability.
- A commissioning, support, or customer-readiness constraint.
- A demand or value-capture constraint despite available useful capacity.

Report unit economics per accepted service or equivalent useful output, alongside resource measures. Show sensitivity to intervention, mission quality, payload configuration, utilization, service costs, integration, and contract terms. Missing observations remain unknown; model ranges do not transform assumptions into observed results.

## 6. First-week scope and acceptance

Preserve H's approximate one-third substantive-effort default. Allocate the remaining campaign attention across A–G, I, and J using decision relevance and evidence availability. Avoid additive universes: the following dossiers attach to the existing six deeper market-stage cells where applicable, rather than requiring six new market studies.

The authoritative acceptance contract is [WEEK_ONE_RESEARCH_SPEC.md](WEEK_ONE_RESEARCH_SPEC.md). Working first-edition minima are:

1. **Three quadruped deployment dossiers across at least two sectors**, including customer/operator evidence of repeated use and a documented limitation, constrained role, trial boundary, or nonadoption. Do not fabricate a failure where only limited disclosure exists.
2. **Three contrasting drone cases across at least two operating/business models**, including a material constraint or negative finding; prefer inspection, delivery/logistics, and agricultural services when evidence supports them. Separate observed activity, measured outcomes, modeled economics, and missing profitability evidence.
3. **Two matched embodiment comparisons**, coordinated with I and the existing market-stage analyses. Include human/digital-improved and simpler technological alternatives where relevant.
4. **Two actual-system component maps, humanoid and quadruped, owned by I** and referenced here. Missing supplier/BOM details remain explicit; this is not a second mapping requirement.
5. **One linked time-indexed supply-to-deployment scenario**, owned by the common engine, showing effects on productive capacity and economics. Mark uncalibrated assumptions.
6. **At least five layered artifact/version-specific openness cards**, shared with I rather than duplicated, with application-specific implications for integration, modification, supply, service, learning, and value capture.
7. **At least four explicit I/J transfer/nontransfer tests**, including a failed or materially limited candidate and spanning more than one of operating scale, supply/industrial structure, openness, coordination and learning. These do not replace H's six digital analogy tests; actual shared inputs or direct technical complementarity receive separate evidence links.

The report must give quadrupeds substantive treatment and explain what drone evidence adds, including negative or nontransfer findings. Identify at least one condition changing embodiment preference and one condition changing feasible deployment scale, or explain why evidence cannot establish either.

Validate common service boundaries, cohort identities, resource allocation, phase/status labels, units, shared rights, and double-counting. Verify that a pilot cannot appear as an established fleet and that a supplier's announced capacity cannot appear as qualified delivered systems.

At the deadline deliver the strongest supported edition with coverage and limitations, preserving open questions. No provider roster, source list, prototype interface, or elapsed idle time constitutes completed deployment research.

## 7. Continuing research questions

Register durable questions in the main roadmap rather than maintaining competing status ledgers:

- Which market-stage conditions favor quadrupeds, other ground systems, aerial systems, or heterogeneous teams?
- Which remaining human roles, interpretation steps, or exception burdens determine repeated operating economics?
- What converts inspection and sensing into customer value, and which party captures it?
- How do payload, compute, autonomy, service, and production improvements change end-market coverage over time?
- Where does fleet experience transfer across sites, versions, and embodiments, under which rights and validation requirements?
- When do open layers, interfaces, and modular components reduce integration or lifecycle costs, and when do missing complements offset those benefits?
- Which drone-industry mechanisms survive the ground-robotics comparison, and which fail?
- What explains deployment expansion, persistent pilots, abandonment, and changes in preferred embodiment?
