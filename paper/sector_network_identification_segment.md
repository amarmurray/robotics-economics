## Why the production-network backbone does not identify a robot deployment (working paper segment)

Input-output accounts describe monetary purchases by industries and commodities; robotic economic opportunities depend on complete qualified service, avoidable resources, alternative production, demand, pricing, and contracts. Those objects are not interchangeable. A raw BEA commodity-by-industry Use table also cannot be treated as a square industry coefficient matrix without an explicit supply/use transformation.

A counterexample makes the identification limitation precise. Two hypothetical economies have the same three-sector input-output transactions, outputs, value added, and labor costs. One assigns a larger share of manufacturing labor to a completely avoidable task; the other assigns that same task share to health services, with complementary labor absorbing the offset. At the same robot service fee, manufacturer adoption is profitable only in the first economy and health adoption only in the second. The aggregate production accounts are observationally identical. Therefore sector economic centrality is neither a measure of deployable robot capacity nor a ranking of obtainable provider value.

Once task-level savings and behavioral responses are measured, the production network can describe **conditional** propagation. In a properly reconciled fixed-coefficient symmetric system, a stipulated final-demand change transmits as \`(I−A)^{-1}Δy\`; customer-demand elasticity and price pass-through are separate inputs. The same final-demand shock can propagate differently when deployment enters different industries. It is a mistake to translate the resulting gross-output multiplier directly into robot revenue or welfare.

The empirical atlas consequently requires two joined but distinct structures: observed BEA aggregate commodity/industry flows and measured workflow–configuration–customer/financing relations, including legal learning links and acceptance. The exact synthetic proof and source-accounting boundaries are in \`research/sector_identification_2026_11_17/RESEARCH_NOTE.md\`. The current numeric BEA export is inaccessible; no observed industry edge has been filled with a hypothetical number. This limitation is a measurement gate, not a reason to abandon the analytical model.
