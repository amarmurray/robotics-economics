## Distinct learning channels and the deployment decision

A robotic firm's choice of where to deploy first cannot be inferred solely from current margins or the number of hours of experience collected. Manufacturing knowledge, field adaptation, and customer access are produced and appropriated by different actors. A manufacturing process improvement may lower the supplier's real unit cost without lowering the purchaser's invoice. A target-relevant field dataset may improve technical performance yet remain unavailable for a new use or leave the qualification bill unchanged. Customer adoption and commercial discovery can increase the profitable quantity or price of future output without creating hardware or control competence. Their relationship is therefore one of **typed, directed production, learning, rights and payment edges**, not a single fungible learning stock.

Consider a source deployment s yielding current net provider receipts m_s, and separate experience stocks X_s for manufacturing, L_s for validated field adaptation and C_s for customer access. At the next date, the provider decides whether to expand an already-qualified fleet or enter a new target configuration. The new unit's invoice is p0 minus an effective manufacturing pass-through rate kappa times supplier cost savings. The qualification expense is Q0 minus a permitted field-reuse fraction delta times target-relevant adaptation savings. The target customer's demand intercept is a0 plus an access-related increment zeta C_s, and the service provider captures a specified share of operating surplus over the customer's best feasible alternative. All obligations must be funded with the provider's dated collections and permitted financing. Internal invoice transfers disappear from a combined supplier-provider resource account, but the invoice paid at the investment date can still determine whether a feasible portfolio is chosen.

This structure yields two distinct thresholds in a tractable exact example. The hybrid source combines one unit of manufacturing process knowledge, one unit of target-useful field experience and two normalized units of customer access while producing less current cash than a factory source. A specialist entry becomes financially possible when kappa+delta is at least 5/3, yet the hybrid first deployment does not dominate cash-rich source plus incumbent expansion until kappa+delta exceeds 9/5. The interval between these thresholds therefore contains investable but inferior source sequences. With full learning rights, a separate destination-demand condition requires its base intercept to exceed 93/8 in the maintained capacity-binding comparison. Eliminating either price pass-through, field reuse or the access-related demand benefit reverses the preferred path. These are conditional synthetic comparisons rather than calibrated estimates, general claims of integration or a theorem unique to robotics.

One additional implication concerns organizational contracting. When manufacturer pass-through increases marginally beyond the private-choice boundary, an otherwise unchosen specialist order may become profitable for the deployer while the supplier retains a strictly positive margin. However, a complete supplier optimum also depends on incumbent G-unit sales, manufacturing fixed costs, other customers, integration and bargaining. Competent contracting can coordinate the gains across legal boundaries; different layers do not imply one integrated owner.

Public relationships are suggestive but currently nonidentifying. Jabil describes both manufacturing Apollo robots and testing them in its own factory. Apptronik reports real-world training data collection at Robot Park and customer sites. DHL documents commercial use and co-development of Boston Dynamics Stretch, with a planned expansion from unloading to case picking. These announcements do not provide a matched manufacturing invoice, source-to-target qualification cost, paid customer output and contractual data-rights panel. Any empirical claim must keep those missing coefficients explicit. The paper's prospective contribution is a theory and measurement of *which* link creates attainable future production value for *which* actor at *which* date, subject to existing dynamic investment and contracting prior art—not another observation that learning has option value.

**Next empirical test.** Compare a fixed acceptance-standard target service with and without source-specific qualified field data, observe total training, adaptation, validation, delivery and support costs, connect to current supplier real costs and invoices for matched build cohorts, and record customer willingness to pay, alternative production and dated budgets. Include rejected opportunities. Reconstructing those edges is a more discriminating test than comparing aggregate robot shipments or benchmark scores.
