## The option to establish service acceptance before adding capacity

An isolated robotic grasp is not itself a saleable customer service. Complete order execution additionally requires the correct SKU set, physical placement, downstream scanning or WMS acknowledgement, deadline compliance and any corrective work. A rational deployer may therefore purchase evidence about **complete-service acceptance** before committing scarce capital to a new workflow. This is analytically distinct from collecting data to improve a policy: the former changes beliefs about a fixed production possibility, while the latter changes the possibility itself.

Consider a known investment in an established unloading workflow, an alternative investment to qualify case picking, and a sequence of held-out target-order trials. The target may be in a high- or low-acceptance regime; both can agree with observed component-level success rates yet differ in correlated within-order failures. Trials produce binary complete-order outcomes but consume upfront cash. Conditional on a fixed regime they are independent; after observing their outcomes, the operator chooses the best still-financeable investment. Target value is derived from customer demand for accepted output, failed-attempt recovery, a credible customer outside production method, and provider surplus capture.

If target values are V_H>J>V_L, where J is the known incremental return from incumbent expansion, a financeable target investment is selected after evidence if the posterior high-state probability exceeds (J-V_L)/(V_H-V_L). This is the standard Bayesian value-of-information threshold. Unlike a free test, a paid trial may make the target qualification expense unaffordable. As a result, the gross expected value of sample information can be positive while the net value of conducting the financed experiment is negative. Perfect-contract alternatives and paid pilots can relax the restriction; this is not an inference in favor of common ownership.

In a transparent synthetic example, target complete-order success is either .95 or .85, and the incumbent investment yields net .30. The new service has high/low net values 1.16 and -.76 under the Q8 customer-demand specification. Without evidence the target's expected value is .20, so the incumbent is chosen. With total liquid budget 2.10 and a .01 cost per trial, exactly eight trials maximize expected net value over n=0,...,40; target entry is conditional on all eight orders being accepted. The optimized value .36085 exceeds the .30 no-test comparator. At budget 2.00, every paid test forecloses later target investment and the optimal test size is zero. Stronger destination demand or lower capturable surplus can also make validation irrelevant because the same action is optimal in both regimes.

RoboCasa's official evaluation offers a methodological precedent for recording full-task binary outcomes and target splits. DROID's curated physical demonstrations are a different measurement object, not paid full-order trials. Neither supplies the service-level failure dependence, validation resources, customer alternatives or contract and financing observations necessary to estimate this model for a warehouse. Staged financing of experiments is existing economic prior art (Nanda and Rhodes-Kropf, 2016). The paper's prospective contribution must arise from observed structure or a genuinely additional restriction—not from renaming Bayesian decision analysis as robotics theory.
