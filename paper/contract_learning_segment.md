## Contract denomination and learning-produced expansion (working segment, Nov. 17, 2026)

A robot's productive capability, the customer's purchased good and the provider's billable unit need not coincide. Consider a robot earning a fixed payment per productive hour, producing accepted output for a customer with a downward-sloping demand curve. Increased speed lowers the hourly fee per accepted unit, inducing customer output expansion, but it may reduce active hours and the provider's cash receipts. With source productivity s, hourly fee h, operating cost c/hour and customer revenue aq-bq²/2, interior provider margin is (h-c)(a-h/s)/(bs), whose derivative has the sign of 2h-as. Customer output rises in s even when provider margin falls.

If the provider next qualifies another configuration, the object generated by production matters. Successful accepted units may reduce downstream adaptation/qualification cost, while productive hours may support an entirely different training regime. With an output fee p, fixed customer quantity and per-*hour* transferable learning, a financed target project yields combined private value pq+P_B-k_B-F_0+(tau-c)q/s while the qualification-cost floor is slack. Whether the provider prefers speed or exposure depends on the marginal qualification saving tau relative to per-hour resource cost c. This conditional comparison may fail under a capacity or participation boundary, an incumbent investment with greater return, different rights, or flexible repricing. Retention of an old qualified policy preserves an available option, so the effect is not unconditional value destruction from technical improvement.

A small exact synthetic example yields the following opposed source choices: fixed productive-hour pricing chooses slower source service (net source plus subsequent investment value 56/5 rather than 15/4), whereas output pricing chooses faster source service (61/5 rather than 56/5). Under output pricing, redefining useful experience from accepted units to hours and increasing its assumed qualification effectiveness reverses the choice back toward slow service (56/5 rather than 51/5). These results establish *sensitivity to the denominators* of demand, billing and transfer, not empirical learning rates.

The observed workflow anchor is Formic's 2023 3D Machine case, reporting active-hour service, a complete machine-tending process, retained manual input loading, ~2,000 production hours and an improvement in capacity. It does not observe a before/after billing or qualification experiment. A separate Formic monthly trial offer illustrates contract heterogeneity, not renegotiation rights for the same site. The research contribution, if any, must come from further economics of coordinated physical qualification and rights, and will need joined data on accepted units, billable hours, support, target validation effort and cash.
