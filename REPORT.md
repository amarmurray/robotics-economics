## 1. Executive argument and strategic implications

Authoritative chapter: [research/integrated_decisions.md](research/integrated_decisions.md).

This campaign asks where a humanoid company should deploy, what must improve, and which organizational and commercial arrangements can sustain useful production. The early evidence supports a conditional strategy organized around customer services and their bottlenecks. It does not yet support a calibrated economy-wide penetration forecast or a claim that one morphology will replace a fixed share of physical labor.

The US labor atlas establishes a broad economic frame. The six deeper cells are automotive assembly/material presentation, third-party logistics handoffs, contract floor cleaning, hospital internal logistics, construction installation and utility inspection. These are deliberately contrasting production systems. They differ in access, quality standards, process authority, environmental variation, customer relationships and the alternatives already available. Cases outside the United States inform mechanisms but do not become US labor observations.

A production system is valuable when it delivers the required service, at the required quality and time, within the available resources and customer demand. Robot capability is one part of that system. A successful route, attractive demonstration, larger fleet, financing announcement or open repository can be relevant evidence without establishing the completed service or its economics. The dossier chapters retain those distinctions so that a commercial decision can be traced back to its actual evidentiary basis.

### A deployment portfolio, rather than a generic robot market

The six cells suggest different bounded entry questions. For automotive assembly, the question is whether a configured system can reliably complete a defined sequence within the production process, and how that compares with fixed or specialized automation. Public pilot evidence can help establish the workflow; it does not disclose a plant-wide labor substitution rate. For contract logistics, the end customer, site operator and robotics supplier may be different organizations. A commercial RaaS agreement is important evidence of an arrangement, while undisclosed price, support and intervention terms still prevent a complete investment case.

Floor cleaning illustrates why simpler embodiments and contract duration matter. The useful outcome is an accepted cleaned area under the site's service standard. A specialized machine can be a stronger competitor than a more general robot. A facilities-service contract may end sooner than the robot asset's economic life, creating utilization and residual-value risks even if a steady-state task comparison looks attractive. Transferring the asset to a service provider reallocates those risks; it does not remove them from the production system.

Hospital logistics tests the difference between a bounded delivery service and an occupation. Useful nonurgent transport can coexist with limited ability to perform time-sensitive or complex nursing workflows. Doors, elevators, handoffs and support work can decide the value of a mobile system. The public evidence should therefore change the definition of addressable work before it changes a productivity coefficient.

Construction installation involves changing sites, access, sequencing and multiple contractors. A bounded technical demonstration cannot establish a general site deployment rate. The next question is which workflow can be repeated with an explicit acceptance standard and sufficient authority over the surrounding process. Utility inspection similarly requires more than access: measurements must be usable, reviewed and linked to an appropriate decision or action. Fixed sensing, wheeled platforms, quadrupeds, drones and human teams may substitute for one part of the chain or complement different parts.

These distinctions prevent a false aggregation. A robot supplier's potential sales, a contractor's labor bill, the customer's value added and the final product's revenue are not additive measures of the same opportunity. The atlas identifies employment coverage and residuals; it does not turn headcounts into physical-work hours by assuming a universal time share. Competing systems share the same underlying work, and service providers can appear in several value chains without creating several labor pools.

### Capability progress changes a constrained production function

The capability evidence makes hardware generation, policy version, task distribution, assistance and observation period explicit. The relevant improvement is a change in accepted output, intervention, adaptation or cost under comparable conditions. Neither a growing demonstration dataset nor a benchmark score establishes a general improvement exponent across markets. Transfer can be useful within a tested setting and fail in another, including when a larger mixed dataset performs worse on a particular target.

The modeling consequence is to keep base capability, directed learning, coordination, hardware qualification and customer readiness separately inspectable. A policy improvement may widen technical eligibility while leaving manufacturing, commissioning, support or demand as the binding constraint. A new generation does not automatically upgrade installed fleets. A replacement consumes supply without increasing net active stock. A component commitment is not a finished robot; a finished robot is not a commissioned customer service.

This is also why learning value needs a counterfactual. If useful experience changes future operating cash flows, its benefit belongs in the comparison of policies with and without that learning, including collection and validation costs. Adding a separate data premium would count the same gain again. Information that changes a later investment decision is another channel and should not be confused with a measured policy improvement.

### The digital counterfactual moves too

Workstream H materially changes the robotics comparison. Human-led and incumbent operations can improve through software, models, routing, planning and better information before or alongside a robot deployment. Comparing a future robot with a permanently frozen human baseline overstates the incremental case. The report therefore uses the four-way logic of current operations, additional digital improvement, additional robotics, and their combination, with matched output and quality.

A combined system can outperform the sum of isolated changes, underperform it, or merely count the same removed bottleneck twice. The common engine exposes a synthetic interaction rather than copying an effect size from a digital study. Evidence from customer support, software development, professional-task benchmarks, simulated enterprises and embodied control remains tied to its own population, system and evaluation conditions.

Coordination is similarly conditional. The updated peer-reviewed agent study narrows the earlier blog's claims and explicitly treats its capability threshold as a within-domain selection rule. That finding supports testing architectures against a strong simpler baseline. It does not prove a universal benefit from adding agents, and it does not transfer a numerical coefficient into a physical fleet. A digital planner, conventional scheduler, enterprise transaction system and robot controller also operate at different timescales and authority boundaries. Connecting them is an engineering and operating claim that requires more than citing each component separately.

### Customer formation and provider strategy are different decisions

A customer-side AI-native newco sells an end-market product or service. Its advantage could come from a different operating design, an earlier adoption window, or access to capacity when human labor is constrained. Those are distinct hypotheses. None follows simply from access to the same robot that an incumbent can also buy. The comparison must include a prompt-adopting incumbent, customer acquisition, ramp losses, working capital, demand that can actually be won and delivered, and the possibility of price or retention responses.

A holding company that creates operating businesses, one that acquires existing firms, a standalone entrant and a hybrid face different constraints. Acquisition can bring customers and operating access while importing process and contractual commitments. Greenfield formation can allow redesign while requiring new customers and complementary assets. Thrive's disclosed structure is a useful organizational comparator, but its financing and digital deployment claims do not establish the economics or financeability of a humanoid-enabled industrial entrant.

The provider decision is separate: sell equipment, finance assets, license software, integrate, manage operations, form partnerships or operate part of the customer's value chain. These choices move cash requirements, operating risks and control rights between actors. They do not create free capital or necessarily change total production value. The published BrainOS terms make a further distinction concrete: ownership of operational data is different from access, improvement-use rights and exclusivity. A customer cannot assume it retains an exclusive learning advantage merely because it owns the data.

### Qualified supply and supported operation limit scale

The physical and digital supply analyses converge on a practical rule: qualify the resource and the workflow before pooling a constraint. Embedded LPDDR memory and datacenter HBM have different configurations and applications. Actuator specifications, environmental qualification, spare compatibility and field support likewise cannot be replaced with an undifferentiated robot count. Product specifications establish an architecture; they do not disclose manufacturing cost, yield or available qualified capacity.

Supplier investment and customer commitments can support expansion but have different accounting meanings. Micron's take-or-pay arrangements illustrate how supply assurance and supplier financing can coexist with customer commitments and restricted uncommitted availability. TSMC's reported capital expenditure illustrates investment already made, without identifying a robot OEM's allocation. Deposits, expenditure, revenue and delivered output must remain separate. A supply backcast gives necessary conditions for a scale target; it does not establish demand or adoption.

For a humanoid company, these findings favor a sequence of testable service propositions with explicit alternatives, measurable acceptance, known process authority and visible support obligations. They do not establish a universal sector ranking. The next investment-worthy evidence is a matched cohort showing accepted output, all human support, retention, full costs and rights. Public disclosures often establish only some of those links. Where they stop, the report preserves a bounded conclusion and a precise next question rather than inventing a margin or penetration rate.

## 2. Economic atlas and deployment pathways

Authoritative chapter: [research/economic_atlas.md](research/economic_atlas.md).

Version 0.1.0 · Evidence retrieved 25 October 2026 · Input revision `campaign launch snapshot (see ignored sync_receipt.json)` · First substantive A/C edition, with unquantified gates retained.

The US employment evidence places much of potentially relevant physical work in large service industries as well as manufacturing and construction. It does **not** yet identify a national number of humanoid-serviceable hours. The most informative deployment evidence instead identifies bounded production systems: correctly placing a known component, transferring a standardized tote, cleaning a navigable floor, delivering a nonurgent hospital item, drilling a planned anchor hole, or collecting a qualified asset observation. Those systems have different economic denominators, alternatives, customer decision rights and bottlenecks.

The strategic implication is to choose deployment sequences using accepted output, avoidable staffing, integration burden and support demand. Employment size supplies context. It cannot rank profitable robot entry points without those additional measurements. The evidence also argues against treating humanoid deployment as a uniform replacement of physical occupations: mature specialized equipment, digital improvements to human workflows, and mixed systems are consequential competitors.

It contains 15 market families, six deeper cells across six markets, an occupation screen, source rows, explicit residuals, conditional waterfalls and unresolved questions. Table 2.1 reports **170.2808 million jobs**, including **160.4586 million wage-and-salary jobs** and **9.8223 million unincorporated self-employed jobs**. Components differ by 100 jobs because of published rounding. BLS explicitly defines the matrix as jobs, rather than individual people; a person can hold more than one job. We use the 2025 column only. The 2035 column is BLS's conditional projection, not an observed future and not a robotics forecast. [A-BLS-EP2025; A-BLS-DEFINITIONS]

The occupation lens is a separate accounting view. May 2025 OEWS reports **155,495,730 wage-and-salary jobs**, covering full- and part-time employment in its establishment-survey scope. It excludes most agriculture, the self-employed, private household employees and unpaid family workers. Its estimates combine six survey panels from November 2022 through May 2025 and use 2018 SOC/2022 NAICS. ### Six deeper market-stage cells

Each cell is anchored to US 2025 activity and includes evidence of other dates and, where specified, foreign comparator deployments. A stable cell ID does not make a foreign result US evidence. Industry employment below is a nonadditive context measure; none is the measured population of the specific workflow.

#### Automotive assembly: narrow production evidence, demanding integration

`CELL-M05-auto-assembly-US-2025` covers component presentation/loading into an automotive production process. The 2025 motor-vehicle-manufacturing context is **285,800 jobs**, encompassing much more work than the cell. The relevant denominator is **correctly placed components meeting takt and quality requirements**, per scheduled period. Inbound sequencing, loading, welding and inspection remain connected but distinct stages. [A-BLS-DETAIL2025]

BMW's February 2026 operator account reports that Figure 02 supported production of more than 30,000 X3 vehicles during a ten-month 2025 Spartanburg pilot, moving more than 90,000 components in approximately 1,250 operating hours. Its work was precise removal and positioning of sheet-metal parts for welding. This is meaningful production-linked evidence beyond a laboratory clip. It does not mean that a robot manufactured 30,000 cars or that the entire production line's value is attributable to it. Nor can the reported ten-hour-shift language be substituted for the separately reported operating exposure. [A-BMW-2026]

The choice is between configured cells: humanoid plus fixtures/control, conventional fixed robot, conveyor/fixture redesign, or AMR plus human handling. Shared IT, occupational safety, process management and shop-floor integration are operator-described prerequisites. Economics depend on accepted components, line stoppage risk, retained intervention staffing, scrap, integration downtime and alternatives' cost. Neither a defensible price interval nor a nationally serviceable share is available from the disclosed numbers.

An incumbent OEM has demand, product qualification and process authority. Acquisition changes control but creates no additional work pool. A greenfield operator can redesign a plant, yet must finance the product, supply chain and customer ramp as well as robots. With equal robot access, a claim that the entrant automatically wins is unsupported. The first stage is repeatable bounded loading; broader SKU and multi-step coverage requires verified recovery, fixture/software compatibility and validated support demand. Repeated geometry may support learning within that envelope; cross-market dexterity is an unvalidated transfer hypothesis.

#### Contract warehousing: the interface and contract are part of the machine

`CELL-M08-3pl-tote-US-2025` concerns tote handoff in a large warehouse already using AMRs and conveyors. Warehousing/storage employment is **1.8657 million jobs**, not a tote-handling labor count. The useful output is a correct tote transfer reaching the downstream process within its dispatch window. [A-BLS-DETAIL2025]

GXO's June 2024 primary announcement describes a multiyear RaaS agreement with Agility, following a late-2023 proof of concept. At its SPANX operation, Digit takes totes from mobile robots and places them on conveyors, orchestrated through Arc. The significance is an integrated bounded workflow and an operator-backed commercial arrangement. Fees, support staffing, accepted throughput, retained fleet exposure and cash savings are undisclosed. A contract is not an ROI measurement. [A-GXO-2024]

The meaningful counterfactual includes a fixed transfer station, altered conveyor geometry, or human/AMR combination. A humanoid can be attractive where brownfield geometry or product variation makes fixed redesign costly, but this remains conditional on actual throughput and support. Improved WMS scheduling might raise both human and robot productivity; it is not an extra benefit to add twice.

The readiness gate crosses several organizations: the logistics operator implements, the customer supplies demand and may control the site, and the provider supplies hardware/support. RaaS can move initial asset cash requirements but does not remove provider financing or service constraints. An operating newco needs contracted volume, customer acquisition and ramp cash. If an incumbent adopts the same Digit/Arc configuration promptly, any advantage must survive in operating execution, contracts or differentiated service. Common totes may create useful transfer; site interfaces and data rights can block it. The next stage should be earned by sustained peak-period output with observed exception queues, not by multiplying independently chosen eligibility percentages.

#### Facilities cleaning: the specialized baseline already moves

`CELL-M11-floor-cleaning-US-2025` covers outsourced floor cleaning. Facilities-support and building/dwelling-services employment totals **2.4741 million jobs**, including landscaping and other work that is not floor cleaning. The denominator should be **area passing specified cleanliness and coverage inspection within the cleaning window**, with total crew and support effort retained. [A-BLS-DETAIL2025]

ISS reports deploying more than 800 sweeping/vacuuming/scrubbing robots across 21 countries. Its March 2026 article says multiple UK customer facilities increased floor-cleaning efficiency by 30%, while humans inspect and perform additional work. It also describes sensor-based occupancy scheduling and a framework checking whether a robot is justified and suitable at a customer site, including privacy/data considerations. This is credible operator evidence of an existing specialized-machine market, with commercial interest in portraying success. The exact efficiency denominator, sample, comparison, labor disposition and US transfer are unknown. **Thirty percent efficiency is not thirty percent payroll removal.** [A-ISS-2026]

A full comparison includes manual walk-behind/ride-on equipment, autonomous machines, and sensor-informed human scheduling at matched quality. The machine's accessible floor area excludes edges, congested spaces or washrooms it cannot finish. Setup, refill, cleaning the cleaner, transport, exception response and manual follow-up can preserve staffing even when autonomous coverage expands.

Customer ownership mechanisms are concrete: a multinational contractor can share engineering and redeploy equipment, while the client retains site access and service requirements; a local contractor may have direct operating authority but less capital and support capacity. Neither label establishes the effect. A robotic-cleaning newco can purchase the same machines as incumbents. Route density, contract retention, implementation skill and customer offer must explain its advantage. Contractor contract duration should be matched to asset payback and redeployment economics; a lease addresses equipment funding, not acquisition costs or losses before route density develops. These mechanisms connect to the customer/value lane's fixed-technology comparisons.

#### Hospital logistics: observed deliveries expose a clinical-work boundary

`CELL-M10-hospital-logistics-US-2025` is a large multi-floor hospital's internal delivery system. Private hospital employment is **5.6538 million jobs**; the all-ownership hospital reference is **6.8184 million**, of which public employment is already in M14. Neither count is a delivery-work denominator. The outcome is **correct authorized delivery with chain of custody intact within the relevant urgency-class service limit**. [A-BLS-DETAIL2025]

ChristianaCare's nurse-authored 2026 account describes three cobots serving more than 80 patient areas, completing over 48,600 deliveries during a 2022–25 grant-funded pilot. The program tested EHR and elevator integration. It found operational value in high-volume nonurgent medication/equipment delivery, while concluding that the robots could not meaningfully augment complex and time-sensitive nursing workflows. Nurses infrequently initiated requests when urgency or informal coordination made delivery automation a poor fit. This is material negative evidence for broad clinical-labor substitution, alongside positive evidence for a narrower service. The $1.5 million grant is program funding, not robot acquisition price or a full operating-cost estimate. [A-HOSPITAL-2026]

A separate qualitative study observed 89 deliveries across 23 sessions and 33 observer-hours in November–December 2022. It reports 107 human interventions, including 72 for elevators. The category counts sum to 111, however, without explaining overlap or error. The headline and discrepancy are both preserved. This sample establishes early integration and assistance burdens; it cannot calibrate a current platform-wide intervention rate. Observer-hours are not interchangeable with robot operating hours, and durations per assist were not supplied. [A-HOSPITAL-2024]

Alternatives include pneumatic tubes, AGVs, carts/porters and digital request batching. Compare accepted on-time deliveries and **all** loading, unloading, intervention and coordination effort. Hospital governance distributes authority among nursing, pharmacy, facilities, IT and executives. Protected staff time and support matter even where capital is available. A service newco must work through those interfaces; delivery activity cannot establish removed nursing payroll. The next stage is reliable nonurgent coverage with measured labor and SLA performance, followed by separate evidence for tighter time requirements. Door/route learning can transfer within compatible sites; clinical prioritization is a different mechanism.

#### Construction: digital preparation can dominate a narrow robot's gain

`CELL-M04-construction-install-US-2025` covers MEP/interior overhead drilling on projects with coordinated plans and accessible interiors. Building-equipment contractors employ **2.6021 million wage-and-salary jobs**. The cell's denominator is **accepted holes at required coordinates, depth and diameter**, per work package; completed installation is a downstream outcome. [A-BLS-DETAIL2025]

Hilti's US Jaibot material describes semi-automated drilling, with digital plans uploaded through cloud/tablet workflows, total-station positioning, autonomous drilling within reach, and progress feedback. People still operate the machine and set up the total station. A 2021 IAARC paper documents ALEC's One Zaabeel implementation in Dubai and explicitly examines workflow, organization, quality, safety and project-management implications. Only its abstract was retrieved here, so numerical productivity effects are not adopted. The foreign case supports a mechanism to investigate in US projects, not a US penetration estimate. [A-HILTI-JAIBOT; A-JAIBOT-UAE2021]

The economics must include coordinated design, transport/mobilization, setup, obstruction, rebar encounters, operator labor and rework. A high drill rate does not equal a similar improvement in all MEP installation, particularly if the next trade is the bottleneck. Digital layout plus manual drilling and prefabrication are material alternatives; a humanoid must beat the configured alternatives on accepted work and utilization.

Authority is split between the general contractor controlling schedule/site access and subcontractors controlling crews/tools. A robotics subcontractor may aggregate work, but loses utilization when plans or upstream work are late. An acquisition can supply relationships and pipeline without automatically giving control over other trades. Future integrated stages require more reliable work-package preparation and access as well as physical capability. BIM-to-execution/as-built feedback is a direct digital contribution; lower drilling effort and reduced coordination error must not be counted twice when they represent the same avoided rework.

#### Utility inspection: sensing is an intermediate product

`CELL-M03-utility-inspection-US-2025` covers generation/substation asset inspection. US electric generation/transmission/distribution employment is **420,500 jobs**, not a count of inspectors or inspections. The useful-output denominator is **observations meeting sensor-quality and defect-detection requirements within the inspection interval**, followed separately by actionable finding, work order, repair and asset outcome. [A-BLS-DETAIL2025]

National Grid's February 2023 operator report describes a three-day Spot trial at its Blyth converter site, with visual, thermal, acoustic and LiDAR functions. It explicitly says results were being reviewed and future investment remained conditional. This supports physical access and data-collection possibilities, not retained routine use, actual outage reduction or a US adoption rate. Broader quadruped/drone operating evidence is owned by the I/J lane; this cell provides the common output boundary. [A-NATIONALGRID-2023]

Fixed sensors, drones, wheeled sensor carts and technicians can be substitutes or complements. A quadruped's mobility only creates value if its payload produces decision-quality data, review distinguishes true faults, and maintenance follows. Repeated missions do not establish lower payroll or avoided outages. Economics include sensor calibration, site mapping, human review, false positives/negatives, support and qualification. A drone failing an inspection-image requirement may be physically capable of flying yet economically unable to deliver the required product; the same distinction applies to ground robots.

Utilities retain asset/security authority; an integrator may assemble payloads, maps and workflows. A new inspection service entrant may compete for contracts while being unable to own or control regulated assets. Scaling therefore needs a service-access and qualification mechanism, not only abundant robots. Repeated routes can improve anomaly baselines; transfer across asset types, failure distributions or into physical repair is unproven.

### Conditional waterfalls and portfolio implications

Each structured cell contains the same logical waterfall: relevant accepted-work population → technical serviceability → full-system economic attractiveness → organizational readiness → deliverable capacity → retained productive output. **All numerical gate values remain null** because a representative matched population and the joint distribution of conditions are not known. This is a substantive limitation, not a zero-adoption result. It preserves the evidence needed to replace a gate later rather than manufacturing percentages now.

The denominator stays the cell's accepted-work unit. Sites, installed robots, robot-hours, observer-hours, human paid hours and currency require separate conversion panels with measured coefficients. A component placed, tote moved or delivery completed is an activity observation. It becomes a productivity result only with matched accepted quality, exposure, human effort and baseline; it becomes savings only if costs are actually avoided.

The supported sequence differs by cell. Cleaning and routine logistics already have specialized operating precedents. Automotive humanoid work has bounded production-linked exposure. Hospital logistics has an explicit urgency boundary. Construction shows digital/site prerequisites. Utility trials require the observation-to-action chain to be established. These are qualitative differences in evidence and mechanism, not a numerical attractiveness ranking.

For a humanoid company, a sensible initial portfolio test allocates scarce integration engineers, local support, qualified hardware and customer ramp funding across **specific cohorts**. Standardized configurations can improve repeatability but also face cheaper fixed alternatives. Higher wages increase potential avoided cost only when staffing can change; healthcare illustrates why wage comparisons alone mislead. A customer with strong capital access but fragmented operating authority can remain less ready than a smaller customer with a simpler process. RaaS changes cash timing and risk allocation while consuming provider capital and support.

Conditional horizons of 0–2, 3–5 and 6–10 years are stored as requirement changes, not extrapolated adoption dates. In the first interval, validate bounded repeated output, assistance and whole-system costs. Expansion requires observed transfer, support capacity and customer demand. Enterprise redesign remains a scenario whose advantage must survive digitally improved human operations, specialized automation and same-technology incumbent response. Announcing a new robot does not upgrade existing fleets or remove qualification lead time.

### What would most change the decision

The highest-priority quantitative gap is matched **accepted output plus total human effort**, rather than another large deployment announcement. For automotive and totes, seek accepted throughput, downtime, intervention duration and complete configured-cell cost. For cleaning, seek inspected area, residual/manual work, support/setup and contract retention at US sites. For hospital delivery, seek urgency-specific SLA performance and post-integration support labor. For construction, seek entire work-package effort, mobilization and utilization across projects. For inspection, link sensor-quality pass rates to actionable findings and completed maintenance.

A separate denominator program should join industry/occupation data to representative hours and measured work shares, retain self-employed and outsourced activity, and validate allocation weights. Until then, the atlas supports a broad map and conditional deployment judgment but cannot support national physical-hour penetration, robot TAM or a market-size-ranked rollout forecast.

Public evidence can improve source coverage, configurations, contract terms and reported outcomes. Several decisive questions require field records: staffing actually avoided, intervention duration, failed attempts, accepted quality, site utilization, commercial pricing and customer-specific authority. Missing access is recorded as a research dependency, not inferred unfavorable economics. The saved questions `A-Q-HOURS`, `A-Q-HOSPITAL-SLA`, `A-Q-CLEANING-CONTRACT` and `A-Q-PORTFOLIO` define bounded next actions and completion criteria.

### What the evidence changes

Productive humanoid operation has crossed the boundary from a demonstration to useful work in some bounded industrial settings. A strategic deployment portfolio therefore needs two separate tests. The first is whether the entire process delivers accepted output at its required quality and timing with a tolerable support burden. The second is whether this deployment yields transferable information or policy improvement worth its incremental collection, training and validation cost. The inspected research supports conditional learning gains, saturation and negative transfer. It does not support treating installed robots or accumulated hours as a universal learning multiplier.

The moving comparator matters. BMW's virtual factory tools improve manual ergonomics and its AIQX system supports quality inspection; Mercedes describes production chatbots and virtual data-science agents alongside Apollo experiments. A humanoid has to compete with the resulting human-led operation, as well as with specialized and mixed machines. #### BMW: unusually useful evidence, with decisive denominator limits

[BMW's February 2026 account](https://www.press.bmwgroup.com/global/article/detail/T0455864EN/bmw-group-to-deploy-humanoid-robots-in-production-in-germany-for-the-first-time?language=en) independently strengthens the manufacturer narrative: the robot did contribute repetitive precise work inside an active plant. But “independent” here means the operator speaks separately; BMW and Figure remain interested commercial partners, and neither supplies an independent audit.

[Figure's November 2025 post](https://www.figure.ai/news/production-at-bmw) distinguishes performance requirements from observations. The 84-second total cycle and 37-second loading phase are requirements. Greater than 99% placement accuracy is a target; zero human pauses/resets per shift is a goal. Recording these as achieved values would eliminate the very support uncertainty that determines fleet economics. The evidence file leaves realized intervention intensity null.

Calendar boundaries differ: Figure describes an 11-month deployment, BMW a ten-month period supporting vehicle production, and Figure separately describes six months of daily runtime. Ten-hour weekday shifts do not make every hour of those months autonomous operating exposure. Dividing rounded cumulative parts by rounded aggregate runtime would also conceal robot count, downtime inclusion and acceptance criteria. This dossier therefore does not publish an inferred intensive throughput rate.

That is evidence for a bounded entry configuration with strong complements, not immediate transfer to a fragmented construction site. A competing explanation for the apparent maturity is unusually favorable site selection and integration effort. Multi-site retained operation with disclosed support and quality would strengthen the broader claim.

Hardware learning is concrete but different from a software update. Figure identifies the forearm as its top hardware failure point and describes replacing the wrist's distribution board and dynamic cabling in Figure 03. This supports a field-exposure-to-design-feedback mechanism. It does not quantify the resulting reliability gain or show that the installed Figure 02 fleet could receive it without replacement.

Which wins depends on payload variation, layout, utilization, expected workflow changes and the cost of exceptions. The MOU for over 1,000 additional robots is a prospective relationship, not installed productive stock.

### Improvement evidence: six distinct mechanisms

#### 1. Diversity can dominate repeated demonstrations

[Hu, Lin and colleagues, v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18647v3) collected over 40,000 demonstrations and conducted over 15,000 real-world rollouts. Their detailed scaling experiments concern Pour Water and Mouse Arrangement; Fold Towels and Unplug Charger test the resulting collection recipe. Training variations cover 1–32 objects or environments, using a Diffusion Policy with a pretrained visual encoder and human-collected UMI demonstrations.

The paper was initially submitted on 24 October 2024; the inspected v3 is dated 13 October 2025. A newer [v4, dated 26 June 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18647v4), exists. A bounded check of §4.2, the diversity/repetition distinction, and Tables 1–2 found the core results below unchanged. This preserves the v3 snapshot and is not a complete comparison of every version change.

The dependent variable in their fits is the gap from a normalized, partially credited task score. It is not successful output per paid hour. Six data points support each displayed fit; the authors explicitly decline a three-parameter irreducible-error fit because of that limited sample. A prediction requiring 1,191 environment-object pairs is left unverified.

Increasing environment/object diversity improves held-out performance in the tested range. Repeated demonstrations within a fixed context saturate: in the recommended environment-object design, reported plateaus occur around 400, 800 and 1,600 demonstrations for 8, 16 and 32 pairs respectively. These are findings for similarly difficult tasks, not acquisition targets for a humanoid business.

Table 1 also prevents a loose “90% everywhere” interpretation: mean task success is 85.0%, 92.5%, 87.5% and 90.0%, with reported variability across eight unseen environments. The detailed scoring results and success percentages are separate metrics. Table 2 gives another negative finding: enlarging the action U-Net does not improve score monotonically, even though enlarging the visual encoder helps. Hardware, architecture, training and data composition interact.

**Model implication:** keep effective context diversity, repeated exposure and policy architecture separate. Do not fit a universal robot-hour exponent or assume that operating ten copies of an unchanged task creates ten times the useful training information.

#### 2. Cross-embodiment transfer can be positive, neutral or negative

[RT-X v9](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.08864v9) is a valuable controlled transfer study. The repository headline describes 22 embodiments, but the policy experiments use data from nine manipulators and conduct 3,600 evaluation trials across six robots. Coarse alignment uses a seven-dimensional end-effector action representation. This does not establish transfer across every sensor and actuation configuration in the larger repository.

Positive transfer appears in small-data settings. Yet Table I reports the Google-robot in-distribution comparison at 92% for RT-1 and 73% for RT-1-X. The authors interpret the small model as underfitting the heterogeneous mixture. Even the larger model's 91% is not above that 92% baseline; a broad prose statement that it outperforms the baselines should not override the table.

The widely cited roughly threefold effect belongs to emergent skills: RT-2-X scores 75.8% versus RT-2's 27.3%. The separate generalization column is 61% versus 62%. Removing Bridge data reduces the emergent score to 42.8%, strengthening the specific transfer interpretation. None of these are industry productivity multipliers.

**Model implication:** transfer is a directed edge tied to source distribution, target, representation, capacity and training recipe. Preserve negative and unchanged-capability cases. Cross-market similarity alone cannot activate an edge.

#### 3. Data selection requires target information

[DataMIL](https://robin-lab.cs.utexas.edu/datamodels4imitation/) makes the selection problem explicit. Its target-policy influence estimates use target validation loss, temporal clustering and co-training with target data. The research spans more than 60 simulation/real tasks, with four real-world OXE target tasks and a new Tiago embodiment example. The project reports that visually similar observations can carry action distributions that help or harm the target policy.

This supports performance-aware curation rather than a monotonic gross-data-volume story. The full paper's computation and sensitivity deserve deeper extraction before assigning costs. No claim here treats every recorded trajectory as accessible, useful or valuable.

**Model implication:** deployment exposure, usable dataset, selected training mixture, validated target capability and captured cash value are five different objects. Include target-data collection, selection computation, training, validation and rollout costs before valuing a learning route.

#### 4. New-environment generalization has progressed without proving complete service

[pi0.5](https://www.pi.website/blog/pi05) uses heterogeneous robot, semantic and web-derived knowledge to perform household manipulation in unseen homes. Its paper evaluates three homes and task/stage-completion rubrics. This is meaningful evidence that new-site adaptation need not always mean training a separate policy from scratch.

It does not establish autonomous housekeeping across representative households. A bedroom-cleaning task, short task evaluation and complete recurring service contract have different output boundaries. The authors acknowledge errors in both high-level semantic choices and motor commands. A service business would additionally need customer-specific quality, safe exceptions, setup, cleaning supplies, access and reliable scheduling.

**Model implication:** allow a conditional reduction in adaptation effort, separately from task reliability, human assistance and full-service eligibility. Human housekeeping improved with scheduling software and specialized appliances remains a comparator.

#### 5. Experience and corrections can improve throughput, with human costs

[RECAP / pi*0.6 v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.14759v2) is more directly relevant to useful throughput than a generic score curve. It measures successful task completions per hour as well as success under task-specific quality and time limits. Evaluations cover laundry variants, double espresso shots and box assembly; deadlines differ by task (200, 500 or 600 seconds).

The reported on-robot improvement more than doubles throughput for espresso and the diverse-laundry experiment. The latter trains on 11 item types but quantitatively evaluates **button-up shirts only**; videos of other clothing do not establish measured throughput across the full laundry mix. The iterative simple-laundry experiment collects 300 trajectories on four robots per iteration without expert corrections. Section VI-C describes box assembly as 600 autonomous trials and 360 trials with expert interventions per iteration, while Appendix A-F calls the 600 demonstrations. This wording conflict prevents treating the count as a fully unassisted collection budget. Box throughput's point estimate initially falls before improving; the early error bars overlap, so this is not evidence of a statistically established decline. A monotonic scenario would still omit the observed point-estimate path.

The initial submission is dated 18 November 2025 and the inspected v2 is dated 19 November 2025; the exact version dates were verified from arXiv submission histories.

Section VII explicitly says the learning system is not fully autonomous: humans provide reward labels, interventions and episode resets. Updates are batched offline, not a continuously improving online controller. Extended execution videos do not eliminate those training and support inputs.

**Model implication:** learning can improve speed, success and recovery separately. Count annotation, correction, reset, maintenance and retraining time; include delay and possible temporary regression. Do not count higher future throughput and then add a second generic “data value” premium.

#### 6. Physical redesign has different diffusion and financing requirements

The Figure wrist case is empirical evidence of a design response, not a calibrated reliability improvement. Similarly, battery exchange in Walker S2 is a proposed way to remove a charging interruption, not evidence of 24/7 accepted production. A longer nominal endurance specification cannot eliminate repairs, calibration, supervision, upstream starvation or demand gaps.

**Model implication:** distinguish base capability progress, software upgrades, hardware replacement, production qualification and supported operating scale. An improvement may be available only to a new cohort and may require validation downtime, spare parts and new capital.

### Learning rights and deployment choice

The most immediately profitable site is not necessarily the most informative site, and the most varied site may overwhelm present capability. A deployment policy can therefore trade production margin against valuable evidence. But the comparison must use the same capital, robots, supervision and engineering resources. An experiment displacing production has an opportunity cost; an operational failure can produce a useful label while still harming the customer.

For a candidate learning edge, ask: what new state or failure is observed; is it recorded and usable; who can reuse it; which target policy changes; what delay and validation are needed; and who captures the economic effect? Provider-mediated transfer may benefit several competing customers, reducing exclusivity while improving the provider's product.

FG-E008 in the customer/commercial lane supplies a concrete rights comparator: a BrainOS/Kärcher agreement distinguishes end-user data ownership from broad licensed reuse and from guaranteed data export. Its applicability is to that agreement and version, not to Figure or Digit. It illustrates why owning the robot or the operating business does not establish exclusive learning rights. The contract's metadata/version ambiguity remains unresolved.

An unchanged-policy/no-transfer scenario should remain available. It is an analytical comparison, not an empirical assertion that transfer is zero. If future gains already enter output and cash-flow projections, additional data premiums would double count them. Information that changes a later investment decision is a separate value-of-information mechanism and needs its own counterfactual.

### Coordination: shared state, authority and scarce resources

Three architectures have different evidence statuses.

1. **An integrated production station.** At BMW, the humanoid loads or sequences while fixed robots, transport systems and people perform other stages. The operator reports standardized integration. Exact decision APIs and a measured incremental coordination effect are not public.
2. **A heterogeneous logistics fleet.** At GXO, Arc connects workflow definition and Digit operations with existing automation. This is evidence of integration and commercial use; it is not an experiment proving that a particular fleet architecture causes a measured gain.
3. **Cross-vendor infrastructure coordination.** [Open-RMF interfaces](https://openrmf.readthedocs.io/en/latest/interfacing/index.html) distinguish state reporting, destination requests, pause/resume and complete-path control, plus doors and lifts. An observation-only fleet permits fewer deconfliction actions than a fully controllable fleet. Documentation establishes a mechanism; it does not establish an LLM contribution or quantify economics. H uses the same source, so it is not independent corroboration.

A productive architecture must distinguish business intent and work orders, scheduling/optimization, fleet dispatch, task-level perception/action generation, low-level motion/safety control and exception escalation. It should identify each layer's state, allowed actions, latency and fallback. Redwood's manufacturer description provides one concrete separation: onboard action generation around 5 Hz and an offboard speech-to-speech goal interface. It does not imply that safety control runs at 5 Hz or depends on a language model.

A simple mechanism model should start with accepted output bounded by demand, upstream feed, robot work capacity and downstream capacity. The units must match at each boundary. Faster picking will not raise shipment output when the conveyor or subsequent process binds; a scheduling gain that improves the same bottleneck cannot be added twice.

Human support is a shared resource. If robot cohort i supplies A_i active robot-hours, experiences lambda_i interventions per active robot-hour, and needs s_i human-hours per intervention, expected direct intervention work is the sum of A_i × lambda_i × s_i. Maintenance, setup, travel, annotation and management add separate demands. This is an accounting identity under declared average-rate assumptions, not a queue forecast.

Support demand below staffed hours is only a necessary condition. Bursty or correlated failures can produce queues, missed takt, blocked corridors and idle robots even when mean workload fits. Network/model failures can affect many units together; task assignment, redundancy and operating slack may matter more than increasing average utilization. A model should expose these mechanisms as scenarios until logs support calibration. Robot waiting also changes active exposure and future intervention arrivals, so avoid inconsistent fixed-arrival assumptions.

### Market-stage consequences and conditional thresholds

The six atlas cells share one useful-output boundary per comparison:

- **Automotive assembly:** accepted loaded/sequenced components at takt. Compare human-plus-digital tools, fixed fixtures/arms, wheeled manipulators and humanoids in the same line. Priority unknowns are sustained intervention/recovery and complete integration cost; do not assign the entire vehicle's value to one station.
- **3PL tote transfer:** accepted totes delivered into downstream flow. Compare docking/transfer redesign, human-plus-AMR and Digit. Measure peak-hour throughput and shared support, not cumulative lifetime activity alone.
- **Facilities floor cleaning:** cleanliness-qualified floor area is distinct from clutter removal, laundry or general housekeeping. Specialized scrubbers/vacuums, humans and manipulators may be complementary. Unseen-home manipulation research does not calibrate commercial floor-cleaning substitution.
- **Hospital logistics:** correct-item delivery with custody and service deadlines. Doors, elevators, prioritization and human handoffs can bind before manipulation or legs. Delivery capability is not clinical care capability.
- **Construction installation:** accepted installed elements under trade/site quality requirements. The inspected tabletop and automotive evidence does not establish transfer to weather, changing geometry, tools, heavy loads or trade-specific recovery. Keep human tools, layout/drilling machines and mixed crews in the comparison.
- **Utility inspection:** an actionable condition assessment and maintenance decision is different from a mission or repair. Fixed sensors, drones, quadrupeds and human engineers may dominate sensing. Humanoid manipulation becomes relevant only for separately validated interaction/repair work; I/J owns the direct field evidence.

These are threshold maps rather than calibrated penetration forecasts. They support staged entry: bounded assisted operation, repeatable productive work, integrated fleets and wider redesign where the evidence warrants it. No common ladder or ownership label guarantees progression.

### Coverage, limitations and next bounded work

This wave establishes a source-verified industrial entry thesis, conditional learning mechanisms and a coordination architecture. It does not establish commercial causal returns, calibrated learning curves for humanoids, representative intervention distributions, validated market penetration or a complete provider census. Primary manufacturer and operator statements remain interested-party reports. Research evaluations are author-run and context-specific. No field audit, training run or hardware experiment was performed.

The highest-value next evidence is full-shift accepted output and intervention logs with compatible robot-hour and human-hour denominators in automotive loading and tote transfer. It can change both near-term economics and support-constrained scale. Next are target-specific transfer ablations with total training cost/delay, upgrade compatibility, and matched fixed/wheeled/human alternatives. Public technical presentations and operator disclosures can reduce some uncertainty; otherwise the exact missing denominator should remain visible and field access recorded as a separate requirement.

For reproducibility, all numerical observations are linked to their source and locator in the evidence JSON; specifications, targets, qualitative interpretations and scenario mechanisms have separate status. Figures from different tasks are never pooled into a universal capability score. Benchmark curves are not inputs to the economic engine unless a later target-specific calibration is explicitly accepted.

### Decision finding

An AI-native operating newco needs a reason to win after incumbents can buy the same robots. Current evidence already includes incumbent adoption: GXO reports a commercial humanoid RaaS deployment, and ISS describes a substantial specialized-cleaning-robot program. The strongest initial entry thesis is conditional: win a defined service obligation, control enough of the workflow to deliver it, survive commissioning and collections, and preserve an advantage in operating design, customer access or constrained capacity after competitors respond. Neither an AI-native label nor cheaper hypothetical robots establishes this sequence.

Customer organization and provider arrangements change different constraints. On the customer side, ownership can affect capital allocation, approval, process authority, implementation and retention of gains. On the supply side, a sale, subscription, lease or managed operation allocates asset funding, utilization risk, service work, control and rights. A structure can relax one party's constraint by moving it to another party. An investment case therefore needs both maps and consolidated cash accounting.

Three first-wave findings are consequential:

1. **Service contracts can matter more than corporate ownership labels.** The same reduction in physical effort has different cash effects under fixed-price, cost-plus and hourly contracts. Contract termination can precede robot-cost recovery. These are disclosed mechanisms, not conjectures about public versus private management.
2. **Incumbents have evidenced complements to common robot access.** Existing customers, software integration, service networks and site knowledge must be included in the same-technology comparator. An entrant may design a better process, but that is a separate hypothesis requiring costs and evidence.
3. **Asset or data ownership does not establish exclusive control.** A retrieved BrainOS agreement gives the end user operational-data ownership while giving Brain durable nonexclusive improvement-use rights. The robot, software, customer relationship, workflow authority, practical data access and economic learning benefit can belong to different parties.

### Two controlled customer comparisons

#### Contract logistics: outsourced operator versus own-account operation

`FG-CMP-01` joins `CELL-M08-3pl-tote-US-2025`. The useful outcome is an accepted tote handoff at required quality and service timing. Hold tote distribution, layout, system/software, throughput, uptime, intervention and robot commercial terms fixed. This is an explanatory counterfactual, not an observed matched experiment.

In the observed GXO configuration, GXO is both the robot customer and logistics operator; SPANX is the served business. Agility supplies the robot and orchestration. The financier and legal robot owner are undisclosed. This single physical activity appears in the logistics and apparel value-chain views but must not become two opportunity totals.

| Mechanism | Multi-site logistics contractor | Integrated brand/retailer doing the same work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capital access/allocation | Can incorporate equipment recovery into a service relationship; still needs finance and customer commitment. | Can invest directly, but fulfillment competes with other internal uses of capital. |
| Appetite | Actual GXO pilot-to-contract progression is evidence of willingness for this use case. | Unknown for the counterfactual; legal ownership alone supplies no estimate. |
| Authority | Operational experience and integration control, subject to client requirements, premises and contract. | Potentially fewer organizational interfaces, but site, safety and business-process approval remain. |
| Implementation | Existing integration and customer onboarding capability can support rollout; no universal cross-site transfer assumed. | May need to build or buy these capabilities even when hardware is identical. |
| Gain retention | Fixed-price contract may retain cost improvements until repricing; cost-plus may pass benefits to the shipper or alter the margin base. | Retains avoidable internal cost reduction, subject to workforce and capacity constraints. |
| Opposing mechanism | Unrecovered investment, performance penalties, client loss and price competition. | Higher implementation burden, stranded dedicated assets and competing capital priorities. |

The pivotal issue is **who can change the process and retain a sufficient share of its gains for long enough to recover the investment**. Under a percentage cost-plus arrangement, reducing allowable labor cost can even reduce the contractor's absolute fee unless gain-sharing or another contract provision compensates it. GXO reports a specified margin, not that every contract has this percentage formula; the reduction is a scenario to test. Fixed-price service instead exposes the contractor to both cost savings and cost overruns. These mechanisms can explain different adoption choices with the same robot, without assigning an ownership multiplier.

The provider implication is to qualify the client contract and approval chain before interpreting an enthusiastic site manager as ready demand. A joint customer/operator deployment agreement may improve incentives, but the negotiation, integration expense and allocation of benefits need explicit treatment. A new logistics operator faces the same technical boundary plus customer acquisition, onboarding capital, service credibility and competitive bidding. GXO's own risk disclosures make a frictionless entrant assumption indefensible.

#### Facilities services: contractor versus in-house facility operator

`FG-CMP-02` joins `CELL-M11-floor-cleaning-US-2025`. Hold accepted cleaned area or service zones, frequency, cleanliness standard, layout, robot configuration, supplies, setup and human quality review fixed. Compare a specialist contractor with a facility owner operating the same equipment. The source-backed present competitor is a specialized cleaner. A humanoid doing broader cleaning remains a conditional system and must earn its added scope against that alternative.

A contractor may pool procurement, training, service and movable equipment across clients. Its workflow authority remains bounded by the client's premises, access, work specification and data requirements. Under fixed-price or square-foot service it may retain efficiency gains; under hourly billing, fewer billed hours can reduce revenue; cost-plus depends on the exact margin and allowable-cost rules. The facility operator may retain savings directly and align investment with building use, but needs integration and support capability. None of these effects is guaranteed by size or ownership category.

ABM's disclosed cancellation window creates a concrete asset-contract mismatch. A long asset life or noncancellable supplier lease can outlast the service customer's effective commitment. The useful questions are the recoverable setup cost, relocation cost, compatible next site, downtime between contracts and residual value. A robot that is cheap per productive hour can still be a poor investment if it is rarely productive or cannot be redeployed economically. Conversely, actual customer retention may be much longer than legal notice: using notice periods as measured customer lifetime would be equally wrong.

ISS's [three-year European customer expansion](https://www.issworld.com/insights/news-and-press/iss-signs-new-deal-with-global-technology-company) shows how incumbents can grow through existing relationships and cross-country standardization. It does not disclose cancellation options or demonstrate robot-cost recovery. The combination argues for modeling contract commitment and actual retention separately.

Digital-only improvement also belongs in this comparator. ISS describes activity-based scheduling and customer requests independently of floor robots. Better scheduling can reduce unnecessary cleaning or improve timeliness without replacing the worker. Assigning the same avoided work to both digital scheduling and robotics would overstate the combined benefit.

### Same-robot entrant, financing and incumbent response

`ENTRY-FG-01` is a deliberately synthetic monthly formation/ramp example linked to the facilities cell. It sells accepted facility-service bundles; these are normalized model units, not measured square metres or observed humanoid capabilities. A present specialized-cleaner competitor remains explicit. The example tests a conditional broadly available robotic configuration and gives the prompt incumbent exactly the same hardware, accepted productivity and robot commercial terms.

The implementation lane owns all numerical values and generated results in the common engine. The scenario distinguishes a three-month commissioning period, phased ramp, robot payments, retained service labor/materials, enterprise overhead, initial setup/customer acquisition, delayed collections and committed funding. No later financing appears automatically. Incumbent response timing changes price and capturable demand, rather than mechanically converting lower costs to market share. A separate operating-redesign override may change retained costs; it is not the equal-technology baseline.

The identities are:

```text
accepted_capacity[t] = commissioned_robots[t] × accepted_units_per_robot[t] × ramp[t]
served_newco[t] = min(accepted_capacity[t], won_and_retained_demand[t])
served_newco = incumbent_share_transfer + existing_unmet_demand_served + new_demand_served
incumbent_output_after_transfer = incumbent_reference_output − incumbent_share_transfer
cash[t] = cash[t−1] + collections[t] + committed_funding[t]
          − setup[t] − customer_acquisition[t] − robot_payments[t]
          − retained_operating_cost[t] − overhead[t] − other_investment[t]
minimum_external_funding = maximum cumulative cash deficit before external funding
```

Accepted capacity is after the workload's quality and downtime allowances; it must not be multiplied by the same loss again. Collections differ from recognized service revenue, and uncollected receivables are not available cash. Acquisition transfers ownership of existing activity; it does not increase market output. A hypothetical shortage of people can support a capacity proposition only if customers will buy the output and robots plus support can reliably supply it.

Three investment hurdles remain distinct: positive repeatable contribution, survival through ramp, and actual capital availability. The first does not establish the second or third. Financeability is strongest when evidence milestones reduce the remaining risk: customer commitment and site permissions before deployment spend; repeated quality with measured intervention/support before fleet expansion; retention, collections and performance under incumbent response before financing the next operating unit.

A delayed incumbent offers a temporary window. A prompt incumbent can cut price, include the same robots within an existing service bundle, improve its digital workflow, retain customers through broader relationships, partner with the entrant, or acquire it. After technical convergence, the newco needs a demonstrated difference in service, integrated process cost, customer reach or execution. Supplier-mediated learning can improve competitors too. Merely owning the first deployed fleet is not evidence of a durable data advantage.

### Pure newco, builder platform, acquisition and hybrid

| Route | What it buys or creates | Main advantage hypothesis | Costs and reversal conditions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standalone operating newco | Genuinely new operating capacity and customer relationships. | Coherent process design; no acquisition premium or inherited constraints. | Formation, qualification, CAC, ramp losses and limited reputation. Prompt incumbent adoption may erase the lead before recovery. |

Acquisition should be compared through the value of purchased customer access, earlier cash flow and reusable operating capability against acquisition premium, transition cost, legacy obligations and financing burden. An observed caution is GXO's Wincanton acquisition: its 2025 filing reports April 2024 completion and June 2025 competition approval subject to divestment of certain UK grocery contracts (FG-E014). This does not identify an AI transformation return. It does show that buying an operator can involve control and portfolio constraints beyond installing robots.

The [OpenAI–Thrive announcement of 1 December 2025](https://openai.com/index/thrive-holdings/) describes owned operating businesses and embedded technical teams, initially in accounting and IT. [Thrive's 12 August 2026 update](https://www.thriveholdings.com/thrive-holdings-fundraise) reports more than $2 billion of new capital at a $12 billion valuation, over 70 owned/operated businesses and expansion into built-environment technical and regulatory services. These issuer descriptions support a structural comparator: capital allocation and deployment engineering under an operating ownership architecture. They do not establish returns, causal productivity or humanoid deployment economics. Technical/regulatory services for the built environment are not interchangeable with robotic construction labor.

The physical extension differs in hardware capital, maintenance, site access, liability, qualified supply, residual values and noncopyable productive capacity. A pure operating-newco platform also differs from buying revenue-producing firms. `FG-AN-THRIVE` therefore permits organizational hypotheses only. It supplies no robotics adoption, productivity, valuation or financing coefficient.

[Hadrian's July 2025 announcement](https://www.hadrian.co/blog/series-c) provides a separate physical-production comparator: an operating manufacturing company, proprietary software, new capital, a loan arrangement and planned factory expansion. It supports the existence of capital formation around a physical operator; it does not validate off-the-shelf humanoid economics. The financing description does not cleanly separate all equity and debt components, so the model must not add an assumed loan amount to the headline figure. Planned factory timing is not verified operating exposure. Defense programs and proprietary production technology are material limits to transfer.

### Provider assets, control and value capture

This inquiry remains separate from the customer pathways. The same newco, acquired operator or incumbent can use several supply arrangements. An integrated robot provider can additionally operate a service business, but its internal robot payment is eliminated when calculating consolidated value.

| Arrangement | Funding and operating allocation | Value-capture question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Equipment sale | Customer or lender funds acquisition; supplier still finances production and receivables. Customer carries utilization and residual risk subject to warranty/support. | Can hardware margin cover product development and service obligations when autonomy, integration and competition constrain price? |
| Asset lease | Lessor funds asset; operator still funds site setup, employees, working capital and losses. | Who can price credit, residual value, redeployment and obsolescence better? Lease availability and cancellation terms are inputs. |
| RaaS | Customer payment may bundle hardware/software/support; actual title and obligations depend on contract. | Does recurring price cover provider capital, upkeep, intervention and idle risk? Lower customer capex does not eliminate system capital. |
| Usage/outcome contract | Provider/operator may carry unused-capacity risk; accepted-output and customer-caused delay rules matter. | Who can control enough of the process to guarantee an outcome and measure it without dispute? |
| Integration/software | Customer or equipment partner pays for setup and recurring autonomy/orchestration. | Reusable integration and a service channel may capture rents, but interoperability and competing suppliers can constrain them. |
| Managed operation, partnership or JV | Adds end-market execution; governance can unify or complicate decisions. | Are control and customer access worth extra operating risk, shared governance and capital? Equity does not itself grant data/model rights. |
| Provider owning the productive business | Provider funds greenfield/acquisition, overhead and demand formation as well as robots. | Are additional operating margin and verified learning/control benefits worth capital and lost focus relative to selling or partnering? |

For matched output, let baseline customer cost be `B`, retained nonprovider customer cost `A`, provider payment `P`, and provider operating cash cost `O`. Customer operating savings are `B − A − P`; provider operating contribution is `P − O`; consolidated operating benefit is `B − A − O`. Price cancels. One-time investments, collections, working capital and replacement are added at their dates; depreciation is not a second cash outlay. Financing payments between modeled participants also cancel in a consolidated resource view, while the cost and availability of external capital remain relevant.

The feasible commercial interval must satisfy both customer and provider cash requirements, with customer savings based on **avoidable** costs. Customer time redeployed to another task is not the same as payroll removed. A provider discount justified by expected learning is a provider investment, not an unexplained increase in customer savings or a second data premium.

The BrainOS artifact is especially informative. Section 5.2 leaves operational-data title with the end user but grants Brain continuing, nonexclusive rights to store, use and copy it for service and improvement purposes; access/export is not automatically assured. Section 5.2 also excludes aggregated data that is not linked to the end user from the defined Operational Data category; personal data is outside that definition. End-user ownership must be read within those boundaries. Sections 4.1–4.3 reserve software rights and link use to service access. This demonstrates the need to record ownership, access, reuse, export, exclusivity and commercial rights separately. It does not establish that any particular data actually improves a model, that those terms govern Digit, or that a customer cannot negotiate another arrangement.

Tennant–Brain illustrates a complementary distribution/software structure rather than inevitable total vertical integration. Its disclosed channel, service and recurring autonomy roles suggest that access to customers and supported deployment can capture value alongside hardware and models. Exact rents remain unquantified. Competing hypotheses stay live: autonomy could commoditize while local service remains scarce; or proprietary orchestration and dependent subscriptions could preserve supplier bargaining power. Observable renewal, switching, service cost and performance evidence would distinguish them.

### Coverage, limitations and next questions

The two cell comparisons provide sourced mechanisms at fixed technical/workload baselines. They do not estimate a causal effect of ownership. The entrant case specifies equal robot access, commissioning, runway, customer acquisition, demand conservation and competitive response as an explicit scenario. The four enterprise formation routes and distinct provider arrangements are preserved. Fifteen market families have a qualitative entry screen, mostly hypotheses rather than investigated field cases.

The highest-value next evidence is a coherent pair of customer and supplier contracts plus measured operating exposure. Exact fees, accepted output, support time, cancellation, collections and redeployment determine whether an attractive unit margin is financeable. Public procurement and regulatory disclosures are the next authorized research route; no outreach is assumed.

Second, seek matched pilot-to-rollout comparisons that observe approval authority, engineering effort and savings retention, instead of treating public/private/holdco labels as causal. Third, obtain authoritative humanoid data and model terms before modeling exclusive learning. Fourth, compare one genuine greenfield operating ramp with one acquired operator, including price paid, retained customers, cash flow and transition costs. Finally, study renewals and prompt same-robot competitors to separate durable operating advantage from a temporary adoption lead.

These gaps are registered as `FG-Q-01` through `FG-Q-05`, linked to the existing OS/BM roadmap. The next bounded question is: **Can a robot-supported service contract recover all commissioning, customer-acquisition and working-capital costs before cancellation or same-technology competition removes its surplus, and who must finance the intervening cash deficit?**

### What changes the robotics judgment

Digital AI changes both the candidate robotic system and the incumbent against which it must compete. A robot operator that beats yesterday's human process can still lose to a human operation with better scheduling, documentation, purchasing, exception handling, or engineering support. These digital improvements can also complement robots. The correct comparison therefore needs four matched operating configurations, with an explicit interaction and full implementation costs.

The strongest source evidence in this wave does not support a universal digital productivity coefficient. A large customer-support deployment increased resolved issues per worker-hour, particularly for less experienced workers. A later study of experienced software developers found its current uplift estimate undermined by participant selection, task selection and concurrent-agent time accounting. These are informative differences in production systems, rather than competing estimates of one economy-wide effect. Likewise, the current peer-reviewed agent-coordination study supports conditional architecture choices; it does not establish that adding agents always increases production.

Direct digital contributions to physical systems are real but occur at distinct interfaces. AutoRT physically used foundation models to select and organize data collection. DrEureka used GPT-4 to generate training configurations subsequently tested on hardware. Astranomy evaluated language-model control and oversight in simulation. Open-RMF documents conventional traffic and building interfaces. SAP announces enterprise agents. Combining those sources into a diagram does **not** establish that a working LLM-to-ERP-to-fleet production stack has been deployed or measured.

Three immediate decisions follow. First, select markets using accepted output, timing, integration and support requirements rather than benchmark success alone. Second, keep the customer process owner, deployment integrator, robot controller, model provider and capital provider distinct even when one entity fills several roles. Third, measure learning and R&D gains through validated improvements and critical-path reduction, not raw episodes, generated code or announced customer access.

### The connected ecosystem dossier

The chain is physical inputs and capital → model research, training, post-training and evaluation → inference and cloud distribution → agent/tool runtimes → integration and change management → operating customers → accepted customer output. Each arrow has a different unit and evidentiary status. Training FLOPs are not usable inference capacity; capital commitments are not commissioned capacity; model access is not permission to write business records; a portfolio relationship is not a deployment; an accepted work product is not eliminated payroll.

The separate HI chapter covers power, qualified compute/memory, capacity and feedback to construction, maintenance and infrastructure. This H chapter covers how capability travels through that chain into work. Its upstream development evidence is deliberately bounded: [Hoffmann et al.'s compute-optimal training paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.15556v1) studies allocation between parameters and training tokens under a specified training-compute budget. The domain is text-model training loss and downstream evaluation. Its fitted allocation exponents are neither robot reliability exponents nor coefficients for adoption, manufacturing cost or firm output. Deployment lifetime inference costs and physical experience collection introduce objectives that differ from this fixed-training-budget optimization. Post-training, data licensing and research-talent markets remain incomplete in this wave.

The [FTC's January 2025 partnership report summary](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-issues-staff-report-ai-partnerships-investments-study) identifies equity/revenue-sharing, cloud-spend commitments, distribution, information access and some control/exclusivity rights in selected Microsoft–OpenAI, Amazon–Anthropic and Alphabet–Anthropic relationships. Its information boundary is September 2024 plus public material through January 2025. It supports a mechanism in which finance can be bundled with compute purchases and switching costs; it does not establish the October 2026 terms of every relationship or realized competitive harm. The implication for robotics is to maintain separate financial, compute, data, operational-control and customer-access edges. An investment cannot be counted again as independent cloud revenue and additional free deployment capital in a consolidated scenario.

A services company helping existing businesses adopt AI differs from a new operating company selling manufactured products, inspections, deliveries or cleaning. The physical entrant needs customer qualification, facilities, assets, working capital and retained contribution; a lab relationship supplies only some possible complements. A model improvement can strengthen an integrator by expanding useful applications, or weaken it by making previously scarce work easier for customers or platforms to perform. Both mechanisms remain open.

The [Census CES 26-25 full paper](https://www2.census.gov/library/working-papers/2026/adrm/ces/CES-WP-26-25.pdf), dated April 2026, supplies a better deployment denominator than announcements. For the November 2025–January 2026 reference period, 18% of firms reported AI use in a business function; the employment-weighted figure was 32%. Among functional adopters, 57% used AI in three or fewer functions. These are broad-AI measures, not LLM-only use or physical automation. The revised core question took effect on 17 November 2025, so a discontinuity from earlier wording is not clean adoption growth. Firm use refers to two weeks, worker-task use to six months, and firms report worker behavior. The study distinguishes adoption, breadth, task use and investment; it does not identify causal productivity from their correlations with commercial performance.

For robotics this favors an adoption record with separate capability, process integration, operating authority, workforce use, economic attractiveness and delivered output. Low adoption need not mean technological incapability, but high benchmark capability does not establish the size of an economically viable deployment overhang. That quantity remains unknown.

### Two contrasting operating dossiers

#### Business-software customer support: assistance inside a repeated production process

[Generative AI at Work](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/140/2/889/7990658), published in the QJE on 4 February 2025, examines 5,172 customer-support agents at a software firm and its subcontractors. The rollout primarily occurred in fall 2020 and winter 2021. The tool used GPT-3-based real-time suggestions; agents remained responsible for conversations and could edit or ignore suggestions. The relevant product is a resolved customer issue, not generated text.

Access increased resolved issues per agent-hour by 15% on average. This combined changes in handling time, parallel chat handling and resolution. Benefits were heterogeneous: less experienced/lower-skilled workers gained more, while highly skilled workers showed little productivity improvement and some quality deterioration. Evidence during outages is consistent with some persistent worker learning, but those outages were rare and estimates noisier. The paper's staggered-adoption/event-study design handles observed worker/time/tenure factors and examines alternative estimators; interpretation still requires parallel-trend and adoption assumptions. It is not simply a randomized trial of the full observed rollout.

The mechanism is concrete: knowledge and communication practices are delivered at the point of decision inside an existing workflow, with a human retaining final authority. This suggests a research question for physical end markets: can digital assistance improve dispatch, maintenance diagnosis, operator training or customer-service work around a physical process? It does not supply a 15% uplift for assembly, floor cleaning, nursing, inspection or robot supervision. Even in the source population, more resolved issues per hour does not identify removed payroll, wage changes or equilibrium employment. At fixed work and quality, time can be released; its realized value depends on staffing, demand and redeployment.

For adoption strategy, the relevant complement is not just model quality. It includes historical examples, an interface fitted to live work, human judgment, feedback and training capacity. It also warns that copying the average effect across different worker cohorts can overstate expert benefits. A digitally improved incumbent may become more competitive before installing any robot.

#### Software development: changed task selection makes uplift difficult to identify

[METR's 24 February 2026 update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/) explicitly rejects treating its new experiment as a reliable current productivity estimate. The original February–June 2025 study found that AI-allowed tasks took 19% longer, with a reported interval of +2% to +39%. The later experiment began in August 2025 and included 57 developers across 143 repositories and more than 800 tasks. Among ten returning developers, the raw task-time estimate was −18% with an interval from −38% to +9%; among new developers it was −4%, interval −15% to +9%. Negative figures mean less time, not negative productivity.

The crucial finding is methodological. Developers increasingly declined participation because they did not want to work without AI. Participants withheld tasks they expected AI to accelerate. Pay also fell from $150 to $50 per hour between studies, creating another selection mechanism. Some work changed in scope or quality; some developers worked on another task while an agent ran, making task-time attribution difficult. Randomizing submitted tasks does not remove selection into submission. The authors considered the data weak evidence about the magnitude of current speedup.

This does not establish that AI slows developers in October 2026, nor that large current gains are proven. It establishes that a dated average task-time result can lose validity as tools, work choice and organization change. For robotics R&D, the unit should be validated engineering output at a fixed objective, with active human time, elapsed critical-path time, model/training compute, failed proposals and physical testing recorded separately. Faster code generation is not equivalent to a shorter hardware qualification campaign. Concurrent agents may improve total throughput while complicating per-task measurement; that is a reason to improve the accounting, not to assume a gain.

Together these dossiers motivate heterogeneous digital effects, an explicit human baseline, and outcome-specific measurement. They cannot be pooled into one “AI productivity” parameter.

| [DrEureka v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.01967v1) | GPT-4-generated rewards/domain randomization, RL training and real-world quadruped/manipulation tests. | Existing simulation infrastructure, multiple candidates and hardware validation are required. Best candidate performance is not one-shot engineering success or a total R&D cost saving. |

**The July coordination paper materially revises the January Google blog.** The earlier blog describes 180 configurations, four benchmarks and cross-validated R² = 0.513. The peer-reviewed article expands to 260 configurations and six benchmarks, with R² = 0.373, or 0.413 with a task-grounded capability measure. It explicitly labels its approximately 45% single-agent capability threshold a practical selection rule rather than a universal scaling principle. Most descriptive patterns do not survive cluster-robust inference; six benchmark clusters limit precision. The model selects the best architecture in 87% of held-out **within-domain** configurations. A separate validation predicts the sign of multi-agent gain in 94% of 16 model-by-benchmark configurations.

The direction remains useful: structured financial reasoning showed large benefits from coordination, sequential planning deteriorated, and verification architecture affected error propagation. The reported 17.2× versus 4.4× amplification comparisons are benchmark trace measures, not robot accident rates. They motivate comparing a strong single agent, conventional solver and alternative coordination structures with equal information and budgets. They do not set a numerical fleet uplift or a 45% robot-autonomy threshold.

Astranomy makes the economic distinction especially visible. Its page reports Agentic Policy success of 74.8% and 13.37 seconds average inference latency per call. Continued full-page retrieval resolved the initial headline ambiguity: Section 4.2 and Table A11 state that cost values cover **successful episodes only**, priced using October 1, 2026 API rates. The Agentic mean is $36.40 over 447 usage-covered successes out of 449 successful episodes. This is not total expenditure on successful and failed attempts divided by delivered successes. Failure costs, resets and physical support would still be needed for commercial cost per accepted output.

Section 4.1 reports that inference accounts for 97.8% of Agentic estimated episode duration, 97.0% for Direct EEF and 94.9% for Orchestrated Policy. Estimated duration adds rollout time and inference time while the original simulation pauses during inference. It is not an observed real-world operating cycle or proof that the same success would persist while the scene changes. Section 7 explicitly says statistical significance of configuration differences has not been established and excludes contact-rich insertion/assembly and highly dynamic tasks. These qualifications constrain both adoption and architecture claims. A stronger simulated score can coexist with a poor time-constrained production proposition.

### Three direct-contribution maps

#### Robot reasoning and control

AutoRT's interface is image/scene understanding → language task proposal → affordance and constitution filtering → chosen scripted, learned or teleoperated policy → observed episode outcome. The physical collection spans four office buildings and seven months. It used 53 robots in total and more than 20 simultaneously; those are different denominators. One human supervised three to five mobile manipulators, and sometimes reset the environment.

Table 1 is important counterevidence to reading “77,000 episodes” as productive autonomous output: 73,293 scripted-policy trials had 21% success, 3,060 teleoperated trials 82%, and 936 RT-2 trials 4.7%. The authors attribute low RT-2 performance to differences between collection environments and its training/inference conditions. Their stated objective is diverse data collection, with a preliminary downstream-learning evaluation. Successful data generation, successful task execution, accepted customer output and profitable operation are four different outcomes.

For the six shared market cells, a proposed digital planner needs explicit state, command authority, validation and fallback. A low-level controller remains separate. The measurable contribution might be improved task selection, fewer interventions, faster recovery or better held-out performance. Each requires a target test; it cannot be inferred from model naming. Astranomy supplies a second, simulated control configuration and highlights why inference cost and timing must be separate from physical-policy quality.

#### Fleet, site, and enterprise operations

[Open-RMF interfacing](https://openrmf.readthedocs.io/en/latest/interfacing/index.html) documents fleet state, destination, pause/resume and path messages, plus door state and requests. A fleet that only reports state gives an orchestrator different options from one accepting paths or pause commands. Coordination therefore depends on negotiated interfaces and control rights, not just planning intelligence. The retrieved documentation is a mutable `latest` page, not a pinned deployment release.

The proposed full interface is ERP/WMS/MES orders, inventory and priorities → validated allocation or schedule → fleet manager → robot/building execution → acknowledged state and business-record reconciliation. The process owner defines priorities; the fleet manager controls movement; human staff resolve exceptions. Stale information, an unacknowledged instruction or duplicate transaction can break the chain. Timeouts, idempotency, reconciliation and escalation are proposed design requirements here, not claimed observations of a deployed LLM stack.

[SAP's May 2026 announcement](https://news.sap.com/2026/05/more-autonomous-supply-chain/) supplies intended planning, manufacturing, logistics, asset/service and ERP roles, with phased general availability through 2026. It supports identifying interfaces to investigate. It does not establish which named agent is available at a selected customer or a measured robotics productivity contribution.

The target-side boundary is stronger with [ChristianaCare's operator-authored account](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13048758/). Its grant-funded 2022–25 pilot used three cobots across more than 80 patient areas, reported more than 48,600 deliveries and tested EHR/elevator integrations. Operational value concentrated in nonurgent medication and equipment flows. Complex, time-sensitive nursing workflows were not meaningfully augmented. No LLM effect is isolated. For `CELL-M10-hospital-logistics-US-2025`, this argues for separate urgent and nonurgent cohorts and an explicit request/dispatch/infrastructure interface, rather than treating all hospital physical support as one eligible pool. A large delivery count is insufficient to infer nurse payroll savings.

#### Robotics research and development

DrEureka takes simulator code, an objective and safety instructions, generates candidate rewards, builds a reward-aware physics prior, proposes domain-randomization settings, trains policies and tests transfer. It used GPT-4, sampled 16 domain-randomization configurations and trained three seeds per configuration. The authors evaluated candidate policies in the real world. The comparisons show useful technical improvements in bounded locomotion and manipulation settings; they do not remove training or hardware-test needs.

The paper reports a three-hour runtime for its bounded domain-randomization comparison versus ten hours for CEM and twenty for BayRn. That is a method comparison, not a measured reduction in total humanoid engineering labor or development cost. It also explicitly lacks a systematic mechanism to select the most promising policy before deployment. Some ablations generated jerky, unsafe behavior that triggered controller protection. Candidate generation has value only when verification and release constraints are included.

The model consequence is to let an R&D intervention change a validated release's cost or delay only through an explicit critical path. Coding, reward search, training and physical validation can constrain different projects. If hardware testing remains binding, faster candidate generation may improve exploration but not calendar time. METR's measurement concerns reinforce recording total accepted engineering output and concurrent resource use rather than adding an unexplained “research acceleration” multiplier.

## 6. Digital infrastructure and physical production

Authoritative chapter: [research/digital_infrastructure.md](research/digital_infrastructure.md).

Research edition 0.1, retrieved 25 October 2026. This is the H1/H6 infrastructure contribution to the campaign, joined to I's configured-system supply analysis. Evidence records HI-001–HI-006 live in `data/digital_infrastructure_evidence.json`. Quantitative observations below do not calibrate the illustrative robot model.

### The decision

For a humanoid company, the relevant question is not whether AI infrastructure is growing. It is whether the particular compute, memory, power, manufacturing and operating complements required by a proposed production system will be available at acceptable cost and latency. The reverse question—whether robots can accelerate construction, manufacture or operation of AI infrastructure—needs its own service-level evidence. Neither direction follows automatically from growth in the other.

The current evidence supports a constrained, two-way relationship. Digital capability production already depends on large physical investments and grid connections. Robot policy development can use that infrastructure, but an onboard controller and a cloud accelerator do not consume interchangeable memory modules. Robotics might help selected infrastructure workflows, yet the public sources reviewed here do not measure a humanoid-induced reduction in data-center construction time or commissioning cost. That link remains a question, not a numerical feedback coefficient.

### What the latest infrastructure evidence establishes

The IEA's *Key Questions on Energy and AI*, published 16 April 2026, estimates global data-center electricity consumption at 485 TWh in 2025 and projects approximately 950 TWh in 2030. The latter is a central projection, not observed consumption or an independently booked pipeline. The population is all data centers, not exclusively LLM inference. Its estimate of 17% growth in total data-center electricity consumption in 2025 and 50% growth in AI-focused consumption describes different populations. Adding the two would double count the AI subset. [HI-001](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary)

The report describes simultaneous efficiency improvement and growth in more intensive workloads. It states that energy per individual AI task has fallen rapidly, while reasoning, video and agentic applications can use much more energy per request than simple text generation. These are not contradictory observations. Aggregate demand depends on service volume, output mix, quality requirements, retries and hardware/software efficiency. A cheap text query is therefore an inappropriate proxy for a long-horizon robot planning episode or an enterprise-agent workflow. No universal energy-per-token or energy-per-robot coefficient is inferred here.

IEA identifies grid access, energy equipment, advanced chips and high-bandwidth memory among constraints, and describes financing as consequential as projects exceed internally funded balance-sheet capacity. Its discussion of an HBM shortage extending at least through 2027 is a dated assessment, not a measured allocation to a robot OEM. The appropriate model input is an accessible qualified resource pool with lead times and contractual commitments, not a share of global semiconductor sales. Regional and application differences remain essential.

The October 15, 2026 TSMC management report supplies a more recent financial observation. It reports third-quarter consolidated capital expenditure of US$13.84 billion and year-to-date expenditure of US$40.64 billion. It reports 4.476 million 12-inch-equivalent wafer shipments in the quarter, with HPC representing 68% of net revenue. Those are distinct denominators: expenditure is not qualified capacity; wafer-equivalent shipments are not AI accelerators; HPC revenue is not exclusively robotics or even exclusively AI. The report attributes a sequential gross-margin decline partly to the initial N2 ramp. This is an actual example of new-process ramp affecting economics, not evidence for a universal robotics manufacturing learning rate. [HI-002](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/qr/phase4_reports/2026-10/f53a64fd40fc0b453f94e077e5aba8cad506f27b/3Q26%20ManagementReport.pdf)

The decision consequence is to separate four states in supplier diligence: announced investment, expenditure already made, qualified output, and output actually available to the customer. Even a profitable expanding supplier does not establish an individual robot firm's access to a specific grade, package or delivery window. Conversely, an aggregate AI bottleneck does not prove every embedded module is unavailable. The present evidence cannot quantify an OEM's priority, contracted price, inventory or qualification yield.

Micron's September 30, 2026 prepared remarks make the contractual allocation problem more concrete. Management says it has signed 26 multiyear take-or-pay strategic customer agreements, estimates they represent more than 35% of revenue through 2030, and reports US$32 billion of customer financial commitments, mostly deposits. Those are issuer disclosures and forward estimates with different meanings. Deposits cannot be added to future revenue as another economic benefit. The company says agreements cover the vast majority of its calendar-2027 HBM bit supply; that does not reveal an individual robot firm's allocation or establish a general LPDDR shortage coefficient. [HI-006](https://s25.q4cdn.com/621799436/files/doc_financials/2026/q4/Q4-FY26-Prepared-Remarks.pdf)

The same source distinguishes initial wafer output from meaningful production ramp several quarters later. Initial output dates for Idaho, Japan, Singapore and New York are management expectations spanning 2027–2030, not current qualified deliveries. This supports a supply-model structure with lead times, ramp and pre-existing commitments, but does not calibrate the timing of a humanoid OEM's production. It also shows a financing mechanism: customer commitments may support supplier investment while tying up customer cash and limiting uncommitted supply available to entrants. Whether a new robot company can secure similar terms is unknown.

Micron predicts that future humanoids could have memory requirements comparable to highly autonomous vehicles. That is a supplier's prospective market thesis, not a measured humanoid bill of materials. The configured products below are concrete counterweights to converting that thesis into a fixed per-robot memory requirement. Future architectures might use more onboard memory, smaller policies, remote inference or heterogeneous processors; workload and latency evidence must decide.

### Memory and compute are configuration-specific

The NVIDIA product pages retrieved at kickoff make the nonfungibility concrete. Jetson AGX Orin 64GB is specified with 64GB LPDDR5 and 204.8 GB/s memory bandwidth, with configurable module power in the 15–60W range. Jetson T5000 is specified with 128GB LPDDR5X and 273 GB/s at 40–130W. H200 instead uses 141GB HBM3e with 4.8 TB/s bandwidth. These are manufacturer specifications of different products, not measured performance in a matched robotics workload. [HI-003](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/autonomous-machines/embedded-systems/jetson-orin/), [HI-004](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/autonomous-machines/embedded-systems/jetson-thor/), [HI-005](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h200/)

Several comparisons would be misleading. Orin TOPS and Thor FP4 TFLOPS are not a common precision/performance measure; a ratio of their advertised values does not identify a robot-speed improvement. Module power is not total robot power, which also includes actuation, sensors and other loads. Installed memory is not all available to model weights: runtime, sensor processing, intermediate activations and state compete for it. Remote inference may relax onboard capacity but introduces connectivity, latency, service-dependence and recurring-cost questions. An inference-capable module does not establish qualified operation in heat, vibration, dust or the target software stack.

The Orin live page itself contains differing introductory and tabular specifications for some NX/Nano variants. This edition uses the clearly identified AGX Orin 64GB fields and does not combine values from different variants or modes. Product-page claims remain dated snapshots; exact carrier board, JetPack release, model precision, thermal design, duty cycle and actual robot installation remain necessary before modeling throughput. The H200 page's illustrative performance comparisons change batch sizes and workload settings; they are not matched per-request latency experiments for robotics.

### Three bounded mechanisms and their counterarguments

**Lower reasoning cost may improve robot economics without changing robot capability.** The source-side evidence supports hardware/software efficiency improvement and changing workload mix. The target requires measured calls per accepted service, retry rates, latency, support and the relevant tariff. A counterexample is a mechanically constrained workflow where inference is a small cost and cheaper calls do not change accepted throughput. Permitted use: scenario structure and a research question, not a copied cost decline.

**Physical infrastructure can delay digital contributions to robots.** Training or remote inference may depend on constrained datacenter capacity and financing. Yet a particular deployed policy may run onboard, use existing contracted capacity, or be small enough that other constraints dominate. This mechanism should be tested with local versus remote architectures and documented allocations. It cannot justify imposing the same HBM delay on all Jetson-equipped robots.

**Robots might relieve infrastructure labor bottlenecks.** Candidate market stages include construction installation, equipment manufacturing, inspection and maintenance. A faster robot process only accelerates the completed facility if that process is on the critical path, its quality and permits remain acceptable, and upstream equipment or grid delays do not bind instead. Existing operator evidence of inspection or a construction task demonstration does not establish whole-project acceleration. The falsifier is a matched project comparison in which the robotic stage improves but energization or output does not. No positive feedback growth exponent is assigned.

### Strategic implications and remaining evidence

For RQ-SC-05, qualify a workload/configuration before selecting an onboard compute roadmap. Memory, latency, power and thermal requirements should precede peak-compute comparisons. For RQ-LLM-01/06, separate capital announcements from operational supply and customer access. For RQ-LLM-09 and the construction/utility cells, test an explicit critical path before claiming that physical automation accelerates AI development. These conclusions narrow the scenarios even without estimating a global robotics market.

The next useful public-evidence step is a matched robotics workload on a pinned embedded module and software stack, documenting memory use, end-to-end latency, sustained power and accepted output. A parallel supplier step is to identify disclosed product-specific qualification and delivery evidence. Neither an aggregate capex series nor a product page supplies those measurements. Public data may not resolve OEM allocations or negotiated commercial terms; those fields stay null and become later access-dependent questions.

### Findings that change deployment judgment

The strongest evidence supports choosing a complete service configuration before choosing a robot morphology. In the cases below, mobility, measurement, interpretation, action and business value are successive conditions. Success at an earlier condition cannot substitute for failure at a later one. PG&E's drone test is particularly informative: an inspection aircraft can fly successfully while producing unusable inspection imagery. A model crediting every successful mission with a completed inspection would overstate useful output before reaching any debate about manufacturing cost.

Quadrupeds have direct productive roles in manufacturing and infrastructure. They need not be justified as a temporary precursor to humanoids. The examined customer accounts support sensing, facility information and remote inspection roles. They do not establish a general replacement rate for production or maintenance workers. A humanoid strategy should therefore distinguish entry into existing sensing services from expansion into manipulation and intervention. The second step changes the accepted output, qualification requirements and physical interfaces; it is not a free extension of the first.

The sources also separate available robots from supported productive capacity. Site mapping, route validation, networking, operator training, human review and spare parts can bind even when chassis supply is ample. A manufacturing announcement does not remove those constraints. Conversely, a more capable policy may widen the feasible service set while increasing onboard memory, power, sensors or support requirements. The same advance can expand addressability and make an existing configuration inadequate.

### Service boundaries and the shared atlas

Cases link to the common atlas without adding independent labor totals. BMW supports the manufacturing anchor `CELL-M05-auto-assembly-US-2025`, but its observed service is UK plant maintenance and information capture, not US assembly output. National Grid, Equinor and PG&E inform `CELL-M03-utility-inspection-US-2025`; Equinor's CO₂ facility is an infrastructure comparator rather than an electrical-utility employment observation. Rwanda informs `CELL-M10-hospital-logistics-US-2025`, with interfacility distribution kept separate from movement inside a hospital. Agricultural application is a bounded service-business comparator within the atlas's agriculture family, not a seventh deep market estimate.

Three rules follow. Keep foreign evidence in its observed geography. Preserve the historical service window instead of treating retrieval in 2026 as current field performance. Allocate an inspection, delivery or treated application once even when several robots and people contribute. A drone observing an asset, a quadruped collecting another viewpoint and an engineer approving maintenance can be one service chain, not three sources of avoided labor.

#### PHY-Q03 — Northern Lights: commissioning and operational authority

Later results are mixed rather than uniformly negative. Vendor A was disqualified before Demonstration 3. Vendor B met the Templeton flight criteria but failed Silverado criteria; image capture was not evaluated because its known sensor hardware remained below PG&E standards. The distribution-sensor alert integration was deferred. These findings appear on printed pages 17–20 (one-based PDF pages 21–24), including Tables 5–6 and section 4.5.

This is a field demonstration with meaningful adverse findings, not evidence that all docked drones fail. The aircraft, sensors, communications, dock placement and inspection requirements jointly determine service feasibility. Later hardware may change that result, but cannot retroactively improve the tested cohort. A drone fleet model should measure accepted imagery separately from completed routes, then include review and any repeat visits.

The permitted transfer to ground robotics is structural: measure the entire service, including sensor quality and workflow. The numerical failure rate does not transfer. A camera on a grounded platform faces different standoff, motion, reach and access limits. Likewise, historical BVLOS approval does not establish economic utilization. Permission, installed infrastructure, technical performance and customer demand remain separate gates.

#### PHY-D02 — Rwanda: observed service outcomes, unresolved cost-effectiveness

Nisingizwe and colleagues studied 20 facilities using 2015–2019 administrative data and drone deliveries during March 2017–December 2019. The initial edition used the PubMed abstract; this revision checks the publisher full text retrieved through Exa. Road times are estimated because planned collection of hospital road records was prevented by COVID-19 restrictions. There is no control group. The 67% reduction in expirations is an interrupted-time-series estimate per facility at 12 months, not a randomized treatment effect. [PHY-E05: full article, Methods, Results and Discussion](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/langlo/article/PIIS2214-109X(22)00048-1/fulltext).

The time boundaries materially change interpretation. The reported mean of 49.6 minutes includes order preparation and packaging; the 41-minute median used in the facility comparison excludes it. The principal road comparator is a hospital vehicle making a round trip to collect blood. The paper reports median advantages of 79 minutes against CBT driving estimates and 98 minutes against Google Maps estimates for this arrangement. A hypothetical center-origin, one-way road service reduces the corresponding median advantages to 19 and 28.5 minutes. These are different service organizations and statistics, not interchangeable speed multipliers. Table 1, Table 2 and the adjacent Results paragraphs provide the locators.

The authors also state in the final Discussion paragraph that drone delivery was more expensive than road delivery in Rwanda at publication. That adverse cost direction belongs in the dossier even though the paper supplies no matched dollar cost or provider margin. Faster delivery and less waste could still have health or economic value, but the cost-effectiveness calculation remains unresolved. No accepted clinical-SLA fraction or causal mortality benefit is established.

This case is materially stronger evidence for a useful delivered service than a flight demonstration, but it is not a provider-margin study. The mechanism includes geography, dispatch, perishable inventory and timely availability. Comparing only aircraft travel to a courier's journey omits launch preparation, receiving, reserve capacity and inventory design. Counting reduced waste once in the logistics model and again as a separate platform benefit would overstate value.

The humanoid analogy largely fails at the physical mechanism. Aerial transport can bypass road geometry; better ground manipulation cannot inherit that shortcut. The useful research question is whether a different production network changes service quality and total cost. For US hospital operations, indoor handoffs, interfacility deliveries and stock positioning need distinct scopes. This study does not supply an indoor robot productivity coefficient or a current US health-logistics adoption rate.

#### PHY-D03 — Agricultural services: activity and illustrative budgets stay separate

North Dakota's regulator reports licensed unmanned application activity for 2025. This is an exposure denominator, not a profitability estimate, and repeated versus unique treated acreage remains unresolved. Missouri Extension's March 2025 T40 budgets separately illustrate farmer ownership and custom-service configurations. Its modeled costs are preserved as hypothetical nominal USD per applied acre; they are not observed costs for the North Dakota population. [PHY-E06: NDAC](https://aero.nd.gov/studies/aviation-statistics/); [PHY-E07: Missouri Extension](https://extension.missouri.edu/publications/g1274).

Missouri Table 2 changes the system as well as annual acreage: one versus two aircraft, one versus two crew members, and different driving, cleaning and wage assumptions. Its cost difference cannot identify a scale effect holding the production configuration fixed.

The operating-model contrast is valuable. Farm ownership uses equipment within one production business; a custom operator must obtain customers and schedule work across sites. Higher annual utilization can spread ownership costs, yet narrow application windows, travel and weather can prevent the apparent capacity from being sold or used. A two-aircraft business is not automatically twice the useful capacity of one aircraft if crews, loading or time windows bind.

The source's discussion associates its roughly 980-acre threshold with inconsistent comparator wording; this edition therefore does not adopt that threshold as a validated break-even. A defensible matched comparison also needs agronomic efficacy, coverage, timing and drift conditions. The analogy to inspection-service entrants is conditional fixed-cost spreading under usable demand, not a transferable cost curve or proof that robotics-as-a-service is more profitable.

### Matched embodiment comparisons and transfer tests

`PHY-C01` compares one accepted utility condition inspection, with equal coverage, resolution and response interval. A digital-improved human process, fixed sensing, wheeled inspection, quadruped, aerial sensing and a possible humanoid are alternatives only where they meet those constraints. Fixed sensing can be attractive for stable viewpoints; accessible level routes can remove the need for legged mobility. Stairs, terrain, energized access and hazardous-zone requirements can change feasibility. A sensing-only service provides no evidence that humanoid hands justify their additional burden. Adding repair or manipulation changes the service and requires another comparison.

`PHY-C02` compares one usable blood order delivered by its deadline. Road courier plus digital dispatch, aerial transport with ground handling and local inventory/replenishment are competing networks. Ground robots may complement indoor handoffs but are not established substitutes for the rural aerial leg. Road access, urgency, payload eligibility and sufficiently dense demand can reverse the preferred arrangement. Neither comparison has enough whole-system cost evidence for a numeric ranking. The correct present result is a feasibility map and break-even condition, not invented prices.

The five structured transfer tests preserve separate source and target evidence. PG&E supports separating navigation from useful information. Rwanda rejects copying an aerial geography advantage into humanoid labor productivity. PX4/ODRI versus Unitree supports testing artifact-specific engineering reuse while rejecting blanket openness. The [FAA's July 2024 UTM authorization](https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/faa-makes-drone-history-dallas-area) motivates shared intentions and operating authority for mixed fleets, but neither airspace rules nor throughput coefficients transfer. Agricultural budgets provide a fixed-cost and usable-demand mechanism, without empirical cost parameters for inspection robots. Each card states a target prediction and falsifier; none calibrates the common model.

### Supply-to-deployment model implications

The common engine owns the time-indexed scenario; this contribution supplies its evidence boundaries. Use distinct compatible component pools, reserve service spares for installed cohorts, apply assembly acceptance loss once, retain lead times and finished inventory, and commission only within site/support capacity. Replacements are not net additions. Qualified receipts cannot be generated from announced annual capacity, and net receipts cannot have external commitments deducted twice.

An explicit supported-fleet target needs a backcast of surviving stock, retirements, replacement and expansion units, required accepted completions, assembly starts, component receipts, service inventory and commissioning/support labor. That is necessary-condition logic. It does not prove orders, customer finance or accepted-service demand. The model agent uses virtual configurations for synthetic coefficients; the named G1/Go2 maps must not be treated as cost-calibrated versions of those virtual systems.

Three decision changes follow. First, product strategy should fund the measurement and workflow bottleneck before assuming extra robot capability creates value. Second, an inspection-service newco needs site access, contracts, review and dense support, not only robot capital. Third, expansion planning should test a support-bound case alongside component qualification and manufacturing delays. The available evidence permits these causal structures but does not establish which bottleneck dominates across the economy.

### What the scenario contrasts teach

The delayed-supply case ends with 60 active expected units versus 44 in the reference, but produces only 249,690 cumulative baseline-equivalent hours versus 376,256. Later cohorts avoid some endpoint retirements. A terminal fleet snapshot therefore gives a misleading ranking of productive contribution; the full time path matters.

Ample supply with unready customers produces 35,302 cumulative baseline-equivalent hours. Service stress produces 68,534. These are constructed counterexamples to the proposition that manufacturing capacity alone determines useful scale. They do not estimate the prevalence or magnitude of those bottlenecks in actual markets.

Digital-first produces 297,633 robot-delivered baseline-equivalent hours, compared with 376,256 in the reference. Less robot deployment is not necessarily a welfare loss: the human-led alternative also improves, changing the incremental economic case. This preset changes both human time reduction and robot coordination; it does not isolate either channel. Hold coordination fixed for a one-control comparison of the human-time mechanism. The model does not estimate economy-wide welfare from these scenario outputs.

In the separate monthly entrant case, delayed incumbent response requires peak funding of $516,500; prompt response requires $716,000 and exposes a $116,000 gap under the same assumed commitment. Equal robot access does not guarantee equal commercial survival. This comparison isolates an assumed response mechanism; it is not evidence that those prices, demand shares or financing commitments are attainable. Its robots are not separately allocated from the annual fleet resource pool, so the two paths must not be added as jointly feasible output.


## 9. What remains unresolved

The sources establish bounded workflows, relationships and constraints. Public disclosures often do not establish accepted-output exposure, all human support, retained deployment, negotiated prices, avoidable staffing, qualified supply or complete costs. These gaps limit market ranking and investment conclusions. A functioning mechanism model does not resolve them by assigning synthetic inputs.

The next research priorities are: reconcile field service/intervention denominators; challenge shared-resource and financial accounting; validate digital coordination against a strong simpler baseline; test customer entry against a prompt incumbent with equal technology access; qualify specific supply/workload dependencies; deepen labor crosswalks without invented physical shares; and expand non-Spot operator and nonadoption evidence. Each has a bounded action, evidence need and stopping condition in the continuing roadmap.

The acceptance register records individual requirements and limitations. Source counts and view counts do not themselves establish substantive completion. The campaign remains active within the original elapsed-time window, with interruptions and next actions recorded honestly in the handoff. At the deadline, the strongest verified edition must be packaged with any coverage gaps, rather than backdating work or resetting the week.
