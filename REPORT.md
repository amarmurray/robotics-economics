## 1. Executive argument and strategic implications

Authoritative chapter: [research/integrated_decisions.md](research/integrated_decisions.md).

The new 7 November decision campaign adds [three provisional deployment and entry packs](research/deployment_entry_decisions.md), preceded by a reassessment of all six deep cells. The strongest immediate questions are residual paid coverage in hospitals, residual mandatory visits in utilities, and workcell/asset-generation qualification in automotive production. These are conditional research decisions; the integrated execution bridges and challenge cases remain work in progress.

This campaign asks where a humanoid company should deploy, what must improve, and which organizational and commercial arrangements can sustain useful production. The early evidence supports a conditional strategy organized around customer services and their bottlenecks. It does not yet support a calibrated economy-wide penetration forecast or a claim that one morphology will replace a fixed share of physical labor.

The November restaurant comparison sharpens the customer hurdle: model-led folding must compete on the full accepted napkin service against qualified specialist equipment and a measured human baseline. Advertised speed and base wages do not reveal a removable paid shift. [FG-CMP-03](research/customer_value.md#restaurant-napkin-workflow-comparison) defines loading, acceptance, rework, handoff and support plus separate customer/provider cash and consolidated resources. It narrows the required evidence without establishing an ROI or winner.

The US labor atlas establishes a broad economic frame. The six deeper cells are automotive assembly/material presentation, third-party logistics handoffs, contract floor cleaning, hospital internal logistics, construction installation and utility inspection. These are deliberately contrasting production systems. They differ in access, quality standards, process authority, environmental variation, customer relationships and the alternatives already available. Cases outside the United States inform mechanisms but do not become US labor observations.

A production system is valuable when it delivers the required service, at the required quality and time, within the available resources and customer demand. Robot capability is one part of that system. A successful route, attractive demonstration, larger fleet, financing announcement or open repository can be relevant evidence without establishing the completed service or its economics. The dossier chapters retain those distinctions so that a commercial decision can be traced back to its actual evidentiary basis.

### A deployment portfolio, rather than a generic robot market

The six cells suggest different bounded entry questions. For automotive assembly, the question is whether a configured system can reliably complete a defined sequence within the production process, and how that compares with fixed or specialized automation. Public pilot evidence can help establish the workflow; it does not disclose a plant-wide labor substitution rate. For contract logistics, the end customer, site operator and robotics supplier may be different organizations. A commercial RaaS agreement is important evidence of an arrangement, while undisclosed price, support and intervention terms still prevent a complete investment case.

Floor cleaning illustrates why simpler embodiments and contract duration matter. The useful outcome is an accepted cleaned area under the site's service standard. A specialized machine can be a stronger competitor than a more general robot. A facilities-service contract may end sooner than the robot asset's economic life, creating utilization and residual-value risks even if a steady-state task comparison looks attractive. Transferring the asset to a service provider reallocates those risks; it does not remove them from the production system.

Hospital logistics tests the difference between a bounded delivery service and an occupation. Useful nonurgent transport can coexist with limited ability to perform time-sensitive or complex nursing workflows. Doors, elevators, handoffs and support work can decide the value of a mobile system. The public evidence should therefore change the definition of addressable work before it changes a productivity coefficient.

Construction installation involves changing sites, access, sequencing and multiple contractors. A bounded technical demonstration cannot establish a general site deployment rate. The next question is which workflow can be repeated with an explicit acceptance standard and sufficient authority over the surrounding process. Utility inspection similarly requires more than access: measurements must be usable, reviewed and linked to an appropriate decision or action. Fixed sensing, wheeled platforms, quadrupeds, drones and human teams may substitute for one part of the chain or complement different parts.

These distinctions prevent a false aggregation. A robot supplier's potential sales, a contractor's labor bill, the customer's value added and the final product's revenue are not additive measures of the same opportunity. The atlas identifies employment coverage and residuals; it does not turn headcounts into physical-work hours by assuming a universal time share. Competing systems share the same underlying work, and service providers can appear in several value chains without creating several labor pools.

### Capability progress changes a constrained production function

The capability evidence makes hardware generation, policy version, task distribution, assistance and observation period explicit. The relevant improvement is a change in accepted output, intervention, adaptation or cost under comparable conditions. Neither a growing demonstration dataset nor a benchmark score establishes a general improvement exponent across markets. Transfer can be useful within a tested setting and fail in another, including when a larger mixed dataset performs worse on a particular target.

The modeling consequence is to keep base capability, directed learning, coordination, hardware qualification and customer readiness separately inspectable. A policy improvement may widen technical eligibility while leaving manufacturing, commissioning, support or demand as the binding constraint. A new generation does not automatically upgrade installed fleets. A replacement consumes supply without increasing net active stock. A component commitment is not a finished robot; a finished robot is not a commissioned customer service.

This is also why learning value needs a counterfactual. If useful experience changes future operating cash flows, its benefit belongs in the comparison of policies with and without that learning, including collection and validation costs. Adding a separate data premium would count the same gain again. Information that changes a later investment decision is another channel and should not be confused with a measured policy improvement.

### The digital counterfactual moves too

Workstream H materially changes the robotics comparison. Human-led and incumbent operations can improve through software, models, routing, planning and better information before or alongside a robot deployment. Comparing a future robot with a permanently frozen human baseline overstates the incremental case. The report therefore uses the four-way logic of current operations, additional digital improvement, additional robotics, and their combination, with matched output and quality.

A combined system can outperform the sum of isolated changes, underperform it, or merely count the same removed bottleneck twice. The common engine exposes a synthetic interaction rather than copying an effect size from a digital study. Evidence from customer support, software development, professional-task benchmarks, simulated enterprises and embodied control remains tied to its own population, system and evaluation conditions.

Coordination is similarly conditional. The updated peer-reviewed agent study narrows the earlier blog's claims and explicitly treats its capability threshold as a within-domain selection rule. That finding supports testing architectures against a strong simpler baseline. It does not prove a universal benefit from adding agents, and it does not transfer a numerical coefficient into a physical fleet. A digital planner, conventional scheduler, enterprise transaction system and robot controller also operate at different timescales and authority boundaries. Connecting them is an engineering and operating claim that requires more than citing each component separately.

### Customer formation and provider strategy are different decisions

A customer-side AI-native newco sells an end-market product or service. Its advantage could come from a different operating design, an earlier adoption window, or access to capacity when human labor is constrained. Those are distinct hypotheses. None follows simply from access to the same robot that an incumbent can also buy. The comparison must include a prompt-adopting incumbent, customer acquisition, ramp losses, working capital, demand that can actually be won and delivered, and the possibility of price or retention responses.

A holding company that creates operating businesses, one that acquires existing firms, a standalone entrant and a hybrid face different constraints. Acquisition can bring customers and operating access while importing process and contractual commitments. Greenfield formation can allow redesign while requiring new customers and complementary assets. Thrive's disclosed structure is a useful organizational comparator, but its financing and digital deployment claims do not establish the economics or financeability of a humanoid-enabled industrial entrant.

The provider decision is separate: sell equipment, finance assets, license software, integrate, manage operations, form partnerships or operate part of the customer's value chain. These choices move cash requirements, operating risks and control rights between actors. They do not create free capital or necessarily change total production value. The published BrainOS terms make a further distinction concrete: ownership of operational data is different from access, improvement-use rights and exclusivity. A customer cannot assume it retains an exclusive learning advantage merely because it owns the data.

### Qualified supply and supported operation limit scale

The physical and digital supply analyses converge on a practical rule: qualify the resource and the workflow before pooling a constraint. Embedded LPDDR memory and datacenter HBM have different configurations and applications. Actuator specifications, environmental qualification, spare compatibility and field support likewise cannot be replaced with an undifferentiated robot count. Product specifications establish an architecture; they do not disclose manufacturing cost, yield or available qualified capacity.

Supplier investment and customer commitments can support expansion but have different accounting meanings. Micron's take-or-pay arrangements illustrate how supply assurance and supplier financing can coexist with customer commitments and restricted uncommitted availability. TSMC's reported capital expenditure illustrates investment already made, without identifying a robot OEM's allocation. Deposits, expenditure, revenue and delivered output must remain separate. A supply backcast gives necessary conditions for a scale target; it does not establish demand or adoption.

For a humanoid company, these findings favor a sequence of testable service propositions with explicit alternatives, measurable acceptance, known process authority and visible support obligations. They do not establish a universal sector ranking. The next investment-worthy evidence is a matched cohort showing accepted output, all human support, retention, full costs and rights. Public disclosures often establish only some of those links. Where they stop, the report preserves a bounded conclusion and a precise next question rather than inventing a margin or penetration rate.

### What the deeper investigations change

The hospital follow-up strengthens the case for defining service eligibility before discussing automation rates. The Guro study excludes urgent requests from its evaluated mission population, while routine handling and a trained accompanying safety operator remain part of the observed arrangement. Its reported success percentage disagrees with the ratio implied by its eligible and completed counts. The report retains both and does not select a convenient reliability coefficient. The Qilu comparison supplies distribution and retrieval phase timings; those shorter phases cannot establish whole-shift payroll removal. The operational question becomes whether a proposed service can satisfy its acceptance standard after counting all residual handling, oversight, failure and recovery work. The source-specific denominators and unresolved economic methods appear in the hospital follow-up in the customer chapter.

The non-Spot quadruped investigation reaches a similarly useful but narrower conclusion than a broad inspection-adoption claim. TenneT's operator-coauthored offshore study shows that a legged platform can traverse and inspect portions of a difficult environment. Instrument visibility varies by room; inaccessible doors, prohibited areas, safety rules and unfinished integration constrain what service the platform can complete. Later onshore pilot material shows additional inspection development, but project duration and a programmed mission repertoire do not disclose productive exposure, retained fleet use or realized avoided labor. The evidence supports a configured inspection proposition with explicit exclusions. It does not establish abandonment, successful scale, or the economics of replacing a human visit. Both possibilities remain open where the operational record stops.

The learning-data follow-up changes the denominator for a capability-investment decision. Cobalt's cloud-cost comparison concerns one collection-stage resource, and its simulated collection and separate physical evaluation have different populations. DexWild demonstrates useful human-data collection and robot-grounding experiments, while changes in dataset size and within-batch sampling mix are different interventions. Neither paper establishes a general price per unit of commercially validated capability. A robotics company choosing between teleoperation, portable human capture, simulation and additional robot deployment needs the full incremental collection, filtering, training, recovery-coverage and held-out validation cost at a fixed target. Lowering one input burden may be valuable; the value depends on which bottleneck actually limits the target policy.

The benchmark reconstruction also makes a material comparison less ambiguous. TheAgentCompany's archived displayed costs include non-perfect attempts, whereas Astranomy's reported conditional cost covers a successful subset. A cheaper-looking number can therefore measure a different event. The reconstructed table improves source-side accounting without resolving omitted infrastructure, other model use, human work or the executed monetary-limit configuration. Astranomy's paused simulation further separates action-clock success from elapsed-time service in a changing environment. Those distinctions constrain any attempt to price physical orchestration from an agent benchmark.

Finally, the pinned coordination interfaces locate a concrete systems question: what is known after a request is accepted, a response times out, or cancellation is acknowledged? Admission, assignment, execution and reconciliation have distinct authorities. A retry may repeat an action whose first acknowledgement was lost; cancellation acceptance need not reverse partial effects or establish a physical stop. A durable business identifier helps maintain intent, but it cannot alone make an external action and a local record one atomic transaction. A credible coordination contribution needs explicit failure handling and a strong deterministic baseline before a language model is added. Local fault tests can establish software behavior under declared assumptions; a compatible installed stack and target operating data are still needed for production claims.

The onboard-compute follow-up gives that timing boundary a concrete implementation example. NVIDIA's published Thor/GR00T optimization recipe is inspectable and reports a large reduction in its component-sum timing. The released measurement code, repeated observation, warm-up rules and conflicting source tables constrain the claim: it does not establish sustained sensor-to-actuator deadlines, runtime memory headroom or the target robot's accepted service. Module specifications and a faster benchmark remain useful inputs to qualification. A deployment decision still needs the exact workload, concurrent perception, action horizon, thermal/power conditions and closed-loop outcomes. The [onboard qualification analysis](research/onboard_workload_qualification.md) records the missing run artifacts and preserves the distinct task populations.

### Decisions to make next, and the evidence that could reverse them

The next deployment decision should begin with one accepted service and a credible alternative, chosen from the broader atlas. Define the customer, process authority, output and quality standard, timing, exclusions and residual human work. Then identify the mechanism that could make the proposed configuration better: a different operating design, improved technical coverage, lower total service cost, additional useful capacity, or a temporary adoption lead. Each mechanism has a different counterexample. A simpler machine may defeat a general embodiment on stable work; a digitally improved human process may remove the apparent savings; a prompt incumbent may eliminate an entrant's timing advantage; unsupported operating work may consume the released labor.

Next, separate economic value from commercial terms and liquidity. Match all alternatives on the same delivered service before allocating price, title, maintenance, control and data rights. Test the cash dates as well as lifetime margins. A refundable deposit, asset lease or supplier commitment changes who bears funding and performance exposure, and may change whether execution is possible. It is not an additional resource saving. The current annual fleet and separate monthly entrant reference illustrate parts of this logic, but their separate resource pools still prevent a joint financeability conclusion.

Research should then target the observation most likely to change that decision. For an inspection proposition, retained measurement quality and avoided follow-up visits may matter more than another mission count. For hospital logistics, complete handling and recovery exposure may dominate a faster transport phase. For an entrant, customer qualification, collections, retention and funded commissioning may matter more than the next robot-price announcement. For a coordination contribution, a reconciled adverse trace may be more informative than another agent score. Public-source work should stop at a documented missing artifact when additional searches merely repeat the same disclosure. Target-site measurement or practitioner validation can follow when separately authorized.

The accepted learning sequence can begin from this report while the campaign continues. Amar's first task is to explain these boundaries, challenge the six initial theses and identify the smallest observation that would change a preferred course. Understanding is not inferred from having a dashboard or reading a report. The thesis register and guided walkthrough supply prompts; they do not predetermine a startup, employer, investment or technical contribution.

#### Hospital logistics — qualify the entire obligation

The accepted service is a uniquely requested permitted item received by the designated person in the required condition and custody state before its urgency-specific deadline. The demand ledger includes ineligible requests, urgent work, deferrals, failed attempts and human fallback. A successful selected robot journey is only one part of that obligation.

Existing Guro evidence distinguishes routine loading and receipt, a trained accompanying operator, and corrective failures. Its conflicting overall success figures remain unresolved. Qilu supplies a manual comparator and lower reported transport-phase means, but its accessible methods do not establish the complete staffing and ROI boundary. The ChristianaCare intervention-count conflict remains open. These studies support different mechanisms and populations; none calibrates a general US nursing-labor saving. See [the denominator audit](research/field_support_denominators.md).

A new adverse workforce account sharpens the qualification question. WSNA reports that members at Tacoma General and Good Samaritan experienced obstructive robot movement and inter-floor escort requirements without meaningful time savings. Its March 2026 article also reports a 2025 withdrawal, partly relying on newspaper reporting; a direct operator closeout was not retrieved. The account is evidence of attributed worker experience and a reported exit, with unspecified respondent selection and exposure. It does not identify a causal failure rate, contract return or the reason for withdrawal. [FSD-E005; WSNA](https://www.wsna.org/news/2026/saying-goodbye-to-moxi-the-ai-powered-robot)

The first comparator is a digitally improved human courier: common dispatch, route batching, congestion information and electronic receipt. Measure it on the same request population. A specialized wheeled carrier is the next alternative where elevators, load, custody and access are qualified. A mobile manipulator or humanoid earns inclusion only when its extra handling or access ability closes an important service gap. A mixed system can assign routine work to robots and urgent/excluded work to people, but the residual roster is still paid. No free digital or human fallback is assumed.

**Provisional recommendation:** proceed with the analytical qualification of a bounded nonurgent workflow and its improved human comparator; wait on scale entry or payroll substitution. A deployment case passes its proceed gate only with complete request-level acceptance, all support work, authorized site/process control and funded fallback. This is a research recommendation, not initiation of a field trial. Reject a specific case that misses mandatory service or assumes removal of a shift that must remain for urgent work. A credible reversal toward automation requires either an actual avoidable paid commitment or independently valued additional service capacity after support. Digital-human improvement, unavoidable escort or low eligible demand can reverse it again.

#### Utility inspection — separate information from the residual visit

The accepted service is a required asset/measurement set, at the specified quality and interval, with reviewer disposition. Maintenance authorization and completed physical intervention are downstream activities. A correctly interpreted absence of an anomaly can be useful information; the model must not assign every anomaly an avoided failure loss.

TenneT's historical coverage results and selected-route planning show why traversing a site and reading selected instruments do not imply complete inspection coverage. Point visibility, doors, operating restrictions, expert interpretation and the original mandatory route remain material. The later public planning record leaves financial results unfilled. These observations are preserved in [the operator follow-up](research/quadruped_operator_followup.md). A targeted later-operator search did not retrieve a new closeout; TenneT's site was blocked to the retrieval tool. This establishes a disclosure/access gap, not abandonment or continuing deployment. The earlier pilot searches are not restarted as a source-count exercise.

Compare improved human inspection and digital reporting with fixed sensing, wheeled systems where routes permit, configured quadrupeds where terrain or viewpoints matter, and mixed aerial/ground/human work. A drone delivering different measurements is a complement, not a cheaper substitute. A manipulator may improve door or instrument access but requires its own qualified configuration and support economics. Retain calibration, communications, charging, review, exceptions and corrective work under every relevant alternative.

**Provisional recommendation:** prioritize a coverage-and-disposition service contract. A monitoring case can pass its proceed gate with valued incremental information, complete measurement quality and explicit operating responsibility even if payroll stays constant. Wait on replicated scale economics until recurring exposure, support, costs, rights, retention and accessible supply are known. Reject the labor-removal claim when the same paid visit remains mandatory; reject a quantified avoided-outage claim without a defensible counterfactual. These rejections do not imply that added monitoring is worthless. Fixed sensing can become preferable for stable accessible points; quadrupeds can become preferable when mobility closes enough required work to avoid a whole visit at acceptable total burden. Mixed service remains plausible when residual work is substantial.

Welding, complete vehicles and downstream assembly are separate activity. Production contribution is useful evidence of context; it is not the value of an entire car produced by a robot.

#### The digital baseline and coordination burden

Each decision needs four matched branches: human work with existing conventional systems; that work with a specified additional digital intervention; the robot-assisted process with its required embedded software; and the combined process with the same additional intervention. The robot increment after digital improvement is `Y11 − Y01`; interaction is `Y11 − Y10 − Y01 + Y00`. Keep output, cash and resources as separate outcomes. Missing or infeasible branches remain missing. Savings that overlap between dispatch software, process redesign and robotics are booked once.

The current transaction fixture establishes failure-state reasoning under a cooperative mock lookup/finality contract. Its naive resend baseline is deliberately weak. It cannot establish the value of multiple agents against a competent conventional integration. The next comparison must give a deterministic scheduler and any proposed agent architecture identical task information, qualifications, resource availability, deadlines and recovery authority. Both must use the same durable identity and execution constraints; proposal quality must not be confused with permissions or completion. The strong conventional baseline should optimize the declared small workload where exact solution is tractable and replan under the same observed events. All proposal, verification, support and delay costs must be charged. Until an actual versioned agent is evaluated, a proposal replay is a mechanism test, not measured LLM uplift.

Similarly, model/R&D improvement needs a chain from generated candidate to validated physical capability. Log code or reward candidates, rejected proposals, simulation exposure, robot-data rights, training resources, held-out qualification, and delivery of compatible updates to the affected cohort. A larger dataset, cheaper collection stage or better simulation score does not itself create accepted service. Keep no-learning and no-transfer worlds. Useful experience requires rights and validation; it never produces a separate financial premium on top of an already counted capability gain.

This H work joins the existing digital ecosystem, infrastructure, learning-data and onboard-workload evidence. Peak compute specifications and summed component timings remain insufficient for sustained deadline qualification. Embedded memory, remote inference and training resources retain their distinct grades and allocations. No new model, GPU or robot run is authorized or claimed by the CPU research fixture.

| Fleet/cohort | Reuse actual commissioned/supported unit IDs. Retire, repair, replace and upgrade explicitly; preserve custody, title and configuration vintage. | Available robots created by a financial contract or old-generation evidence applied to new units |
| Architecture costs | Resolve each architecture's adaptation, training, inference, support and license obligation once. Allocate startup costs and monthly resources on declared dates; fees cancel only at the proper consolidation boundary. | Adding an allocated lifetime cost to already-paid monthly investment, or fees to the same provider resources |
| Support and learning | Allocate support and validation from finite shared pools before expansion. Tie learning/updates to eligible data, rights, paid resources, target evaluation and compatible cohorts. | Free maintenance, free learning, cross-embodiment transfer by label or duplicate data value |
| Finance | Fund a draw from a real counterparty; accrue interest by a stated convention, settle dated obligations, mature principal and apply an explicit default/cure/recovery policy. Keep liabilities after a missed payment. | Turning a commitment, receivable or unfunded loan into cash, or deleting an insolvent party's obligations |
| Reinvestment | Allow new starts only after operating/support and policy reserves, and after actual available funds pay the required obligations. | Reinvesting accounting profit or all future receivables before collection |
| Entry and ownership | Transfer existing customer assignments/assets and pay purchase/transition obligations for acquisition; create genuinely new demand only in a separate input. Hold technology access equal under prompt/delayed response. | Acquisition producing market growth or one demand unit served by both firms |

These rules are implementation acceptance conditions, not a claim that the bridges now exist. Target contracts remain unknown. Synthetic interest, support, rights and recovery terms can test a decision but must not be presented as available financing, legal advice, observed adoption or calibrated ROI.

## 2. Economic atlas and deployment pathways

Authoritative chapter: [research/economic_atlas.md](research/economic_atlas.md).

Version 0.1.0 · Evidence retrieved 25 October 2026 · Input revision `campaign launch snapshot (see ignored sync_receipt.json)` · First substantive A/C edition, with unquantified gates retained.

The US employment evidence places much of potentially relevant physical work in large service industries as well as manufacturing and construction. It does **not** yet identify a national number of humanoid-serviceable hours. The most informative deployment evidence instead identifies bounded production systems: correctly placing a known component, transferring a standardized tote, cleaning a navigable floor, delivering a nonurgent hospital item, drilling a planned anchor hole, or collecting a qualified asset observation. Those systems have different economic denominators, alternatives, customer decision rights and bottlenecks.

The strategic implication is to choose deployment sequences using accepted output, avoidable staffing, integration burden and support demand. Employment size supplies context. It cannot rank profitable robot entry points without those additional measurements. The evidence also argues against treating humanoid deployment as a uniform replacement of physical occupations: mature specialized equipment, digital improvements to human workflows, and mixed systems are consequential competitors.

The authoritative evidence snapshot is [market_evidence.json](data/market_evidence.json). It contains 15 market families, six deeper cells across six markets, an occupation screen, source rows, explicit residuals, conditional waterfalls and unresolved questions. Evidence identifiers below refer to that file. No physical-hour estimate, national automation percentage, calibrated adoption date or robot-market revenue total is claimed. Runtime model selection was requested as GPT-6 Astra/Ultra; independent model/reasoning metadata is not exposed to this lane, so configuration verification belongs to the lead's actual spawn record.

### The US labor anchor and its limits

BLS's latest retrieved National Employment Matrix historical baseline is **2025**, published with 2025–35 projections. We use the 2025 column only. The 2035 column is BLS's conditional projection, not an observed future and not a robotics forecast. [A-BLS-EP2025; A-BLS-DEFINITIONS]

### Six deeper market-stage cells

Each cell is anchored to US 2025 activity and includes evidence of other dates and, where specified, foreign comparator deployments. A stable cell ID does not make a foreign result US evidence. Industry employment below is a nonadditive context measure; none is the measured population of the specific workflow.

#### Automotive assembly: narrow production evidence, demanding integration

`CELL-M05-auto-assembly-US-2025` covers component presentation/loading into an automotive production process. The 2025 motor-vehicle-manufacturing context is **285,800 jobs**, encompassing much more work than the cell. The relevant denominator is **correctly placed components meeting takt and quality requirements**, per scheduled period. Inbound sequencing, loading, welding and inspection remain connected but distinct stages. [A-BLS-DETAIL2025]

BMW's February 2026 operator account reports that Figure 02 supported production of more than 30,000 X3 vehicles during a ten-month 2025 Spartanburg pilot, moving more than 90,000 components in approximately 1,250 operating hours. Its work was precise removal and positioning of sheet-metal parts for welding. This is meaningful production-linked evidence beyond a laboratory clip. It does not mean that a robot manufactured 30,000 cars or that the entire production line's value is attributable to it. Nor can the reported ten-hour-shift language be substituted for the separately reported operating exposure. [A-BMW-2026]

The choice is between configured cells: humanoid plus fixtures/control, conventional fixed robot, conveyor/fixture redesign, or AMR plus human handling. Shared IT, occupational safety, process management and shop-floor integration are operator-described prerequisites. Economics depend on accepted components, line stoppage risk, retained intervention staffing, scrap, integration downtime and alternatives' cost. Neither a defensible price interval nor a nationally serviceable share is available from the disclosed numbers.

An incumbent OEM has demand, product qualification and process authority. Acquisition changes control but creates no additional work pool. A greenfield operator can redesign a plant, yet must finance the product, supply chain and customer ramp as well as robots. With equal robot access, a claim that the entrant automatically wins is unsupported. The first stage is repeatable bounded loading; broader SKU and multi-step coverage requires verified recovery, fixture/software compatibility and validated support demand. Repeated geometry may support learning within that envelope; cross-market dexterity is an unvalidated transfer hypothesis.

#### Contract warehousing: the interface and contract are part of the machine

`CELL-M08-3pl-tote-US-2025` concerns tote handoff in a large warehouse already using AMRs and conveyors. Warehousing/storage employment is **1.8657 million jobs**, not a tote-handling labor count. The useful output is a correct tote transfer reaching the downstream process within its dispatch window. [A-BLS-DETAIL2025]

GXO's June 2024 primary announcement describes a multiyear RaaS agreement with Agility, following a late-2023 proof of concept. At its SPANX operation, Digit takes totes from mobile robots and places them on conveyors, orchestrated through Arc. The significance is an integrated bounded workflow and an operator-backed commercial arrangement. Fees, support staffing, accepted throughput, retained fleet exposure and cash savings are undisclosed. A contract is not an ROI measurement. [A-GXO-2024]

The meaningful counterfactual includes a fixed transfer station, altered conveyor geometry, or human/AMR combination. A humanoid can be attractive where brownfield geometry or product variation makes fixed redesign costly, but this remains conditional on actual throughput and support. Improved WMS scheduling might raise both human and robot productivity; it is not an extra benefit to add twice.

The readiness gate crosses several organizations: the logistics operator implements, the customer supplies demand and may control the site, and the provider supplies hardware/support. RaaS can move initial asset cash requirements but does not remove provider financing or service constraints. An operating newco needs contracted volume, customer acquisition and ramp cash. If an incumbent adopts the same Digit/Arc configuration promptly, any advantage must survive in operating execution, contracts or differentiated service. Common totes may create useful transfer; site interfaces and data rights can block it. The next stage should be earned by sustained peak-period output with observed exception queues, not by multiplying independently chosen eligibility percentages.

#### Facilities cleaning: the specialized baseline already moves

`CELL-M11-floor-cleaning-US-2025` covers outsourced floor cleaning. Facilities-support and building/dwelling-services employment totals **2.4741 million jobs**, including landscaping and other work that is not floor cleaning. The denominator should be **area passing specified cleanliness and coverage inspection within the cleaning window**, with total crew and support effort retained. [A-BLS-DETAIL2025]

ISS reports deploying more than 800 sweeping/vacuuming/scrubbing robots across 21 countries. Its March 2026 article says multiple UK customer facilities increased floor-cleaning efficiency by 30%, while humans inspect and perform additional work. It also describes sensor-based occupancy scheduling and a framework checking whether a robot is justified and suitable at a customer site, including privacy/data considerations. This is credible operator evidence of an existing specialized-machine market, with commercial interest in portraying success. The exact efficiency denominator, sample, comparison, labor disposition and US transfer are unknown. **Thirty percent efficiency is not thirty percent payroll removal.** [A-ISS-2026]

A full comparison includes manual walk-behind/ride-on equipment, autonomous machines, and sensor-informed human scheduling at matched quality. The machine's accessible floor area excludes edges, congested spaces or washrooms it cannot finish. Setup, refill, cleaning the cleaner, transport, exception response and manual follow-up can preserve staffing even when autonomous coverage expands.

Customer ownership mechanisms are concrete: a multinational contractor can share engineering and redeploy equipment, while the client retains site access and service requirements; a local contractor may have direct operating authority but less capital and support capacity. Neither label establishes the effect. A robotic-cleaning newco can purchase the same machines as incumbents. Route density, contract retention, implementation skill and customer offer must explain its advantage. Contractor contract duration should be matched to asset payback and redeployment economics; a lease addresses equipment funding, not acquisition costs or losses before route density develops. These mechanisms connect to the customer/value lane's fixed-technology comparisons.

#### Hospital logistics: observed deliveries expose a clinical-work boundary

`CELL-M10-hospital-logistics-US-2025` is a large multi-floor hospital's internal delivery system. Private hospital employment is **5.6538 million jobs**; the all-ownership hospital reference is **6.8184 million**, of which public employment is already in M14. Neither count is a delivery-work denominator. The outcome is **correct authorized delivery with chain of custody intact within the relevant urgency-class service limit**. [A-BLS-DETAIL2025]

ChristianaCare's nurse-authored 2026 account describes three cobots serving more than 80 patient areas, completing over 48,600 deliveries during a 2022–25 grant-funded pilot. The program tested EHR and elevator integration. It found operational value in high-volume nonurgent medication/equipment delivery, while concluding that the robots could not meaningfully augment complex and time-sensitive nursing workflows. Nurses infrequently initiated requests when urgency or informal coordination made delivery automation a poor fit. This is material negative evidence for broad clinical-labor substitution, alongside positive evidence for a narrower service. The $1.5 million grant is program funding, not robot acquisition price or a full operating-cost estimate. [A-HOSPITAL-2026]

A separate qualitative study observed 89 deliveries across 23 sessions and 33 observer-hours in November–December 2022. It reports 107 human interventions, including 72 for elevators. The category counts sum to 111, however, without explaining overlap or error. The headline and discrepancy are both preserved. This sample establishes early integration and assistance burdens; it cannot calibrate a current platform-wide intervention rate. Observer-hours are not interchangeable with robot operating hours, and durations per assist were not supplied. [A-HOSPITAL-2024]

Alternatives include pneumatic tubes, AGVs, carts/porters and digital request batching. Compare accepted on-time deliveries and **all** loading, unloading, intervention and coordination effort. Hospital governance distributes authority among nursing, pharmacy, facilities, IT and executives. Protected staff time and support matter even where capital is available. A service newco must work through those interfaces; delivery activity cannot establish removed nursing payroll. The next stage is reliable nonurgent coverage with measured labor and SLA performance, followed by separate evidence for tighter time requirements. Door/route learning can transfer within compatible sites; clinical prioritization is a different mechanism.

#### Construction: digital preparation can dominate a narrow robot's gain

`CELL-M04-construction-install-US-2025` covers MEP/interior overhead drilling on projects with coordinated plans and accessible interiors. Building-equipment contractors employ **2.6021 million wage-and-salary jobs**. The cell's denominator is **accepted holes at required coordinates, depth and diameter**, per work package; completed installation is a downstream outcome. [A-BLS-DETAIL2025]

Hilti's US Jaibot material describes semi-automated drilling, with digital plans uploaded through cloud/tablet workflows, total-station positioning, autonomous drilling within reach, and progress feedback. People still operate the machine and set up the total station. A 2021 IAARC paper documents ALEC's One Zaabeel implementation in Dubai and explicitly examines workflow, organization, quality, safety and project-management implications. Only its abstract was retrieved here, so numerical productivity effects are not adopted. The foreign case supports a mechanism to investigate in US projects, not a US penetration estimate. [A-HILTI-JAIBOT; A-JAIBOT-UAE2021]

The economics must include coordinated design, transport/mobilization, setup, obstruction, rebar encounters, operator labor and rework. A high drill rate does not equal a similar improvement in all MEP installation, particularly if the next trade is the bottleneck. Digital layout plus manual drilling and prefabrication are material alternatives; a humanoid must beat the configured alternatives on accepted work and utilization.

Authority is split between the general contractor controlling schedule/site access and subcontractors controlling crews/tools. A robotics subcontractor may aggregate work, but loses utilization when plans or upstream work are late. An acquisition can supply relationships and pipeline without automatically giving control over other trades. Future integrated stages require more reliable work-package preparation and access as well as physical capability. BIM-to-execution/as-built feedback is a direct digital contribution; lower drilling effort and reduced coordination error must not be counted twice when they represent the same avoided rework.

#### Utility inspection: sensing is an intermediate product

`CELL-M03-utility-inspection-US-2025` covers generation/substation asset inspection. US electric generation/transmission/distribution employment is **420,500 jobs**, not a count of inspectors or inspections. The useful-output denominator is **observations meeting sensor-quality and defect-detection requirements within the inspection interval**, followed separately by actionable finding, work order, repair and asset outcome. [A-BLS-DETAIL2025]

National Grid's February 2023 operator report describes a three-day Spot trial at its Blyth converter site, with visual, thermal, acoustic and LiDAR functions. It explicitly says results were being reviewed and future investment remained conditional. This supports physical access and data-collection possibilities, not retained routine use, actual outage reduction or a US adoption rate. Broader quadruped/drone operating evidence is owned by the I/J lane; this cell provides the common output boundary. [A-NATIONALGRID-2023]

Fixed sensors, drones, wheeled sensor carts and technicians can be substitutes or complements. A quadruped's mobility only creates value if its payload produces decision-quality data, review distinguishes true faults, and maintenance follows. Repeated missions do not establish lower payroll or avoided outages. Economics include sensor calibration, site mapping, human review, false positives/negatives, support and qualification. A drone failing an inspection-image requirement may be physically capable of flying yet economically unable to deliver the required product; the same distinction applies to ground robots.

Utilities retain asset/security authority; an integrator may assemble payloads, maps and workflows. A new inspection service entrant may compete for contracts while being unable to own or control regulated assets. Scaling therefore needs a service-access and qualification mechanism, not only abundant robots. Repeated routes can improve anomaly baselines; transfer across asset types, failure distributions or into physical repair is unproven.

### Conditional waterfalls and portfolio implications

Each structured cell contains the same logical waterfall: relevant accepted-work population → technical serviceability → full-system economic attractiveness → organizational readiness → deliverable capacity → retained productive output. **All numerical gate values remain null** because a representative matched population and the joint distribution of conditions are not known. This is a substantive limitation, not a zero-adoption result. It preserves the evidence needed to replace a gate later rather than manufacturing percentages now.

The denominator stays the cell's accepted-work unit. Sites, installed robots, robot-hours, observer-hours, human paid hours and currency require separate conversion panels with measured coefficients. A component placed, tote moved or delivery completed is an activity observation. It becomes a productivity result only with matched accepted quality, exposure, human effort and baseline; it becomes savings only if costs are actually avoided.

The supported sequence differs by cell. Cleaning and routine logistics already have specialized operating precedents. Automotive humanoid work has bounded production-linked exposure. Hospital logistics has an explicit urgency boundary. Construction shows digital/site prerequisites. Utility trials require the observation-to-action chain to be established. These are qualitative differences in evidence and mechanism, not a numerical attractiveness ranking.

For a humanoid company, a sensible initial portfolio test allocates scarce integration engineers, local support, qualified hardware and customer ramp funding across **specific cohorts**. Standardized configurations can improve repeatability but also face cheaper fixed alternatives. Higher wages increase potential avoided cost only when staffing can change; healthcare illustrates why wage comparisons alone mislead. A customer with strong capital access but fragmented operating authority can remain less ready than a smaller customer with a simpler process. RaaS changes cash timing and risk allocation while consuming provider capital and support.

Conditional horizons of 0–2, 3–5 and 6–10 years are stored as requirement changes, not extrapolated adoption dates. In the first interval, validate bounded repeated output, assistance and whole-system costs. Expansion requires observed transfer, support capacity and customer demand. Enterprise redesign remains a scenario whose advantage must survive digitally improved human operations, specialized automation and same-technology incumbent response. Announcing a new robot does not upgrade existing fleets or remove qualification lead time.

### What would most change the decision

The highest-priority quantitative gap is matched **accepted output plus total human effort**, rather than another large deployment announcement. For automotive and totes, seek accepted throughput, downtime, intervention duration and complete configured-cell cost. For cleaning, seek inspected area, residual/manual work, support/setup and contract retention at US sites. For hospital delivery, seek urgency-specific SLA performance and post-integration support labor. For construction, seek entire work-package effort, mobilization and utilization across projects. For inspection, link sensor-quality pass rates to actionable findings and completed maintenance.

A separate denominator program should join industry/occupation data to representative hours and measured work shares, retain self-employed and outsourced activity, and validate allocation weights. Until then, the atlas supports a broad map and conditional deployment judgment but cannot support national physical-hour penetration, robot TAM or a market-size-ranked rollout forecast.

Public evidence can improve source coverage, configurations, contract terms and reported outcomes. Several decisive questions require field records: staffing actually avoided, intervention duration, failed attempts, accepted quality, site utilization, commercial pricing and customer-specific authority. Missing access is recorded as a research dependency, not inferred unfavorable economics. The saved questions `A-Q-HOURS`, `A-Q-HOSPITAL-SLA`, `A-Q-CLEANING-CONTRACT` and `A-Q-PORTFOLIO` define bounded next actions and completion criteria.

#### What the measurements mean

Seven dimensions are selected for each occupation: mean workday standing, mean workday sitting, gross manipulation, fine manipulation, reaching at or below shoulder level, overhead reaching and low postures. Their denominators differ. Standing and sitting are mean shares of the workday. The other five records are percentages of workers whose jobs require an activity at some point. They are not percentages of the workday devoted to that activity. [PRC-002/003]

[BLS definitions](https://www.bls.gov/ors/factsheet/orsprofiles.htm) include walking in standing; sitting also includes lying down and qualifying choice-of-position situations. These are exhaustive postural classifications, not a division between physical and digital production. A worker can reason, speak, inspect, enter information or operate equipment while standing. The distinction matters for the annual digital scenarios: an improvement in human information work may affect a physical workplace without reducing a measured standing requirement. [PRC-002/005]

[Gross and fine manipulation](https://www.bls.gov/ors/factsheet/gross-and-fine-manipulation.htm) can occur concurrently, and the third-wave fine-manipulation definition includes keyboarding. Gross manipulation covers handling with the hand; fine manipulation includes actions primarily using the fingers. Neither label is a robot dexterity benchmark. These marginals cannot be summed to measure total physical work, multiplied to estimate their joint occurrence, or used to rank control architectures without object, force, reach, timing and recovery requirements. [PRC-003/007]

Ranges and missingness remain explicit. The source's `>99.5` and `>95` are preserved as bounds, with the underlying point value unknown. The [range methodology](https://www.bls.gov/ors/factsheet/range-estimates.htm) distinguishes endpoint ranges from other ranges based on a one-sided confidence-interval method. The workbook's standard-error footnote 6 means below 0.5 percentage points; footnote 15 means unavailable. A printed dash alone does not tell the reader which applies. The three absent Team Assemblers rows remain unpublished; no sitting estimate is created by subtracting standing from 100. [PRC-006]

#### Four operating comparisons

**Facilities and building services.** Janitors and cleaners have a published mean standing share of 97.7% of the workday and gross/fine manipulation incidence above 99.5%. The matched OEWS occupation has 2,209,760 jobs. These national occupation measures cover more than floor cleaning. They do not establish how much of a customer's workload an autonomous scrubber, a mobile manipulator or a humanoid could complete. The existing cleaning cell therefore still needs a workflow inventory: open floors, edges, stairs, waste, surfaces, tools, chemicals and exceptions, together with the associated service standard and human work. A broad manipulation requirement cannot be used to exclude specialized floor equipment or to award a humanoid all of the occupation's employment. [PRC-002/003/004]

**Construction.** Construction laborers have an 89.2% mean standing share, 96.1% fine-manipulation incidence and 75.8% overhead-reaching incidence, with 1,096,780 OEWS jobs in the matched code. These are separate marginal estimates. They do not identify a single group jointly performing all three requirements or the frequency of any specific installation task. Changing geometry, access, material presentation, tooling, transport and responsibility for exceptions remain central to a deployment case. The national wage-and-salary denominator also omits self-employed construction work, and the occupations in a particular site package need not match this broad laborer category. [PRC-002/003/004/005]

**Logistics and material handling.** Hand laborers and freight, stock and material movers have a 93.7% mean standing share and 52.1% overhead-reaching incidence. OEWS reports 2,950,280 jobs in the exact occupation code. The occupation spans industries and workflows; the existing 3PL tote-handoff cell is one supporting case, not its full economic definition. A standardized tote interface may eliminate part of a reaching or manipulation burden, while other workers in the occupation handle different loads or routes. Measure the local mix, accepted transfers, peak windows and interventions before assigning any of these national jobs to the cell. [PRC-002/003/004]

**Manufacturing and assembly.** ORS publishes a 95.6% mean standing share for Team Assemblers and 65.7% incidence of low-posture requirements. The latter has a published standard error of 10.3 percentage points. The selected sitting and two reaching estimates are absent, so the packet cannot establish a complete posture/reach profile. There is no exact `51-2092` employment match in the current OEWS workbook. Its `51-2090 Miscellaneous Assemblers and Fabricators` category contains 1,405,030 jobs and appears once as broad and once as detailed. Those two rows do not represent 2,810,060 jobs, and neither is substituted for the Team Assemblers population. The exact employment join remains unknown. [PRC-002/003/004]

#### Why exact codes are only the first join

ORS civilian workers combine private industry with state and local government, excluding the federal government, military, agriculture, private household workers and the self-employed. OEWS includes federal executive and Postal employment and certain agricultural industries, while excluding the self-employed and other specified populations. The exact-code matches for three occupations are therefore labeled **population mismatch**, not interchangeable denominators. [PRC-005]

Even after population reconciliation, converting a mean workday percentage into annual physical-work hours requires a compatible work schedule, annual exposure and an operational definition of physical work. A requirement that is present for many workers can consume little time, occur alongside another requirement, or be served by an existing specialized machine. National occupation employment also requires an industry/outsourcing allocation before it can be assigned across the atlas's end-market families without duplication. No such weights or annual-hour conversions are invented here.

#### Decision changes and next evidence

1. **The deployment screen needs a measured service denominator.** Use occupation employment to locate substantial labor pools, then establish the actual workflow and accepted output in a market-stage cell. Do not place an ORS percentage directly into the penetration waterfall's physical-hours or capability gates.
2. **Embodiment and intelligence must be evaluated together.** Reaching, posture, loads and tooling constrain the configured body and integration. A model release or cross-embodiment claim supplies no proof that these local requirements are met at the necessary quality and reliability. Workstream K's OEM, licensed-model, open-adaptation and model-led-operator comparison needs the same service test and full support boundary.
3. **The human baseline remains a live competitor.** The presence of keyboarding within fine manipulation and of information work within standing illustrates why physical workplaces can improve digitally. The annual model can explore the consequence, but this crosswalk supplies no coefficient for human efficiency, coordination or robot digital-service expense.

The immediate K research question is which architecture reduces adaptation and support effort for one defined service while preserving required hardware, rights and reliability. Before making that comparison quantitative, collect the same task distribution, accepted-output definition, all intervention and recovery work, latency budget, available robot configuration, license/data rights and cash costs for the alternatives. Unknown terms remain unknown. This crosswalk improves the question and its measurement boundaries; it does not settle an investment or penetration forecast.

### What the evidence changes

Productive humanoid operation has crossed the boundary from a demonstration to useful work in some bounded industrial settings. It has not established a general, measured substitution rate for physical labor. The strongest examples in this wave are BMW's Figure 02 sheet-metal loading pilot and GXO's commercial Digit tote transfer. They are complementary stations inside systems that already contain transport robots, conveyors, fixed automation and people. Those surroundings are part of the configured solution.

A strategic deployment portfolio therefore needs two separate tests. The first is whether the entire process delivers accepted output at its required quality and timing with a tolerable support burden. The second is whether this deployment yields transferable information or policy improvement worth its incremental collection, training and validation cost. The inspected research supports conditional learning gains, saturation and negative transfer. It does not support treating installed robots or accumulated hours as a universal learning multiplier.

The moving comparator matters. BMW's virtual factory tools improve manual ergonomics and its AIQX system supports quality inspection; Mercedes describes production chatbots and virtual data-science agents alongside Apollo experiments. A humanoid has to compete with the resulting human-led operation, as well as with specialized and mixed machines. No measured digital productivity coefficient is assigned to those operator descriptions.

#### BMW: unusually useful evidence, with decisive denominator limits

But “independent” here means the operator speaks separately; BMW and Figure remain interested commercial partners, and neither supplies an independent audit.

[Figure's November 2025 post](https://www.figure.ai/news/production-at-bmw) distinguishes performance requirements from observations. The 84-second total cycle and 37-second loading phase are requirements. Greater than 99% placement accuracy is a target; zero human pauses/resets per shift is a goal. Recording these as achieved values would eliminate the very support uncertainty that determines fleet economics. The evidence file leaves realized intervention intensity null.

Calendar boundaries differ: Figure describes an 11-month deployment, BMW a ten-month period supporting vehicle production, and Figure separately describes six months of daily runtime. Ten-hour weekday shifts do not make every hour of those months autonomous operating exposure. Dividing rounded cumulative parts by rounded aggregate runtime would also conceal robot count, downtime inclusion and acceptance criteria. This dossier therefore does not publish an inferred intensive throughput rate.

That is evidence for a bounded entry configuration with strong complements, not immediate transfer to a fragmented construction site. ### Improvement evidence: six distinct mechanisms

#### 1. Diversity can dominate repeated demonstrations

[Hu, Lin and colleagues, v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18647v3) collected over 40,000 demonstrations and conducted over 15,000 real-world rollouts. Their detailed scaling experiments concern Pour Water and Mouse Arrangement; Fold Towels and Unplug Charger test the resulting collection recipe. Training variations cover 1–32 objects or environments, using a Diffusion Policy with a pretrained visual encoder and human-collected UMI demonstrations.

The paper was initially submitted on 24 October 2024; the inspected v3 is dated 13 October 2025. A newer [v4, dated 26 June 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18647v4), exists. A bounded check of §4.2, the diversity/repetition distinction, and Tables 1–2 found the core results below unchanged. This preserves the v3 snapshot and is not a complete comparison of every version change.

The dependent variable in their fits is the gap from a normalized, partially credited task score. It is not successful output per paid hour. Six data points support each displayed fit; the authors explicitly decline a three-parameter irreducible-error fit because of that limited sample. A prediction requiring 1,191 environment-object pairs is left unverified.

Increasing environment/object diversity improves held-out performance in the tested range. Repeated demonstrations within a fixed context saturate: in the recommended environment-object design, reported plateaus occur around 400, 800 and 1,600 demonstrations for 8, 16 and 32 pairs respectively. These are findings for similarly difficult tasks, not acquisition targets for a humanoid business.

Table 1 also prevents a loose “90% everywhere” interpretation: mean task success is 85.0%, 92.5%, 87.5% and 90.0%, with reported variability across eight unseen environments. The detailed scoring results and success percentages are separate metrics. Table 2 gives another negative finding: enlarging the action U-Net does not improve score monotonically, even though enlarging the visual encoder helps. Hardware, architecture, training and data composition interact.

**Model implication:** keep effective context diversity, repeated exposure and policy architecture separate. Do not fit a universal robot-hour exponent or assume that operating ten copies of an unchanged task creates ten times the useful training information.

#### 2. Cross-embodiment transfer can be positive, neutral or negative

[RT-X v9](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.08864v9) is a valuable controlled transfer study. The repository headline describes 22 embodiments, but the policy experiments use data from nine manipulators and conduct 3,600 evaluation trials across six robots. Coarse alignment uses a seven-dimensional end-effector action representation. This does not establish transfer across every sensor and actuation configuration in the larger repository.

Positive transfer appears in small-data settings. Yet Table I reports the Google-robot in-distribution comparison at 92% for RT-1 and 73% for RT-1-X. The authors interpret the small model as underfitting the heterogeneous mixture. Even the larger model's 91% is not above that 92% baseline; a broad prose statement that it outperforms the baselines should not override the table.

The widely cited roughly threefold effect belongs to emergent skills: RT-2-X scores 75.8% versus RT-2's 27.3%. The separate generalization column is 61% versus 62%. Removing Bridge data reduces the emergent score to 42.8%, strengthening the specific transfer interpretation. None of these are industry productivity multipliers.

**Model implication:** transfer is a directed edge tied to source distribution, target, representation, capacity and training recipe. Preserve negative and unchanged-capability cases. Cross-market similarity alone cannot activate an edge.

#### 3. Data selection requires target information

[DataMIL](https://robin-lab.cs.utexas.edu/datamodels4imitation/) makes the selection problem explicit. Its target-policy influence estimates use target validation loss, temporal clustering and co-training with target data. The research spans more than 60 simulation/real tasks, with four real-world OXE target tasks and a new Tiago embodiment example. The project reports that visually similar observations can carry action distributions that help or harm the target policy.

This supports performance-aware curation rather than a monotonic gross-data-volume story. The full paper's computation and sensitivity deserve deeper extraction before assigning costs. No claim here treats every recorded trajectory as accessible, useful or valuable.

**Model implication:** deployment exposure, usable dataset, selected training mixture, validated target capability and captured cash value are five different objects. Include target-data collection, selection computation, training, validation and rollout costs before valuing a learning route.

#### 4. New-environment generalization has progressed without proving complete service

[pi0.5](https://www.pi.website/blog/pi05) uses heterogeneous robot, semantic and web-derived knowledge to perform household manipulation in unseen homes. Its paper evaluates three homes and task/stage-completion rubrics. This is meaningful evidence that new-site adaptation need not always mean training a separate policy from scratch.

It does not establish autonomous housekeeping across representative households. A bedroom-cleaning task, short task evaluation and complete recurring service contract have different output boundaries. The authors acknowledge errors in both high-level semantic choices and motor commands. A service business would additionally need customer-specific quality, safe exceptions, setup, cleaning supplies, access and reliable scheduling.

**Model implication:** allow a conditional reduction in adaptation effort, separately from task reliability, human assistance and full-service eligibility. Human housekeeping improved with scheduling software and specialized appliances remains a comparator.

#### 5. Experience and corrections can improve throughput, with human costs

[RECAP / pi*0.6 v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.14759v2) is more directly relevant to useful throughput than a generic score curve. It measures successful task completions per hour as well as success under task-specific quality and time limits. Evaluations cover laundry variants, double espresso shots and box assembly; deadlines differ by task (200, 500 or 600 seconds).

The reported on-robot improvement more than doubles throughput for espresso and the diverse-laundry experiment. The latter trains on 11 item types but quantitatively evaluates **button-up shirts only**; videos of other clothing do not establish measured throughput across the full laundry mix. The iterative simple-laundry experiment collects 300 trajectories on four robots per iteration without expert corrections. Section VI-C describes box assembly as 600 autonomous trials and 360 trials with expert interventions per iteration, while Appendix A-F calls the 600 demonstrations. This wording conflict prevents treating the count as a fully unassisted collection budget. Box throughput's point estimate initially falls before improving; the early error bars overlap, so this is not evidence of a statistically established decline. A monotonic scenario would still omit the observed point-estimate path.

The initial submission is dated 18 November 2025 and the inspected v2 is dated 19 November 2025; the exact version dates were verified from arXiv submission histories.

Section VII explicitly says the learning system is not fully autonomous: humans provide reward labels, interventions and episode resets. Updates are batched offline, not a continuously improving online controller. Extended execution videos do not eliminate those training and support inputs.

**Model implication:** learning can improve speed, success and recovery separately. Count annotation, correction, reset, maintenance and retraining time; include delay and possible temporary regression. Do not count higher future throughput and then add a second generic “data value” premium.

#### 6. Physical redesign has different diffusion and financing requirements

The Figure wrist case is empirical evidence of a design response, not a calibrated reliability improvement. Similarly, battery exchange in Walker S2 is a proposed way to remove a charging interruption, not evidence of 24/7 accepted production. A longer nominal endurance specification cannot eliminate repairs, calibration, supervision, upstream starvation or demand gaps.

**Model implication:** distinguish base capability progress, software upgrades, hardware replacement, production qualification and supported operating scale. An improvement may be available only to a new cohort and may require validation downtime, spare parts and new capital.

### Learning rights and deployment choice

The most immediately profitable site is not necessarily the most informative site, and the most varied site may overwhelm present capability. A deployment policy can therefore trade production margin against valuable evidence. But the comparison must use the same capital, robots, supervision and engineering resources. An experiment displacing production has an opportunity cost; an operational failure can produce a useful label while still harming the customer.

For a candidate learning edge, ask: what new state or failure is observed; is it recorded and usable; who can reuse it; which target policy changes; what delay and validation are needed; and who captures the economic effect? Provider-mediated transfer may benefit several competing customers, reducing exclusivity while improving the provider's product.

FG-E008 in the customer/commercial lane supplies a concrete rights comparator: a BrainOS/Kärcher agreement distinguishes end-user data ownership from broad licensed reuse and from guaranteed data export. Its applicability is to that agreement and version, not to Figure or Digit. It illustrates why owning the robot or the operating business does not establish exclusive learning rights. The contract's metadata/version ambiguity remains unresolved.

An unchanged-policy/no-transfer scenario should remain available. It is an analytical comparison, not an empirical assertion that transfer is zero. If future gains already enter output and cash-flow projections, additional data premiums would double count them. Information that changes a later investment decision is a separate value-of-information mechanism and needs its own counterfactual.

### Coordination: shared state, authority and scarce resources

Three architectures have different evidence statuses.

1. **An integrated production station.** At BMW, the humanoid loads or sequences while fixed robots, transport systems and people perform other stages. The operator reports standardized integration. Exact decision APIs and a measured incremental coordination effect are not public.
2. **A heterogeneous logistics fleet.** At GXO, Arc connects workflow definition and Digit operations with existing automation. This is evidence of integration and commercial use; it is not an experiment proving that a particular fleet architecture causes a measured gain.
3. **Cross-vendor infrastructure coordination.** [Open-RMF interfaces](https://openrmf.readthedocs.io/en/latest/interfacing/index.html) distinguish state reporting, destination requests, pause/resume and complete-path control, plus doors and lifts. An observation-only fleet permits fewer deconfliction actions than a fully controllable fleet. Documentation establishes a mechanism; it does not establish an LLM contribution or quantify economics. H uses the same source, so it is not independent corroboration.

A productive architecture must distinguish business intent and work orders, scheduling/optimization, fleet dispatch, task-level perception/action generation, low-level motion/safety control and exception escalation. It should identify each layer's state, allowed actions, latency and fallback. Redwood's manufacturer description provides one concrete separation: onboard action generation around 5 Hz and an offboard speech-to-speech goal interface. It does not imply that safety control runs at 5 Hz or depends on a language model.

A simple mechanism model should start with accepted output bounded by demand, upstream feed, robot work capacity and downstream capacity. The units must match at each boundary. Faster picking will not raise shipment output when the conveyor or subsequent process binds; a scheduling gain that improves the same bottleneck cannot be added twice.

Human support is a shared resource. If robot cohort i supplies A_i active robot-hours, experiences lambda_i interventions per active robot-hour, and needs s_i human-hours per intervention, expected direct intervention work is the sum of A_i × lambda_i × s_i. Maintenance, setup, travel, annotation and management add separate demands. This is an accounting identity under declared average-rate assumptions, not a queue forecast.

Support demand below staffed hours is only a necessary condition. Bursty or correlated failures can produce queues, missed takt, blocked corridors and idle robots even when mean workload fits. Network/model failures can affect many units together; task assignment, redundancy and operating slack may matter more than increasing average utilization. A model should expose these mechanisms as scenarios until logs support calibration. Robot waiting also changes active exposure and future intervention arrivals, so avoid inconsistent fixed-arrival assumptions.

### Market-stage consequences and conditional thresholds

The six atlas cells share one useful-output boundary per comparison:

- **Automotive assembly:** accepted loaded/sequenced components at takt. Compare human-plus-digital tools, fixed fixtures/arms, wheeled manipulators and humanoids in the same line. Priority unknowns are sustained intervention/recovery and complete integration cost; do not assign the entire vehicle's value to one station.
- **3PL tote transfer:** accepted totes delivered into downstream flow. Compare docking/transfer redesign, human-plus-AMR and Digit. Measure peak-hour throughput and shared support, not cumulative lifetime activity alone.
- **Facilities floor cleaning:** cleanliness-qualified floor area is distinct from clutter removal, laundry or general housekeeping. Specialized scrubbers/vacuums, humans and manipulators may be complementary. Unseen-home manipulation research does not calibrate commercial floor-cleaning substitution.
- **Hospital logistics:** correct-item delivery with custody and service deadlines. Doors, elevators, prioritization and human handoffs can bind before manipulation or legs. Delivery capability is not clinical care capability.
- **Construction installation:** accepted installed elements under trade/site quality requirements. The inspected tabletop and automotive evidence does not establish transfer to weather, changing geometry, tools, heavy loads or trade-specific recovery. Keep human tools, layout/drilling machines and mixed crews in the comparison.
- **Utility inspection:** an actionable condition assessment and maintenance decision is different from a mission or repair. Fixed sensors, drones, quadrupeds and human engineers may dominate sensing. Humanoid manipulation becomes relevant only for separately validated interaction/repair work; I/J owns the direct field evidence.

These are threshold maps rather than calibrated penetration forecasts. They support staged entry: bounded assisted operation, repeatable productive work, integrated fleets and wider redesign where the evidence warrants it. No common ladder or ownership label guarantees progression.

### Coverage, limitations and next bounded work

This wave establishes a source-verified industrial entry thesis, conditional learning mechanisms and a coordination architecture. It does not establish commercial causal returns, calibrated learning curves for humanoids, representative intervention distributions, validated market penetration or a complete provider census. Primary manufacturer and operator statements remain interested-party reports. Research evaluations are author-run and context-specific. No field audit, training run or hardware experiment was performed.

The highest-value next evidence is full-shift accepted output and intervention logs with compatible robot-hour and human-hour denominators in automotive loading and tote transfer. It can change both near-term economics and support-constrained scale. Next are target-specific transfer ablations with total training cost/delay, upgrade compatibility, and matched fixed/wheeled/human alternatives. Public technical presentations and operator disclosures can reduce some uncertainty; otherwise the exact missing denominator should remain visible and field access recorded as a separate requirement.

Benchmark curves are not inputs to the economic engine unless a later target-specific calibration is explicitly accepted.

#### Cobalt's inexpensive demonstrations are a cloud-cost scenario

[Cobalt v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19138v2), submitted 20 May 2026 after the initial 18 May version, describes cloud teleoperation using smartphones and other devices. Its architecture separates client sessions, media streaming and simulation services, with an in-memory state exchange and vectorized simulations. That is a direct digital contribution to robotics data production, rather than an analogy from an unrelated industry.

Section IV-B's cost example assumes an NVIDIA T4 instance at an estimated $0.92 per hour and 120 demonstrations per user-hour. It reports $7.67 per thousand demonstrations without vectorization and $1.92 with the proposed sharing. The authors limit active sessions to four per GPU for user experience despite reporting feasible teleoperation at eight. Those are **conditional compute charges**, not an audited labor-inclusive cost per usable demonstration. The arithmetic prices the cloud resource. It does not price the operators, onboarding, unsuccessful attempts, task construction, curation, policy training, hardware or physical validation. Calling the fourfold compute reduction a fourfold reduction in the complete data-production cost would change the denominator.

The same paper provides a useful, different empirical object: Table V contains 7,510 recorded demonstrations and 50.61 hours, comprising 6,746 crowdsourced manipulation demonstrations plus 764 user-study demonstrations. Six manipulation tasks range widely in recorded duration. These are trajectory hours, not a disclosed payroll or fully loaded project-labor account. The paper selects demonstrations within the 50th percentile for total path length and evaluates Table VI simulation policies over 50 rollouts per task. Exact before/after curation counts remain unextracted. The low kitchen-task results (0.02–0.12 across the four reported policies) show why a large collection count is not equivalent to a solved target service.

Section IV-D separately reports a physical Franka Lift experiment: 98 expert demonstrations train BC-RNN, with 52% success over 25 rollouts. This is a small real-robot learning result, beyond a hardware illustration. It remains distinct from the crowdsourced simulation pilot and Table VI evaluation, and neither establishes retained field-service reliability. Those populations must not be pooled. Likewise, a simulated-client stress test measures system load, not how many trained humans can simultaneously operate physical robots at an acceptable safety and task-success standard. The source's small device/training studies inform interface design; they do not establish a world-scale labor supply curve or a universal productivity coefficient for novice operators.

The operational inference is specific: vectorization can reduce the compute share of a simulated collection workflow when concurrency, latency and utilization permit. If labor, task development, quality filtering or physical validation dominates total cost, the whole-program saving can be much smaller. The next investment test should measure these shares and useful target-policy gain at fixed validation quality.

#### DexWild changes collection embodiment, with a target-grounding obligation

[DexWild v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07813v2) was submitted on 17 May 2026, a year after the original 12 May 2025 submission. Its human-worn collection system combines motion capture and cameras, then co-trains human and robot demonstrations. The inspected paper reports 9,290 human demonstrations across 93 environments, separate from 1,395 robot demonstrations used for target grounding. Its comparative study reports 201 demonstrations per hour for the human-worn system and 43 for the Gello-based robot-teleoperation comparator across five representative tasks.

That is a reported collection-rate comparison. It is not the same as a labor-inclusive dollar-cost ratio, and it does not establish that a human demonstration and robot demonstration have identical learning value. The paper identifies haptic feedback, resets and hardware setup as practical differences, but the inspected description does not expose a complete cost ledger or enough participant/timing detail to independently reconstruct the whole comparison. Faster capture at a location also does not settle travel, setup, processing and rejection costs across locations.

The downstream pipeline is explicit enough to reveal additional work: tracking reconstruction, filtering, outlier handling, smoothing and retargeting. Appendix VII-C discards episodes when wrist pose cannot be reliably tracked for more than 75% of the duration. That criterion should not be mistaken for a reported rejection fraction; the denominator of attempted versus retained collection is still needed. Table V provides training steps and batch size, but those are not a measured GPU-hour or dollar bill.

The target-policy results also resist a generic scaling rule. Appendix Table III's task rows are not monotonically improving: spray performance changes from 0.605 at half of the human dataset to 0.565 at the full dataset; toy-cleanup scores initially decline before rising at the full dataset. The average improves across the listed fractions, but the author's broader “steady” wording should not erase task-level variation. These are reported point estimates; uncertainty and matched repeated evaluations are needed before attributing significance to a particular decrease.

Table IV provides a second warning: increasing the human sampling share, changing the robot:human training-batch ratio from 1:2 to 1:5 reduces the reported mean score in both in-distribution and in-the-wild comparisons. These ratios concern samples within a training batch, not the sizes of collected datasets. Human-only results are much weaker in those inspected tasks. The paper expressly retains robot demonstrations to bridge the embodiment gap and identifies missing recovery examples and lack of tactile information as limitations. A lower-cost upstream data source therefore comes with a composition and target-grounding problem, not an automatic replacement for robot-specific evidence.

#### What changes in the research model and investment question

The existing engine correctly leaves useful-data production and causal learning response uncalibrated. These studies strengthen that boundary. Exposure hours, recorded trajectories, retained datasets, target-training mixtures and validated policies should be separate quantities. Rights and actual access remain additional conditions, not consequences of publicly available paper text.

A prospective collection ledger should distinguish:

1. Attempted collection and all paid human time, including onboarding, resets and quality checks.
2. Recorded and retained demonstrations, with explicit rejection reasons and the same population/time window.
3. Compute, equipment, task construction, travel, processing and physical target-validation costs.
4. Dataset composition, source/target embodiment, rights and reuse restrictions.
5. Change in held-out target policy performance at matched accepted output and operating quality, including recovery and support.

The economic objective is the full incremental cost of a validated target capability, not minimum cloud spend per recorded trajectory. A pipeline can improve one stage yet leave the critical path unchanged because hardware access or validation remains limiting. Conversely, cheaper portable capture could be valuable precisely because it expands environmental diversity, even when it does not reduce total operator hours proportionally. Those are separable, testable mechanisms.

For a humanoid company, this favors a small matched collection-and-validation experiment before a generalized data-factory investment: fixed target tasks, alternate collection mixes, complete human/compute accounts, common policy/evaluation budgets and retained failure/recovery coverage. The public sources support the mechanism and identify risks; they do not supply a commercial wage, usable-data yield, program ROI or field learning coefficient.

#### Stopping condition and remaining evidence

This bounded public-source investigation stops with two versioned primary cases that materially distinguish compute cost, collection rate and target usefulness. It does not add a speculative total cost to fill missing components. The next step requires the underlying study logs/supplements or a new controlled operating dataset: full collection person-hours, failed/rejected episodes, target-training/validation compute, equipment amortization and rights. If those remain unavailable, retain the null full-cost estimate and test sensitivities explicitly rather than call the data pipeline commercially validated.

### Decision finding

An AI-native operating newco needs a reason to win after incumbents can buy the same robots. Current evidence already includes incumbent adoption: GXO reports a commercial humanoid RaaS deployment, and ISS describes a substantial specialized-cleaning-robot program. The strongest initial entry thesis is conditional: win a defined service obligation, control enough of the workflow to deliver it, survive commissioning and collections, and preserve an advantage in operating design, customer access or constrained capacity after competitors respond. Neither an AI-native label nor cheaper hypothetical robots establishes this sequence.

Customer organization and provider arrangements change different constraints. On the customer side, ownership can affect capital allocation, approval, process authority, implementation and retention of gains. On the supply side, a sale, subscription, lease or managed operation allocates asset funding, utilization risk, service work, control and rights. A structure can relax one party's constraint by moving it to another party. An investment case therefore needs both maps and consolidated cash accounting.

Three first-wave findings are consequential:

1. **Service contracts can matter more than corporate ownership labels.** The same reduction in physical effort has different cash effects under fixed-price, cost-plus and hourly contracts. Contract termination can precede robot-cost recovery. These are disclosed mechanisms, not conjectures about public versus private management.
2. **Incumbents have evidenced complements to common robot access.** Existing customers, software integration, service networks and site knowledge must be included in the same-technology comparator. An entrant may design a better process, but that is a separate hypothesis requiring costs and evidence.
3. **Asset or data ownership does not establish exclusive control.** A retrieved BrainOS agreement gives the end user operational-data ownership while giving Brain durable nonexclusive improvement-use rights. The robot, software, customer relationship, workflow authority, practical data access and economic learning benefit can belong to different parties.

#### Facilities services: contractor versus in-house facility operator

#### From released time to a cash decision

Four outcomes need different evidence:

1. **Released task time:** less active human effort for matched output. Workers may perform other paid duties.
2. **Capacity:** more on-time accepted service, valuable only against a binding demand or service constraint.
3. **Avoided hiring or overtime:** a credible counterfactual staffing requirement and a documented avoided commitment.
4. **Actual paid labor removed:** lower paid hours or payroll under feasible schedules, after remaining duties and coverage are met.

The advertised base wage cannot determine which outcome occurs. A team might spend less time folding while retaining the same paid shifts to greet, handle cash, run food and reset service. Another operating pattern could avoid overtime or a future hire. Neither can be inferred from the job posting or a folding rate. Schedule constraints, peaks, breaks, coverage and management authority determine what is actually avoidable; loaded employer cost remains a separate missing input.

Keep three ledgers. **Customer cash** compares actually avoidable baseline payments with retained wages, utilities and materials, equipment/integration outlays, fees, ramp, working capital and financing at their dates. **Provider cash and resources** records collected fees against hardware, commissioning, local/remote service, spares, model/compute and capital obligations, naming the supplier, operator, integrator and financier. **Consolidated resources** counts the underlying labor, materials, energy, asset use and support once, eliminating internal fees and finance transfers. A bundled fee and the provider resources it funds cannot both be charged as additional consolidated resource use. Capital spending and depreciation are not two independent cash outlays.

The published sources leave contract prices, staffing/schedules, the accepted-output denominator, asset responsibility, model/data rights, service obligations, ramp and financing unqualified. Therefore FG-CMP-03 keeps ROI, payback, cost per accepted napkin and paid labor removed explicitly unknown. A conditional price interval could be computed only after those boundaries are populated; multiplying a vendor rate by a job-posted wage would not supply them.

#### What changes the entry and provider thesis

This comparison raises the hurdle for a model-led provider: qualifying the robot's service is necessary, but the relevant customer outside option includes a specialist machine with retained human work and a better organized manual process. A ratio of BETA's maximum item rate to Dyna's reported rate would compare different denominators and does not rank productivity. If a qualified specialist configuration meets the same service at lower full cost and acceptable flexibility, it weakens a general-purpose-model premium. If varied linens, presentation, layout or other useful work require costly specialist changes, a flexible robot could earn a premium—but only with measured reconfiguration, support and contract evidence.

For a **standalone operating newco**, napkin capacity is not a complete restaurant business. A linen-service proposition must acquire customers and fund setup, collection/delivery, inventory, staff, ramp and receivables. For an **acquisition**, existing demand, laundry assets and schedules may shorten ramp, while purchase price and inherited obligations can capitalize or absorb the expected benefit. A **hybrid** could combine shared preparation with existing laundry and site partners; transport, hygiene, buffers, permission and partner fees might undo pooling benefits. These are distinct hypotheses within F, not new observed firms.

Workstream G remains separate: equipment supplier, robotics/model provider, operator, integrator, asset owner and financier can coexist. RaaS does not identify their actual obligations. The same qualified technology must be available to a prompt incumbent in the entry counterfactual; incumbents may already own the customers, workforce and specialist assets. Any durable advantage must survive that comparison and any provider-mediated learning available to competitors.

The implementation lane owns all numerical values and generated results in the common engine. The scenario distinguishes a three-month commissioning period, phased ramp, robot payments, retained service labor/materials, enterprise overhead, initial setup/customer acquisition, delayed collections and committed funding. No later financing appears automatically. Incumbent response timing changes price and capturable demand, rather than mechanically converting lower costs to market share. A separate operating-redesign override may change retained costs; it is not the equal-technology baseline.

The identities are:

```text
accepted_capacity[t] = commissioned_robots[t] × accepted_units_per_robot[t] × ramp[t]
served_newco[t] = min(accepted_capacity[t], won_and_retained_demand[t])
served_newco = incumbent_share_transfer + existing_unmet_demand_served + new_demand_served
incumbent_output_after_transfer = incumbent_reference_output − incumbent_share_transfer
cash[t] = cash[t−1] + collections[t] + committed_funding[t]
          − setup[t] − customer_acquisition[t] − robot_payments[t]
          − retained_operating_cost[t] − overhead[t] − other_investment[t]
minimum_external_funding = maximum cumulative cash deficit before external funding
```

Accepted capacity is after the workload's quality and downtime allowances; it must not be multiplied by the same loss again. Collections differ from recognized service revenue, and uncollected receivables are not available cash. Acquisition transfers ownership of existing activity; it does not increase market output. A hypothetical shortage of people can support a capacity proposition only if customers will buy the output and robots plus support can reliably supply it.

Three investment hurdles remain distinct: positive repeatable contribution, survival through ramp, and actual capital availability. The first does not establish the second or third. Financeability is strongest when evidence milestones reduce the remaining risk: customer commitment and site permissions before deployment spend; repeated quality with measured intervention/support before fleet expansion; retention, collections and performance under incumbent response before financing the next operating unit.

A delayed incumbent offers a temporary window. A prompt incumbent can cut price, include the same robots within an existing service bundle, improve its digital workflow, retain customers through broader relationships, partner with the entrant, or acquire it. After technical convergence, the newco needs a demonstrated difference in service, integrated process cost, customer reach or execution. Supplier-mediated learning can improve competitors too. Merely owning the first deployed fleet is not evidence of a durable data advantage.

| Route | What it buys or creates | Main advantage hypothesis | Costs and reversal conditions |

| Standalone operating newco | Genuinely new operating capacity and customer relationships. | Coherent process design; no acquisition premium or inherited constraints. | Formation, qualification, CAC, ramp losses and limited reputation. Acquisition should be compared through the value of purchased customer access, earlier cash flow and reusable operating capability against acquisition premium, transition cost, legacy obligations and financing burden. The physical extension differs in hardware capital, maintenance, site access, liability, qualified supply, residual values and noncopyable productive capacity. ### Provider assets, control and value capture

This inquiry remains separate from the customer pathways. The same newco, acquired operator or incumbent can use several supply arrangements. An integrated robot provider can additionally operate a service business, but its internal robot payment is eliminated when calculating consolidated value.

| Arrangement | Funding and operating allocation | Value-capture question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Equipment sale | Customer or lender funds acquisition; supplier still finances production and receivables. Customer carries utilization and residual risk subject to warranty/support. | Can hardware margin cover product development and service obligations when autonomy, integration and competition constrain price? |
| Asset lease | Lessor funds asset; operator still funds site setup, employees, working capital and losses. | Who can price credit, residual value, redeployment and obsolescence better? Lease availability and cancellation terms are inputs. |
| RaaS | Customer payment may bundle hardware/software/support; actual title and obligations depend on contract. | Does recurring price cover provider capital, upkeep, intervention and idle risk? Lower customer capex does not eliminate system capital. |
| Usage/outcome contract | Provider/operator may carry unused-capacity risk; accepted-output and customer-caused delay rules matter. | Who can control enough of the process to guarantee an outcome and measure it without dispute? |
| Integration/software | Customer or equipment partner pays for setup and recurring autonomy/orchestration. | Reusable integration and a service channel may capture rents, but interoperability and competing suppliers can constrain them. |
| Managed operation, partnership or JV | Adds end-market execution; governance can unify or complicate decisions. | Are control and customer access worth extra operating risk, shared governance and capital? Equity does not itself grant data/model rights. |
| Provider owning the productive business | Provider funds greenfield/acquisition, overhead and demand formation as well as robots. | Are additional operating margin and verified learning/control benefits worth capital and lost focus relative to selling or partnering? |

For matched output, let baseline customer cost be `B`, retained nonprovider customer cost `A`, provider payment `P`, and provider operating cash cost `O`. Customer operating savings are `B − A − P`; provider operating contribution is `P − O`; consolidated operating benefit is `B − A − O`. Price cancels. One-time investments, collections, working capital and replacement are added at their dates; depreciation is not a second cash outlay. Financing payments between modeled participants also cancel in a consolidated resource view, while the cost and availability of external capital remain relevant.

The feasible commercial interval must satisfy both customer and provider cash requirements, with customer savings based on **avoidable** costs. Customer time redeployed to another task is not the same as payroll removed. A provider discount justified by expected learning is a provider investment, not an unexplained increase in customer savings or a second data premium.

The BrainOS artifact is especially informative. Section 5.2 leaves operational-data title with the end user but grants Brain continuing, nonexclusive rights to store, use and copy it for service and improvement purposes; access/export is not automatically assured. Section 5.2 also excludes aggregated data that is not linked to the end user from the defined Operational Data category; personal data is outside that definition. End-user ownership must be read within those boundaries. Sections 4.1–4.3 reserve software rights and link use to service access. This demonstrates the need to record ownership, access, reuse, export, exclusivity and commercial rights separately. It does not establish that any particular data actually improves a model, that those terms govern Digit, or that a customer cannot negotiate another arrangement.

Tennant–Brain illustrates a complementary distribution/software structure rather than inevitable total vertical integration. Its disclosed channel, service and recurring autonomy roles suggest that access to customers and supported deployment can capture value alongside hardware and models. Exact rents remain unquantified. Competing hypotheses stay live: autonomy could commoditize while local service remains scarce; or proprietary orchestration and dependent subscriptions could preserve supplier bargaining power. Observable renewal, switching, service cost and performance evidence would distinguish them.

#### 1. Guro medication delivery: corrective intervention is only one part of support

[Lee et al., Digital Health](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13039597/) studied one DOGU IROI robot on a pharmacy-to-emergency-department route at Korea University Guro Hospital. A web request, secure medication drawer and communication module integrated the robot with a shared, staff-only TK50M elevator. The robot moved from the pharmacy at B2F to the emergency department at 1F. This configured route is the unit of evidence—not hospital-wide material handling.

The primary full text and [public JATS record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13039597/fullTextXML) were retrieved and inspected. The JATS electronic publication date is **31 March 2026**. Search/DOI metadata giving February 2026 is not used as the authoritative publication date. Observation occurred **18–29 June 2025**.

##### The matched mission cohort

Section 2.4 reports 135 logged delivery requests and excludes 13 urgent runs, leaving 122 eligible nonurgent missions: 80 weekday and 42 weekend missions. Missions were selected to cover high- and low-congestion conditions; they followed clinical demand, with inter-mission gaps ranging from six to 1,044 minutes. These are sampled missions over a calendar window. They do not establish continuously staffed robot-hours, full-fleet utilization, or a fixed number of deliveries per shift.

Section 2.4 defines success literally as the robot “completing the entire route and handing over the medication without any human intervention.” Our distinction between **corrective assistance and routine handling or safety presence** is a contextual interpretation of the human roles disclosed in sections 2.3–2.4. The paper does not formally delimit its intervention term, so the tension between that wording and the staffed workflow remains explicit. Failure includes remaining stationary for more than one minute **or** requiring manual assistance because of passenger/cargo obstruction. Consequently, a failed mission is not automatically an intervention event, and the number of failures cannot be multiplied by an assumed recovery duration.

The source identifies the human work more explicitly than most deployment announcements:

| Role or activity | What the study establishes | Missing economic denominator |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trained accompanying operator | Accompanied the robot for medication-handling safety and recorded passenger/cargo observations. Presence also applied during the study's no-correction workflow. | Time devoted to accompaniment versus research annotation; whether/how accompaniment could be removed after validation; paid staffing allocation. |
| Pharmacist | Prepared medication, loaded the drawer and initiated delivery. | Incremental loading/interface time versus the existing courier workflow. |
| Support staff | Requested/dispatched the robot and usually retrieved medication at the destination before handing it to a nurse. | Dispatch, monitoring, retrieval and transfer minutes; concurrent work and avoidable paid time. |
| Nurse | Sometimes retrieved medication directly when support staff were unavailable. | Frequency and duration of the additional walk/interface/verification tasks. |
| Exception response | Corridor, communication and elevator-blockage failures were classified. | Recovery time, number of people, repeated attempts and ultimate medication delivery after a failed mission. |

A safety observer in a research trial does not prove permanent commercial escort requirements. It does prove that “no corrective intervention” cannot be read as “no person present” or “zero human support” for this observed sample. Conversely, keeping an escort in the cost boundary forever without further evidence would also overstate what the study establishes.

##### What can be calculated—and what cannot

Section 3.2 reports 14 failures: eight elevator blockages, two elevator-call communication errors and four corridor-navigation failures. These categories sum correctly. Thus **8/14 = 57.14% of classified failures were elevator blockages**. That is a failure-composition statistic, not an intervention rate or a share of staff time. The study-selection calculation **122/135 = 90.37%** also holds for the logged request cohort; it does not estimate the eligible share of all hospital transport demand.

The overall success figures conflict. Section 2.4 gives 122 eligible missions, section 3.2 gives 14 failures, and section 3.4 analyzes 108 completed trials. Those counts are internally consistent with each other, and **108/122 = 88.52%**. Section 3.1 nevertheless states **87.03%** overall success, a difference of about 1.49 percentage points. The source does not explain a different denominator or additional exclusions. The JSON records the count-implied percentage as an **audit calculation only** and leaves the selected success probability null. Arithmetic alone cannot establish which published field is erroneous.

Another boundary remains uncertain: sections 2.6–2.6.1 describe questionnaires after a four-week testing phase, whereas mission dates span June 18–29. The relationship between these phases is not explained. No four-week robot exposure is reconstructed from the survey wording.

##### Service quality and the proposed dispatch improvement

The fitted elevator-operating-rate threshold is 59.01%, and the paper reports 95.52% success in the selected lower-congestion subgroup. This is an association and a threshold fitted in the study sample. It is not a prospective experiment demonstrating the causal effect of a congestion-aware dispatch policy. Restricting dispatch to quiet periods may defer useful work or shift urgent deliveries to people. Filtering out non-elevator failures to reach approximately 100% is particularly unsuitable as an all-cause service-success claim.

Elevator waiting and travel analyses use the **108 completed trials**. Their delay relationships therefore omit the failed-mission burden and cannot determine end-to-end service time. Table 1's temporal definitions and section 3.4's shorthand are not fully identical; the package does not rebuild a mission cycle from those plots. The public paper says the underlying datasets are available from the corresponding author on request, rather than providing a retrieved mission-level dataset. No outreach was made.

The 22 survey respondents comprise seven pharmacists, seven support staff and eight nurses. Support staff report higher usability, while nurses and pharmacists acquire small handling/interface tasks. Overall NASA-TLX scores do not differ significantly across the groups. These are subjective workload comparisons, not a before/after time-and-motion estimate of labor removed. The next useful experiment would measure normal handling, accompaniment and exception recovery alongside all requested services, including urgent/manual fallbacks and deferrals.

### Focused follow-up: Monthly commercial funding and title

Authoritative analysis: [research/monthly_commercial_finance.md](research/monthly_commercial_finance.md).

The commercial arrangement can change whether a technically identical plan is executed, because different actors must settle different obligations at different dates. The new [monthly contract model](model/MONTHLY_COMMERCIAL.md) isolates this mechanism using the accepted Treasury and physical-flow engines. It compares outright sale, independent lease and provider-owned availability service. All amounts and operating terms are **synthetic accounting witnesses**, not observed prices or deployment economics.

This distinction matters for the provider/customer workstreams and Workstream K's value-capture question. A model or operator may have a positive allocated lifetime result and still lack the cash needed for the next manufacturing, installation or support step. The K architecture model is a separate conditional lifetime comparison; these monthly contracts do not yet import its adaptation costs, model fees, support deltas or qualified configurations. No claim of joint financeability follows by placing their tables next to one another.

#### What the funded comparison isolates

Each world begins with the same requested manufacture, qualified-component access, site demand, shipping and commissioning slots, and support requirements. There is one month-0 paid start, month-1 acceptance/shipment, month-2 installation, then a quarter first month of output followed by two full months. Each funded world supplies 225 accepted units over the five-month horizon and incurs $37,400 of included physical vendor outlays. The provider pays manufacture and transport in every world. The sale customer or lessor buys the hardware; customers pay separate installation/support in sale and lease. The service provider retains the robot and pays those operating costs.

The price and fees determine who holds the cash, asset and obligations. In the synthetic reference, the $60,000 acquisition price is paid once. An independent lessor pays it; the operator does not also buy the robot. Lease payments are $10,000 per installed robot-month, including commissioning without proration. Provider service charges $10,000 per actually supported robot-month and collects one month later. The final service fee remains a receivable at the horizon, rather than being counted as cash already collected. None of these fees estimates an actual market price, target return or model-company margin.

Under ample funding, increasing the hardware price by $5,000 transfers $5,000 from purchaser to provider without changing physical resource use or output. That invariance is conditional: if the higher price crosses the purchaser's cash limit, shipment fails and realized output falls. Prices therefore redistribute cash directly and can alter physical activity indirectly through financeability. A comparison that forces output to remain equal after that failure would hide the mechanism it is supposed to explain.

#### What the adverse cases change

A provider opening with $10,000 cannot pay the $36,000 start. A willing customer with enough money to buy the eventual robot does not create a current receipt: no unit is manufactured, no purchase is collected and no output appears. Conversely, after a funded start has produced an accepted unit, a settled shipment receipt may fund a later start under the explicitly ordered phase-5 policy. The engine does not borrow from the future to manufacture the unit that must be sold first.

An independent lessor with one cent less than the purchase price cannot buy the robot. The provider retains the finished asset; transport is not charged, title does not change and output stays zero. If the purchase would settle but transport cannot be paid even afterward, the grouped shipment fails without committing the purchase receipt. This avoids a financially paid transfer that silently disappears from the physical ledger.

A lease customer with $20,000 can pay the first $10,000 rent and $1,100 commissioning/support package, leaving $8,900. The next $10,000 rent cannot settle. The full unpaid fee remains outstanding, support stops and the robot remains installed with the lessor as title owner. At the end of this five-month case, $20,000 rent is unpaid and output is only the first 25 units. The installed-robot count alone would conceal that production stopped. A separate test supplies later customer cash, pays arrears first, and resumes ordinary support without recharging installation or applying another commissioning ramp.

Provider service changes the party bearing the operating gap. With opening provider cash of $37,200 and a two-month collection lag, manufacture, shipment and commissioning exhaust available cash after the first 25 output units. The next month's support cannot be paid. The first service receipt arrives in month 4, funds actual support, and permits 100 units. Total output is 125, with no service invoice generated for the unsupported month. A receivable can describe future cash without supplying current maintenance.

Refundable deposits expose a different risk. A restricted $30,000 deposit added to $10,000 of provider cash still leaves only $10,000 spendable, so the start is blocked and cancellation can refund the deposit. If the same deposit is spendable, the provider can pay the $36,000 start. Cancellation the next month leaves an accepted, unsold unit, $4,000 cash and a $30,000 refund liability. Manufacturing expenditure is not reversed. An explicit later receipt can finance the refund; there is no automatic write-off or recovery value.

#### Interpretation and next evidence

This result supports a conditional proposition: changing asset ownership or payment terms can overcome one actor's funding constraint while moving exposure to another. It does not prove that leasing or service expands aggregate finance, lowers complete lifecycle resource costs or produces a superior investment. The lessor's funds are explicit opening money, not costless bank credit; its acquisition is a transfer rather than another manufactured robot. All arrangements compete for the same physical inputs and support capacity.

The remaining empirical question is which actual contract terms, billing milestones, qualification obligations, capital costs and service responsibilities govern a matched deployment. The remaining model question is whether an operating entrant can acquire customers and meet due enterprise obligations while drawing from the same robot, support and customer-demand pools as the incumbent. That requires a common demand ledger and company funding schedules. The present synthetic contract extension advances this question while leaving the annual fleet, separate entrant reference and K lifetime architectures explicitly distinct.

All prices, capacities, customer moves and financing terms are **synthetic**; this is a mechanism test, not evidence that a real newco can raise capital or operate at these margins.

The practical distinction is between technological access and delivered production. A customer can be willing to buy a service before its provider has installed the robots, funded staffing, secured working capital or received payment. A supplier can own a robot and retain a customer obligation while delivering nothing. A shared ledger makes those mismatches visible instead of resolving them with an independent capacity assumption or a negative cash balance.

#### Customer capture can change distribution without adding the same work twice

The experiment begins with 1,000 recurring units served by an incumbent. It separately assumes 200 unmet and 100 hypothetical new units of potential demand. The entrant attempts to acquire 70 incumbent units, 20 unmet units and 10 new units. When it delivers all 100 and the incumbent delivers its remaining 930, total service is **1,030**, not 1,100. The 70 transferred units are the same underlying work with another supplier. The increment comes from serving 20 previously unmet units and 10 assumed new units; the model does not estimate their welfare value or infer that demand from the robot's existence.

A customer assignment and delivered work are different records. In the reference commissioning month, the entrant has acquired the 100-unit obligation but its one commissioned robot supplies only 25 units at the single quarter ramp. The incumbent supplies 930, so combined delivery is **955**, temporarily below the original 1,000. The entrant's 25 units are allocated proportionally: 17.5 transferred-baseline, five unmet and 2.5 new units. A model that subtracted only the entrant's delivered baseline units from incumbent demand would silently assume immediate fallback for its missed service. This model requires an explicit reassignment and actual incumbent capacity before such fallback occurs.

#### Common robot access still leaves procurement and utilization choices

The provider funds five starts, while two incumbent robots already exist as historical stock. The shared commissioning and support limits permit only three additions. Under prompt incumbent procurement and the declared allocation cycle, two additions go to the incumbent and one to the entrant; two new accepted robots remain finished. The entrant requested ten. Its full supported capacity is 100 units, with 25 in its commissioning month; a ten-robot calculation with the correct quarter ramp would still give 250 rather than 25 units, overstating actual initial capacity tenfold.

Delaying incumbent procurement changes allocation rather than changing technology. The entrant receives three of the five new robots under the same support ceiling. Its initial robot capacity is 75 and later capacity 300, but its acquired demand is still only 100. Eight-month market delivery rises from 8,105 in the reference to 8,155. The entrant's closing cash is nevertheless lower: $3,650 versus $20,350, under the stipulated lease and customer-return schedules. More early robot access improves ramp output while increasing lease exposure on underused assets. This is a conditional utilization/finance mechanism, not a general argument against faster deployment.

A prompt customer-return attempt can alter entrant cash substantially while leaving combined delivery unchanged. Returning the 70 baseline customers in month 3 rather than month 6 reduces entrant delivery from 385 to 175 units across the horizon. Incumbent capacity supplies the returned work, so both worlds still deliver 8,105 total units. The entrant closes with $1,450 rather than $20,350. Market production and entrant value capture can therefore diverge even with identical technology and the same total delivered service. After the return, the incumbent uses more of its already-paid human shift; unchanged cash outlays do not establish unchanged human work or economic resource consumption. The model assumes a successful dated reassignment; it does not establish real switching rights, retention, bidding or preemptive incumbent strategy.

#### Cash failure leaves obligations, assets and unserved customers

The default reference has one-month collection lags. A stress case instead delays collection of entrant work billed from month 3 to four months after delivery and starts with $20,500 cash. The entrant delivers 25 in month 2 and 100 in month 3. In month 4 it cannot pay its due obligations. Its installed robot remains owned by the lessor and its customer assignments persist, but support and entrant delivery stop. The incumbent still serves 930, so combined delivery falls from **1,030 to 930**. The missing 70 baseline units do not reappear automatically at the incumbent.

A separate case schedules and pays for customer return in month 5. Incumbent delivery then restores the baseline to 1,000 using its available funded capacity; the entrant's unmet/new customer assignments still receive no service. Across eight months, delivery is 7,705 without this switchback and 7,915 with it. Both remain below the incumbent-only 8,000 in this stress. These comparisons make failure and recovery assumptions economically consequential rather than treating customer transfer as a reversible accounting identity.

The fixed overhead and lease commitments remain owed during suspension; they are not canceled because production stopped. This is a stated contract assumption. The aggregate end buyer, suppliers, lessor and investor have explicit accounts, but the model does not reconstruct every supplier's payroll, bank credit or working capital. Actual firms may renegotiate or terminate obligations, recover assets, use alternative staff, or face different switching costs. None of those outcomes is silently supplied by the fixture.

#### Asset leasing does not finance enterprise formation and receivables

The entrant must pay formation/site work, acquire customers, cover overhead, commission its allocated robot, support it and bridge collection lags. Leasing shifts acquisition funding to the lessor; it does not pay those other bills. With only $1,000 and no added capital, formation and committed operating obligations cannot settle, customer capture fails and no robot is allocated to the entrant. The incumbent continues serving the baseline. The cash remains in the entrant's account under full-or-nothing settlement while unpaid commitments accumulate; a positive bank balance does not establish operational readiness.

A separate, actually funded equity path starts from that same $1,000. The fixed-policy search finds $18,950 of additional month-0 equity sufficient to execute the reference without a failed payment. One cent less fails the month-3 support requirement. Minimum actual cash reaches zero at the threshold. This is a transparent cash-timing boundary for the invented schedule, not evidence that an investor will provide the capital or accept its return. A late contribution cannot finance earlier work, undo a missed customer-acquisition attempt or manufacture a share of robots already allocated elsewhere.

Human capacity also has a financial boundary. The incumbent's human shift is a paid fixed commitment; its wage bill does not automatically decline when robots or the entrant reduce human work. Human service requires that staffing commitment to settle. The model therefore distinguishes capacity substitution, paid idle capacity and observed payroll removal; the last remains unknown. Whole-system economics would additionally require investment recovery, supplier fixed costs, actual support requirements and terminal asset/receivable treatment.

#### Implication for the research program

The result strengthens the form of the entry thesis: an AI-native operating company needs a funded route from customer commitments through shared physical deployment to reliable delivery and collection, plus a reason to retain enough work after incumbents respond. Fast adoption can help, but excess leased capacity, working-capital timing, fixed staffing and customer rights can outweigh its benefit to the entrant even when aggregate service improves.

This common ledger should now be constrained with consequential observed evidence. Prioritize real service obligations and cancellation/renewal terms, operator support and accepted-output denominators, accessible qualified supply and deployment milestones. Regional frontier-model evidence also remains important: model-level demonstrations, fees or announced partners do not by themselves establish any of the customer, fleet or treasury conditions above. The K architecture reference still needs an explicit monthly cost bridge. Autonomous reinvestment, loan schedules and recovery remain separate extensions; the current result does not close W1-CASH or the overall campaign.

### What changes the robotics judgment

Digital AI changes both the candidate robotic system and the incumbent against which it must compete. A robot operator that beats yesterday's human process can still lose to a human operation with better scheduling, documentation, purchasing, exception handling, or engineering support. These digital improvements can also complement robots. The correct comparison therefore needs four matched operating configurations, with an explicit interaction and full implementation costs.

The strongest source evidence in this wave does not support a universal digital productivity coefficient. A large customer-support deployment increased resolved issues per worker-hour, particularly for less experienced workers. A later study of experienced software developers found its current uplift estimate undermined by participant selection, task selection and concurrent-agent time accounting. These are informative differences in production systems, rather than competing estimates of one economy-wide effect. Likewise, the current peer-reviewed agent-coordination study supports conditional architecture choices; it does not establish that adding agents always increases production.

Direct digital contributions to physical systems are real but occur at distinct interfaces. AutoRT physically used foundation models to select and organize data collection. DrEureka used GPT-4 to generate training configurations subsequently tested on hardware. Astranomy evaluated language-model control and oversight in simulation. Open-RMF documents conventional traffic and building interfaces. SAP announces enterprise agents. Combining those sources into a diagram does **not** establish that a working LLM-to-ERP-to-fleet production stack has been deployed or measured.

Three immediate decisions follow. First, select markets using accepted output, timing, integration and support requirements rather than benchmark success alone. Second, keep the customer process owner, deployment integrator, robot controller, model provider and capital provider distinct even when one entity fills several roles. Third, measure learning and R&D gains through validated improvements and critical-path reduction, not raw episodes, generated code or announced customer access.

### The connected ecosystem dossier

The chain is physical inputs and capital → model research, training, post-training and evaluation → inference and cloud distribution → agent/tool runtimes → integration and change management → operating customers → accepted customer output. Each arrow has a different unit and evidentiary status. Training FLOPs are not usable inference capacity; capital commitments are not commissioned capacity; model access is not permission to write business records; a portfolio relationship is not a deployment; an accepted work product is not eliminated payroll.

The separate HI chapter covers power, qualified compute/memory, capacity and feedback to construction, maintenance and infrastructure. This H chapter covers how capability travels through that chain into work. Its upstream development evidence is deliberately bounded: [Hoffmann et al.'s compute-optimal training paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.15556v1) studies allocation between parameters and training tokens under a specified training-compute budget. The domain is text-model training loss and downstream evaluation. Its fitted allocation exponents are neither robot reliability exponents nor coefficients for adoption, manufacturing cost or firm output. Deployment lifetime inference costs and physical experience collection introduce objectives that differ from this fixed-training-budget optimization. Post-training, data licensing and research-talent markets remain incomplete in this wave.

The [FTC's January 2025 partnership report summary](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-issues-staff-report-ai-partnerships-investments-study) identifies equity/revenue-sharing, cloud-spend commitments, distribution, information access and some control/exclusivity rights in selected Microsoft–OpenAI, Amazon–Anthropic and Alphabet–Anthropic relationships. Its information boundary is September 2024 plus public material through January 2025. It supports a mechanism in which finance can be bundled with compute purchases and switching costs; it does not establish the October 2026 terms of every relationship or realized competitive harm. The implication for robotics is to maintain separate financial, compute, data, operational-control and customer-access edges. An investment cannot be counted again as independent cloud revenue and additional free deployment capital in a consolidated scenario.

The [Census CES 26-25 full paper](https://www2.census.gov/library/working-papers/2026/adrm/ces/CES-WP-26-25.pdf), dated April 2026, supplies a better deployment denominator than announcements. For the November 2025–January 2026 reference period, 18% of firms reported AI use in a business function; the employment-weighted figure was 32%. Among functional adopters, 57% used AI in three or fewer functions. These are broad-AI measures, not LLM-only use or physical automation. The revised core question took effect on 17 November 2025, so a discontinuity from earlier wording is not clean adoption growth. Firm use refers to two weeks, worker-task use to six months, and firms report worker behavior. The study distinguishes adoption, breadth, task use and investment; it does not identify causal productivity from their correlations with commercial performance.

For robotics this favors an adoption record with separate capability, process integration, operating authority, workforce use, economic attractiveness and delivered output. Low adoption need not mean technological incapability, but high benchmark capability does not establish the size of an economically viable deployment overhang. That quantity remains unknown.

### Two contrasting operating dossiers

#### Business-software customer support: assistance inside a repeated production process

[Generative AI at Work](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/140/2/889/7990658), published in the QJE on 4 February 2025, examines 5,172 customer-support agents at a software firm and its subcontractors. The rollout primarily occurred in fall 2020 and winter 2021. The tool used GPT-3-based real-time suggestions; agents remained responsible for conversations and could edit or ignore suggestions. The relevant product is a resolved customer issue, not generated text.

Access increased resolved issues per agent-hour by 15% on average. This combined changes in handling time, parallel chat handling and resolution. Benefits were heterogeneous: less experienced/lower-skilled workers gained more, while highly skilled workers showed little productivity improvement and some quality deterioration. Evidence during outages is consistent with some persistent worker learning, but those outages were rare and estimates noisier. The paper's staggered-adoption/event-study design handles observed worker/time/tenure factors and examines alternative estimators; interpretation still requires parallel-trend and adoption assumptions. It is not simply a randomized trial of the full observed rollout.

The mechanism is concrete: knowledge and communication practices are delivered at the point of decision inside an existing workflow, with a human retaining final authority. This suggests a research question for physical end markets: can digital assistance improve dispatch, maintenance diagnosis, operator training or customer-service work around a physical process? It does not supply a 15% uplift for assembly, floor cleaning, nursing, inspection or robot supervision. Even in the source population, more resolved issues per hour does not identify removed payroll, wage changes or equilibrium employment. At fixed work and quality, time can be released; its realized value depends on staffing, demand and redeployment.

For adoption strategy, the relevant complement is not just model quality. It includes historical examples, an interface fitted to live work, human judgment, feedback and training capacity. It also warns that copying the average effect across different worker cohorts can overstate expert benefits. A digitally improved incumbent may become more competitive before installing any robot.

#### Software development: changed task selection makes uplift difficult to identify

[METR's 24 February 2026 update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/) explicitly rejects treating its new experiment as a reliable current productivity estimate. The original February–June 2025 study found that AI-allowed tasks took 19% longer, with a reported interval of +2% to +39%. The later experiment began in August 2025 and included 57 developers across 143 repositories and more than 800 tasks. Among ten returning developers, the raw task-time estimate was −18% with an interval from −38% to +9%; among new developers it was −4%, interval −15% to +9%. Negative figures mean less time, not negative productivity.

The crucial finding is methodological. Developers increasingly declined participation because they did not want to work without AI. Participants withheld tasks they expected AI to accelerate. Pay also fell from $150 to $50 per hour between studies, creating another selection mechanism. Some work changed in scope or quality; some developers worked on another task while an agent ran, making task-time attribution difficult. Randomizing submitted tasks does not remove selection into submission. The authors considered the data weak evidence about the magnitude of current speedup.

This does not establish that AI slows developers in October 2026, nor that large current gains are proven. It establishes that a dated average task-time result can lose validity as tools, work choice and organization change. For robotics R&D, the unit should be validated engineering output at a fixed objective, with active human time, elapsed critical-path time, model/training compute, failed proposals and physical testing recorded separately. Faster code generation is not equivalent to a shorter hardware qualification campaign. Concurrent agents may improve total throughput while complicating per-task measurement; that is a reason to improve the accounting, not to assume a gain.

Together these dossiers motivate heterogeneous digital effects, an explicit human baseline, and outcome-specific measurement. They cannot be pooled into one “AI productivity” parameter.

### Three direct-contribution maps

#### Robot reasoning and control

AutoRT's interface is image/scene understanding → language task proposal → affordance and constitution filtering → chosen scripted, learned or teleoperated policy → observed episode outcome. The physical collection spans four office buildings and seven months. It used 53 robots in total and more than 20 simultaneously; those are different denominators. One human supervised three to five mobile manipulators, and sometimes reset the environment.

Table 1 is important counterevidence to reading “77,000 episodes” as productive autonomous output: 73,293 scripted-policy trials had 21% success, 3,060 teleoperated trials 82%, and 936 RT-2 trials 4.7%. The authors attribute low RT-2 performance to differences between collection environments and its training/inference conditions. Their stated objective is diverse data collection, with a preliminary downstream-learning evaluation. Successful data generation, successful task execution, accepted customer output and profitable operation are four different outcomes.

For the six shared market cells, a proposed digital planner needs explicit state, command authority, validation and fallback. A low-level controller remains separate. The measurable contribution might be improved task selection, fewer interventions, faster recovery or better held-out performance. Each requires a target test; it cannot be inferred from model naming. Astranomy supplies a second, simulated control configuration and highlights why inference cost and timing must be separate from physical-policy quality.

#### Fleet, site, and enterprise operations

[Open-RMF interfacing](https://openrmf.readthedocs.io/en/latest/interfacing/index.html) documents fleet state, destination, pause/resume and path messages, plus door state and requests. A fleet that only reports state gives an orchestrator different options from one accepting paths or pause commands. Coordination therefore depends on negotiated interfaces and control rights, not just planning intelligence. The retrieved documentation is a mutable `latest` page, not a pinned deployment release.

The proposed full interface is ERP/WMS/MES orders, inventory and priorities → validated allocation or schedule → fleet manager → robot/building execution → acknowledged state and business-record reconciliation. The process owner defines priorities; the fleet manager controls movement; human staff resolve exceptions. Stale information, an unacknowledged instruction or duplicate transaction can break the chain. Timeouts, idempotency, reconciliation and escalation are proposed design requirements here, not claimed observations of a deployed LLM stack.

[SAP's May 2026 announcement](https://news.sap.com/2026/05/more-autonomous-supply-chain/) supplies intended planning, manufacturing, logistics, asset/service and ERP roles, with phased general availability through 2026. It supports identifying interfaces to investigate. It does not establish which named agent is available at a selected customer or a measured robotics productivity contribution.

The target-side boundary is stronger with [ChristianaCare's operator-authored account](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13048758/). Its grant-funded 2022–25 pilot used three cobots across more than 80 patient areas, reported more than 48,600 deliveries and tested EHR/elevator integrations. Operational value concentrated in nonurgent medication and equipment flows. Complex, time-sensitive nursing workflows were not meaningfully augmented. No LLM effect is isolated. For `CELL-M10-hospital-logistics-US-2025`, this argues for separate urgent and nonurgent cohorts and an explicit request/dispatch/infrastructure interface, rather than treating all hospital physical support as one eligible pool. A large delivery count is insufficient to infer nurse payroll savings.

#### Robotics research and development

DrEureka takes simulator code, an objective and safety instructions, generates candidate rewards, builds a reward-aware physics prior, proposes domain-randomization settings, trains policies and tests transfer. It used GPT-4, sampled 16 domain-randomization configurations and trained three seeds per configuration. The authors evaluated candidate policies in the real world. The comparisons show useful technical improvements in bounded locomotion and manipulation settings; they do not remove training or hardware-test needs.

The paper reports a three-hour runtime for its bounded domain-randomization comparison versus ten hours for CEM and twenty for BayRn. That is a method comparison, not a measured reduction in total humanoid engineering labor or development cost. It also explicitly lacks a systematic mechanism to select the most promising policy before deployment. Some ablations generated jerky, unsafe behavior that triggered controller protection. Candidate generation has value only when verification and release constraints are included.

The model consequence is to let an R&D intervention change a validated release's cost or delay only through an explicit critical path. Coding, reward search, training and physical validation can constrain different projects. If hardware testing remains binding, faster candidate generation may improve exploration but not calendar time. METR's measurement concerns reinforce recording total accepted engineering output and concurrent resource use rather than adding an unexplained “research acceleration” multiplier.

### Contract for the executable joint scenario

Use the authoritative CPU engine, not a second calculation implementation in this chapter. At matched output, quality, demand and period, let costs be:

| | Existing digital operations | Specified additional digital intervention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Human-led process | C00 | C01 |
| Specified robot-assisted process | C10 | C11 |

The robot's incremental saving under the improved digital baseline is `C01 − C11`; total joint saving is `C00 − C11`. The cost interaction is `C11 − C10 − C01 + C00`, with a negative value indicating complementary cost reduction. Output and service-quality contrasts must be displayed separately. A cell that cannot operate without required embedded robot software is not a valid “robot without software” counterfactual; hold that mandatory software fixed across the robot row and vary only the additional intervention.

Digital assistance to humans, robot planning latency, inference expense, integration amortization, intervention labor and coordination effects need separate controls. Evidence observations remain fixed when those scenario controls change. Shared scheduling or data improvements appear once. Demand expansion and altered service scope require a separate scenario. Cancellation also has a bounded meaning. [`TaskManager::_handle_cancel_request`](https://github.com/open-rmf/rmf_ros2/blob/7f7b247a618c22f76bf01ff0fb393f72317f6d9a/rmf_fleet_adapter/src/rmf_fleet_adapter/TaskManager.cpp#L3036) validates the cancel schema and sends a success response when `cancel_task` accepts the operation. If `cancel_task` returns false, the inspected handler sends no success response; it does not emit an explicit negative cancellation reply there. The pinned [web task route](https://github.com/open-rmf/rmf-web/blob/7aa265337936a5bd9a920b3fa2360a43244a015c/packages/api-server/api_server/routes/tasks/tasks.py#L219) validates the response and stores task state/request records after successful dispatch. ## 6. Digital infrastructure and physical production

Authoritative chapter: [research/digital_infrastructure.md](research/digital_infrastructure.md).

Research edition 0.1, retrieved 25 October 2026. This is the H1/H6 infrastructure contribution to the campaign, joined to I's configured-system supply analysis. Evidence records HI-001–HI-006 live in `data/digital_infrastructure_evidence.json`. Quantitative observations below do not calibrate the illustrative robot model.

### The decision

For a humanoid company, the relevant question is not whether AI infrastructure is growing. It is whether the particular compute, memory, power, manufacturing and operating complements required by a proposed production system will be available at acceptable cost and latency. The reverse question—whether robots can accelerate construction, manufacture or operation of AI infrastructure—needs its own service-level evidence. Neither direction follows automatically from growth in the other.

The current evidence supports a constrained, two-way relationship. Digital capability production already depends on large physical investments and grid connections. Robot policy development can use that infrastructure, but an onboard controller and a cloud accelerator do not consume interchangeable memory modules. Robotics might help selected infrastructure workflows, yet the public sources reviewed here do not measure a humanoid-induced reduction in data-center construction time or commissioning cost. That link remains a question, not a numerical feedback coefficient.

### What the latest infrastructure evidence establishes

The IEA's *Key Questions on Energy and AI*, published 16 April 2026, estimates global data-center electricity consumption at 485 TWh in 2025 and projects approximately 950 TWh in 2030. The latter is a central projection, not observed consumption or an independently booked pipeline. The population is all data centers, not exclusively LLM inference. Its estimate of 17% growth in total data-center electricity consumption in 2025 and 50% growth in AI-focused consumption describes different populations. Adding the two would double count the AI subset. [HI-001](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary)

The report describes simultaneous efficiency improvement and growth in more intensive workloads. It states that energy per individual AI task has fallen rapidly, while reasoning, video and agentic applications can use much more energy per request than simple text generation. These are not contradictory observations. Aggregate demand depends on service volume, output mix, quality requirements, retries and hardware/software efficiency. A cheap text query is therefore an inappropriate proxy for a long-horizon robot planning episode or an enterprise-agent workflow. No universal energy-per-token or energy-per-robot coefficient is inferred here.

IEA identifies grid access, energy equipment, advanced chips and high-bandwidth memory among constraints, and describes financing as consequential as projects exceed internally funded balance-sheet capacity. Its discussion of an HBM shortage extending at least through 2027 is a dated assessment, not a measured allocation to a robot OEM. The appropriate model input is an accessible qualified resource pool with lead times and contractual commitments, not a share of global semiconductor sales. Regional and application differences remain essential.

The October 15, 2026 TSMC management report supplies a more recent financial observation. It reports third-quarter consolidated capital expenditure of US$13.84 billion and year-to-date expenditure of US$40.64 billion. It reports 4.476 million 12-inch-equivalent wafer shipments in the quarter, with HPC representing 68% of net revenue. Those are distinct denominators: expenditure is not qualified capacity; wafer-equivalent shipments are not AI accelerators; HPC revenue is not exclusively robotics or even exclusively AI. The report attributes a sequential gross-margin decline partly to the initial N2 ramp. This is an actual example of new-process ramp affecting economics, not evidence for a universal robotics manufacturing learning rate. [HI-002](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/qr/phase4_reports/2026-10/f53a64fd40fc0b453f94e077e5aba8cad506f27b/3Q26%20ManagementReport.pdf)

The decision consequence is to separate four states in supplier diligence: announced investment, expenditure already made, qualified output, and output actually available to the customer. Even a profitable expanding supplier does not establish an individual robot firm's access to a specific grade, package or delivery window. Conversely, an aggregate AI bottleneck does not prove every embedded module is unavailable. The present evidence cannot quantify an OEM's priority, contracted price, inventory or qualification yield.

Micron's September 30, 2026 prepared remarks make the contractual allocation problem more concrete. Management says it has signed 26 multiyear take-or-pay strategic customer agreements, estimates they represent more than 35% of revenue through 2030, and reports US$32 billion of customer financial commitments, mostly deposits. Those are issuer disclosures and forward estimates with different meanings. Deposits cannot be added to future revenue as another economic benefit. The company says agreements cover the vast majority of its calendar-2027 HBM bit supply; that does not reveal an individual robot firm's allocation or establish a general LPDDR shortage coefficient. [HI-006](https://s25.q4cdn.com/621799436/files/doc_financials/2026/q4/Q4-FY26-Prepared-Remarks.pdf)

The same source distinguishes initial wafer output from meaningful production ramp several quarters later. Initial output dates for Idaho, Japan, Singapore and New York are management expectations spanning 2027–2030, not current qualified deliveries. This supports a supply-model structure with lead times, ramp and pre-existing commitments, but does not calibrate the timing of a humanoid OEM's production. It also shows a financing mechanism: customer commitments may support supplier investment while tying up customer cash and limiting uncommitted supply available to entrants. Whether a new robot company can secure similar terms is unknown.

Micron predicts that future humanoids could have memory requirements comparable to highly autonomous vehicles. That is a supplier's prospective market thesis, not a measured humanoid bill of materials. The configured products below are concrete counterweights to converting that thesis into a fixed per-robot memory requirement. Future architectures might use more onboard memory, smaller policies, remote inference or heterogeneous processors; workload and latency evidence must decide.

### Three bounded mechanisms and their counterarguments

**Lower reasoning cost may improve robot economics without changing robot capability.** The source-side evidence supports hardware/software efficiency improvement and changing workload mix. The target requires measured calls per accepted service, retry rates, latency, support and the relevant tariff. A counterexample is a mechanically constrained workflow where inference is a small cost and cheaper calls do not change accepted throughput. Permitted use: scenario structure and a research question, not a copied cost decline.

**Physical infrastructure can delay digital contributions to robots.** Training or remote inference may depend on constrained datacenter capacity and financing. Yet a particular deployed policy may run onboard, use existing contracted capacity, or be small enough that other constraints dominate. This mechanism should be tested with local versus remote architectures and documented allocations. It cannot justify imposing the same HBM delay on all Jetson-equipped robots.

**Robots might relieve infrastructure labor bottlenecks.** Candidate market stages include construction installation, equipment manufacturing, inspection and maintenance. A faster robot process only accelerates the completed facility if that process is on the critical path, its quality and permits remain acceptable, and upstream equipment or grid delays do not bind instead. Existing operator evidence of inspection or a construction task demonstration does not establish whole-project acceleration. The falsifier is a matched project comparison in which the robotic stage improves but energization or output does not. No positive feedback growth exponent is assigned.

### Strategic implications and remaining evidence

For RQ-SC-05, qualify a workload/configuration before selecting an onboard compute roadmap. Memory, latency, power and thermal requirements should precede peak-compute comparisons. For RQ-LLM-01/06, separate capital announcements from operational supply and customer access. For RQ-LLM-09 and the construction/utility cells, test an explicit critical path before claiming that physical automation accelerates AI development. These conclusions narrow the scenarios even without estimating a global robotics market.

The next useful public-evidence step is a matched robotics workload on a pinned embedded module and software stack, documenting memory use, end-to-end latency, sustained power and accepted output. A parallel supplier step is to identify disclosed product-specific qualification and delivery evidence. Neither an aggregate capex series nor a product page supplies those measurements. Public data may not resolve OEM allocations or negotiated commercial terms; those fields stay null and become later access-dependent questions.

#### What the timing includes—and what it omits

`benchmark_components` times backbone and action-head calls with CUDA synchronization before and after each stage. Input processing is benchmarked separately and its same timing array is reused across backend comparisons. `compute_e2e_from_components` then sums those arrays; the summary takes the median of the sums and computes frequency as `1000 / median_ms`. It does not time a single uninterrupted production request. Adding component medians would also not necessarily reproduce the median of their sums. [OWQ-E02, functions `benchmark_data_processing`, `benchmark_components`, `compute_e2e_from_components`, `main`]

The recipe invokes `BenchmarkConfig` without enabling `use_trajectory`, whose default is false; the harness extracts episode zero, step zero. A trajectory option exists, but its existence does not establish that the published latency used it. Model loading, engine construction, video capture, observation freshness, network serving, final action denormalization/robot command transport, actuator dynamics and safety handling are outside the measured component sum. The code times `action_head.get_action` and discards its result; it does not measure motor execution or a completed task. There are no recovered per-iteration result arrays tied to the published run.

For the tutorial's advertised 25 Hz, the corresponding nominal interval is 40 ms. The reported 39.9 ms median leaves only 0.1 ms against that interval, while the reported mean is 1.0 ms above it. These are arithmetic comparisons using rounded, component-sum statistics, not a target robot's validated deadline or an estimate of the fraction of missed cycles. The source frequency of 25.1 Hz is the rounded reciprocal of 39.9 ms. Neither a reciprocal median nor a mean determines sustained throughput under queueing, thermal limits and contention. A robot might execute an action chunk with a faster independent low-level controller, but the correct scheduling and feedback contract must then be specified and tested; the nominal policy-call frequency is not its motor-control frequency.

Memory qualification is similarly incomplete. The tutorial's approximately 60 GB free-space recommendation concerns disk storage for containers, engines and assets, not peak runtime RAM. The pipeline's 8192 MB builder-workspace default is a build limit, not a measured runtime footprint. The harness explicitly deletes prior policy instances to avoid holding two full copies simultaneously, with a comment referring to Orin out-of-memory behavior. That comment is evidence of a software resource precaution, not a measured Orin/Thor capacity comparison. HI-003/004 remain the source of module memory specifications; actual memory occupancy, headroom for perception and worst-case concurrent workloads remain null. ### Findings that change deployment judgment

The strongest evidence supports choosing a complete service configuration before choosing a robot morphology. In the cases below, mobility, measurement, interpretation, action and business value are successive conditions. Success at an earlier condition cannot substitute for failure at a later one. PG&E's drone test is particularly informative: an inspection aircraft can fly successfully while producing unusable inspection imagery. A model crediting every successful mission with a completed inspection would overstate useful output before reaching any debate about manufacturing cost.

Quadrupeds have direct productive roles in manufacturing and infrastructure. They need not be justified as a temporary precursor to humanoids. The examined customer accounts support sensing, facility information and remote inspection roles. They do not establish a general replacement rate for production or maintenance workers. A humanoid strategy should therefore distinguish entry into existing sensing services from expansion into manipulation and intervention. The second step changes the accepted output, qualification requirements and physical interfaces; it is not a free extension of the first.

The sources also separate available robots from supported productive capacity. Site mapping, route validation, networking, operator training, human review and spare parts can bind even when chassis supply is ample. A manufacturing announcement does not remove those constraints. Conversely, a more capable policy may widen the feasible service set while increasing onboard memory, power, sensors or support requirements. The same advance can expand addressability and make an existing configuration inadequate.

### Service boundaries and the shared atlas

Cases link to the common atlas without adding independent labor totals. BMW supports the manufacturing anchor `CELL-M05-auto-assembly-US-2025`, but its observed service is UK plant maintenance and information capture, not US assembly output. National Grid, Equinor and PG&E inform `CELL-M03-utility-inspection-US-2025`; Equinor's CO₂ facility is an infrastructure comparator rather than an electrical-utility employment observation. Rwanda informs `CELL-M10-hospital-logistics-US-2025`, with interfacility distribution kept separate from movement inside a hospital. Agricultural application is a bounded service-business comparator within the atlas's agriculture family, not a seventh deep market estimate.

Three rules follow. Keep foreign evidence in its observed geography. Preserve the historical service window instead of treating retrieval in 2026 as current field performance. Allocate an inspection, delivery or treated application once even when several robots and people contribute. A drone observing an asset, a quadruped collecting another viewpoint and an engineer approving maintenance can be one service chain, not three sources of avoided labor.

#### PHY-D01 — PG&E: successful flight can fail the service test

#### PHY-D02 — Rwanda: observed service outcomes, unresolved cost-effectiveness

Nisingizwe and colleagues studied 20 facilities using 2015–2019 administrative data and drone deliveries during March 2017–December 2019. The initial edition used the PubMed abstract; this revision checks the publisher full text retrieved through Exa. Road times are estimated because planned collection of hospital road records was prevented by COVID-19 restrictions. There is no control group. The 67% reduction in expirations is an interrupted-time-series estimate per facility at 12 months, not a randomized treatment effect. [PHY-E05: full article, Methods, Results and Discussion](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/langlo/article/PIIS2214-109X(22)00048-1/fulltext).

The time boundaries materially change interpretation. The reported mean of 49.6 minutes includes order preparation and packaging; the 41-minute median used in the facility comparison excludes it. The principal road comparator is a hospital vehicle making a round trip to collect blood. The paper reports median advantages of 79 minutes against CBT driving estimates and 98 minutes against Google Maps estimates for this arrangement. A hypothetical center-origin, one-way road service reduces the corresponding median advantages to 19 and 28.5 minutes. These are different service organizations and statistics, not interchangeable speed multipliers. Table 1, Table 2 and the adjacent Results paragraphs provide the locators.

The authors also state in the final Discussion paragraph that drone delivery was more expensive than road delivery in Rwanda at publication. That adverse cost direction belongs in the dossier even though the paper supplies no matched dollar cost or provider margin. Faster delivery and less waste could still have health or economic value, but the cost-effectiveness calculation remains unresolved. No accepted clinical-SLA fraction or causal mortality benefit is established.

This case is materially stronger evidence for a useful delivered service than a flight demonstration, but it is not a provider-margin study. The mechanism includes geography, dispatch, perishable inventory and timely availability. Comparing only aircraft travel to a courier's journey omits launch preparation, receiving, reserve capacity and inventory design. Counting reduced waste once in the logistics model and again as a separate platform benefit would overstate value.

The humanoid analogy largely fails at the physical mechanism. Aerial transport can bypass road geometry; better ground manipulation cannot inherit that shortcut. The useful research question is whether a different production network changes service quality and total cost. For US hospital operations, indoor handoffs, interfacility deliveries and stock positioning need distinct scopes. This study does not supply an indoor robot productivity coefficient or a current US health-logistics adoption rate.

### Matched embodiment comparisons and transfer tests

`PHY-C01` compares one accepted utility condition inspection, with equal coverage, resolution and response interval. A digital-improved human process, fixed sensing, wheeled inspection, quadruped, aerial sensing and a possible humanoid are alternatives only where they meet those constraints. Fixed sensing can be attractive for stable viewpoints; accessible level routes can remove the need for legged mobility. Stairs, terrain, energized access and hazardous-zone requirements can change feasibility. A sensing-only service provides no evidence that humanoid hands justify their additional burden. Adding repair or manipulation changes the service and requires another comparison.

`PHY-C02` compares one usable blood order delivered by its deadline. Road courier plus digital dispatch, aerial transport with ground handling and local inventory/replenishment are competing networks. Ground robots may complement indoor handoffs but are not established substitutes for the rural aerial leg. Road access, urgency, payload eligibility and sufficiently dense demand can reverse the preferred arrangement. Neither comparison has enough whole-system cost evidence for a numeric ranking. The correct present result is a feasibility map and break-even condition, not invented prices.

The five structured transfer tests preserve separate source and target evidence. PG&E supports separating navigation from useful information. Rwanda rejects copying an aerial geography advantage into humanoid labor productivity. PX4/ODRI versus Unitree supports testing artifact-specific engineering reuse while rejecting blanket openness. The [FAA's July 2024 UTM authorization](https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/faa-makes-drone-history-dallas-area) motivates shared intentions and operating authority for mixed fleets, but neither airspace rules nor throughput coefficients transfer. Agricultural budgets provide a fixed-cost and usable-demand mechanism, without empirical cost parameters for inspection robots. Each card states a target prediction and falsifier; none calibrates the common model.

### Supply-to-deployment model implications

The common engine owns the time-indexed scenario; this contribution supplies its evidence boundaries. Use distinct compatible component pools, reserve service spares for installed cohorts, apply assembly acceptance loss once, retain lead times and finished inventory, and commission only within site/support capacity. Replacements are not net additions. Qualified receipts cannot be generated from announced annual capacity, and net receipts cannot have external commitments deducted twice.

An explicit supported-fleet target needs a backcast of surviving stock, retirements, replacement and expansion units, required accepted completions, assembly starts, component receipts, service inventory and commissioning/support labor. That is necessary-condition logic. It does not prove orders, customer finance or accepted-service demand. The model agent uses virtual configurations for synthetic coefficients; the named G1/Go2 maps must not be treated as cost-calibrated versions of those virtual systems.

Three decision changes follow. First, product strategy should fund the measurement and workflow bottleneck before assuming extra robot capability creates value. Second, an inspection-service newco needs site access, contracts, review and dense support, not only robot capital. Third, expansion planning should test a support-bound case alongside component qualification and manufacturing delays. The available evidence permits these causal structures but does not establish which bottleneck dominates across the economy.

The consequential result is that industrial inspection coverage can remain incomplete after autonomous navigation works. TenneT’s 2018 offshore trial assessed 385 instruments in three rooms, with room-specific inspectable shares of 92%, 85% and 54%. Hidden valves, doors and operating restrictions constrained coverage. Its later onshore program explicitly selected points suited to the robot, while the public evaluation material left costs and results unfilled. #### Episode QOF-D01: offshore ANYmal B300, 2018

The [field study](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-981-15-9460-1_18) by Gehring and colleagues includes TenneT authors Marcel Potz and Lars Gerstenberg alongside ANYbotics researchers. Its ETH bibliographic wrapper dates publication to January 2021; the observed deployment occurred in 2018 on an unnamed TenneT offshore HVDC platform in the North Sea. This is firsthand, operator-coauthored evaluation, with supplier participation—not independent third-party telemetry. The full chapter was retrieved through Exa; a separate direct ETH PDF request returned HTTP 429. Section and figure locators are retained rather than inventing PDF page offsets. [QOF-E01]

The tested system was ANYmal B300 with a prototype inspection payload, not today’s ANYmal X. The chapter describes stronger actuators for steep stairs, onboard visual/thermal/acoustic sensing, lidar-based mapping and a charging station. It does not publish a software build or serial revision. A qualified operator could program missions, observe status and intervene remotely. The supplied GUI produced a post-mission report covering navigation, inspection and unexpected events.

##### Exposure and the service denominator

Section 6 reports more than 30 autonomous missions over two weeks. One illustrated route covered 19 points of interest. The study reports an average tour time of approximately 25 minutes, including six minutes of inspection on average; the timing sample and distribution are not specified. Those figures describe route timing and a field-test window. They do not establish 30 successful missions out of 30 attempts, continuous two-week unattended uptime, or a productive rate applicable to every instrument. The contemporaneous [manufacturer announcement](https://www.anybotics.com/news/worlds-first-autonomous-offshore-robot/), dated 25 October 2018, instead describes a one-week September pilot and a video mission with 16 points. The publication does not explain whether these are different subsets or phases. They remain distinct observations; this edition does not average the windows or add the routes. [QOF-E01 §6/Fig.11; QOF-E02]

The study’s more useful denominator is its coverage assessment: 385 instruments across a pump room, water-treatment room and HVA/C room. It reports that 92%, 85% and 54% of the respective points could be inspected. Individual room counts are absent, so even a pooled percentage cannot be calculated defensibly. These are coverage results, not repeated-measurement precision, defect-detection sensitivity, mission reliability or completed maintenance actions. They also concern an evaluated subset, not a complete inventory of every mandatory site inspection. Several HVA/C valves faced walls or were otherwise hidden from view. [QOF-E01 §6]

Section 3.1 adds two upstream constraints. About half the site was inaccessible to both people and the robot while energized because of discharge risk; access occurred during the annual shutdown. Of approximately 200 rooms, the authors identify 50 as interesting for robot inspection during normal operation. Neither ratio is a measured labor-substitution share. The robot did not erase the site’s operating restrictions.

##### Human work and unfinished integration

Field engineers needed safety training. The test team installed Wi-Fi and charging infrastructure because the working areas lacked a wireless network. Commissioning involved manually guiding a mapping pass, a second pass to register inspection points using an AR marker, defining pressure/temperature criteria, editing paths, and programming reactions to communications loss and unexpected obstacles. Simulation and real-site tests followed. Waiting for operator instructions at an unknown obstacle is a specified fallback, not evidence that its labor cost was zero. [QOF-E01 §§3.1, 4.3, 5]

The study identifies closed doors as a major remaining barrier. Fire rules prevented automating every door in both directions; the authors proposed manipulation as a possible solution. An arm might also improve sensor visibility and operate switches, but its reliability and economics were not tested in this case. The conclusion describes daily inspection as a future possibility relative to monthly human inspections and says platform control-system integration and long-distance communications still needed work. The demonstrated local Wi-Fi GUI must therefore not be read as an already operational, integrated onshore maintenance service. [QOF-E01 §7]

The inspected sources give no intervention count, reviewer minutes, verified defect dispositions, avoided trips, maintenance work orders, downtime avoided, all-in purchase/support price, battery replacement cost or routine-use continuation. No acceptance tolerance is provided for the room-coverage percentages. Useful data acquisition is demonstrated; a priced, fully accepted inspection-and-action chain is not.

#### Episode QOF-D02: Sparky at Wateringen, 2024

The strongest operator artifact is [Thijs Pruijssers’ TenneT presentation](https://www.nen.nl/media/wysiwyg/Presentatie_TenneT_Digitale_transformatie_onder_hoogspanning.pdf), hosted publicly by NEN and dated 18 September 2024 on its title slide. Slide 11 describes a nine-month January–September 2024 pilot at a 380/150 kV substation. That is a stated project window, not nine months of measured robot exposure. Slide 13 visibly depicts an ANYbotics-branded quadruped with visual, thermal and ultrasonic sensors, lidar, a spotlight and optional measurement payloads; it does not pin the hardware/software revision. A [same-day operator post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/thijspruijssers_innovation-digitalization-ai-activity-7242222109484023808-_RHU) confirms a visit discussing Sparky and inspection learning with other European transmission operators. Neither source establishes a retained production fleet. [QOF-E03, QOF-E04]

A provenance detail matters: slide 10 reproduces a planning card dated **24 October 2022** inside the 2024 presentation. It calls for selecting 36 points that are frequently inspected and can be inspected well by a robot, securing communications, ensuring physical access, assigning robot maintenance, testing output quality and cost savings, and reviewing whether to scale. Its cost, benefit and result fields are blank. Thus 36 is a planning selection, not a completed measurement count or proof that the 2024 pilot covered all required assets. Visual review and PDF text extraction confirm the older footer and empty financial fields. [QOF-E03 slide 10]

This selection rule could yield a high pass rate within the chosen route while leaving expensive residual human visits untouched. A matched service evaluation needs the original scheduled inspection inventory, excluded points and the retained human route. A robot that eliminates an entire mandatory visit has a different economic proposition from one that collects more frequent readings while people still visit for the remaining checks. Both can be valuable, but their labor accounting differs.

##### What the public economic claims actually establish

In a [June 2024 interview](https://solarmagazine.nl/nieuws-zonne-energie/i37700/robothonden-en-drones-efficienter-werken-aan-betrouwbaar-hoogspanningsnet-en-meer-capaciteit), Pruijssers describes 37 inspection missions at Wateringen and says deployment across 350 locations could save 55,000 person-hours per year. The statement is conditional on broad deployment being considered; it is not an observed annual saving, a staffing reduction or a utilization study. It does not net out commissioning, human review, exceptions or robot servicing. [QOF-E05]

An [August 2024 trade-press visit](https://energeia.nl/slimme-technologie-bespaart-op-schaarse-menskracht/) describes 31 programmed missions and reports €90,000 **excluding sensors**. Unlike August’s explicitly programmed repertoire, June’s 37-mission figure does not specify whether it describes a repertoire or executions, or over what counting interval. The figures therefore cannot establish a subtraction, trend, completion rate or accepted-inspection denominator. The price is a reported figure without a procurement contract, tax basis, service term or full configuration; it cannot be used as deployed lifecycle cost. The article describes a two-person human access rule and potential release of technician time. This motivates measuring a route’s staffing boundary, but does not verify net saved labor. [QOF-E06]

A [June NRC site report](https://www.nrc.nl/nieuws/2024/06/13/de-onderhoudsinspecteur-bij-de-hoogspanningscentrale-van-tennet-iseen-robothond-a4856397) observes inspectors reviewing a robot photograph on a tablet and interpreting the absence of a leak. This is concrete evidence of human interpretation within the inspection workflow, albeit one reported scene rather than a time study. It describes the test lasting to the end of 2024, differing from the September presentation’s January–September window. That discrepancy is retained. The reports do not justify a claim that the robot independently diagnosed, authorized and executed corrective maintenance. [QOF-E07]

The interviews also discuss fixed sensors, drones, data-driven maintenance and network capacity. Their combined benefits cannot all be assigned to Sparky. In particular, weather-based dynamic line rating is not a measured quadruped effect. No avoided-outage or network-capacity coefficient is taken from these accounts.

### Physical Intelligence: buying model infrastructure while retaining the deployment problem

[Pi's October 13 beta announcement](https://www.pi.website/blog/api-beta) describes uploaded data, managed fine-tuning and hosted inference. Selected partners use the service to iterate from demonstrations and failures. The post also reports connectivity difficulties in some applications. It does not name an immutable latest hosted checkpoint or supply a complete price, hardware, service-level and data-rights contract. Public pi0/pi0.5 research lineage must therefore not be substituted for the unspecified beta model. [K-E001]

This structure could reduce the ML infrastructure a robot builder needs to own. The customer still has to specify useful work, collect appropriate examples, integrate its control stack, validate behavior and handle exceptions. The relevant economic comparison is the total cost and time to a repeatable service outcome. An elapsed iteration anecdote cannot establish that cost without failed attempts, demonstration labor, engineering work, inference, monitoring and recovery. A report of no person inside a machine's cab does not establish absence of remote supervision.

The public [openpi repository](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/tree/215abfb217dbac7d5f1273282331b9b1866c0479) is a separate route. Its pinned README offers checkpoint locators and robot examples, with GPU-memory guidance that differs across inference, LoRA and full fine-tuning. Its remote-serving examples move policy compute off the robot; they do not remove network latency, controller interfaces or robot-side qualification. The repository LICENSE is Apache-2.0, while effective rights for every external checkpoint, component dataset and hosted beta agreement were not independently resolved here. No model was downloaded or run. [K-E002]

An independent supplier's advantage depends on reusable adaptation across customers. If each new site still needs extensive custom engineering and support, the supplier may be selling a labor-intensive integration service despite a common model. If model improvements transfer reliably and contractual reuse is permitted, shared research and serving infrastructure could lower the next deployment's cost. Those are alternative hypotheses, not an automatic data-network premium. For a customer-side operating newco, access to such a service may reduce one startup burden while leaving acquisition, site operations, capital and accepted-output risk intact.

The next discriminating evidence is a versioned, repeated service comparison with the same hardware and workload: adaptation hours and failures, all-attempt inference cost, latency tails and fallback, supervision, update/rollback obligations and data/fine-tune rights. A beta access form is neither that evidence nor authorization to submit an application.

### Skild: model development with deployment and acquired distribution

Skild connects general model development to field deployment and integration. #### 1. Adaptation speed and qualification are separate costs

Generalist's August 19 GEN-1.5 account reports **59% average success** across ten short manipulation tasks with a demonstration in context, and **83%** under a separate regime using ten gradient steps on five minutes of task data. It labels the accompanying ±10 and ±9 figures standard deviations. The inspected prose does not establish the exact trial denominators or the statistical population behind those deviations, so this edition does not convert them to confidence intervals. Physical prompts normally include sensorimotor trajectories, not just ordinary video; human-hands imitation is a narrower demonstrated case. [Generalist GEN-1.5](https://generalistai.com/blog/gen-1.5) (K-E031).

The economic hypothesis is specific: reusable demonstrations and a stronger pretrained model could reduce task-specific collection and updating. The claim does not remove demonstration selection, workflow definition, safety/quality qualification, integration, monitoring or recovery. Few gradient steps say little about total dollars without model size, batch size, hardware, pretraining allocation and the cost of reaching a customer acceptance standard. The 30-second context and 100 Hz action trajectories are not a measured full sensor-to-action deadline or proof of embedded hardware feasibility.

GEN-1 is a different system and evaluation regime. Generalist describes post-training, experience-based improvement and inference harnessing; its selected mastery demonstrations are not the same experiment as GEN-1.5's low-data prompting. The GEN-1 speed comparison restricts the box-folding clock to the folding portion, omitting the surrounding workflow. Its limitations explicitly acknowledge attempted tasks that do not reach the desired performance. Comparing a selected mastery result with the later adaptation average would confuse changes in task, training and measurement with a model trend. [GEN-1 system and limits](https://generalistai.com/blog/gen-1) (K-E032).

The public [homepage](https://generalistai.com/) and [GEN-1.5 waitlist](https://generalistai.com/waitlist) establish an access invitation, not a signed license, priced API, released checkpoint or qualified productive fleet. No form was submitted. Private arrangements may exist; this inspection does not establish their terms. A customer-side operating newco must therefore test access, rights and support instead of assuming the same technology is immediately procurable by every incumbent and entrant (K-E033).

#### 2. What Dyna's scaling evidence measures

Dyna-2's study varies nested human-data budgets of 1,000, 10,000, 100,000 and 1,000,000 hours, keeping source proportions fixed. A separate 100-hour human validation set and ten late checkpoints support its offline comparisons. Held-out robot prediction uses 39 internal/external tasks. The hand-pose extraction and filtering pipeline creates pseudo-action labels; human video is not a costless, already aligned robotics dataset. The study leaves model-size and compute scaling for future work. Its empirical result is evidence about this data axis and recipe, not a universal return to training expenditure. [Dyna-2, section 3](https://www.dyna.co/dyna-2) (K-E034).

The physical evaluation then **post-trains on robot data**. Four pretraining budgets use the same fourteen downstream task datasets, each with at most ten hours of demonstrations. Thirteen tasks have ten trials per budget and the language task twelve. The average reported score rises **20 → 28 → 45 → 53% of task-specific ceilings**. Some tasks score completed actions; others count objects handled. The aggregate is therefore not a 53% probability of finishing a customer job. The highest budget is reported best on nine of fourteen tasks, which also limits a claim of uniform per-task improvement. “Blind” scoring means evaluators uninvolved in model development in this account, not an independent replication (K-E035).

The report distinguishes its scaling-study variants from production-recipe models. A separate held-out-site comparison gives 46% and 87% under customer production criteria after task post-training, but does not expose the site/episode denominator, weighting or raw logs. This cannot become fleet uptime or a cross-industry success rate. Another ablation reports 35%, 67% and 96% for instruction following while awarding half credit to a correct primitive that fails to finish. That last number measures a graded score, not 96% completed tasks (K-E036/037).

These distinctions matter for H's LLM analogy. Promptability and scaling can help explain a mechanism, but physical error cost, elapsed service time, hardware wear and authority to act intervene before economic output. The data-scale experiment supports a reason to investigate human-to-robot transfer; it does not establish a fleet-level learning coefficient, a compute-optimal training plan or the marginal value of another customer's data.

#### 3. A service threshold, with financial terms still missing

Dyna's restaurant account states that a Din Tai Fung robot must deliver **1,500 table-ready napkins in eighteen hours**. It reports 95 folds per hour, 93% acceptable quality and approximately 1,590 acceptable napkins per window. Multiplying the reported rate, quality and duration gives **1,590.3**, consistent with whole-unit rounding. The excess is **90.3 napkins, or 6.02% of the requirement**. These are source-arithmetic results, not an independently observed station audit. [Dyna deployment account](https://www.dyna.co/research/scaling-customer-deployments) (K-E039).

The published earlier rates are approximate; this edition does not replace the source's “about 480” figure with a new measured value. The earlier [Dyna-1 account](https://www.dyna.co/research/dyna-1) also separates a reported 99.4% task-success result in a 24-hour run from 75% meeting its strict fold-quality threshold. Completion, acceptable presentation and reliable site service have different denominators. Multiplying all the headlines together would risk applying overlapping filters (K-E038).

At the later reported rate and window, the arithmetic quality threshold is about **87.72%**. Dyna describes recording robot state, controls, video and application events; storing episodes locally before asynchronous verified upload; labeling work by customer SOP; and combining automated coverage with targeted human review. A training improvement and a maintenance fix are separate mechanisms, even when the same recordings help both.

Customer authority also matters. The operator defines acceptable folds and stack placement; staff still move finished work into the dining workflow. Access to recordings does not establish permission for cross-customer training, model redistribution or retention after contract termination. Model supplier, integrator, asset owner and operator can coexist within one relationship; their internal charges cannot all be added as net economic value.

Partial progress can still be useful engineering evidence, but stages are not equal units of human time, cost or accepted customer service. RynnBrain's evaluation is more explicit about two distinct measures: complete-task success and subtask progress. #### Implications and remaining evidence

The evidence changes three decision checks. First, a proposed commercial architecture needs artifact-specific rights and an actual service agreement; public availability alone does not settle access, price or reuse. Second, model comparisons need common accepted-work criteria and a qualified control/serving configuration before resource or cash comparisons become meaningful. Third, operator evidence should distinguish customer authority, data capture, model cooperation and productive use, even when they appear in the same announcement.

The shared monthly model can expose the cost of an unfunded installation, support period or customer obligation. It cannot supply the missing license, intervention rate, deployment identity, retention term or supplier cost. The conditional architecture model likewise remains a mechanism comparison with unobserved target costs and compatibility. The new source records constrain what would need to be measured or contracted; they do not turn either model into an observed forecast.

Further work should test consequential counterparties and configurations, including additional regional developers and the still-unreviewed Generalist/Dyna mechanisms, while retaining the broader end-market, digital-AI, supply and quadruped/drone inquiries. Exact deployed-policy identity, commercial rights, complete support exposure and accepted-output economics remain high-value gaps. No outreach, model execution or GPU/hardware run is implied by this source audit.

### Focused follow-up: Conditional architecture resources and value allocation

Authoritative analysis: [research/intelligence_architecture_scenarios.md](research/intelligence_architecture_scenarios.md).

Edition 0.7 implements the conditional four-architecture comparison proposed in [the K evidence chapter](research/frontier_models.md). The [common-engine method](model/ARCHITECTURES.md) holds a service boundary and configured-system assumptions while making adaptation, validation, serving, support and commercial transfers explicit. Every numerical profile is synthetic. Actual model prices, effective rights and qualified target service remain unknown in the evidence packet.

The first decision is whether a policy/integration arrangement creates a resource advantage over the available process. The second is whether the contract distributes that advantage so the participants can sustain it. The model now separates those questions. A license payment is an expense to its payer and a receipt to its supplier. Changing that payment alone leaves consolidated resources and output unchanged. Combining the model supplier with an OEM or operator eliminates an internal transfer; it does not remove the associated engineering, compute or support work.

#### A positive combined result can coexist with an undercompensated supplier

The reference manufacturing example at the selected 2030 conditions has a positive allocated cash benefit for the independent-model architecture. At the invented $4,000 annual model payment, however, its model supplier has a negative $200 annual-equivalent amount after the declared costs and upfront payment. The computed model-fee interval begins at $4,200 and ends at $11,554.90 per year under the zero-surplus-hurdle assumptions. These are conditional accounting results, not market prices or forecasts of a lab's margins.

Raising only the fee to $7,000 moves $3,000 a year from customer/operator to model supplier. It changes neither the architecture's $35,415.65 annual-equivalent resource cost nor its $11,629.90 combined allocated cash benefit. The source of the benefit must be an avoided resource or changed service outcome; a pricing change cannot create it. Hardware price and service payment obey the same rule within the specified actor boundary.

For an integrated OEM, hardware and integration contributions can share an entity with the model activity. The feasible bundled payment can therefore differ from an independently licensed model's fee, even with some common costs. This does not prove a superior model or reveal an internal business-unit margin. The separate legal-entity and logical-role traces show exactly what has been combined.

#### Adaptation, operating support and outside options affect different mechanisms

Adding 100 licensed-policy adaptation-engineering hours at the assumed $100/hour increases the annual-equivalent cost by $2,000 over the five-year life. It leaves the assumed physical output unchanged and raises the supplier's fee floor by the same annual amount. This makes the adaptation question concrete: reduced fine-tuning effort matters through all the labor, compute and validation it actually avoids, not a generic claim that a model is easier to use.

Increasing intervention demand has a broader effect. It both adds support expenditure and reduces accepted robot capacity under the existing operating equation. Human fallback maintains the common service target. In the exported support-stress witness the independent architecture's combined cash benefit becomes negative; reallocating a fee cannot repair a negative total under the fixed other assumptions. This connects model performance to the full operating process rather than merely pricing a successful inference.

The default open-adaptation profile has more engineering and maintenance work. A separate witness lowers those explicit inputs and reverses its resource-cost ranking against the licensed alternative. The result is conditional on the inputs; an open/proprietary label has no automatic cost or productivity advantage. Rights and target qualification remain separate assumed premises. A false premise returns an unadmitted case, not a zero-cost or equivalent-performance substitute.

The moving human alternative is equally consequential. With stronger human digital efficiency, the combined robot process can become cheaper while its incremental cash benefit shrinks. The exported improved-human witness reproduces that mechanism in the architecture comparison. The model does not import an LLM productivity estimate or a robotics benchmark score to choose the change.

#### Customer formation and provider organization remain distinct

The same technology and costs produce the same service comparison for incumbent and newco labels. An explicit $20,000 additional newco setup charge adds $4,000 a year over the five-year reference life; it does not change robot capability or useful output. An incumbent retooling charge can be specified separately. These are cost differences to investigate, not inherent advantages assigned to an ownership label.

In the model-led operating-provider structure, the provider owns the purchased robot and bears the remaining service work. Customer payment and provider costs are separate, and the customer's unavoidable released-payroll proxy remains visible. Internal policy payments cancel within the combined provider. The computed service-price interval is conditional on this responsibility boundary; it is not a real RaaS quote.

A positive lifetime annual equivalent is still insufficient for deployment. Upfront resource charges and purchases need funding before later benefits arrive. Contract duration, cancellation, credit, working capital, shared factory/service capacity and actual staffing schedules are not supplied by this static reference. The earlier treasury and monthly paid-physical fixtures show why timing matters, but they are not yet integrated with these architecture or entrant comparisons.

#### What this closes and what remains next

The first-edition K architecture target now has an executable matched-service mechanism with human/digital and specialized alternatives, explicit resource-versus-transfer accounting, qualification assumptions, adverse sensitivities, equal-technology customer paths and reproducible fee bounds. It supplies no calibrated vendor ranking, commercial-rights clearance or jointly feasible company plan.

The next priority is the missing monthly commercial extension: sale, independent lease and provider service over the same paid physical plan, with separate title, cash, customer/operator obligations and support consequences. That work should use the existing integer-cent treasury and shared physical ledger. It must preserve the distinction between an attractive allocated lifetime result and an operation that can actually fund its next obligation.

## 11. What remains unresolved

The sources establish bounded workflows, relationships and constraints. Public disclosures often do not establish accepted-output exposure, all human support, retained deployment, negotiated prices, avoidable staffing, qualified supply or complete costs. These gaps limit market ranking and investment conclusions. A functioning mechanism model does not resolve them by assigning synthetic inputs.

Workstream K now contributes nine actor-role records, four contrasting dossiers, six model/release cards and eight artifact-rights cards. Their source and cost boundaries remain explicit. The four-architecture service comparison now has a separate synthetic calculation and sensitivities. Actual target costs, effective rights and qualified configurations remain unknown; the calculation does not fill those source gaps. The next research priorities are: reconcile field service/intervention denominators; challenge shared-resource and financial accounting; validate digital coordination against a strong simpler baseline; test customer entry against a prompt incumbent with equal technology access; qualify specific supply/workload dependencies; deepen labor crosswalks without invented physical shares; and expand non-Spot operator and nonadoption evidence. Each has a bounded action, evidence need and stopping condition in the continuing roadmap.

The acceptance register records individual requirements and limitations. Source counts and view counts do not themselves establish substantive completion. The original campaign window has ended with incomplete requirements. This separately dated continuation preserves the earlier edition and its gaps; the handoff records the interruption and next actions without backdating work or resetting the week.
