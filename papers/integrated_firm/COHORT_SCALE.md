## Economic premise and exact horizon projection

An asset may alter the feasible research program without altering the feasible final service program. This distinction is particularly important for a procedure whose output is another qualified procedure, rather than an accepted job. In the present finite public-state economy, installed robots have terminal salvage, while unused kits, qualified models and research procedures have no separate liquidation value. The final date permits service with inherited installed equipment and qualification bits; no construction or training is lawful. The economically relevant terminal state before dispatch is therefore the installed arm/mobile stock, joint qualification masks and clipped cash. Neither unused kit holdings nor procedure generation changes terminal menus, price or salvage.

**Proposition.** Under the stated payoff-based terminal pure-equilibrium selections, projecting out kits and procedure generation at the final date preserves selected terminal incremental payoffs and the lifted dispatch policy (up to payoff-tied actions). Proof: the original and projected terminal games have identical lawful service menus and payoffs at each profile, including all unilateral deviations. The salvage depends only on installed capital. Initial cash is additive; clipping is lawful by the earlier funding quotient. The equilibrium selection applies the same payoff comparisons to identical games. Backward induction gives the same selected continuation payoff wherever this projected terminal game is called. This is not a claim that every conceivable public-history-dependent selection ignores economically irrelevant public signals.

**Corollary (funded penultimate successor dominance).** With two dates left and own cash at least the full two-date cash bound, remove a successor-development, outside-development or guaranteed-license action while holding its simultaneous production and manufacturing actions fixed. The reduced action is feasible, because it uses weakly fewer scarce resources, and strictly cheaper by the declared technology. It leaves own accepted service and current receipts, rival cash, installed capital and final qualification masks unchanged. Its only possible effects on the final state are the procedure generation and a higher own cash balance; the generation is projected out and both cash outcomes clip to the same terminal bound (nonnegative receipts and the uniform outlay bound guarantee this). Every rival action therefore yields exactly the same terminal incremental payoff for both firms while the deviator saves current cost. The successor action is strictly dominated in the selected continuation game. The claim is not valid merely because a successor has no immediate productive effect: if actual cash binds, carries credit information, or selection conditions on the discarded procedure, the continuation may change. No such pruning is applied to binding penultimate states.

The projection is of particular economic interest because it specifies the minimum time needed for an otherwise usable research capacity to affect production. Additional generations do not generate direct accepted output without the intervening qualification and service windows. Strategic signaling and changed credit institutions are separate mechanisms, not direct productivity.
