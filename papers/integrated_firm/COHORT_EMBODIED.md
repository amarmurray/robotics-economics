## Premise and exact state reduction

A firm's qualification stock is not homogeneous: qualification for one customer order is not necessarily qualification for another. The economic value of an archive, model or research procedure depends on remaining unqualified and monetizable targets, rivals' access to those targets, and current scarce complementary inputs. Separate financing effects from strategic use of cash as a public signal.

Consider a finite public-state game with additive terminal cash, no borrowing, no dividends, nonnegative receipts at every feasible profile, and noncash transitions that do not depend on actual cash once actions are fixed. With h dates left, let cbar_h bound current outlay uniformly, including all feasible unilateral deviations. Define L_0=0, L_h=L_(h-1)+cbar_h, and the clipped cash state b*=min(b,L_h). No other demand, pricing, rights or technology depends on cash. The current payment constraint is cost <= b; all costs <= cbar_h. If b<L_h, it is retained exactly. Otherwise every current legal noncash action is affordable and b+R-c >= L_(h-1). Hence the next clipped state depends only on (b*,a,R), and can be computed by clipping b*+R-c at L_(h-1). This applies separately to each player and to every unilateral deviation. At the terminal date, remove already-held cash from the value and retain salvage.

**Proposition (partial funding quotient).** Under these assumptions, solve the finite public-state game with clipped cash and incremental value, using payoff-based pure equilibrium selection where available. Lift its selected policy to the full game by clipping actual cash before every decision. By induction, the full continuation payoff is actual cash plus the quotient value; each one-stage payoff in the quotient is current net receipts plus expected next incremental value. Every unilateral comparison is preserved. The lifted policy is subgame perfect on the solved domain. The quotient does not require all initial states to be funded. It does not represent all possible cash/history-dependent equilibrium selections, nor extend to a game where actual cash directly determines outside credit terms or signals private type. The theorem is a conditional state reduction, not a claim that finance does not matter.

The earlier funded action quotient must **not** be applied mechanically in the binding region: two actions with the same output and noncash transition but unequal current costs can imply different future strategic cash states. A cheaper action need not dominate when the selected continuation payoff changes adversely with that state. Retain distinct cost levels unless the clipped continuation states agree for all rival actions and outcomes (or a stronger value theorem is available). In particular, cost pruning is valid in the fully funded region and at the last date. Identical cost, output vector and transition actions remain economically equivalent in the present restricted model, which has no carryover of unused slots. This corrects a tempting inference from the funded case; no general strategic cash-burning claim is established.
