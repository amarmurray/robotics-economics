## 1. Premise: evidence access and research capacity are distinct complements

A successful warehouse operation may produce a record of a useful physical interaction. Without a lawful reuse right or a retained record it need not expand the operator's future training menu. Even with a retained record, the operator may lack the lab or time to qualify a better controller. Conversely a paid outside experiment can supply the qualified release without the operator owning either the data-generating activity or a lab. Scope is a dated production decision, not a permanent firm label. A customer-facing deployment can earn service revenue and generate evidence at the same date, but reserving and validating the record uses scarce inputs; the record is not a current execution improvement.

Take the exact parent three-recipient technology: one physical site, two separate exchangeable digital recipients, intercepts (18,14,14), price A_j−2Q_j, 3 human and 5 compute slots, wages 2, compute price 1, qualified and old recipes, per-copy inference and quadratic coordination, paid kit order and subsequent assembly, independent quarter-grid qualification draws, installed salvage (1,2). No added capital bonus or learning premium. Each firm has state (K_arm,K_mobile,I_arm,I_mobile,M,p,B,D,g). M is the local qualification bitmask, D a separate usable archive mask, g∈{0,1} an inherited maintained lab. R is the publicly observed contractual retention mask; identical across firms within each intervention. All receipts follow outlay; no borrowing or terminal archive/model/procedure resale.

On a date with at least three periods remaining, a firm can retain one previously unarchived and unqualified local workflow's case from an accepted job, using one compute slot. The case is delivered next date with certainty in this restriction; it may be sourced from human or robotic service. A paid archive purchase uses one human slot and fee f_D, delivers the same archive next date, and is available without retention rights. Internal qualification uses the inherited local archive, active lab and (1 human,2 compute); a paid outside qualification supplies the data and release attempt for (1 human,0 compute) and fee f_X, independent of g,D. Retention/purchase are not offered on the last two dates; new research and assembly are not offered on the last date. These are explicit finite menu restrictions, not a claim that useless public spending is generically dominated. In the three-date positive cases hardware is inherited and fixed (mobile); two-date cases allow paid-kit assembly.

## 2. Terminal scope is different from a research-only stock

**Proposition (conditional scope projection and closure).** At the final date, D, unused kits and p can be removed from the state without changing any unilateral payoff or feasible service choice, because no action uses them and their terminal salvage is zero. In contrast g cannot generally be projected out: it charges an inherited maintenance tick and can make current service unaffordable. If own cash is at least L_2 on the penultimate date, choosing next scope closed instead of open while holding all current research, retention, assembly and production fixed saves weakly current entry costs and exactly one final-date maintenance tick. Both own terminal menus are fully funded and have the same lawful service quantities and operating costs apart from the additive maintenance tick. Rival terminal menu and payoff functions are unchanged. Under either stated payoff-based selection (highest/lowest total, then highest firm-0 payoff), an additive constant to own payoff changes neither best-response sets nor payoff-ranking selection, so the reduced action strictly increases own continuation wealth against every rival action. It is therefore strictly dominant relative to its otherwise identical open alternative. The penultimate successor pruning follows the same projection argument with g held fixed; it has no final execution use. The proof does not cover a binding-cash terminal subgame, history-conditioned strategic signaling, scope-dependent customer prices, maintenance-linked model usability, resale or current scope changes. In binding penultimate states both scope choices remain in the menu.

**Implication for valuation.** Let ΔV(D,g) be a private continuation-value increment from a lawful retained case under a specified rival continuation. The net value of generating that case in an admitted job is its expected marginal continuation value less the current incremental cost and the changed current service contribution from allocating scarce retention resources. If g=0, the same case has no internal qualification use before entry completes. A positive value requires an available training/qualification window and a profitable subsequent service margin. For a fixed rival policy, optional rights and feasible external substitutes imply weak own menu expansion; no unconditional equilibrium private-value monotonicity follows when both firms reoptimize. An external experiment delivering the same lawful qualified release earlier and at lower opportunity cost can eliminate the incremental value of local retention. The causal chain is D→M→recipes→quantity/cost→price/collections; it is not an extra term in physical MPK or a second reward attached to output. The production branch's graph/reference-identification result needs a richer transition law and the recursive branch's capable ordinary contingent policy remains a feasible comparator.

These are synthetic matched interventions, not estimates or a search for a universal positive sign. The lower-total selection gives identical values in both funded three-date comparisons and cheap outside comparison. Binding retention comparison gives (62,60) under either rights regime, with 12 accepted jobs and surplus 20. This does not establish that the same retention program is chosen in all possible equilibria.

The high-fee rights treatment changes F0's dated net receipts relative to no-right equilibrium from (17,18,18) to (16,11,27): −1,−7,+9 = +1. F1 changes (16,17,17) to (16,18,16), net zero. The new case creates one qualified physical controller and frees scarce labor/compute for digital service; it does not imply an increase in the underlying engineering efficiency of the unqualified machine. Customer surplus rises by three ticks, which is distinct from the one private tick. The experiment assigns retention rights to both firms but only one finds it privately optimal to exercise them. The archived/active case compares *inherited states* and excludes the unknown historical cost of acquiring those states; the observed 13-tick wealth difference is not a return on buying a full-stack company. In the two-date cases an inherited lab reduces combined private wealth by two ticks in both funding regimes and both selections, because no research is selected and active labs pay the current overhead before exit. Assembly is still available; it is selected in the funded lower-total case. The optimal organizational boundary therefore depends on qualification timing, archive availability and the next best external route, not just the number of owned layers.
