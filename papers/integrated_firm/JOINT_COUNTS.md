## 1. Economic reason for joint configuration counts

A retained procedure is reusable across distinct tasks, but a qualified execution model earns its return where it can lawfully serve. The spatial/task pairing of qualified models across firms determines the rival supply faced by each model. Meanwhile human reviewers, inference, pre-receipt cash and coordination are global within each firm. Thus neither the number of qualified targets nor the independent-cohort operating gain G alone determines the continuation.

Let J be a finite collection of target markets, and x_j the **joint** local state (both firms' installed/qualified embodiments, models, evidence and access rights; local demand and vintage; any local latent or pending state necessary for a Markov kernel). Global state g holds separate cash accounts, shared capacities, procedures, internal scopes, common public posterior b, and any global demand/regime. Let n_x count tasks of local type x. A global capacity or cost can depend on sums and symmetric functions of local assignments (for example, copy coordination cost depends on the total number of copies), not their arbitrary names.

Assume: (i) simultaneous permutation of task labels for both firms preserves primitives, feasible joint actions and all stage payoffs; (ii) the full law of next public state and legally observed reports is equivariant under that permutation, including common shocks; (iii) terminal payoffs are invariant. These are stronger than equal marginal task success rates. A persistent reference identity, particular supplier contract, relation graph, or cross-task interference can violate them unless incorporated as extra state or a smaller symmetry group. Arbitrary graphs do not reduce to vertex counts.

## 2. Exact state reduction and its strategic boundary

**Proposition (joint-count state quotient).** Under (i)–(iii), two states with the same global coordinates and joint counts have isomorphic finite continuation games, including every pure or mixed subgame-perfect equilibrium payoff correspondence under relabeling. For a single decision maker the Bellman optimal value is exactly a function of these counts. In the game an invariant selection of continuation payoffs can use that state quotient; arbitrary label-dependent tie-breaking need not do so.

Proof: equal counts imply a bijection between labels within each local type. Apply it simultaneously to each player's local action vector, leaving global actions unchanged. The map is a bijection on feasible sets by (i). Current payoffs agree. Push the full next-state kernel forward through the bijection; (ii) implies equality of the distribution of next joint counts and global state, with an isomorphism of legal observations. At the terminal date (iii) closes the induction. Pull back any continuation strategies through the same mapping at every history. The deviation sets correspond bijectively, so all best-response inequalities are preserved. For a planner maximize over this bijection; for a game transport each player's equilibrium, not a joint optimum. This proves equality of correspondences, not uniqueness. For a Markov selection, choose an equilibrium of the canonical representative and transport it; if a deterministic choice breaks within-type symmetry, retain a public label ordering or use a publicly announced uniform permutation before actions and play the chosen equilibrium conditional on it. The latter is a public randomization device, not private coordination.

**Important distinction:** a count of each firm's actions separately is not a valid simultaneous action quotient. Two firms each choose one out of two identical tasks. Each has the same action counts whether they choose the same target or distinct targets, but prices and next joint types differ. A planner can choose joint counts n_(x,a1,a2) satisfying the type totals; the firms in a noncooperative game cannot jointly choose those counts. The safe computational implementation uses a canonical labeled representative per state orbit, enumerates unilateral action vectors and their joint profiles (or orbit profiles with correctly reconstructed deviations). A mixed symmetric policy requires specifying its distribution over subsets and calculating overlap, not substituting expected quantities into nonlinear payoffs. This avoids a spurious equilibrium based on an implicitly coordinating allocator.

Conditional independent local transition draws with common regime H admit a multinomial representation: for each joint action type (x,a1,a2) and conditional on H and other common shocks, multiply the appropriate multinomial probabilities of next local types and report categories; sum over compatible allocations to next counts. Then mix over H using the *single* inherited posterior. Update that posterior with the joint likelihood of the actual observable report categories, not the unobserved latent state. If a report is associated with a particular action or source, preserve that association in its category. If conditional dependence remains, use the full joint conditional kernel instead. With prior 3/5, and two reports each succeeding with probabilities 3/4 and 1/4 under the two regimes, both successes have probability 29/80, not (11/20)^2=121/400; the posterior is 27/29. Averages of separate firm posteriors are not the common posterior.

The parent game's unit-discount payoff uses inherited cash only once: each terminal action adds external revenue less total outlay, and the earlier stage evaluates the next value including the updated cash. No independent research reward or continuation stake is added.

## 3. Solve a shared-resource terminal restriction from the parent recipes

Fix hardware and scope at the terminal date; disable physical service for this restriction, one digital service maximum per firm per local market. Demand price in each local market is 8-2Q, where Q is both firms' accepted services. The alternative demand intercept 12 is the other parent demand state. Let each target's digital qualification be m=0 or 1. The core's one-service recipes are: idle (0,0), human (2 human, 0 compute), agent (1-m human, 3-m compute), with one copy per automated job. A firm using c copies adds c(c-1)/2 compute units for cross-task coordination, the parent coordination rule. Wages 2, compute price 1. For each firm, sum all local labor/compute, charge coordination once, enforce its shared H,C and pre-receipt B, and deduct the resulting invoice once. No research, construction or scope change can reach service within this terminal restriction. Unspent cash is recovered principal. This is the digital-only, one-job subtechnology of core.py, not a new payoff coefficient.

For binary local quantities q_i, profit is sum_j q_ij(8-2(q_ij+q_-ij))-cost_i. The exact potential is sum_j [6(q_1j+q_2j)-2q_1j q_2j]-cost_1-cost_2. Every unilateral change, including simultaneous reassignment of labor or copies across sites, changes potential by exactly the firm's profit change. Each firm's feasible set is independent of the rival's current choice (separate prefunding); the finite game therefore has a pure Nash equilibrium. This does not imply the same for earlier research stages, whose continuation values need not have a potential. An external shared input supplier or credit market can also invalidate the independent-feasible-set premise.

Compare three identical local markets with one qualified digital model per firm. The aligned configuration is [(1,1),(0,0),(0,0)]; split is [(1,0),(0,1),(0,0)]. With H=2, C=2 and inherited B=3 each, the only positive-service action is a qualified agent job, costing two compute ticks. Human service is technologically possible but cannot be prefunded (cost four); an unqualified agent also exceeds compute and cash. Thus both firms strictly prefer their single qualified target to idle even in direct competition.

Both produce two accepted jobs. Aligned supply depresses price to 4 in the contested market; split supply earns 6 in each served market. Private profit rises by 4 while customer surplus falls by 2. These equal marginal qualification counts have different unique continuation payoffs, even without any equilibrium-selection ambiguity. With fixed total output, this is a distributional/product-market effect, not a physical MPK increase. Changing qualification position can be worth more than changing its aggregate count. The model does not assert collusive market division is permissible or optimal in the dynamic game.

The witness has a binding prepayment restriction; relaxing cash restores human alternatives and may change the sign, output and equilibrium multiplicity. The computed report enumerates those regimes too. In an isolated firm with the same scarce pool, qualifying another task need not raise total accepted service: if C=2 and B=3, the first qualified job already uses all available compute. Its extra value can instead be an option to serve a different demand or rival configuration. The additive independent-cohort G is recovered only after restoring the independent local resource pools, excluding rival supply and restoring the full physical/digital recipes and operating horizon. In particular it cannot be multiplied by qualified counts in this coupled market.
