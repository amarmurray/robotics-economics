## Premises and terminal-block lemma

An improvement has value over the accepted services for which it can actually be reused before obsolescence. Increase economic reuse opportunities by adding N independent terminal service shifts, rather than attaching a value coefficient to a model. Each shift resets the original human and compute resource endowments and buyer demand; there is no training, retention, capital installation, lab change, financing friction, cross-shift state change or discounting inside this final operating block. Existing controllers and hardware are held fixed in the block. Per-shift revenues are actual linear-demand customer receipts. Repeated operation consumes N times the human and compute inputs and pays N times the current operating costs, including any inherited lab bill. Installed salvage occurs once. The initial cash exceeds the maximum cost of all investment dates plus all terminal shifts.

For a fixed inherited public state x and specified pure Nash selection of the original terminal one-shift game, write its net-receipts vector u(x). The policy playing that equilibrium every shift is a Nash continuation of the repeated terminal game, since payoffs are additive, resources reset and there is no productive state transition. A Markov strategy conditioned on the fixed public noncash state and remaining shifts can implement it. Its payoff is N u(x)+salvage(x); no claim about all history-dependent repeated-game equilibria is made. Consequently backward induction of the first three dated investment decisions with that exact continuation is valid. The original funded cash clipping and penultimate successor/scope dominance apply with L(h)=12N+620(h-1) for h>=1. The maximum terminal spend per shift is 12, early spend bounded by 620; cash 10000 covers both N=40 and N=60. Cash is not a strategic signal under the specified quotient selection.

For two fixed full contingent strategy profiles a and b, the difference in combined value has the form D(N)=d_early+N d_operating. The intercept includes all earlier net cash and one terminal installed salvage; the slope is the difference in expected per-shift receipts net of resources and rivalry. It can be negative or zero. The identity is not a global equilibrium comparative-static theorem: the profiles, qualification distribution and selection can change with N.
