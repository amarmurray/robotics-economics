## 1. Economic premise and clock

The previous release test could save operating inputs but could not turn them into additional accepted output or change market admission. A retained model is valuable only when a feasible operating recipe can use it. The marginal product of a robot under the old allocation and under a dispatch rule allowed to respond after a qualification are different interventions.

At the initial block decision, each firm selects the old legal joint action, including first-shift quantity mask q, recipe, ordinary/internal or outside research, hardware, rights/retention and next lab scope. A block has n shifts, H=3 human and C=5 compute units per shift. The first-shift constraints include the actual research and manufacturing work; later shifts do not get extra units because of earlier completion. With inherited lab bill g and per-shift first recipe human/compute use h,c, let b=2h+c+g. Gross cash n b plus incremental first-shift setup/research/retention/build costs is reserved before service. This is the same old reservation and no additional credit is granted. All customer receipts and refunds settle at the original boundary. A unit spent or reserved does not create new usable cash earlier.

At the end of shift one, a successful train or contract releases its target bit in the qualified mask. Neither merely developing a procedure nor buying an archive releases an execution recipe. If at least one such qualification occurs and n>1, a public event opens a simultaneous suffix quantity/recipe game. Both firms see the relevant qualification outcomes. If neither qualifies, the original service commitment remains fixed for all shifts. Digital demand is admitted from the original date 3. In these calendars the only multi-shift nonterminal research opportunity is date 3; no second attempt, extra archive acquisition, early hardware delivery or scope change is allowed. New suffix jobs generate receipts but no additional archive stock in this restriction. Original retention is tied to first-shift committed service. New qualification is physically usable only with inherited installed hardware; a successful target bit alone need not change the feasible output set.

On each remaining shift, each firm can use any old or newly qualified legal recipe with h<=3, c<=5 and actual variable bill at most its own b. The lab remains inherited for the block. The previously committed recipe remains feasible. Unused variable reservations are refunded at settlement. The suffix cannot use receipts to enlarge reservations; independent capacity purchases, sunk resource commitments, costly changeover, and earlier settlement are alternative institutions. There is no joint capacity coupling across firms yet.

This specifies a particular public-state extensive-form game. The success-only gate is a separate contractual choice, not a logical consequence of learning. Both-open, privately observed and irrevocably committed service are distinct comparators.

## 2. Solve the suffix, then propagate it upstream

For each quantity mask q let c_i(q;s_i,b_i) be the smallest actual operating bill of all its feasible recipes in the released state. A mask with no recipe is infeasible. The bill and reservation are distinct. Recipe minimization is exact in the suffix because no further state change, evidence, payment availability or action depends on its recipe except accepted service and realized inputs. All raw feasible recipes, not just minimizing representatives, are checked against the selected stage equilibrium.

Let q_im be a binary admission for market m and a_m in {18,14}. One-shift payoff is

$$u_i(q_i,q_j) = \sum_m (a_m-2-2q_{jm})q_{im}-c_i(q_i;s_i,b_i).$$

**Proposition (finite potential restriction).** The suffix stage has a pure equilibrium for every nonempty pair of independent menus. The function

$$P(q_0,q_1)=\sum_{i,m}(a_m-2)q_{im}-2\sum_m q_{0m}q_{1m}-\sum_i c_i(q_i;s_i,b_i)$$

has exactly the same change as the deviating firm's payoff under each unilateral quantity change. For raw recipe deviations replace c_i with the actual recipe bill and the identity still holds. A maximizer of P over the finite product menu is therefore a pure equilibrium. This is standard exact-potential logic, not a novel game-theory theorem. Enumerate all equilibrium quantity pairs after valid within-mask bill reduction; select by highest or lowest combined payoff, then firm 0's payoff and deterministic mask ordering. Multiple cost-identical raw recipes may implement the same quantity equilibrium. The reported number of equilibria is the number of such quantity pairs, not a count of all recipe aliases or mixed equilibria. With no further state change within the suffix and no borrowing, repeating the specified one-shift equilibrium for n-1 shifts is a subgame-perfect continuation (backward induction with same state and menus at each suffix date). No history-dependent or mixed suffix correspondence is exhausted.

For a given pair of old block actions, independently enumerate their inherited quarter-probability draws (only the accepted local-archive restriction, not a common-latent report model). Let I be the public qualification gate, and U_i^* the selected suffix payoff in that realized released state. Let u_i^old denote the original committed one-shift net receipts minus the service reservation. The correction to the original block value is

$$E[I(n-1)(U_i^* - u_i^{old})].$$

This is calculated before outer best-response comparisons, not added to a frozen selected root. The old stock transition at the boundary is unchanged: only the old qualification, archive, hardware and scope outcomes enter future decisions. Prefunding makes actual cash levels above the bound irrelevant to feasible future choices; gross reservation already fits inherited cash, refunds are nonnegative and actual market receipts nonnegative. Thus projected successor-state distributions still support the old cash quotient. In a binding-cash economy the actual suffix receipts and refunds must enter successor cash before clipping; the funded reduction cannot simply be reused there.

The backward code uses integer numerator over 16 for independent qualification corrections. The forward ledger adds actual receipt changes and actual refunds only on the corresponding outcome branch, then checks probability mass, terminal cash/salvage and the dated net account. First-shift resource and outlay reconstruction is inherited from the old block solver; suffix resources are explicitly enumerated and checked. Disabling the gate sets the correction and new reduction signatures to zero, making the wrapper algebraically identical to the accepted locked game. Direct exact replay of the previously difficult locked S=10 exclusive case agrees on all recorded wealth, roots, dated quantities and cash, all 3,898 states, 114,578 retained deviations, and nine mixed continuation matrices/probabilities.

## 3. Reservation-aware action reduction is economically necessary

A cheapest-recipe representative for a quantity and future stock transition was valid in the old funded game. It is not generally valid now. Equal q and transition laws do not determine the new feasible suffix set because b is a commitment selected before the release. Two macro actions can be reduced by their total gross cost only when q, projected successor kernel, b, qualification gate bit and its probability agree, so that their suffix correction and feasible continuation choices agree against every rival action. In that class the lower gross cost is weakly better under the funded quotient. The implementation keeps distinct signatures in both the initial menu reduction and transition-class reduction; the outer solver checks every retained unilateral action. Terminal and funded penultimate successor/scope projections remain valid: they affect no present qualified bit before the suffix and have no extra nonterminal recipient payoff in the original timing.

**Exact reservation witness.** The initial firm has an arm, a qualified physical recipe, an eligible digital archive and an open lab. It can train digital locally using ordinary probability p=1/2 and first-shift 1H,2C, for a setup bill of 4. The rival has no service reservation and remains idle. Both candidate initial recipes deliver the same physical job and have the same stock kernel; both satisfy first-shift H/C when combined with training.

| | Cheap qualified first recipe | Costlier old first recipe |
|---|---:|---:|
| Per-shift reservation including lab | 5 | 8 |
| One-shift committed payoff | 11 | 8 |
| On qualification: selected suffix markets | physical | physical and digital |
| On qualification: one-shift net payoff | 11 | 20 |

For n shifts the expected difference, excluding common setup and future boundary continuation, is

$$V_{high}-V_{low}=-3n+12p(n-1).$$

The formula follows from the extra three-tick precommitment cost on every shift and a 12-tick incremental suffix payoff relative to the high-reservation old recipe conditional on success. For n=5 and p=1/2, current expected net (including common setup 4) is 51 versus 60: the larger reservation adds 9. Both suffix equilibria are unique quantity pairs and the projected successor stock kernel is identical. The threshold for n>1 is p>n/[4(n-1)]. This is an exact conditional action comparison, not a selected root policy or a general argument for wasteful service. A separate, independently chosen refundable reservation would allow the cheaper first recipe with the larger later option budget. Under binding cash, the liquidity cost of holding that reserve would have to be priced. The witness identifies a consequential omitted state/control object: spendable or enforceably reserved capacity available at release, not just the current efficient marginal recipe.
