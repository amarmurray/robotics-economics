## Opportunity cost of improving the improver

At a state with an unqualified target, an incumbent research procedure, two investment dates left and an available archive, compare two feasible fixed contingent programs. Let p be incumbent execution-qualification success probability, q the successor's qualification probability and b the probability of successfully developing and qualifying the successor. Conditional on independent attempts given the state, retaining the old procedure on failure, and a feasible attempt in each date, the retry program qualifies by the terminal date with probability 1-(1-p)^2. The successor-first program qualifies with probability bq+(1-b)p. Their difference is b(q-p)-p(1-p). If q>p, superior terminal qualification probability requires b>p(1-p)/(q-p); if q=p there is no positive probability advantage. This is an endpoint comparison of two programs, not an equilibrium dominance claim. Their early service opportunities, human/compute bills, cash, rival responses and feasible contingencies also differ. Earlier qualification is usable in the second investment date, so equal terminal probability is insufficient to make successor development beneficial.

At p=3/4, q=1 the threshold b is 3/4. The original internal procedure's b=3/4 exactly ties repeated training at 15/16. A deterministic paid license clears the threshold. The one shared research/manufacturing slot makes the substitution particularly stark. For fixed rival actions and a terminal receipt increment D per shift for qualified rather than unqualified service, the expected terminal private benefit difference is N D [b(q-p)-p(1-p)] with identical salvage and no other terminal state differences. Add early incremental net receipts E to obtain the economic comparison. In competition D is conditional on rival state and actions; integrate joint outcomes and resolve the game, not multiply aggregate MPK by a fixed price. These identities are elementary probability/dynamic investment, not new mathematical prior art.

## Positive internally produced second generation and failure modes

Under improved internal development (one compute slot; success 3/4 from procedure zero and 1 from procedure one), both firms develop at date 1. On success, cap 1 trains on date 2 and retries on failure; cap 2 produces the second procedure on date 2 and trains on date 3. On failure at date 1, ordinary qualification attempts remain available. Expected cap-2 development attempts are 2 at date 1 and 1.5 at date 2; no licenses or external successor development are purchased. The corresponding terminal qualification probability is (3/4)*1+(1/4)*(3/4)=15/16, versus (3/4)*(15/16)+(1/4)*(3/4)=57/64 for cap 1 and 3/4 for cap 0. Each additional physical qualification releases complementary resources into digital production. The terminal accepted-job difference is 2*60*(15/16-57/64)=5.625, matching the exact forward ledger (226.875 to 232.5). Both private values rise by 11.4375 and 11.736328125; combined 23.173828125. Lower-total selection preserves positive increments 12.404296875 and 11.736328125. This establishes an internally produced two-generation witness in a competitive embodied service-window restriction with active market quantities, not merely license acquisition. Actual kit production is feasible but not selected.

With the original success law and costly successors, neither cap purchases or develops successors; ordinary retry remains selected even when internal compute falls to two. Under improved success but two compute slots and costly licenses, cap 2 buys its first successor and develops its second; combined wealth falls 30.25 relative to cap 1. This is a competitive menu effect, not a single-firm value-monotonicity failure. Cheap external training and archives erase the original premium (zero) and make the improved cap-2 increment slightly negative (-0.2578125), with no selected second successor. Cheap paid successor contracts induce a hybrid path (first licensed, second produced). Under original expensive outside training the lower-total selection still yields positive paid second-generation premium 15.5.
