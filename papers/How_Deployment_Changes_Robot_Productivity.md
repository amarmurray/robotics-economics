# How deployment changes the productivity of robot capital

*Proposed introductions and research design for the RoboEcon working papers · November 25, 2026*

## Opening for *When Capabilities Become Production*

What does another hour of robot capacity produce? At an Amazon Robin workcell, an arm takes parcels from a pile and places them on mobile robots. Many grasps are physically possible. Checking every trajectory would consume time the operation cannot spare. The team trained a model on the outcomes of attempted picks and used it to rank which ones the system should check first. The fleet also contained different workcells, arms and end effectors. These details give *capacity* economic content. A motion the arm can perform, a parcel it can move on the first attempt, and an order that clears the operation by its cutoff are three different outcomes. The study measures the first stages of this chain. [Li et al., especially §§ IV–VI](https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss19/p023.pdf)

At National Grid’s Sandy Pond site, the scarce input was access. For people to inspect the hall, the utility had to shut it down. After local tests, a remotely controlled robot could collect visual and thermal readings while the hall remained in operation. Operators could use the information to plan maintenance. The same number of inspections can therefore represent a different service and a different cost. The operator’s account establishes a change in how and when inspection could be performed. Estimating avoided outage costs or improvement at another site would require additional evidence. [National Grid’s account of the Sandy Pond deployment](https://www.nationalgrid.com/stories/grid-work-stories/spot-robot-dog-carries-out-safety-inspections)

We start with an order, a buyer or internal user who decides what counts as completion, and the time at which that service is useful. A firm chooses among human, robotic and mixed ways of delivering it. A better policy adds output when it relaxes a constraint on accepted work. It can also preserve output while reducing the labor, downtime or equipment needed. This gives the production paper its question. **Which input does a technical advance make more productive, which input becomes scarce next, and who can use the resulting capacity?**

## Opening for *Recursive Firm Theory*

When the Robin arm attempts a grasp, the operation learns how that action turned out. The other grasps the system considered leave no observed physical outcome on that attempt. Changing the operating rule therefore changes the experience available to train its successor. The engineers also found failures associated with suction-cup degradation, a factor absent from their model inputs. A new model, a different sample of cases, and better maintenance are economically distinct uses of the next engineering shift. [Li et al., especially §§ V–VI](https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss19/p023.pdf)

A fleet operator can go one step further. It can use experience to revise which cases enter review, which test a technician runs on a new installation, or who may release a correction before the next shift. A revised controller changes what the machine can execute. A retained rule for selecting tests changes what the *next hour of experimentation* can discover. We call the latter change recursive when the firm’s own operation helped produce the rule and it carries forward into subsequent decisions. A human-led operator can exhibit this property. A fleet with highly autonomous execution can also run a fixed learning program for years.

This gives the second paper a sharper question. **Can two firms with the same machines, initial model and service opportunities develop different abilities to learn from the next deployment because they organized earlier work differently?** A firm earns a private advantage only to the extent that it can exercise the improvement on valuable work and keep some of the saving. Ownership of the hardware, permission to pool traces, authority to release the update, and the right to the resulting margin can reside with different actors. The Robin study establishes learning in production. Whether production improves the firm's procedure for learning at a fresh site is a further, testable proposition.

## Why these are economic objects

*Data-Driven Automation* motivates why data belongs in the production technology. Relevant data can improve machine performance, production generates the data, and its usefulness can travel between tasks. Those properties allow the allocation of work to change the future scope of automation. *Organizing Intelligence* starts from the firm as a unit of production, asks which capabilities can be copied and which people can complement each other, and gives competence and coordination different economic roles. One changes output at a given size. The other changes how output scales with the organization. Both papers earn their illustrative examples by telling the reader what the example needs to explain. [Farboodi, Koh and Xia, introduction](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35320) · [Imas, Koh and Legg, §§ 1–2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05417)

Three premises do the corresponding work here. A machine and the people who support it occupy scarce time at a particular site. Producing a service reveals a **selected** set of outcomes, determined in part by the policy choosing actions and by what the firm can observe and retain. A learned component can sometimes travel to another machine, while local hardware, permission and time to check the change can remain scarce. The extent and even the sign of transfer are empirical. These premises explain why the same installed robot can have different marginal products and why the firm can change its own future marginal products by choosing where and how to work.

Learning by doing and investment in the capacity to learn have established precedents. Our proposed contribution is to specify which field experience a deployed fleet produces, which parts can travel, and the economic decision on which the change can earn a return. Compounding is a possible result. The starting assumptions also admit an organization that learns little from extra operating hours or gains chiefly by improving ordinary maintenance. [Arrow, *The Economic Implications of Learning by Doing*](https://www.haverford.edu/sites/default/files/Arrow1962.pdf) · [Cohen and Levinthal, *Innovation and Learning*](https://academic.oup.com/ej/article-abstract/99/397/569/5188256)

## A small model with an operating interpretation

Consider one type of accepted job in one service window, with a common quality standard. The buyer has $D$ units due. A particular embodiment supplies $K_r$ hours at rate $a$ technically qualified units per hour. The operation supplies $L$ hours of complementary labor at rate $b$ units per hour. Each unit needs both stages. Suppose the work is divisible and the stages can be scheduled within the window. Then feasible accepted output is

$$Q=\min\{D,\,aK_r,\,bL\}.$$

The marginal physical product of an additional block $h>0$ of robot hours is

$$\frac{Q(K_r+h)-Q(K_r)}{h}=\frac{\min\{ah,\,[\min(D,bL)-aK_r]_+\}}{h},\qquad [x]_+=\max(x,0).$$

The numerator is extra completed units. If an operator can already serve every order due, a faster controller may save operating time while the next robot hour adds zero units. A different improvement that makes each recovery worker more effective can make extra robot hours productive by releasing the labor bottleneck when demand remains. Thus “better software” alone does not sign the change in the marginal product of another robot. Which resource the update releases and whether there is work waiting determine the prediction.

This expression measures a flow of capital services. For a purchase, compare feasible schedules with and without one additional installed machine of a specified embodiment. For a financial return, count the discounted change in the firm’s contribution, including future changes in what it learns, the alternative use of displaced resources, and the purchase cost once. Distinct services retain their physical units. Valuing them together requires prices or explicit valuations and an account of transfers between firms.

In Robin, the underlying counts in Table III imply about 14.4 fewer failed picks per 1,000 attempts with learned package ranking and center-based picking than with the topological baseline using center-based picking. This is an observed attempt-level comparison. Recoveries, repeat attempts on the same parcel, labor reassignment, original order eligibility and dispatch cutoffs determine how much of that difference reaches output or operating cost. The available table cannot supply those quantities. In power inspection, record the inspection accepted, the online or offline operating state, and the labor or outage time required. Treat avoided downtime and inspection counts as distinct outcomes. [Li et al., Table III](https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss19/p023.pdf) · [National Grid](https://www.nationalgrid.com/stories/grid-work-stories/spot-robot-dog-carries-out-safety-inspections)

## Where to look for differences across firms

| Economic question | Parcel handling | Industrial inspection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Which input could bind? | Arm time, availability of a destination for the parcel, or human recovery during a busy wave | Access to the asset while it is operating, ability to acquire a usable reading, or qualified human review |
| What could an extra shift teach? | The outcome of a chosen grasp, including traces that could distinguish package geometry from hardware wear | How to obtain a repeatable reading. Detecting a fault additionally requires a way to establish the equipment’s actual condition |
| What could travel down or up the chain? | A grasp-ranking update might benefit compatible sites. Field failures might instead change a gripper supplier’s maintenance guidance | A route or sensing procedure might transfer. An operator's maintenance outcome might improve a supplier’s detector if the outcome is linked to the reading and reuse is permitted |
| How would we measure the economic result? | Original parcels cleared by cutoff, human recovery hours and total resources per completed unit | Inspections accepted in the required operating state, downtime and review hours, plus any separately verified effect on maintenance decisions |

These are hypotheses for comparative work. Count the type of labor, record which stage of the value chain bears its cost, and check who can act on the evidence. A vertically integrated operator may both fund learning and receive its return. A site, fleet contractor or model supplier may hold those positions separately. Establish the actual rights, information and payments before predicting which organization invests more. This makes data richness a property of a decision. Another normal thermal image may add little evidence about a detector’s missed faults. A maintenance finding linked to an earlier image might change which inspection the operator orders next. More records can coexist with fewer informative cases when updates suppress the exceptions that previously generated labels.

## Tests that could change the theory

**Test the production constraint.** Keep track of all work offered, including jobs declined, retried or sent to a human. Compare equivalent changes in available robot hours at independently varied levels of support, or use preassigned support rotations with comparable order mix. If engineering gains disappear precisely when downstream capacity binds, the model explains why. If additional machines keep adding accepted units in supposedly saturated windows, inspect the proposed bottleneck and the service definition. A history containing only completed orders can conceal demand that another machine could have served. [Existing capacity analysis](https://github.com/amarmurray/robotics-economics/blob/e8cc97fed54e3815dda113bac3a7b4a333955331/research/theory_integration_2026_11_17/data_value/CENSORED_CAPACITY_ENVELOPE.md)

**Test a retained learning rule.** At a fresh site cross the incumbent and improved execution policies with two ways of choosing the next diagnostic, the firm’s retained rule and a capable fixed procedure chosen in advance. Give each arm the same starting evidence, access, test slots and budget. Let all arms update from their own ordinary work afterward. Assign at the level where updates are shared. Measure time and resources to qualify the new work, then orders accepted by the cutoff. A rule effect with the executor held fixed supports learning how to learn. A gain confined to the executor favors direct skill transfer. If the gain occurs only when both are present, the rule and controller have to be valued together. Independent inspection of normal work can also test whether the incident queue changed the sample. [Fleet learning study](https://github.com/amarmurray/robotics-economics/blob/909f738326475d6fe4a0679b5c51ee8af7594bb9/research/recursive_firm/learning_dynamics/README.md)

**Test competition where the institution fits.** The public-response model assumes a supplier sees a rival’s qualification before it commits its own engineering time. Look for dated notices and subsequent changes in review or procurement decisions. A market with private qualifications and precommitted budgets supplies a reason to expect a different result. A lower winning price alone cannot distinguish better physical production, a transfer to the buyer, or a rival that abandoned investigation. [When Faster Learning Pays](autonomous_firm/When_Faster_Learning_Pays.md)

## How I would edit the papers

- **Production.** Move a deployment and its economic unit ahead of the optimization program. Follow the same original orders through attempted execution, qualification, recovery and acceptance. Introduce the bounds when the reader encounters the missing observations that make bounds necessary. The compact paper already has the field evidence and the censored-order result. [Working manuscript, §§ 1, 4b and 5](https://github.com/amarmurray/robotics-economics/blob/e8cc97fed54e3815dda113bac3a7b4a333955331/research/theory_integration_2026_11_17/data_value/WHEN_CAPABILITIES_COMPACT_DRAFT.md)
- **Recursive firm.** Lead with the existing finding that a digital routing improvement can remove the incidents a second robot would have learned from. Identify it as a mechanism demonstrated in simulation, lay out how selective review could create it in a plant, and put the independent sample and credible fixed learning policy beside the claim. Use the portfolio of experiments to test the mechanism’s reach. [Founding argument](https://github.com/amarmurray/robotics-economics/blob/909f738326475d6fe4a0679b5c51ee8af7594bb9/research/theory/recursive_firm.md) · [Fleet study](https://github.com/amarmurray/robotics-economics/blob/909f738326475d6fe4a0679b5c51ee8af7594bb9/research/recursive_firm/learning_dynamics/README.md)
- **Autonomous firm and the learning notes.** Retain the dated, feasible allocation in *Choosing the First Deployment*. In *When Faster Learning Pays*, motivate why a capture program might change discovery, state which part is assumed, and introduce the public rival only after the private production problem is clear. The numerical examples then locate a threshold or show that a sign can reverse. Field evidence determines whether a particular operator faces it.
