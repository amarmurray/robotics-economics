# Unified treasury and entrant deployment extension

Design proposal v0.2a · 25 October 2026 · **Not implemented or calibrated**

Implementation update: the [stage 1 financial core](TREASURY_CORE.md) now executes the bounded settlement portions of this design. The complete extension described here, including monthly physical allocation, common entrant supply and service/default consequences, remains unimplemented. The proposal's numerical examples remain synthetic.

This proposal extends the executable mechanisms in [METHODS.md](METHODS.md). It changes no current engine code or saved result. The numerical examples below are synthetic accounting witnesses, not observations about robot companies, financing availability or commercial terms. Implementation should follow an independent design challenge and a scoped acceptance decision.

## 1. Decision and minimum useful outcome

The current engine correctly separates manufacturing starts, accepted units, commissioning, active stock and commercial cash allocation. Its annual funding envelopes refresh exogenously; manufacturer cash generated by earlier cohorts does not finance later cohorts. ENTRY-FG-01 has a substantive monthly demand/cash model but its assumed ten robots do not consume the annual portfolio's shared production and commissioning resources.

The next implementation should answer a bounded question:

**When does an economically attractive order or operating business fail to deploy because its actual cash, payment timing or shared physical capacity is insufficient, and which explicitly financed commercial arrangement changes that result?**

The minimum extension needs one monthly physical/financial simulation for the same modeled portfolio. It should include an incumbent and entrant competing for one compatible robot pool, a manufacturer/provider, an optional financier, actual payments and overdue obligations, and conditional reinvestment. It does not need a full banking, accounting or insolvency simulator.

Maintain four separate tests and statuses:

| Test | Question | Insufficient evidence or failure means |
|---|---|---|
| Economic feasibility | Do matched useful output and expected lifecycle resource costs create value; do proposed prices satisfy actor criteria? | An unfavorable or unknown economic case, even if money is available. |
| Contractual eligibility | Are required control, ownership, credit, customer and supply conditions satisfied? | An order or financing contract is not executable under its stated assumptions. |
| Liquidity survival | Can each actor settle due obligations and fund the next action while preserving required reserves? | A funding gap, deferred action or default state; expected revenue does not fill it. |
| Physical deliverability | Are compatible parts, accepted systems, commissioning and ongoing support available? | Delayed or unsupported capacity despite economic attractiveness and cash. |

A funded experiment can continue with negative unit economics only under an explicit loss-financing policy. A profitable operation can fail its liquidity test. Neither condition should be hidden inside a single adoption gate.

## 2. Scope and accounting perimeter

Use stable **legal actor IDs**, not separate cash pools for every role. The minimum actor set is:

- One manufacturer/provider, which may also own RaaS assets and provide maintenance.
- One incumbent operating customer and one newco operating customer.
- One optional independent asset financier/lessor.
- External counterparties for investors, suppliers, employees, installers and end-market buyers, represented as settlement endpoints rather than fully simulated businesses.

An actor may hold several roles. If the provider is also the lessor, its transactions use the same cash account; a role change does not create capital. If a parent creates several subsidiaries, each operating actor has an explicit opening balance and intercompany transfer, rather than every subsidiary inheriting the parent's entire commitment.

Every result declares its consolidation perimeter. Transfers between included actors cancel. Supplier/payroll outlays and receipts from end-market buyers cross the perimeter. Equity injections and borrowing are financing flows, not operating revenue or economic surplus. An acquisition payment transfers existing ownership; it is not additional productive capacity or new demand.

Keep operating business ownership, robot title, operational custody, workflow authority, maintenance responsibility, data rights and financing claims as separate fields. A single transaction can change some of those fields without changing the others.

## 3. One clock, explicit order of events

Use integer monthly periods for this extension. Annual reporting is an aggregation of monthly flows and an end-of-year stock snapshot; annual stock must never be summed into a robot count. A month contains deterministic event phases so later receipts cannot finance earlier spending:

1. Carry opening cash, restricted cash, receivables, payables, debt, physical stocks, orders and outstanding reservations forward.
2. Settle receivables and previously authorized financing draws due at the start of the month. A draw requires an available commitment and satisfaction of conditions known from prior verified state. Settlement failures leave obligations outstanding.
3. Process due mandatory payments and apply contractual grace/default rules. Preserve unpaid balances. Do not spend money assigned to segregated deposits, legally restricted accounts or explicit minimum liquidity reserves.
4. Receive scheduled qualified inputs and complete previously scheduled assembly/acceptance and commissioning events. Release or reclassify physical and financial commitments according to their contract milestones. Default/cancellation may change the receiving actor; it cannot create a new physical unit.
5. Admit discretionary actions against **both** remaining financial and physical capacity: component/start commitments, new production, shipping, customer acquisition and installation. Accepted units completed in phase 4 can ship in this phase; they incur no accidental extra month in finished stock. Reserve resources atomically, then commit accepted actions. A rejected action releases all tentative reservations.
6. Operate commissioned, supported cohorts. Charge or accrue the specified costs, deliver output limited by demand, and issue end-market and robot-service invoices. Invoices issued now become cash only on their specified settlement date, ordinarily a later month.
7. Close books, age overdue obligations, calculate economic and liquidity diagnostics, and emit the next state's stocks and scheduled events.

Within-month delivery followed by a same-month deposit-funded start is excluded initially. Permit such event chains only later through explicitly ordered timestamps. This conservative convention avoids circular financing, for example recognizing a future robot sale to fund the start required to make that robot.

Specify lead times between events, not merely year/month labels. A start in month `t`, phase 5 with assembly lead `L ≥ 1` completes in month `t+L`, phase 4; it may ship that month in phase 5. A shipment in month `s`, phase 5 with commissioning lead `C ≥ 1` commissions in month `s+C`, phase 4. Its first operating month is `s+C`, phase 6, at the explicitly configured first-month ramp (0.25 in the entrant fixture). Do not also subtract an inherited annual half-year ramp. Zero-lead-time actions are outside this initial phase contract.

Annual qualified receipts from the existing synthetic scenario require a **declared monthly receipt profile**. Equal monthly delivery, a ramp or year-end batching are alternative assumptions, not recovered observations. Preserve the annual total and test timing sensitivity. Observed annual capacity must not silently become observed monthly availability. Lead times become declared month counts; conversion from the old annual convention must be documented and tested rather than inferred from a year label.

## 4. Cash, invoices and financing contracts

For each actor and month:

```text
closing_cash = opening_cash
             + collected_operating_receipts + financing_draws + equity_contributions
             + recoveries + other_settled_receipts
             - settled_operating_payments - investment_payments
             - interest_paid - principal_repaid - distributions - refunds

spendable_cash = cash - restricted_cash - minimum_liquidity_reserve
                - unconsumed_earmarked_commitments
```

Cash is a bank-balance stock. Revenue, an invoice, an approved order, an undrawn commitment, expected collateral recovery and future refinancing are not cash. Earmarking is a reservation, not a second payment. A reservation must be released when its linked payment occurs or its action is canceled.

Encumbrances must be disjoint: a segregated dollar cannot simultaneously be subtracted as restricted cash, an unrestricted earmark and a liquidity-buffer dollar. A payment may consume its **own** eligible earmark atomically. For payment A, availability includes A's reservation while excluding reservations for unrelated actions; the transaction debits cash and consumes A's reservation together. A balance of 100 earmarked entirely for invoice A of 100 can therefore pay A, even though free cash for new action B is zero. An escrow refund similarly consumes the matching restricted balance rather than borrowing unrelated operating cash. Reject overlapping reservations at creation.

Each invoice has a face amount, payer, payee, issue date, due date, paid amount, outstanding amount and status. A partial payment reduces the same obligation. Payment produces one transaction with a payer debit and payee credit; the receiving actor cannot collect money the payer did not settle. A contractual write-off changes the receivable/payable accounting according to the agreed treatment; it is not another cash outflow. Recoveries have their own later cash events.

Specify settlement granularity. The minimum's default is `full_or_nothing`: if the entire due amount cannot be settled under the cash/reserve policy, retain the cash and mark the full amount unpaid. An explicit `partial_allowed` contract instead settles the permitted amount and carries the remainder. Service availability may still require full settlement. Do not allow an undocumented partial-payment rule to change a default witness.

Default financing should be an explicitly committed equity schedule plus, optionally, one simple loan/credit contract. Define commitment size, availability dates, permitted uses, draw conditions, revolving/nonrevolving status, principal outstanding, interest, repayment schedule, maturity and default consequences. A revolving line permits redraw only after actual repayment. A nonrevolving line tracks cumulative draws separately from outstanding principal. Do not recreate the full commitment each year.

Interest accrual and interest payment are separate. Principal repayment reduces debt, not operating profit. No refinancing occurs unless an additional commitment and its terms are supplied. A maturity balloon is due even if a later horizon would have made the operation profitable.

Reinvestment is a **decision rule applied to real cash once**. After mandatory obligations, restrictions and liquidity buffers, a configurable fraction of the residual may be reserved for new production or deployment. Retained cash stays in the account. Do not add operating cash generation to an unchanged exogenous annual capital allowance: that would count the same funding twice. External injections remain separately dated and identified.

## 5. Manufacturing costs and commercial settlement

Preserve a single manufacturing cash boundary. For the minimum extension, retain the existing **all-in cash outlay per assembly start**. It includes the configured component use and conversion cost. Component receipts are a synthetic supplier-financed, qualified-access/consignment pool until issued to a start. The provider pays the all-in outlay at start; it does not also pay a second component invoice on receipt or a per-accepted-unit manufacturing amount at commissioning.

This is a restrictive financing boundary, not a claim that physical inventory is free. Upstream supplier working capital lies outside the modeled actors and is an explicit exclusion. A later itemized variant may pay component purchases on receipt and conversion labor over time, but its contract must **replace**, not supplement, the bundled cost. Require a mutually exclusive `manufacturing_cash_basis` value to prevent both conventions being active.

Physical yield is applied once to starts. Actual start cash includes the rejected units. A projected per-accepted-unit economic cost can equal expected start cost divided by expected yield, but that estimate does not trigger a second cash charge. Realized cohort cost attribution follows the start lots and acceptance results. Zero accepted output leaves a sunk loss and possible remaining work in progress; it does not erase the cash spent.

Commercial contracts determine payment timing and title without changing physical manufacturing expenditure:

| Arrangement | Minimum settlement policy | Asset/control consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Sale | Optional order deposit, then an invoice at a stated acceptance/title milestone with a stated collection lag. | Customer owns the accepted asset under the title clause; provider may retain service obligations. |
| Lease | Financier pays manufacturer at acceptance; operator pays rent and separately specified service fees. | Financier owns the robot. The operator must not also pay the full acquisition price. |
| Provider-owned service | Provider funds starts and retains the asset; operator pays availability/usage fees on their invoice schedule. | Provider owns the robot; workflow authority and data rights remain separately contracted. |

Default to no customer deposits for comparability with the current entrant case. If enabled, deposits need a refund obligation and a contractually explicit `restricted` or `spendable` status. Segregated deposits cannot finance manufacturing. A spendable refundable deposit can finance it but creates refund exposure; cancellation does not reverse the original manufacturing cash.

Sale proceeds arrive only when paid. Acceptance revenue and cash collection are separate. Integration outlays occur at their actual work milestones, not both inside hardware price and again as an unexplained system cost. Invoice IDs link each payment to its actor, order, physical lot or service period.

## 6. Connect the entrant to the common physical ledger

Replace the newco's independent equation `10 × 100 × uptime × ramp` with requested capacity and **assigned, commissioned cohorts** from the common ledger. Ten is an order target, not an installed fleet. The entrant and incumbent compete for the same compatible components, accepted finished units, shipment capacity, commissioning crews and support resources under the declared allocation rule.

For each cohort, maintain:

```text
system configuration → production lot → accepted units → shipped units
→ commissioning cohort → owner/custodian → activity assignment → supported output
```

The same unit cannot be simultaneously active at two operating customers. Reallocation transfers custody/assignment through a downtime and recommissioning event. It is neither new manufacture nor retirement. A financier ownership change likewise does not add a robot. Existing service demand is reserved before net additions.

Accepted capacity becomes:

```text
sum(assigned commissioned robot-months
    × work-profile accepted units per fully productive robot-month
    × commissioning ramp × incremental support/availability factors)
```

The output-rate contract says which downtime, quality and intervention effects are already included. ENTRY-FG-01's 100 bundles per robot-month is already after its reference downtime; do not multiply reference uptime again. If an uptime sensitivity remains, use an explicit ratio to that reference or replace the net rate with a fully decomposed rate—never both.

The annual floor-cleaning example's `cleaning zone` and ENTRY-FG-01's `service bundle` have **no observed conversion**. The recommended minimum is to create one versioned work profile, `WORK-FLOOR-BUNDLE-SYN-02`, for the integrated entrant/incumbent case. Both operators deliver that same accepted bundle and service standard. In the joint competition scenario this activity pool replaces the old floor-cleaning fixture; it does not sit beside an unchanged copy of the same market activity.

Other market cells may retain their own work units while drawing on the common physical robot pool. Summing robot stocks is permitted. Summing unlike services is not. Baseline-equivalent labor-hour aggregation for the new bundle profile requires an explicit synthetic hours-per-bundle assumption. If it is absent, return an incomplete aggregate and its excluded coverage; do not substitute zero or infer a conversion from prices. Preserve the old six-cell scenario as a separate versioned reference, not an additive business case.

Use two distinct experiment modes:

1. **Alternative worlds:** the same initial supply, customer and demand state is replayed under sale/lease/service or different incumbent timing. Only one world contributes to an aggregate.
2. **Joint competition world:** incumbent and entrant simultaneously share supply and one demand ledger. Their output, cash and allocations reconcile together.

A standalone entrant reference cannot claim that the incumbent has equal access to ten additional robots while also assuming the entrant owns the entire available pool. Technology access, actual procurement priority and physical availability are separate conditions.

## 7. Demand, customer receipts and cash survival

Maintain one demand ledger by activity and period. Separate incumbent-served baseline demand, existing unmet demand and genuinely additional demand. An entrant taking an incumbent customer transfers the same underlying work; it does not increase baseline activity. Only delivered accepted output generates a bill, and only the payment rule generates cash.

Demand capture is bounded by customer qualification, switching, retention, prices and contract timing. Competitor response does not itself produce capacity. If an entrant fails, its customer demand becomes unserved or enters a switching queue; the displaced incumbent share is not automatically restored without incumbent capacity and a reassignment decision. Revenue, contracted orders, produced output, customer receipts and outstanding receivables stay separate.

The newco must fund formation, customer acquisition, overhead, site work, robot terms, complementary assets, ramp losses and receivables. Asset finance covers only the items its contract permits. Leasing the robots cannot automatically fund the enterprise's payroll or customer acquisition.

Run two explicitly separate paths:

- **Funded execution:** no unauthorized overdraft. Block discretionary starts/installations when cash is insufficient. Preserve existing commitments and unpaid liabilities. Operate only under funded inputs or explicitly allowed trade credit.
- **Additional-funding counterfactual:** calculate the funding bridge necessary to follow the proposed plan, with injected amounts shown as synthetic additional equity. It is a different scenario, not the funded path continuing below zero cash.

Report lifetime unit economics, minimum cash balance, peak external funding needed, draw use, first missed obligation, time in financial distress, actual served output and stranded capital. A positive NPV or gross margin does not override a missed payment. Investor returns and valuations remain outside the minimum scope.

## 8. Loss, delinquency and asset recovery policy

Keep financial default, contract delinquency, service withdrawal, physical outage, retirement and repossession as separate states.

The minimum policy is configurable but simple: a due noncancelable obligation that cannot be paid remains outstanding; contractual grace determines when it is delinquent/defaulted; default freezes new discretionary investment and unavailable credit. Maintenance or software/support withdrawal changes **supported productive capacity** according to the contract. It does not delete the physically installed robot.

An asset owner may recover an asset only through an explicit repossession/custody event after a stated delay and cost. Recovery can require transport, inspection, repairs and recommissioning. A returned robot moves into a recoverable/finished/refurbishment stock, with one persistent physical identity. Residual sale proceeds accrue to the rightful owner subject to the financing waterfall; they are not separately credited to the manufacturer and financier.

For v0.2a, use deterministic zero-recovery or fixed-recovery stress assumptions, not an estimated default probability. Retain losses and unpaid balances through the horizon. No bankruptcy discharge, debt acceleration, government support, capital raising or asset sale is assumed unless its rule is present. Pending orders can be canceled; already spent WIP remains sunk and refund liabilities survive.

## 9. Minimal data contracts

All quantities carry units, period, synthetic/evidence status and links to their source assumptions. IDs join the current market, system, work and evidence records.

| Record | Minimum new fields |
|---|---|
| Actor cash account | `actor_id`, legal entity, roles, opening cash, restricted cash, reserve rule, consolidation group, currency |
| Financial obligation/invoice | `invoice_id`, payer/payee, contract/order/cohort link, issue/due dates, face amount, settled amount, outstanding amount, cancellation/grace/write-off status |
| Cash transaction | `transaction_id`, date/phase, payer, payee, amount, category, invoice/financing link, restriction change, settlement status |
| Funding contract | `funding_id`, funder/borrower, equity/debt type, commitment, availability, draw condition, revolving rule, interest, maturity, repayment, permitted use, default/recovery rules |
| Deployment contract | `contract_id`, actors/roles, title milestone, ownership/custody/control, price/fee basis, deposit treatment, billing/collection terms, termination, service/default/recovery clauses |
| Physical lot/cohort | Existing system/lot/cohort IDs plus order allocation, start cash links, accepted/rejected quantities, owner/custodian, commissioning state, replacement link, activity assignment |
| Resource reservation | `reservation_id`, resource/actor, action/cohort, quantity, reserve/release/consume dates, status; cash reservations are not expenses |
| Work profile | `work_id`, accepted outcome/quality, unit, baseline workload, output-rate convention, already-included downtime, compatible systems, optional explicit baseline-hour conversion |
| Demand account | `activity_id`, period, baseline-served/unmet/new-demand pools, customer assignment, contracted/qualified/delivered/billed quantities, switching/retention state |
| Monthly actor result | Opening/closing cash, collected receipts, payments, accrued obligations, debt, funding gap, blocked actions, distress state, explicit hypothetical injections |

Transactions should be append-only within a run and reproducible from the scenario. Reversals reference the original transaction; they are not silent edits to balances. No second independent newco supply or finance calculator should survive integration.

## 10. Adverse-test witnesses

These are exact, deliberately small synthetic fixtures. Monetary amounts below are in **thousands of USD** unless a service unit is specified. They test mechanics, not commercially plausible scale.

| Witness | Initial conditions and action | Required result |
|---|---|---|
| Profitable but temporarily unfundable | Provider cash 50; payroll 20 due now; minimum reserve 10; new start costs 40; customer receipt 60 arrives next month. | After payroll, spendable cash is 20: block the start despite its expected 20 gross margin. With an explicitly available 20 debt draw, pay payroll/start and close with cash 10, debt 20. Next month's 60 receipt and 20 principal repayment leave cash 50. The draw is not operating revenue. |
| Yield loss paid once | Provider cash 100; two starts cost 36 each; final yield 50%; one accepted unit sells for 60. | Cash is 28 before collection and 88 after collection: loss 12. Accepted-unit manufacturing cost is 72, but acceptance/commissioning creates no second 72 payment. In a lease, financier pays the 60 price and operator does not also pay it. |
| Restricted refundable deposit | Customer cash 30; provider unrestricted cash 30; customer pays a segregated refundable deposit 20; proposed start costs 40. | Customer cash 10; provider cash 50, of which 20 restricted. Start remains blocked. Cancellation refunds 20, restoring customer 30 and provider unrestricted 30. No revenue or new financing appears. |
| Shared entrant supply/support | Five accepted robots available; incumbent requests four, entrant ten. Three commissioning slots; existing two robots consume two of five support units. Allocate two new robots to incumbent, one to entrant. | Commission three, leave two finished, and end with five installed including the prior two. Entrant's one robot at 100 bundles/month and 25% initial ramp supplies capacity 25, not 1,000. Component, financing, commissioning and support reservations reconcile once. |
| Default preserves assets and liabilities | Entrant has one leased robot and cash 8; a noncancelable availability fee 10 is due; no grace/borrowing; service requires payment. | Fee payable remains 10, cash stays 8, financier collects zero, current production is zero. Robot remains installed/inactive until recovery. Repossession is a transfer, not manufacture. |
| Failed entrant does not restore output magically | Before failure, entrant delivers 100 units: 70 incumbent-share, 20 unmet-demand, 10 new-demand; original incumbent output 1,000. | Combined output before failure is 930 + 100 = 1,030. On failure, no phantom entrant output remains and the 70 lost incumbent units are not automatically restored without capacity/customer switching. |
| Consume an earmark atomically | Cash 100, all earmarked for due invoice A of 100; unrelated proposed action B costs 1. No other restriction/buffer. | B is blocked. A settles: cash and earmark both become zero, invoice A becomes paid. Repeating A cannot release or pay another 100. Treating the earmark as an extra cash expense is invalid. |
| Event lead time and first exposure | Start one unit in month 0 phase 5; assembly lead one month; accepted yield one; shipping unconstrained; commissioning lead one month; full capacity 100 bundles/month, initial ramp 0.25. | Accept in month 1 phase 4, ship month 1 phase 5, commission month 2 phase 4, and offer capacity 25 in month 2 phase 6. No extra finished-stock month and no second annual half-year penalty. |

Additional invariants must test: double-entry settlement; nonnegative actual cash without explicit overdraft; commitment exhaustion; principal/interest separation; persistent overdue balances; no double consumption of reservations; no free commissioning after an unfunded order; no use of future invoices; no phantom activity from acquisitions; exact annual-to-monthly receipt totals; output limited by assigned cohorts/demand; one title/custodian per physical unit; replacement versus expansion; recovery stock conservation; and unchanged empirical evidence under financial overrides.

## 11. Implementation sequence and acceptance boundary

1. Add ledger data structures and pure settlement/reservation functions with the numeric witnesses. Keep the existing annual reference runnable while the new engine is developed under an explicit version.
2. Port one system's component/start/acceptance/commissioning/support identities to the monthly clock. Use a funded incumbent-only fixture to prove conservation before adding competition.
3. Implement sale, lease and provider-owned service as alternative payment/title schedules on the same physical plan. Verify consolidated resource expenditure is invariant to internal price transfers.
4. Replace the independent entrant capacity calculation with actual cohort assignments; use one floor-service activity pool and a matched incumbent. Add the joint competition world and the separate additional-funding counterfactual.
5. Extend the tested ledger to the other configurations/cells, add resource/finance sensitivities, then expose cash survival and blocked-action reasons in the existing dashboard. Regenerate all report tables from the common engine.

Acceptance requires the eight exact witnesses, ledger/stock conservation, an economically favorable but cash-infeasible case, an adequately funded but supply-constrained case, a funded negative-economics case under an explicit experiment budget, and a case in which leasing frees customer cash while constraining financier/provider cash. The newco cannot report ten deployed robots unless the common ledger actually commissions them.

The minimum remains a continuous expected-flow model where supply yields produce fractional quantities. It is not an executable integer factory schedule. It excludes taxes, stochastic credit underwriting, bank capital rules, full insolvency law, supplier treasury outside the bundled-start boundary, endogenous equity valuations, borrower-specific observed credit terms, general equilibrium demand and empirically calibrated default/learning functions. These exclusions must remain visible; the extension closes cash-timing and shared-entrant-resource gaps, not the program's calibration gap.

Next design challenge: **Is the restricted all-in-start cash boundary sufficient to answer the first financing decision, or does the selected case require itemized component receipts and supplier payment terms before its liquidity result is meaningful?** If that boundary changes the sign or timing of the funding conclusion, prioritize the itemized variant before interpreting the scenario.
