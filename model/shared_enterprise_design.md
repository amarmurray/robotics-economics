# Shared entrant demand and enterprise cash: stage 4 design

This design replaces the independent entrant capacity calculation in the **new joint comparison**, while preserving ENTRY-FG-01 as a historical separate reference. It extends the existing monthly physical/Treasury/commercial loop; it does not create another robot supply or cash-balance engine. All terms remain explicitly synthetic. One agent implements and reviews it.

## One work profile and one market

Use `WORK-FLOOR-BUNDLE-SYN-02`, with one accepted bundle definition for both operators. Robot capacity comes only from assigned, commissioned and supported unit IDs, with the shared engine's single first-month ramp. The rate already includes stipulated downtime/quality; no second uptime multiplier applies. A separately declared human shift provides a finite alternative capacity only when its committed staffing obligation is paid. The monthly physical ledger continues to report robot output; the market ledger reports human output and their combined accepted service once.

Maintain recurring monthly demand in three disjoint pools: incumbent baseline, existing unmet demand, and hypothetical newly created demand. Baseline customers initially belong to the incumbent; the other pools start unassigned. Explicit dated customer moves transfer assignments, subject to qualification, paid formation/acquisition and source availability. A customer's work is not added a second time when captured. Within each operator, actual accepted output is allocated proportionally across its assigned pools, with exact rational quantities. Unserved work is lost for that service period, not silently carried into future demand.

Customer assignments persist after a supplier fails. Work returns to another operator only through a later explicit, eligible reassignment and available delivery capacity. An incumbent response may change procurement eligibility, prices or customer assignments, but cannot manufacture robots or customer receipts. Same technology is common access to the declared system; finite procurement allocations remain competitive.

## Enterprise obligations and event order

Each operator has explicit activation, qualification and procurement dates, formation expense, recurring overhead, human-staffing obligations, acquisition cost per captured recurring unit, output price and material-use cost. Staffing capacity is paid as a committed shift, not automatically reduced when robots or competitors release work. Material use is billed on delivered output under explicit one-month supplier credit. End-market invoices are issued only for accepted delivered service, with separately stated collection lags. Exact rational charges are rounded once per operator/month to the nearest cent, half up, retaining the unrounded amount and rounding difference.

Phase 1 issues formation, fixed operating/staffing obligations and contingent customer-acquisition bookings; month 0 also records explicit future equity commitments. Phase 2 collects scheduled opening receivables, due end-market invoices and actual funded equity transfers, then the inherited contract collection rules. Phase 3 attempts refunds and due operator obligations oldest-first before physical support; unpaid noncancelable obligations remain. Phase 4 retains the physical acceptance/commissioning rules. Phase 5 retains shipment/start priority and then tries declared customer moves. Phase 6 delivers service from funded robots/human shifts, issues output/material invoices, and applies the inherited supported-availability billing. Phase 7 reconciles demand, delivered output, stock, cash, commitments and unpaid obligations.

A missed due operator obligation freezes its new procurement, customer acquisition and delivery until arrears are paid. Installed robots and customer assignments survive. The physical loop's conservative existing-support priority still blocks additions when any opening installed unit lacks support. This is a declared deterministic suspension rule, not insolvency law or observed default probability. Future receivables, equity promises and credit commitments are not cash.

Equity is a transfer from an explicitly funded external investor, recorded separately from sales. No valuation, investor return, bank-money creation or automatic loan draw is inferred. Compare ordinary funded execution with a distinct additional-upfront-equity counterfactual. If searching for a bridge, test the smallest integer-cent addition within a declared interval that executes the fixed requested action path without unpaid due operator obligations. This is a sufficient-funding search for one fixed policy, not optimal fundraising or a claim that capital is available.

## Required witnesses and integration

Reproduce the 70/20/10 customer split without double counting: baseline 1,000, entrant delivery 100 with 70 transferred baseline, 20 unmet and 10 new gives combined delivery 1,030 when the incumbent delivers its remaining 930. On entrant failure, retain its 70 customer assignments and show unserved work rather than automatically restoring incumbent output. Test explicit switching back separately. Compare prompt/delayed incumbent procurement and customer response using the same technology, physical pool and original demand. Show that ten requested entrant robots need not mean ten installed; actual unit IDs and support determine capacity.

Test formation/overhead before investment, acquisition eligibility, zero supplier/robot capacity, exact fractional invoices, collection timing, restricted cash, unpaid equity commitments, finite investor cash, preserved title, customer assignment conservation, delivered-work-to-invoice joins and both prior stage result payloads unchanged. Replace the primary customer dashboard example with this shared calculation; retain the old entrant illustration only as a labeled historical reference, never sum it into the new market.

The initial stage keeps flat recurring demand levels, explicit move schedules, deterministic human capacity and zero asset recovery. Autonomous reinvestment, an optimal competitor, loan repayment/maturity scheduling, full insolvency, real customer retention, labor release, prices and physical qualification remain open. Annual cleaning-zone units have no observed conversion to these bundles; baseline-equivalent labor hours are unknown unless separately assumed. The K lifetime architecture model remains separate until an explicit monthly cost bridge is verified.
