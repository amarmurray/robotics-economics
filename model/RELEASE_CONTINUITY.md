# Reviewed releases and continuing service

This CPU-only extension binds a later software release to the existing paid-development, physical unit, Treasury, legal-role, qualified-resource, paid-crew and actual-award history. The hardware configuration does not change. Candidate coordination, review outcome and entitlements are explicit synthetic premises. No training, physical experiment, empirical learning rate or vendor contract is simulated or inferred.

## Separate clocks and rights

The retained version is annual index 0 (2026); the candidate is index 1 (2027). The eight-month window runs July 2026 through February 2027. The candidate cannot be reviewed before its specified availability. The default review occurs in January, month 6, on the named already installed and initially qualified robot. A distinct evidence identity, collection permission and evidence-use rights are required. A paid declared pass allows adoption only in the following month. Review evidence rights and candidate operating rights are separate; one does not establish the other.

Until adoption is eligible, the already qualified version remains usable only while its declared legacy support entitlement is in force. Hardware ownership and a recorded old qualification cannot extend that entitlement. A candidate pass supplies no parts, annual support capacity, actual service or paid crew. A changed performance signature still needs the existing funded deployment-qualification booking and all normal resource gates. There is no automatic same-month old-version rollback after a failed changed-version booking. Service missed before adoption is never backfilled.

## Work, funding and conservation

The review adds specialist evaluation, separate from initial model work and the existing deployment qualification. It uses the same finite architecture engineering pool. The default 40 hours at the existing assumed engineering price cost $4,000, paid by the model-supplier legal account to external resources. No new customer fee is invented. A blocked booking performs no work and is cancelled without leaving a partial payment. A completed failed review retains its hours and payment. Moving funds to another legal party does not fund this payer.

A changed performance signature retains the existing $500 deployment-qualification invoice and two hours in the shared provider-support pool. If the candidate has identical modeled performance, the separate review remains paid but this existing qualification rule adds no deployment bill; this is an explicit convention, not a real vendor entitlement. Initial development, review engineering, provider support and site crews remain distinct work. Every external resource payment is counted once; internal fees are transfers. Cash, physical stock, qualified components, crew time, customer awards and terminal obligations use the existing conservation checks. The model does not impose unmeasured review wear, invent a new fleet, or supply an alternative product without qualification.

## Comparisons and limitations

Six complete executions compare a prospective passed release; failed review with supported legacy service; failed review with expired legacy support; retained paid staffing under expiry; paid pass without candidate operating rights; and a deliberate supported-legacy policy without review. All start with the same service units: cleaning zones and separate inventory jobs. The staffing policy may differ; that policy and its costs remain visible.

The supported-legacy no-review execution exactly preserves the preceding paid-development control's cash and service. With expiry after December and a prior staff reduction, January and February can leave market demand unassigned even when both companies complete their assigned work and inventory duties. Retaining staff can preserve whole service without restoring robot support. This is a conditional policy comparison, not a real vendor recommendation, universal wage threshold, service price or lifecycle ROI. Terminal hardware and future entitlements remain unvalued.

## Reproduction

Run `python3 scripts/export_release_continuity.py`, then `python3 scripts/export_release_continuity.py --check`. Replay a saved case with `--case FILE --output FILE`. Run `python3 -m unittest tests.test_release_continuity -v`. Complete opening contracts, dated plans and rights are replayed; derived results and saved hashes are ignored. `/api/release-continuity` and the existing Customer/Commercial selector expose six cases, legal-party funding, engineering, crew/support, review outcome, evidence rights, operating rights and legacy-expiry controls. Pending, invalid or stale edits preserve the last applied export.
